# noticed a pattern they were never told about.

# THREE GENERATIONS OF TROLL STONEWORK, AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT STILL KNOWS
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Zul'Marosh in the north is a drowned stair held by a remnant who cannot tell you what it was for.
# Zul'Kundaraz in the south is under the sea. The Gurbashi never lost anything, because they stopped
# being a war band and became a business, and the arena is the only piece of that stonework still being
# used for the thing it was cut for. Zul'tamo answers the HOW and says so: the ring is not a tradition,
# it is a floor plan, and the two faces that would let you choose your order were never cut because a
# mason three generations back did not want the order to be a choice. The WHY he points south, under
# water, and says he has not been. Northern asks the question. This zone answers half. Southern keeps
# the rest.
#
# Grom'gol's supply line is Northern's: referenced twice, built on never. The Bay's tariff fight with
# Ratchet is Southern's and is not here. The Bay's appetite for the arena is, and it is a better seam.
#
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# VOICE
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Nobody in this zone is a careful narrator. Vrak keeps a tally nobody asked for. Dubo talks in camber
# and kerb stone. Bexxil talks in prices and says the quiet part on purpose. Zul'tamo talks about
# courses of stone the way a man talks about his family, because it is. Ordek is glad to see you and
# cannot stop being wrong out loud. Ulua answers questions with the number and nothing else. Garza is
# covering a mistake for eleven weeks and the prose knows it before she says it.
#
# Every turn-in is a REACTION and every one of them disagrees with its own accept text about something.
# No thank-yous. No "brave adventurer", no "these lands", no rhetorical-question openers.

@ stvc_no_bed_between
Title: No Bed Between Here and the Bay
D: Sentry Vrak has stood at Grom'gol's south gate for three years telling people that the road is the whole of the
   accommodation between here and the harbour. She has stopped enjoying saying it. She keeps a tally because nobody
   asked her to. Eleven went south this month and nine came back through her gate, and she does not know which two
   are still walking.
A: Walk it. Not the first mile - down to the ford where the Veilfall crosses the road, which is where the raptors
   have been working, because that is the one place on the road a person has to slow down. Ten of them, if you can
   manage ten, and look at the stones in the ford while you are standing in it. If anyone went into that water I
   want to know before their mother does.
T: Nothing in the ford. Good. No. Not good. I wanted them in the ford, because a man in a ford is a man I can
   account for. Two people walked off the end of my tally and the road has nothing to say about it, and that is the
   part of this post nobody warned me about.

@ stvc_ganger_cut
Title: The Road Does Not Keep Itself
D: Road-Ganger Dubo's gang keeps the Vine Road cut, and the Gurbashi pay for it, because the arena needs a crowd
   and a crowd needs something to walk on. Bloodvine takes a cut road back in nine days. He has measured it. He
   will tell you the number the way other people tell you their children's names.
A: You will want to know how a road works, so. There is a camber on it - the middle sits high and the water runs
   off the sides, and if the water does not run off the sides you have a river with ambitions. Kerb stone holds the
   camber. Vine comes through the kerb. Cut me six good lengths of it out of the kerb line and put eight of the
   green adders off the verge while you are down there. They nest in the drainage. They should not be in the
   drainage.
T: You cleared the drains. I did not ask you to clear the drains. I explained the drains. Those are different and I
   am aware they are different. Where you're from they would have been somebody else's drains, I expect. Down here
   the road is whoever last cut it. Right. Bexxil is up on the apron and she has been wanting a name. I will give
   her yours, and you can decide afterward whether that was a kindness.

@ stvc_tout_entry
Title: Somebody Has Already Written Your Name
D: Tout Bexxil works the board, and the board is in Booty Bay, nailed up inside the tavern where the Baron can see
   it and pretend not to. She entered you this morning. She opened you at a price, the price moved twice before
   noon, and she is not going to tell you either number. What she wants now is a card worth watching, and the
   Gurbashi have been thinning it themselves.
A: The drovers are running undersized stock into the crates and signing it in at weight. Thin card, thin crowd,
   thin money, and I am the one holding paper on a thin afternoon. Nine of the drovers and four of their tallies.
   And before you ask why I bet on somebody I have never watched fight - that is the entire reason. Nobody down
   here knows what you people can do. Not knowing is where my money lives. Nobody has a form line on you lot, and
   the rim likes that in a fighter more than it likes a fighter.
T: You did it where they could see you. The price came in. Do you understand what that means? The price coming in
   is the money going out, and the money was going to come to me. I have had to put my own purse on you to hold the
   line. That is not a compliment. That is a relationship, and you did not agree to it, and neither did I.

@ stvc_tout_staked
Title: Staked
D: Bexxil's own money is on the board now, which has changed what she is. A tout can walk away from a bad
   afternoon. A backer cannot. There is one piece of paper left between you and the sand, and it goes to the
   Gurbashi gatehouse, and the gate crew do not take a goblin's word for anything that is not a weight.
A: Take the chit up the apron to the broker and put it in his hand. Not on the table. In the hand, and stand there
   while he reads it. Seven of the gate crew will try to stop you getting that far, because that is the job they
   are paid for and they will do it without any feeling about it. Do not take it personally and do not take it
   slowly.
T: She has filled the chit out in her own hand. Name, weight, reach, standing - and in the box where a house goes
   she has put outsider. Zul'tamo reads it out flat, the way he would read a weight. 'Outsider. Good. Not from here
   is a column on this sheet, not an insult - we have had it longer than you have been in the world and it prices
   better than any other line on it. You are on the list. Come down when the drums start.'

@ stvc_fenn_weather
Title: Ordek Asks About Rain
D: Ordek Fenn has had a drying rack and a tarp on the dry shoulder of the Veilfall lip for two years, and he sells
   to Grom'gol through whoever is walking north. He is pleased to see you in a way nobody else in this jungle has
   been, and he does not make a performance of it. He has a question he has been saving and it is not about the
   jungle.
A: There is a band of moss along the lip where the spray reaches and nowhere else - seven good handfuls would do me
   the season. Mind the leeches in the slack water under the pool. Eight of them and the shallows are yours. And
   while you are gone, think about this for me: where you're from, does the rain come in a season, or does it come
   all year? Everything I know about a plant assumes a wet month and a dry month. If it rains all year where you
   came from then half of what I know is local, and I would very much like to find out which half.
T: A season, then. Good. That is one thing I do not have to throw out. Now the moss. Look at where you picked it -
   only the band. Only where the water reaches. That is not a jungle plant. That is a waterfall plant, and there is
   one waterfall. I have been selling it to the camp as common for two years and pricing it accordingly, and I have
   just made myself considerably poorer by asking you a question.

@ stvc_fenn_lit_fall
Title: Somebody Lights It From Inside
D: After dark the sheet of the Veilfall glows. Ordek has watched it do that from a camp stool for two years and
   told himself it was the water doing something clever with the moonlight. Water does not do that. Lamps do that.
   Lamps mean wicks and oil and somebody trimming them, and that means the cave behind the fall has had people in
   it the entire time he has been sitting outside its door.
A: The panthers work the lip at dusk, which is the honest reason I have never walked round to look - seven of them
   between my rack and the notch, most evenings, and I am a man with a trowel. Go round to the mouth. That is all.
   Stand at it and come back and tell me whether there is a door there, because I have spent two years deciding
   there was not one.
T: Carved steps. Carved steps and a lamp bracket with oil still in it. Two years. Two years I have been camped at
   somebody's front door, selling their moss, and I never knocked, and I told myself a story about moonlight so I
   would not have to. You knocked. I am going to need a moment about that and I would rather have it alone.

@ stvc_zuljana_names
Title: It Has a Name. Several.
D: Attache Zul'jana is the only person inside Grom'gol's fence who is from this jungle, and she has spent three
   years listening to a garrison name things. The camp calls it the waterfall cave. The Zul'Rath who keep it have
   four names for it and which one they use depends on which terrace they are standing on. She will give you one.
   She is keeping the others and she does not feel she owes you an explanation for that.
A: Zul'Dakai. That is the one you may have, and it means the stone the water argues with - you can carry that word
   north and the camp can put it on a map and be pleased with itself. Each terrace has a name stone set into its
   lip. Bring me four of them. The mistcallers will not let you stand on a terrace long enough to read one, so you
   will have to go through eight of them first, and I am not going to pretend to you that I like asking for that.
T: Four stones, four names, and the fourth one is not a name for the cave at all. It is a name for the water. And
   it is the same word the Gurbashi use for the sand on their arena floor - the exact same word, same two sounds,
   and one lot means a river and the other lot means a killing-ground. I have known both of those words my whole
   life and never once put them next to each other. Go and ask the gatehouse what they think they are saying.

@ stvc_zuljana_font
Title: The Font, and the Argument With It
D: The shrine terraces are the part a visitor notices. The Font at the east end is where the Zul'Rath do the actual
   work, and the work is measurement: a knotted cord let into the water every morning and read against the one from
   the morning before. Three years ago the fall changed. More water, and in the wrong month. They have been arguing
   with it since, and the argument is written down.
A: Five cords and the chamber they are read in. Nothing else - leave the shrine alone, it is not ours and it is not
   yours. The spraywater will come at you in the Font because the Font is what they are made of; eight of them will
   clear you a quarter of an hour to work in. Read nothing. Carry it. I will do the reading.
T: Up. The fall has been up since the year the sky changed over and it has not come back down once. I am not
   carrying this to Overseer Mokra. She will ask what it means, and the honest answer is that the river is now
   draining a country that was not there to drain, and that the water has to be coming off something, and that
   nobody has gone to look at what. She would write it down. That is the problem. She writes everything down.

@ stvc_veilspeaker
Title: The Veilspeaker Will Not Be Argued With
D: The Veilspeaker has reached a conclusion about why the fall is wrong, and the conclusion is that somebody is
   standing where nobody should stand. The somebody is Ordek Fenn. On a Tuesday, a party of his shrinekeepers came
   up the shoulder and took the drying rack apart, piece by piece, without hurrying and without saying one word to
   him.
A: I want you to understand what I am asking and that I have thought about it for four days. He is on the south
   shore of the plunge, and he has decided my tarp is why his water is wrong, and he is not going to be talked out
   of it because I have tried. I went in. I went in on the Wednesday with a jar of oil as a gift and I came out
   again without saying a word, and the difference between us is that he is right about the shoulder being his and
   I am right about having nowhere else. Go down and end it. Please do not tell me it was quick.
T: It was not quick. Thank you for not saying it was. I have his bracket. The one from the mouth, with the oil
   still in it. I am going to put it on my own rack and that will be the whole of the monument, because nobody is
   coming to make him one. And I am still going to have to decide about the camp. Killing the man did not move the
   shoulder.

@ stvc_garza_report
Title: What Watcher Garza Has Not Sent
D: Watcher Garza was posted out of Grom'gol to sit on the one road up onto the Mosh'Ogg Shelf and report what comes
   down it. She has sent nine reports in eleven weeks. All nine say the same thing, and the reason they say the
   same thing is that in the second week she put the spyglass - signed for, numbered, drawn against her name - down
   the notch, and everything since has been written from memory and a good imagination.
A: The rockthrowers pick up anything that catches the light and they put it on the midden behind the camp, and I
   have been looking at that midden through nothing at all for nine weeks. Eight of them, and bring me five things
   off the pile. Not the bones. The made things. If the glass is in there it is in there with a brass collar and my
   number stamped on the collar, and if it is not in there then I am going to have to write a tenth report that
   says something different from the other nine.
T: Fifth one. There it is, brass collar, number and all, and I have had eleven weeks of being quietly sick about
   it. Now look what it was lying against. That is a crate nail. Square cut, stamped, Gurbashi arena stock - and
   arena stock does not come up this road, because this road goes nowhere but up. So the ogres have been trading
   with the gate, and that is a real report, and the only way to file it is with the ten weeks attached. I asked
   you to tidy a midden and you have handed me a confession.

@ stvc_shelf_road
Title: One Road Up, and They Know It
D: The Mosh'Ogg hold the shelf because the shelf has mesas on every side and exactly one notch through them, and
   they were the people standing nearest the notch when it mattered. That is the whole of their claim and they have
   never pretended otherwise. Garza's tenth report is going to be a bad one. She would like at least one number in
   it to be going the right way before she attaches the ten weeks to it.
A: This is not strategy and I am not going to dress it as strategy. It is bookkeeping. Ten of the maulers off the
   approach and six of the spellgorgers out of the clearing on top, and then walk into the clearing itself so that
   somebody has actually stood in it this season. A post that reports a camp it has never entered is a post that is
   guessing, and I have done eleven weeks of guessing and would prefer to stop.
T: They moved the camp. Six feet. Six feet, west, and then they stopped and put the fire back in the middle of it
   and sat down. That is not a rout. That is a shuffle, and it is the entire history of that rock in one gesture -
   they have been shuffling on it for generations and holding it the whole time, because nobody has ever wanted the
   notch badly enough. I have been watching a people shuffle for eleven weeks and calling it a garrison in writing.

@ stvc_ulua_netsetter
Title: A Week of Ulua's Nets
D: Netsetter Ulua has set nets in the Snapjaw Marsh her whole life and her mother set them before her, in the same
   water, off the same landing. She does not want help. She wants somebody to pull her lines for one week, she is
   not going to say why, and her hands are shut most of the way into fists and have been since the cold month.
A: Eight lines, out past the gangplank where the water stops moving. Pull them, bring the catch in, and reset them
   the way you found them and not the way you think would be better. Seven of the crocolisks have been taking the
   nets with the catch still in them, which is the only part of this I will complain about out loud. And do not
   tell anyone in the Bay you did this. I sell into the Bay.
T: Eight, and you reset them right, which I did not expect. Something I will ask you and then we will not discuss
   it again. Where you're from - is there standing water? Brackish, slow, the kind that does not go anywhere? And
   does anyone fish it. I am not making conversation. I would like to know whether this is a trade that exists in
   more than one place, in case it stops existing in this one.

@ stvc_wager_slip
Title: Somebody Did Not Want To Be Found With This
D: You cut it out of a crocolisk's gut, which is not where paper goes. It is a betting slip. Salt-stiff, gone brown
   at the folds, and still perfectly readable: a fighter's name, a round, and a price. You do not know the fighter.
   You do know that a price that good is a price somebody was offered because they knew something, and that there
   is exactly one reason to carry a slip like this out into standing water and stand in it.
A: The handwriting is small, fast and very legible, and there is a goblin up on the arena apron who writes exactly
   like that. Take it to her. Go back along the marsh track to the gangplank first and look at the water either
   side of it, and go through eight more of the crocolisks on the way, because whatever else is in that stretch of
   marsh is inside one of them.
T: She knows the hand before she has the slip the right way up, because it is hers. 'That card was pulled. That
   fighter never went on the sand - I wrote this, I priced it, and then the house scratched the round and I bought
   the paper back in. All of it. I counted it back in.' She turns it over twice. 'Where in the marsh. Exactly
   where. Because there is one reason to walk a dead slip out into water, and it is so that nobody ever finds you
   holding it.'

@ stvc_arena_first_four
Title: Anticlockwise
D: Gatehouse Broker Zul'tamo's father kept this list and his grandfather cut part of the wall it is nailed to. He
   does not care who wins. He cares that the list is right. What he will give you before you go down is the one
   thing nobody in the Bay can sell you: how the place is built, and why it is built that way.
A: You come down the stair at the top of the ring and you will see you can only go one way round it. People tell
   visitors that is tradition. It is masonry. The two faces that would let you choose were never cut, three
   generations back, because the mason did not want the order to be a choice. First is Vek'tar, and his round is
   the one the rim likes, because it ends in a number of minutes everybody can count. Go at him and keep going -
   under a third he will drink the fight back out of you. Then the grate. Mok'tal keeps a fire under that floor and
   it never lapses, and he closes off whatever you were going to use to answer it. Nobody has ever made a patient
   fight of Mok'tal and come up the stair.
T: Two off the list. The drum crew had you on the long count and they lost money on it, which I enjoyed. They keep
   a separate count for fighters who came through from somewhere else - you open too fast, all of you, every time,
   and it is not an opinion, it is a drumming problem. They have had to learn a second rhythm since your lot
   started coming down that stair. You did not open too fast. I will tell them. They will not believe me.

@ stvc_arena_pair
Title: The Handler Is the Fight
D: The rim has noticed whose money is on you, which means the third station has been changed. The pair-handlers
   have put Jin'sha in with Rakjan, and they do that when the house wants a round to stop being predictable.
   Zul'tamo tells you this flatly and without apology. It is not his decision and it is on his list, and both of
   those things are true at once.
A: Rakjan is loud and Rakjan is not the problem. The handler behind him is the problem, and if you spend the round
   on the big one you will die to the small one and the rim will say you fought the wrong man, which you will have.
   Then Zul'gar. Understand what he is before you go at him: he is not a fighter, he is a door. He stands in the
   mouth of the passage to the hall and there has been somebody standing in that mouth every day for three
   generations - the job has a name and the name is older than the family that holds it. Three swings, all of them
   enormous, and one of them takes your armour off you. Hitting him harder does not change that arithmetic.
T: Four crossed off. Now a thing I should not tell you and am going to. The house does not open the centre gate
   more than once in a season. It has never had to. There is no rule about it - there has simply never been more
   than one in a season who got as far as the dead end. It has opened twice this season. The first one was eleven
   days ago and nobody has said his name since, and I have begun to wonder whether the gate is a prize or a
   tidying-up.

@ stvc_arena_favourite
Title: The House's Own
D: Four rounds have made Bexxil's stake worth a great deal more than she paid for it, and she has done what anybody
   holding paper that good does: she sold most of it on. What is left of her position is the awkward half. It pays
   if you go the distance with Jen'shala and lose honestly. It pays very little if you win. She has decided to tell
   you this to your face, which is not kindness and is not quite its opposite either.
A: Here it is, flat. I laid off four fifths of you this morning at a price your rounds made me, and the fifth I
   kept pays best on a long loss. Eleven minutes and down. That is my number. And I am telling you because I cannot
   buy eleven minutes off somebody who does not know I want them, and because you would have found out, and because
   I would rather be the one who said it. She is one and four at once - she drinks the fight back out of you AND
   she takes your armour off, and she is the house's own, and the house has never put her on the sand to lose. Go
   down. Do what you are going to do. Collect in the Bay, not here.
T: The Quiet Man counts it out without looking up, which is how you know it is the real number and not the one
   Bexxil would have preferred. 'She is not ruined. She is annoyed, which on her is nearly the same shape.' He
   squares the coin off with one finger. 'The board has a field for where a fighter came from. It has had that
   field since before the Baron. Since you lot started walking down that stair it is the only line on the sheet
   anybody argues about, and somebody has to sit down tonight and re-price it, and it will be me.' He finally looks
   up. 'Do not come back for a season. Let the number settle.'

@ stvc_scrip_drums
Title: What Scrip Thinks the Drums Are
D: Scrip has never been a mile out of the cove. He has heard the arena drums come down the cape on a still night -
   twice, both times in the wet month - and he has built an entire theory out of it. The drums are gorilla hide.
   One of them is bigger than a boat. He is completely certain and he is prepared to pay in the only currency he
   has, which is knowing which crates on the pier are not nailed down.
A: Right, so you go up the road and you count them. I want how many drums, how wide across the top, and what they
   are made of, and do not just say gorilla because I know it is gorilla, I want it written down by somebody who
   was there. Eight of the big apes, the ones they call drumhides - that is a clue, that is practically the answer
   - and five bits of hide so I can feel it. And stand on the flat bit in front of the gate so you can tell me how
   many people fit.
T: No. No, you have looked at the wrong ones. Crocolisk. You are telling me the big drums are crocolisk belly off
   the marsh and the gorillas are the little ones round the edge, and that is - no. He folds the hide scrap up

   says where the crate went.
A: There is only one man in this jungle who would recognise a run number on sight, and he is in Booty Bay behind a
   desk, and he will be unbearable about being asked. Go through the brutes on the way down off the shelf - eight
   of them hold the notch, and they are the ones with the nailed timber in their camp, which is the part you want
   to be sure of before you say any of this out loud to a clerk.
T: Clerk Bexx looks at it for less time than it takes to say the number. 'Mine. That run went out of this office,
   to the gate, under weight, signed. It should be at the bottom of a pit full of broken crates behind an arena.'

   shelf, which means somebody is opening my crates before the gate counts them. And do you know what I am angry
   about? The stamp. The stamp is legible. A legible stamp is a thing a person can audit me with, and I have spent
   eleven years making sure nobody could.'
