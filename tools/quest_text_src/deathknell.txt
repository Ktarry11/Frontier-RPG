# Deathknell: the first zone anyone sees as a Forsaken, so it gets the most care. Mostly warm, grim-funny; tension only in a few.

@ dk_first_steps
Title: First Steps in Undeath
D: Overseer Krell runs Deathknell the way a farmer runs a field: by walking it every morning and counting what is still standing. The Mangy Duskbats along the village treeline are the gentlest test the valley has. If your arms still answer when you tell them to swing, you are halfway to useful. If they do not, Krell would like to know before the rest of the valley does.
A: You woke up dead. Congratulations, and I mean that more than it sounds. Most don't wake at all. The duskbats nest in the trees at the edge of the village. They bite, but they bite like a bad-tempered cat. Six of them. Go and see whether the body remembers what the mind does not.
T: Still on your feet, and still holding together. That's most of the battle, this early, and I've seen people lose it to a duskbat. Sit down a moment if you need to. Not that you'll tire. It's just that the living used to do it, and old habits stick to the bones. You have the look of the newly woken, and I do not mean our kind of waking. Whatever you were before, it is three years behind you now.

@ dk_scavenger_scraps
Title: Stolen Supplies
D: The village stores have been shrinking, and not because anyone in Deathknell eats. Ragged scavengers have been dragging off salt tins, rope, bandages, anything with a glint or a smell. Krell wants every piece back. The Dark Lady's bookkeeping is meticulous, and he has decided his own should be too.
A: Birds, if you can believe it. A man dies, rises, goes to work, and the first thing that happens is he gets robbed by crows. They hang about the road outside the village. Bring me five scraps, whatever they are. I'm not fussy. I'm only counting.
T: That's the rope. That's the salt. That's... what is that? Never mind, I'll find a use. Good work. I'll write it down as recovered, which is better than lost, and a great deal better than eaten.

@ dk_stray_cull
Title: Strays at the Gate
D: Dogs and rats came to the graveyard the way they always do, for what is in the ground. Something in the soil has sickened them. The plagued strays now tear at gravediggers and the Newly Risen alike, and the rats carry the sickness into the village stores. Krell wants them thinned before it spreads past the fence.
A: They bite, and then you rot a little faster than you were already rotting. I will not pretend that is a small thing. Seven strays and four rats, in the fields around the graves. Hit them hard. They are not clever enough to run, and they are too sick to be sorry.
T: Fewer teeth in the dark. I can sleep a little easier, metaphorically speaking. Take something from the pile. Somebody left it for the person who does the unpleasant work, and I have decided that is you.

@ dk_claim_spiders
Title: The Overrun Claim
D: The old goldmine to the north-west was a decent little operation until the spiders arrived. Now the camp is web, bone and bad news, and Foreman Gresk is still in the middle of it, which is its own kind of miracle. Clear the spiders from the claim so Gresk can breathe, then hear what he needs. He will have a list.
A: North-west, past the old gold-road. Eight spiders on the claim, and they are not the small kind. Then talk to Gresk at the mine camp. He's cranky, and he's usually right, which is the worst combination in a foreman.
T: Eight. Good. The Dark Lady has plans for that gold, and none of them include spiders. Gresk will want a word, probably several. Let him say them. He has been alone with that mine for a very long time.

@ dk_scout_hollow
Title: Into the Hollow
D: The spiders are not just wandering in. They are being fed. A web-choked cave beside the claim, Night Web's Hollow, pours them out like a spring. Krell wants eyes on the cave's inner strand before anyone commits to a fight, because someone in the Undercity will ask questions, and he would like to have answers.
A: Past the claim's camp there's a cave mouth wrapped in silk. Walk the spiral until you reach the inner strand. Do not poke anything. Do not name anything. Come back and tell me what you saw, in detail, and in order.
T: A broodmother. Of course there's a broodmother. All right, now we know what we are dealing with. Next time we go in with a purpose and a plan. You did well to come back with your wits. Most of my scouts come back with fewer legs.

@ dk_neshka
Title: Broodmother Nesh'ka
D: At the centre of the Hollow, wrapped in her own silk, sits Nesh'ka. Every spider the claim has ever seen came from her, and while she lives the cave will keep filling the valley. Krell does not need a diplomatic solution. He needs a corpse, and a handful of her brood for the report.
A: She is in the heart of the spiral. She stings, she poisons, she wraps. Kill the spiders on the way in, one at a time, and keep your head about you. Nesh'ka is clever in the way hungry things are clever. The Dark Lady's name be with you, or whatever passes for luck down here.
T: Dead. Properly dead. You have more teeth than I gave you credit for. I'll note it in the report, favourably, for once. The Undercity will hear your name, and I am told that is either a blessing or a very long conversation.

@ dk_to_brill
Title: Word for Brill
D: The valley is quiet for the first time since you were stitched together, and that is Krell's cue to be rid of you. The road east leaves Deathknell for the Tirisfal Glades. Follow it to the portal in the glade's heart and it brings you to Brill, the Forsaken's first real town. Krell has written a note for Deathguard Rennick. It says "Take this one, they bite." He is not sorry.
A: Take the east road out of the valley, cross the glades, and follow the road into Brill. Find Deathguard Rennick and show him my note. Tell him I did not ruin you. He won't believe it, but you should say it anyway.
T: You made it out of the valley, which is farther than most. The rest of the Forsaken are going to find you very inconvenient. Good. We need inconvenient. It's the only thing that has ever kept us alive.

@ dk_risen_grave_dwellers
Title: Restless in the Ground
D: The woman beside the graves cannot remember her own name, but she remembers the sound they make: the scratching from underneath. Grave-Dwellers, freshly risen and not yet coherent, claw their way out of the mass grave and wander the field in the dark. She wants them put back where they belong, and she asks very politely that it not take long.
A: They don't know they're dead yet. It's the saddest part. They stand there and wonder what's wrong with the world. Please, quickly, before they work it out. Six should do it. I'll keep watch from here. I find I cannot leave this spot.
T: Thank you. I think I knew one of them. I don't know which. Please, take this. It doesn't feel like mine, and I would rather it went to someone who will have use of it.

@ dk_risen_grave_markers
Title: Names on the Stones
D: Grave markers are meant to be names. Up here they are mostly splinters, and the restless dead keep tearing them out of the ground and carrying them off, as though they still wanted to read their own. The Newly Risen thinks that if she gathers enough of them, she may find hers.
A: The Grave-Dwellers carry them. I think they are looking too. They hold them up to their faces, as if someone might have written it small. Please bring five back. I'll sort them by hand, and read every one aloud, in case any of them is mine.
T: ...Not one of them is mine. But this one has a name I almost... no. Never mind. Thank you. I will keep it with the others. It seems wrong to bury a name without anyone to read it.

@ dk_risen_mawgrin
Title: The Gaunt Alpha
D: One of the plagued strays has grown larger than the rest, a gaunt grey dog that leads the others through the graves at night and drags off whatever it likes. The villagers call it Mawgrin. The Newly Risen calls it "the one who sits on my grave," and says it with a kind of weary patience.
A: He is out in the field. You'll hear him before you see him: a low sort of rattle in the throat. He's bigger than the others and angrier than he looks. Kill five of his pack first, so he'll come to you alone. He was somebody's dog, once.
T: ...Gone? That's very good. That's very good indeed. I can sit by my grave in peace. I don't know whose grave it is, but I like to be near it. Please, take whatever you like.

@ dk_instructor_bones
Title: Drill: Bones
D: The Instructor believes a Forsaken learns by hitting things that are already dead. Rattlecage Skeletons, crypt-dwellers, long dead and bad-tempered about it, pace the Shadow Grave to the south, and they make the best sparring partners the valley offers. They never complain and they never stay down for long.
A: Shadow Grave, south of the graves. Skeletons. Hit them until they stop. Then hit them a little more. They'll hit back, and that's the lesson. You people always arrive wanting a manual. There isn't one. There's the bone, and the other bone.
T: Not graceful. Not clever. But you're upright and they aren't, and that is the entire syllabus. Stand straighter next time. Elbows in. You swing like someone who learned to fight from a story, and I have no quarrel with the stories, but the stories do not hurt.

@ dk_instructor_wights
Title: Wights of Shadow Grave
D: Wights are a different kind of dead, the kind that never got the Dark Lady's gift of will, just hunger. They haunt the Shadow Grave's old stones and drain the living of whatever is left in them. The Instructor wants them put down before they wander up into the village proper.
A: They are not like us. There is no will behind the eyes, only hunger wearing a corpse. It's an ugly thing to meet and an uglier thing to be. Put them down. Eight, in the Shadow Grave. Do not let them touch your face. They love the face.
T: That is the kind of mercy they cannot thank you for, but it is mercy all the same. Take your pick of what's left on the table. I find it helps to think of them as a rough draft of us, and you as the editor.

@ dk_instructor_ossric
Title: The Unburied
D: One of the Shadow Grave's dead refuses to stay in his hole. Ossric was a sexton once, and he buried half the village. Now he rises every night with the spade still in his hand, searching for the grave nobody dug for him. The Instructor would very much like someone to dig it.
A: He is in the crypt-yard, by the old headstones. The zombies follow him around like mourners, which, in fairness, is what they are. Clear the mourners first, then Ossric. Be kind about it. He did his job. Nobody did his.
T: Finally. He can rest. And for what it is worth, you did that well. Take something from the pile. I mean it. I do not often say it twice in a lifetime, and I have had two now.

@ dk_marrow_shrouds
Title: Shrouds for the Unburied
D: Sexton Marrow tends the Shadow Grave's crypt-yard, and he takes pride in never leaving anyone uncovered. The zombies and skeletons wandering the yard wear scraps of burial shroud: his shrouds, the good linen. He would like them back, preferably before the next burial, and preferably clean.
A: I stitched every one of them myself, with a double seam. The zombies took them off the corpses like children playing dress-up. Bring six back to me, and mind the stains. They are not blood. They're the other thing.
T: These are still good. Washed and pressed, they'll do someone another burial. You've a good eye for linen. Few do. Take your pick, and don't tell the others I said that. They'll all want shrouds.

@ dk_gresk_diggers
Title: Dead Men Don't Dig
D: Gresk's crew was undead before the spiders arrived, which turned out to be convenient for the spiders. The dead diggers kept working after Gresk gave up supervising them, and now they swing their picks at anyone who comes near the shaft. He cannot reason with them. He would like you to try something else.
A: My crew. Every one of them. They don't stop, they don't recognise me, they just dig. Eight of them are working the tunnels by the shaft. Please put them out of it. I've asked them to knock off a dozen times and they've never once looked up.
T: I thought I would feel worse. I do not. Honest work, that, and quick. They would have wanted a quick end. Everyone does. Take something for it, and thank you for not asking me to say a few words. I don't have any.

@ dk_gresk_thread
Title: Gold on a Thread
D: The spiders weave with more than silk. They have been lacing real gold dust through their webs, taken from the old veins and spun into lines that glint in the dark. Gresk wants every strand back. The Dark Lady likes gold, and Gresk likes being alive.
A: Look for the spiders that sparkle. They've got gold in their webs, and I want it in my ledger. Five strands should square the books. Don't use your fingers. Use something that won't mind being cut.
T: That is gold. Honest gold. Spider-spun, but gold. You've saved my neck, or what is left of it. I'll tell the Undercity the ledger is closed. I'll tell them it was the spiders, and I won't mention how you looked while you did it.

@ dk_gresk_goldfang
Title: Goldfang
D: One of the claim's spiders has grown fat on the gold, and bright with it. Its fangs have gone yellow and it hunts the crew's old tunnels. The diggers called it Goldfang. Gresk calls it a problem with a price, and he has been working out the price for some time.
A: It is big, it is gold-toothed, and it is mine. Not literally, but the gold in its fangs is the Dark Lady's, and I want it back. Kill it, and five of the claim spiders besides. They follow it around like it's a banker.
T: There it is. Pure gold, in a spider's jaw. The world is a funny place. Take your pick of the lot, and tell no one where it came from. If anyone asks, say you found it. Everyone in the valley believes the dead find things.

@ dk_gresk_hatchery
Title: Clearing the Hatchery
D: The Hollow's hatchery is where the claim's spiders come from: a chamber in the north-west of the cave where egg-clutches hang in the silk, guarded by Hatchery Warden Skrix. The Pale Weaver lurks on the far side, spinning cocoons around the dead. Gresk wants both gone, and the clutches cleared with them.
A: The Hollow's four corners are nurseries. Skrix guards the north-west one. The Weaver is in the south-east brood chamber. Kill both, and thin the hatchlings while you're at it. If you hear something humming through the walls, do not hum back.
T: Both of them, and the hatchlings. You are a very efficient little horror. Take your pick. I'll tell Krell the Hollow is clean, and then I'll stop telling anyone anything. I think I've earned the quiet.

@ dk_isolde_wretches
Title: Waters Gone Wrong
D: The pond west of the graves was a mourners' spot once, a place to sit and talk to the dead. Now the dead talk back: the drowned wretches that crawl from the silt, dripping and moaning, grabbing at anyone near the bank. Isolde sits there all day anyway. She asks only that the pond be quiet when she comes.
A: They crawl out of the water at dusk, grey as the silt, and they cry. I can't hear myself think. Eight of them, and I'd be grateful. I only sit here because I'd rather grieve somewhere with a view.
T: Quieter. Thank you. It is a poor kind of grief that has to compete with the dead for volume. I'll sit here a while and think of nothing, which is the best I can do, and an improvement on yesterday.

@ dk_isolde_keepsakes

A: The wretches and the shades both carry them. They don't know what the tokens mean. They only know they were loved. Five of them, if you can manage. I will ask nothing about how you got them, only that you bring them dry.
T: ...Oh. This one is mine. This one is... someone's. Thank you. I will see they are kept. Take your pick of mine. I would rather a stranger had the use of them than the mud.

@ dk_isolde_wren

@ dk_orrel_scarecrows

@ dk_orrel_seeds
Title: Seeds Worth Saving
D: The fire took the house and most of the harvest, but not the seed-packets. Orrel buried them in the cellar, and the burned farmhands have been digging them up, as though they remember what the seeds were for. He wants them back. Nothing grows in Tirisfal, but he intends to try.

@ dk_verrick_pickets
Title: Eyes on the Crusade
D: The Scarlet Crusade has made a camp outside the old crypt at the valley's north-east edge: pickets, torchbearers and a great deal of righteous noise. Verrick has watched them for a week from the rocks and concluded they will not leave on their own. He would like fewer of them.
A: They burn what they can't convert. They convert what they can't burn. Thin them out before they burn the valley. Six pickets and four torchbearers, and stay low among the rocks. The torchbearers see best at night, and they pray out loud. You'll hear them coming.
T: Good. Quiet, clean, and no survivors to report. You've done this before, maybe in a life you don't remember. I don't ask. It isn't polite, and most of us have things we'd rather not be reminded of. Pick whatever you can use.

@ dk_verrick_orders
Title: Burning Orders
D: The Scarlets write everything down: dispatches, sermons, orders for the next day's burning. Verrick wants those orders. A dispatch in hand tells him what the Crusade plans, which tells him what to plan against. The pickets and torchbearers carry them stuffed in their belts.
A: Their pockets are full of paper. Paper is wonderful. Bring me four dispatches and I'll bring you the war. Don't read them aloud, and don't let any survivor see you do. They're easily offended, and they remember who has read what.
T: Hm. They plan to move on the crypt's reliquary: a patrol, a priest, a lot of banners. This is useful. Have your choice of the lot. I'll send word to the Undercity tonight. Whatever else you may be, you are a very good thief, and I do not mean that as an insult.

@ dk_verrick_halbrook
Title: Sergeant Halbrook
D: Sergeant Halbrook runs the Crusade's picket line: eight years of service, a halberd he has named, and a certainty that undeath is a sin. Verrick has marked him for a long time. If the forecourt loses its sergeant, the pickets lose their spine.
A: He stands by the crypt gate. Big, loud, and disciplined. Break the line and he'll break with it. Kill four of his pickets first and he'll come to you. He prays between swings, so count the pauses.
T: Without Halbrook, that camp is a rabble with torches. Excellent. Now we can talk about the crypt. He named the halberd, you know. I found out last week. It was called "Mercy." I didn't laugh. I wanted to.

@ dk_verrick_crypt
Title: Into the Scarlet Crypt
D: The Scarlet Crypt is the Crusade's foothold in the valley: a chapel, an ossuary, a reliquary and a High Inquisitor with a fondness for confessions. Verrick wants someone to walk its nave and tell him what is holding the doors shut. He will not go himself. He has seen what happens to scouts who do.
A: The crypt gate is behind the picket line. Go down the stair, through the nave, to the chapel crossing. Look at it. Count the doors. Come back and tell me. Do not stay. Do not pray. Do not answer if someone asks you to confess.
T: A chapel crossing, an inquisitor, vaults on both sides. It is worse than I thought and better than I feared. Choose well. I'll draw it up properly tonight, with the doors in the right order. Somebody is going to need that map.

@ dk_verrick_fairbanks
Title: The High Inquisitor
D: High Inquisitor Fairbanks has broken more Forsaken than the valley holds. He sits at the sanctum's far end, behind a loop of candlelit corridors, and he does not leave. Verrick has no clever plan. He has only an axiom: nobody who goes down there should expect to come back, and so far the exception is you.
A: The sanctum, past the penitent's walk. He smites, he curses, and he cleanses. Kill six of his initiates first, or they'll heal him faster than you can hurt him. And if he offers you absolution, remember it comes with a price.
T: Fairbanks. Dead. The Crusade will bleed for it. I will not, but I'll drink to it, in the way the dead drink. Take your reward. I'd say you've earned a rest. I'd also say you won't take one, and I'd be right.

@ dk_verrick_vaults
