# Silverpine South (Forsaken). Location hints from the old accept texts are kept in the new ones.

@ sps_scout_report
D: Deathguard Lyle has watched the south road from the gate for months, and what he has seen fits on a single scrap of parchment. It is a short, grim report for Sergeant Dallow at Valgan's Field, and Lyle has no one he trusts to carry it. He would very much like it to arrive without having been read along the way.
A: South along the trunk road, past the crossroad, under the banners. That's Dallow's camp at Valgan's Field. Take this to him and don't open it. It isn't a secret, exactly. It's just that I'd rather nobody had to hear what I wrote out loud.
T: Lyle always did write short. Two lines, a smudge, and a warning. I'll make something of it. The man never wasted a word when he was alive, and he's not wasting any now that he's dead. Good. You'll do.

@ sps_forsaken_march
D: The March exists to keep the road to Ambermill open, and the road is full of wolves and the leavings of bears that died of something the Apothecaries have never named. Dallow needs the trunk road clear before the next supply train, and he has more graves to dig than soldiers to dig them.
A: Anywhere in the pines south of the gate. Wolves and bears. If it's limping and drooling, it's the one. The Apothecaries say the sickness doesn't spread to us. They say that about everything. Eight wolves, five bears, and keep your mouth shut when you're close.
T: Quieter roads, fewer drovers screaming. You're a credit to the March, and I don't say that to the living very often. Mostly I've nothing to say to them at all. Report to me when you want more. I'll find it.

@ sps_deserters
D: Some of the March did not stay. A few dozen Forsaken, tired of orders and tired of the dark, simply walked off Valgan's Field and set themselves up as brigands in the ruined farm. Dallow takes it personally, because the walking-off happened under his nose, and he cannot decide whether he is angrier at them or at himself.
A: The ruined farm around my camp. They wear March coats with the insignia torn off, which is how you know they once cared. I won't ask you to be gentle. I won't ask you to hate them either. Eight of them, and don't bother asking where they came from. None of us ask that.
T: I'd hoped they'd come back on their own. They didn't. Thank you for doing what I couldn't. I've given orders to men I'd rather have died fighting beside than buried. It's a poor soldier who can't tell the difference. I'll sleep poorly tonight, which for us is a long and elaborate lie.

@ sps_deserter_tags
D: Every March soldier is issued an iron tag stamped with a number, and Dallow keeps the roll himself, in a ledger nobody else is allowed to touch. He wants the tags of the deserters, so that when someone in the Undercity asks who left, he can answer with names and numbers instead of a shrug.
A: Off the bodies of the deserters, in the same farm. Iron tags on a cord, six of them. Don't read the numbers out loud. If you do, you'll remember them, and then you'll be like me.
T: Six names off the roll. They'll be remembered, though perhaps not kindly. I'll write them in the ledger in my own hand, with a line through each. A line is more than most of us get.

@ sps_deserter_letter
D: A letter, taken from a dead deserter's pocket, addressed to someone in the Undercity. The seal is unbroken. Whatever it says, someone in the March ought to read it before it's posted, and the only man in the March with the stomach to do that is Sergeant Dallow.
A: Take it to Dallow, at Valgan's Field. He'll want to know who the deserters were writing to. I wouldn't read it first. Not because it's a crime. Because once you know, you can't un-know. I've found that to be most of death's lessons.
T: A mother, in Brill. The man was nineteen. Gods. I'll see she's told the right way. A soldier shouldn't die twice, once on a field and once in a letter. I'll write it myself. I'll make it kind. It's the one lie I'm still good at.

@ sps_wight_bones
D: Field Surgeon Ashe has run out of replacement bone. The battlefield wights of Valgan's Field wear the bones of better men, and Ashe is a practical woman who has never been too proud to recycle. A good long bone, cleaned, is worth a great deal in a camp where limbs are lost faster than they can be mended.
A: The wights out on the field. Take the long bones, arms and legs, and clean them if you can. I'd do it myself, but I'm needed here, and the wights don't make allowances for bedside manner. The living heal on their own. Mine needs scaffolding. Six, please.
T: These'll do. Slightly used, slightly regretful. Thank you. Hold still, there's a stain on your sleeve, and I can't stand it. There. I'll have a dozen soldiers walking by morning who'd have been sitting in a corner thinking about a leg.

@ sps_deathguard_captain
D: A Deathguard Captain has been fighting the Third War on Valgan's Field since before Dallow arrived. He does not answer to orders, rank or reason, and the March cannot hold the field while he walks it. Dallow was under his command once, and has never said so to a living soul.
A: Deep in the field, where the wights gather. He's the one still giving commands. Don't answer if he calls you by a name. He's very convincing, and he'll use whichever one you least expected. I won't ask you to be kind. I'll only ask you to be quick.
T: He was a good officer once. The best of us, maybe. Thank you for ending the watch. I'd have done it myself, but I wasn't sure I'd be able to stop afterwards. I'll tell the others he was relieved. It isn't the whole truth, but it's the part that will let them sleep.

@ sps_hunter_pelts
D: The Forsaken Hunter wants six clean wolf pelts. "Diseased" is acceptable; "dissolving" is not. He is making a cloak for someone he used to know, and he will not say who. In a camp where everyone's past is a closed door, nobody has tried to open that one.
A: The diseased wolves along the road. Skin them carefully, and don't nick the hide. I want the fur, not the sickness. The cloak is a gift. Don't ask for whom. If I could remember her name properly I'd tell you.
T: Good work. A bit mangy, but a cloak that's been through something is worth more than one that hasn't. She'll like it, if she's still the woman I think she is. If not, I'll wear it myself, and it'll do the job just as well.

@ sps_hunter_roost
D: The carrion birds of the pines have learned to follow the Hunter's traps, and take the kills before he can reach them. He doesn't mind sharing, much. What he minds is the birds taking the traps themselves, with the cord and the wire, as if they were building something.
A: Anywhere in the pines. They're bold. Show them what boldness gets. I'd shoot them myself but they know my face, and the moment I lift the bow they scatter. You, they've never seen. Eight, if you're willing.
T: That'll teach them manners. Doubt it, but it's a start. I'll sleep better. Well. I'll lie down and look at the roof. You see how it is. Thank you.

@ sps_hogs
D: The Bucklebree hogs went wrong the same week the plague reached the farm. They still root in the dirt, but what they root up is worse than what they find. Farmer Bucklebree wants the herd put down before it breeds, and the barn cleared of whatever the ghouls have made of his hay.
A: The pens and the barn. The hogs won't run, but the ghouls will, and then come back. I raised these animals from piglets. I named every one. I can't do it, and I can't watch someone else do it. Please be quick about it.
T: It's quiet on the farm for the first time in months. Odd how much I miss the noise. I'll sit with the empty pens a while, if you don't mind. Thank you. It isn't much, but I'd like you to have a story to tell about hogs.

@ sps_quilts
D: Goodwife Merriam sewed a quilt for every child born on the Bucklebree farm. The barn ghouls drag them off and sleep on them, and what was once a family's history is now a bed for the dead. She wants six scraps back, to piece together whatever she can of what was left.
A: The barn ghouls, in the barn. They keep the scraps tucked into their rags like they're something precious. I suppose they are, to them. Six pieces, if you can. I don't need them whole. I only need them mine.
T: That's the blue one. That's my mother's stitching. Thank you. Thank you. I'll start tonight. I haven't had a reason to thread a needle in two winters, and my hands remembered. I did not know hands could do that.

@ sps_gorra
D: Tusk-Mother Gorra was the biggest sow in the Bucklebree herd, and the biggest hog in Silverpine. She did not die with the others. She grew, and the plague that dissolved the rest only gave her more of herself to be. Bucklebree has a knife and no stomach to use it.
A: In the east field, past the fence. You'll hear her before you see her. She's not a monster, whatever the others say. She's a mother who can't stop. Make it quick, and make it clean. She deserves that.
T: She nursed forty litters. She was a good mother, in her way. Thank you. I'll bury her under the oak. I think she'd like the shade. I'll tell no one it was you. They'd only ask what she looked like at the end, and I'd rather not remember.

@ sps_pond_ooze
D: Marshwalker Thorne has walked the edge of Olivia's Pond for two years, and for the last three weeks something has walked it with him. Marsh oozes, swamp ghouls and bloated things have crowded the banks, and Thorne, who prefers silence to nearly everything, would like fewer companions.
A: Around the pond. The oozes hug the shore, the ghouls come out at dusk. Don't go in. The water takes your footing and doesn't give it back. Eight oozes, six ghouls, and stay on the firm ground.
T: A little quieter. A little less of me sinking in the mud. Thank you. I'll miss nothing, except maybe the ghouls, who at least had the courtesy to groan. The oozes just watch.

@ sps_pond_edge
D: Thorne wants someone to circle the pond on foot and tell him what has changed. He has a theory about where the water is going, and a stronger theory about where it is coming from, and he is afraid of being right about either.
A: Three points: the east bank, the south bank, and the south-west reeds. Count the dead fish. If there are none, run. I'd go myself, but I've been looking at this pond so long I've stopped seeing it.
T: No fish at all. That's the answer I was afraid of. The water is dead, and it is spreading. I'll need to know why. Thank you for the honest report. Most would have said it looked fine, to be kind.

@ sps_pond_samples
D: The bloated corpses of Olivia's Pond carry bladders of marsh gas, and Thorne wants five of them, sealed, to test. He is certain the gas is the source of the oozes, and that the cure is as simple as stopping it at the root, if only he could prove it.
A: The bloated corpses, by the south bank. Pierce them carefully into the jar, and don't breathe. I mean it. I've breathed it once, and I saw my mother, and she was not as I remember her. Five, please. Seal them well.
T: That smell is worse than I'd hoped. That's how I know it's the right stuff. Thank you. I'll sit with these tonight and see what they tell me. The pond has been trying to tell me something for two years.

@ sps_bloated_one
D: At the heart of Olivia's Pond lies a corpse so swollen it can no longer rise. It lashes at anything that nears, and what it bursts into is worse than what it was. Thorne has finally found the source of the oozes, and it is not something he can walk away from.
A: The middle of the pond, up to your ankles in muck. Strike and step back. Strike and step back. It's slow, but it will take you with it if you let it. And whatever comes out when it bursts, don't swallow.
T: It's burst, and it's done. I'll wash for a week. Thank you. The pond may even be clear by spring. I think I'll like that. I think I'd even like to see a fish again.

@ sps_mine_kobolds
D: Overseer Hobart held the Deep Elem Mine through the plague, the Scourge and the second strike, and he is not about to give it up to a kobold. The ones that moved in after the collapse have settled for good, and he wants the gallery cleared, and wants to see them go first.
A: At the mine mouth and the old galleries. Kobolds, rotting and digging. Both bite. I've held this mine since before the war, and I'm not leaving it to something that can't count to ten. Eight rotting ones, six diggers.
T: That's a clear gallery. I can almost smell the ore again. It's not much of a smell, but it's mine. Thank you. I'll tell the company the way is open, even though there's no company to tell.

@ sps_mine_lamps
D: The shamblers of dead miners still walk Deep Elem's galleries, carrying the lamps they died with. Hobart has counted them for years and knows their names. He wants six of the lamps back. "They belonged to the company," he says, a little too firmly, for a man with no company left.
A: The shamblers at the mine mouth. Take the lamps from their hands. Mind the oil. I know what I'm asking. They were good men. They would want the lamps used, if they could remember what lamps were for. Six, please.
T: Six lamps, six names. The company will be pleased. I can stop pretending there's a company. I think I'll light them tonight, all six, and let them burn in the office. It isn't much of a memorial, but it's all I've got.

@ sps_mine_gallery
D: The main gallery collapsed the day of the plague, and Hobart has never been back to look. He has run the numbers a thousand times, and every answer ends the same way. He wants someone to stand at its mouth and tell him whether the supports held, because he cannot stand to learn it for himself.
A: The mine mouth, to the west. Don't go in. Stand at the threshold, and tell me what you see. I don't need details. I need to know whether I should keep hoping.
T: Supports gone. Ore everywhere. And the foreman's lamp, still lit. I knew it. I always did. Thank you. A man who's lost a mine will forgive nearly anything, but not a lie. You didn't tell me one.

@ sps_gnawbone
D: Gnawbone was the kobold foreman of Deep Elem, and the only one to keep his job after the collapse. He wears the company's hard-hat and gives orders in a tongue Hobart half-remembers, and the miners that are left obey. He has to go, or the mine will never be Hobart's again.
A: Deep in the mine, among the carts. He's the one with the bone pick and the helmet. Take the helmet back. It was my foreman's. I'd know it anywhere. He wore it to his own funeral, and the kobold's wearing it still.
T: My old foreman's helmet, on a kobold. I'll take it back. Thank you. I think he would've laughed. He always had a sense of humour about that helmet. I'll hang it in the office, where the lamps are.

@ sps_pyre_ambush
D: The worgen of Pyrewood Village were people once, and ambush from the treeline is still how they hunt, patient and silent. Hearthwarden Brand has watched them from behind the barred shutters of the one house still standing, and wants the ambushers and the ferals thinned before the dead come to the village next.
A: The village streets and the treeline. They hunt in packs, so don't be alone. If you hear nothing, that's when to worry. I'm a hearthwarden. I keep fires. I can't keep one with them at the door.
T: Fewer fangs in the dark. I'll almost let myself breathe. Thank you. I haven't opened these shutters in a month. I think tonight I might, just a little, to see the stars.

@ sps_pyre_cultists
D: A cult has taken up in Pyrewood's burned houses, casting spells over the dead hearths. Brand does not know which god they pray to, or whether it is a god at all. He only knows that it answers them, and that whatever answers does not sound like anything he is willing to hear twice.
A: The burned houses on the east side. They chant. They don't stop for you, and they don't stop for anything. Don't listen to the words. If you start to find them beautiful, it's already working. Eight, please.
T: Quiet at last. Whatever answered them has gone quiet too. I don't know whether to be glad. A thing that stops talking has usually decided something. Rest, and be careful. I'd like to find out what it decided from a distance.

@ sps_pyre_sigils
D: Each Pyrewood cultist carries an iron sigil, stamped with a cipher Brand cannot read. He wants five, for a scholar in the Undercity who might. If the cult is only a cult, the sigils are junk. If it is something else, they are the only proof he will ever have.
A: From the cultists. Iron discs on cords. Don't read the cipher aloud, and don't hold them too long. They get warm in the hand. I'll send them to the Undercity once I've got five.
T: I'll have them sent to Sennarath in the Undercity. If anyone can read them, she can. Thank you. I think I'll feel better knowing someone brighter than I is looking at it.

@ sps_pyre_hall
D: Pyrewood Hall stands at the north end of the village, its roof half-gone and its doors barred from the inside. Brand has watched it for weeks, and nothing has come out. He wants someone to stand at its steps and tell him what is behind the doors, because he can't see anything from his window, and he cannot stand not knowing.
A: The Hall, at the north end of the green. Don't go in. Look at the doors. If they've changed, tell me how. Even a little. Even a scratch.
T: Barred from the inside, as I feared. Whatever's in there wants to stay. Thank you. That's more than I can say for myself, standing at the window. I'll wait with the lamp lit. If it opens, at least I'll see it.

@ sps_godfrey
D: What stands in Pyrewood Hall is what the lord of the village left behind: a ghost in a noble's coat, pistol in one hand and cane in the other, still giving the dead their orders. He was a decent man once. Brand wants him put to rest, and has not decided whether it is mercy or fear.
A: Inside the Hall. Mind the pistol. It never misses, and it never reloads. He'll speak to you as if you were a footman. Don't argue. Agree with him until you can't. Then do what you must.
T: He was a decent man, once. Not a good one. A decent one. That's more than most. Thank you. I'll see the Hall opened and aired. It'll be good to have the village looking at its lord for what he was, not what he's become.

@ sps_talon_stalkers
D: Berard's Talon is a ridge of broken stone above the old keep road. Crag stalkers hunt its slopes, and wraiths drift along its crest, and Trapper Hask cannot set a line without losing it. He wants both cleared so he can finally work, and he wants it done before the first frost.
A: The hill west of the keep road. Stalkers on the rocks, wraiths on the crest. Don't linger on the crest after dark. They have a way of turning your name over in their mouths. Eight stalkers, six wraiths. I'll set the lines when you're done.
T: The lines can go out tonight. Thank you. I'd offer you a pelt, but I don't think you'd want what the rocks have left. Another time, perhaps. I'll remember who cleared the ridge.

@ sps_talon_tokens

Hask wants five, and plans to return them to the graves they came from, whoever it takes and however long. It is the only thing he has found that makes the hill feel like his.
A: Off the wraiths. Don't hold them too long. They remember. I'll be glad of five, if you can manage. I don't mind the wait. I've got all the time in the world, and nowhere to put it.
T: Five graves to visit. It'll take all winter. Good. I'm not in a hurry. I think it'll do the wraiths some good, having their keepsakes returned. And it'll do me some, too, having somewhere to walk.

@ sps_berard
D: The ridge is named for Berard, a man whose face no longer appears in any record. He walks the hill each night, looking for something nobody remembers, and the lines of Hask's traps rattle as he passes. Hask doesn't want to destroy him. He wants him to rest.
A: On the crest, under the old stone ring. He moves like a man trying to remember. If he asks you a question, don't answer it. Whatever the answer is, he's heard it already, and it hasn't helped.
T: Maybe he'll rest now. Maybe someone will remember his face. Thank you. I'll put a stone under the ring. It won't have a name, but it'll be somewhere to put one, if anyone ever finds it.

@ sps_peril_pack

@ sps_peril_teeth

@ sps_bloodmaw

@ sps_wall_breakers

@ sps_wall_seals

@ sps_shut_gate

@ sps_wall_warden

@ sps_amber_ghosts

@ sps_amber_resin

@ sps_amber_waygate

@ sps_quillfire
