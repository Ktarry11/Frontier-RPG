# Silverpine North (Forsaken Rear Guard, Sepulcher approaches). Location hints kept.

@ spn_trunk_walk
D: The Trunk Road is the only thing keeping the Rear Guard's garrison connected to the Sepulcher, and nobody has walked its whole length since the first frost. Captain Vesk has had supply wagons turn back for reasons nobody can name. He wants the road walked to the Crossroads and described to him honestly, down to the last broken wheel.
A: South from the gate. Past the scout's post, past the Dead Field turning, down to the Crossroads. Keep your eyes open. I'd rather you kept them in your skull, too. I don't need heroics out there, I need a report. Tell me what's wrong with my road.
T: No ambush, no wreckage, no trouble but the usual. That's the best news I've had all week, and I include the news that I'm still standing. Here, you've earned this. Don't spend it all in one tavern. The Rear Guard doesn't have taverns.

@ spn_worg_cull
D: The worgs have taken to the verge of the Trunk Road, and the ghouls have taken to following whatever the worgs leave behind. Between them they have made a ditch of the road's shoulder, and drovers are starting to ask for an escort. Vesk would like both pushed back out of range, preferably in pieces.
A: Anywhere in the Silverpine woods. Worg Prowlers and Feral Ghouls, eight of the first, six of the second. Make them think twice about the road. I'm not asking for a massacre. I'm asking for a lesson, and for the lesson to last the winter.
T: A quieter road. Nobody will notice, which is exactly how it should be. If they ever do, it means something went wrong. I'll have a word put in at the Sepulcher about you. A short one. It'll say you're useful.

@ spn_pell_glands
D: Apothecary Pell has a theory that Silverpine spider venom, properly cut, can slow the Plague's advance. Pell has a great many theories, and the Rear Guard has learned to leave a respectful distance between itself and each one. This one, at least, requires something killable. Six venom glands, cut out whole.
A: Silverpine Spiders, out in the woods. Cut the gland out cleanly. Don't squeeze. I cannot stress this enough: don't squeeze. I have a list of apprentices who squeezed, and I am running short of apprentices.
T: Beautiful. Green, fresh, still faintly pulsing. If this works, the Dark Lady will hear my name. If it doesn't, you never met me. Come back in a week and I'll tell you how it went, or someone will.

@ spn_ravenna_rite
D: Dark Cleric Ravenna has prepared a Rite of Quieting for a woman in Olsen's Farthing, whose husband will not stop walking past her window. The Rite must be read at dusk, by the widow, in her own voice. Ravenna cannot leave the gate, and so she needs someone gentle to carry it.
A: West along the Farthing road. The widow lives in the house nearest the dead orchard. Tell her to read it at dusk, aloud, and not to stop if he answers. And do not read it yourself. Words like these hear more than they should.
T: She said I'd send someone kind. I'd hoped she was joking. Thank you. It's easier to be gentle at a distance than up close, and I have been at a distance for a very long time. I'll light a lamp for the man at the window.

@ spn_scout_survey
D: The Silverpine Scout has watched the Trunk Road from the verge for a season and knows its tracks like his own handwriting. This week he has found fresh prints on three of its spurs, all the same, all heading the same way. He wants someone to follow the three spurs to their ends before he reports it, because he does not want to be wrong in front of Vesk.
A: Dead Field road, Pool path, Orchard lane. The tracks are the same on all three, and I don't like that. One scout doesn't cover three roads in a night. Either there are three of them, or one of them is lying. Go and look at the ends.
T: Same boot on all three roads. Someone is scouting the Rear Guard's approaches. Vesk needs to hear this, and he is going to need a stiff drink before he does. Thank you. You saved me from sounding foolish. I appreciate it more than I'll say.

@ spn_farthing_hounds
D: Gaffer Olsen kept hounds for forty years. Since the plague, they have kept him: hungry, rabid and pacing the fence all night, and the risen farmhands open the gate for them. He wants both dealt with, and is not above admitting that he is afraid of his own dogs.
A: The fields around my farm. The hounds first, eight of them, then the farmhands. Well. The farmhands. They were good lads, once. I'm not asking you to enjoy it. I'm only asking that you do it before they come in for supper.
T: Quieter. Thank you. I'll fill the troughs again, I think, when I'm brave enough. It's a strange thing, missing the sound of something that wanted to eat you.

@ spn_olsens_seed
D: Olsen believes that if he can plant one decent row of wheat, the Farthing might remember it was a farm. The risen farmhands still carry the last of the seed sacks in their pockets, from habit more than purpose. He needs five of them, and he will do the rest himself.
A: The farmhands still carry them. I don't know why. Habit, I suppose. Five sacks. I'll do the rest. I've got a spade and a good spot by the barn, and nothing in the world to stop me but my own hands, which have started shaking.
T: Good grain, if a little singed. I'll plant it at dawn. Somebody has to believe in dawn. It may as well be an old man with seed in his pocket.

@ spn_old_hob
D: Old Hob was Olsen's best dog, and he leads the pack now. He was a gentle old thing once, who slept at the foot of Olsen's bed and cried when it thundered. The plague did not take his size. Olsen asks that you not make it slow.
A: In the fields south of the farm. The big one with the grey muzzle. He was a good boy. He still is, somewhere. Just do it quickly. Please.
T: He'd have followed you home, once. Thank you for being quick. I'll bury him under the apple tree, where he used to sleep. It's the only tree on the farm that still has a shadow.

@ spn_farmhand_locket
D: A silver locket, pried from the pocket of a risen farmhand. Inside, a name: Marta. And a lock of hair the same colour as hers. Whoever wore it before the plague kept it close enough to die with it, and Marta is the one person in the Farthing who should have it back.
A: Take it to Widow Marta, at the house nearest the orchard. She will want it back. She'll tell you she doesn't. Leave it on the table.
T: He kept it. All this time, he kept it. Thank you. I won't forget this. I'd offer you tea, but I haven't had the kettle on in a year. Tonight I might.

@ spn_rh_husks
D: Keeper Oswin has buried the same corpse three times this month. The risen husks of Raven Hill pull themselves out of the soil faster than he can fill the graves, and he has run out of reasons to keep digging. Thinning them might buy him a night to think.
A: Raven Hill Cemetery. They climb out at dusk, eight of them at least. Beat them back. If you can bury them again afterwards, so much the better, but I'll settle for them staying down long enough for me to finish my tea.
T: That will keep the shovel busy for another night, at least. I find I'm grateful for the work. A keeper with nothing to keep is only a man standing in a field, and I've been that man before.

@ spn_rh_moss
D: Acolyte Dessa believes the moss growing on Raven Hill's grave markers is feeding the dead, and that the dead are feeding it back. She wants six clippings from the Grave Moss Creepers that tend the stones, so she can study what they have been eating.
A: The creepers at the lower graves. Take the moss from the body, not the stones. The stones are still sacred. Six clippings. I'll know if you've taken them from a headstone, so please don't.
T: Grey-green, thick with bone dust. It's feeding on them, and they're feeding on it. How very efficient. I'd call it beautiful if it weren't so disgusting.

@ spn_rh_vigil
D: To find where the dead are climbing from, Dessa needs someone to stand at the three oldest crypts and listen. It is, she admits, a poor job. She has asked three other people. None of them came back with an answer, and one has not come back at all.
A: Three crypts: east, north-west, south. Stand at each and count to ten. Listen for anything that isn't wind. Don't go inside. I'd tell you not to be afraid, but fear will keep you honest.
T: Scratching. Under all three. As if something is digging up from below. Oswin needs to hear this. Thank you. I'll bring it to him myself. It's the sort of thing a man should hear from someone who isn't you.

@ spn_rh_watchman
D: A Forsaken Watchman still patrols the upper cemetery. He no longer answers challenges, or his own name. Oswin knew him, and has not been able to watch him walk the same circle for another winter. He asks that it end, and asks it quietly.
A: The top of the hill, by the iron gate. He walks a circle. Don't be on the circle. He was my friend, once, if friends is the word. Make it clean.
T: He'll stand a post somewhere kinder now. Thank you for sending him. I'll finish the grave tomorrow, when I've had a rest. I'd like him to have a proper one. I think he'd laugh at that.

@ spn_df_crows
D: Huntress Verrel has been trying to shoot the crows of the Dead Field for a week. They don't die the way crows should. They look at her, and settle on the next fence-post, and wait. She has stopped trusting her own bow, and she would like someone else to try.
A: The Dead Field, east of the Trunk Road. Blighted crows, ten of them. Aim for the eyes. They notice. They notice everything. Don't stand still, and don't look away when they look at you.
T: Fewer eyes on me, finally. I'm in your debt. Take a bow, if you like. I won't use it. I think I've decided to take up knitting.

@ spn_df_harrowers
D: Tobb the farmhand was the last to leave the Dead Field and the first to come back. He sleeps in the barn, because it is the only place that still feels like his. He says the scarecrows walk at night, in the furrows, and that he is tired of watching them from the loft.
A: In the furrows, and along the fence lines. They wear farm coats. Don't mistake them for farmers. Eight of them. I know you'll be quick, but be quiet too. They don't like a fuss.
T: I sleep a little easier. I'll sleep a lot easier when I know where the straw goes. Thank you. If you ever come by the barn, there's a hat on the nail by the door. It's yours. It's the only thing I've got.

@ spn_df_straw
D: Every harrower carries a straw charm sewn into its coat, and Tobb recognises the stitching. It is his own wife's. He wants five charms back, so that at least the stitches will be in a decent place, and not in the pockets of walking scarecrows.
A: Take the charm from the coat, the left breast. Don't pull the thread. It unravels. Five of them. Don't tell me what's inside them. I've decided I don't want to know.
T: That's her hand, on every one. I'll bury them by the barn. Thank you. It's not much of a grave, but it will be hers. That's the only way I've ever understood what a grave is for.

@ spn_df_ring
D: A tarnished gold band, pecked loose from a crow's nest. The inside is engraved: T. & L., forever. Someone in the Dead Field would know whose it is, and probably already does.
A: Take it to Tobb, at the barn. He'll know it. He'll pretend he doesn't. Don't push. Just leave it where he can see it.
T: Lena's. I thought the crows had it. I hoped they hadn't. Thank you. I'll put it with the charms. A man ought to have something to leave beside a grave, even if it's only the truth.

@ spn_df_king
D: Something rules the Dead Field's scarecrows: a tall one in a straw crown, wrapped in a farmer's shroud, who watches Verrel from the middle of the field. She has shot it twice. It is still standing, and very much interested in her.
A: The middle of the Dead Field, where the crows circle. The tall one. You'll know the crown. It'll look at you first, and then it'll look at me. I'd like you to be done before it decides which of us to hate.
T: A quieter field, finally. I'll have to find a new hobby. I suppose I could try gardening, now that the garden is no longer looking back.

@ spn_gate_hold
D: Gatewarden Ilsa guards the iron door of the Sepulcher. The dead are welcome inside, but the things that follow them are not. Treants and ghouls have been crowding the approach for days, and she has run out of patience, and, she admits, of sleep.
A: The woods around the gate. Treants, ghouls. Anything that shuffles toward the door. Five treants, six ghouls. I'd do it myself, but if I step away, the door opens, and I do not like what opens it.
T: The door stays closed to what should be kept out. Welcome inside, whenever you like. I'll tell the Sepulcher you held the gate. It's not a glorious thing to have done, but it's the sort the world rests on.

@ spn_orchard_blight
D: Malden's Orchard has not borne fruit in years, but the trees still drop it: a brown, heavy windfall that moves on its own. The orchard-warden wants the shamblers cleared, and the wasps that hive in the rot driven out, before the whole grove becomes one animal.
A: Under the old trees. The shamblers wear bark, the wasps wear a grudge. Eight of each. The wasps are worse. They don't give you the courtesy of dying.
T: Fewer shamblers means more room for the trees to recover. Maybe. I have been telling myself that for years. Thank you. It's good to say it to someone who might believe it.

@ spn_orchard_cores
D: At the heart of every shambler is a seed-core of rotapple: black, dense, and faintly warm. Malwick believes the cores are what keep the blight spreading, and that burning them in the orchard's last furnace might end it. He needs six.
A: Take them from the shamblers' chests. They are heavier than they look. Wrap them in cloth. They won't hurt you, but they'll remember you, and I'd rather they didn't.
T: Black as pitch. Warm as a living heart. They go to the furnace tonight. I'll light it myself. It's the last good thing I know how to do with fire.

@ spn_orchard_keeper
D: Under the biggest tree in the orchard stands a thing that was Malden's gardener. It tends the blight now, with the same patience it once tended roses. Malwick offers a bitter smile. "He always did love his work."
A: At the old tree, east of the trough. He holds a pruning hook the size of a plough. Don't let him prune you. He'll start with the extremities. Do it fast, and don't talk to him. He's very persuasive when he talks about pruning.
T: He'll tend something quieter now. Thank you. I'll plant something at his tree, if I can find a seed that will take. I think he'd like roses.

@ spn_pools_beasts
D: The Shadowspring Pools were a Forsaken drinking-hole before the springs turned black. Now bog beasts and crocolisks hunt the shallows, and nobody has dared fill a flask in a month. Yaelis wants the pools made safe enough to stand beside, even if no one drinks from them again.
A: The three pools, south-east of the crossroads. Six bog beasts, six crocolisks. Don't wade. They can swim, and you can't. Keep to the bank, and watch the reeds. The reeds move when they shouldn't.
T: A quieter shoreline. I'll stand a little closer to the water now. Thank you. I can almost remember what it was like to be thirsty. Almost.

@ spn_pools_fangs
D: Rot Hide Slitherers hunt at the pools' edge, and their fangs carry a venom the Apothecary Society would pay handsomely for. Yaelis would settle for six, and the satisfaction of seeing them drained. It is, she says, the only revenge she can still afford.
A: Slitherers, in the reeds. Pull the fang with tongs. Please, use tongs. I have lost two fingers to people who did not use tongs.
T: Six fangs, still wet. Pell at the Rear Guard will weep with joy. Thank you. I'll bring them to her myself. It's good to give someone something that makes them cry for the right reasons.

@ spn_muckmaw
D: Something large and very old lives in the deepest pool. It has eaten two of Yaelis's scouts and a good portion of her patience. The locals call it Muckmaw, and they say it has been there since before the Forsaken, before the Scourge, before the road. Kill it.
A: The southernmost pool. Wait for the bubbles. Then don't wait. It strikes from underneath, and it strikes once. If you're quick enough, it won't strike twice.
T: The water's still black. But it's quiet, and that's enough for now. Thank you. I'll write the names of the two I lost on the stone by the shore. You can add yours, if you like. In pencil.

@ spn_sd_skitterers
D: Webhunter Corvane has tracked the spiders of the Skittering Dark for months, and the last month has been the worst: bigger, bolder, and drifting toward the Trunk Road. He wants the breeding stock culled before they swarm, and he wants it done before the next frost slows him down.
A: East of the Crossroads, in the hollows. Keep to the road till you see the webs. Eight skitterers, four of the giants. Don't walk through a web if you can go around. If you can't, walk fast.
T: Fewer eyes in the dark. I'll almost sleep tonight. Almost. A spider doesn't have to be there to be watching.

@ spn_sd_sacs
D: To end the swarm, Corvane needs the next generation. Egg sacs, carried by the skitterers themselves, and the only thing that makes the Widow's brood listen to anyone. Six of them would break the nest's rhythm and buy the road a season.
A: Take the sacs from their bodies. Don't puncture them. Carry them in silk, if you've got it. I wouldn't ask, but you've seen what the brood does to people who carry the wrong thing.
T: Six. Sealed. Warm. I'll burn them at the road. Never let a spider make a nest next to a road. It's the first rule I taught my apprentices.

@ spn_sd_longleg
D: A spider the size of a cart prowls the Skittering Dark and takes a hunter in a week. The others call it Longleg. Corvane has lost three apprentices to it, and he has run out of apprentices who are willing to take the job.
A: Deep in the Dark, where the webs get thick. It moves faster than it should. I'd say aim for the legs, but it has so many. Aim for what's holding it up.
T: No more apprentices. Thank you. I'll tell their families. It won't be what they hoped for, but it'll be an ending, and I think that's the most I can give.

@ spn_sd_lair
D: Longleg's brood came from somewhere, and Corvane thinks he knows where: the cave at the end of the Skittering road, the Widow's Lair. He wants someone to stand at the mouth and tell him how deep the webs go, and whether the cave is breathing.
A: East end of the Skittering road. Don't go in unprepared. And don't go in alone. Stand at the mouth, and look. If the webs are moving, you'll know.
T: As I feared. Silk to the ceiling. I'll hold the rest of the road. You, if you've the stomach, can hold the cave. Not today. But soon.

@ spn_rg_reckoning
D: Vesk has four reports on his desk: the Watchman is down, the Scarecrow-King is down, the Orchardkeeper is down, and Muckmaw is down. Whoever carried those four threads through the pines should stand at his desk and be thanked properly, and Vesk, who has not thanked anyone in a decade, has been practising.
A: Come back to the gate. Walk through it. I'd like you to see what you've protected. Stand in the yard. Look at the road. I'll be along.
T: Four bosses, four reports, one hero. The Dark Lady's Rear Guard doesn't hand out praise. Take this instead. And let me say it, because no one else will: well done.
