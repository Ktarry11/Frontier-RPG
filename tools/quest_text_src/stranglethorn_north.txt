# THE ZONE'S ONE IDEA:  NOBODY IN GROM'GOL CHOSE STRANGLETHORN. THEY WERE POSTED TO IT.
# Everything in that camp came off an airship and the jungle takes some of it back every week. The
# supply line is the spine: a weekly shortfall turns out to be a building programme, the building
# programme is on a stair nobody left alive built, and the camp's own three-year-old grievances are
# closer to hand than anybody's origin.

@ stn_the_weekly_shortfall
Title: The Weekly Shortfall
D: Storesman Pell counts everything into Grom'gol and everything out of it, and the gap between those
  two numbers has been the same gap for eleven weeks. Six crates, give or take one. He has written it
  up as spoilage twice and did not believe it either time.
A: You will want to know what a tally is. A tally is the number of things, written down, so that the
  number cannot be argued with later. Mine says six crates a week go off the Vine Road between my south
  gate and the first bend. The raptors work that stretch and raptors chew. So go and work it back the
  other way, and when you find something chewed, bring me the stave off it.
T: These are cut. Look at the end of one. A raptor leaves you a splintered end and every one of these
  is flat, and I have spent eleven weeks writing spoilage in my own hand while somebody walked off with
  the boards. I would rather have been robbed loudly. At least then the book would be right.

@ stn_cut_not_chewed
Title: Cut, Not Chewed
D: Overseer Mokra has Pell's flat-ended stave on her table and a camp that is six crates a week light.
  She does not ask where you are from and she does not offer a reason for not asking. She asks whether
  you can get as far as the cutline fork and back before the light goes.
A: Stonehand. The stone-armed ones off the crag, and they have been working my road. I want fewer of
  them on it, and I want eyes on the fork where the cutline splits, because that is where the road
  stops being mine and starts being theirs. Do not bring me anything back. Bring me what you saw.
T: Stacked. You are standing there telling me they stacked it. Not broken into, not eaten, not burned
  for the warmth of it. Stacked and squared with the lids still on. That is worse than thieves. Thieves
  I can post a sentry against. Somebody out there has a use for my timber and a number in their head
  for when they have enough of it.

@ stn_what_the_wood_is_for
Title: What the Wood Is For
D: Smith Dorgun has rebuilt the same three things for three years, because nothing in this jungle
  seasons and nothing dry grows here. Every board in Grom'gol came off an airship. He has heard what
  Mokra found at the fork and he already knows what it is.
A: Battens. Thin, dressed, cut to one length. If they are dressing boards they are not burning them,
  and you do not build with another camp's timber unless you have run out of your own. Get me six off
  the Stonehand, and keep the apes off your back while you do it. They come down the cutline for
  anything that smells of the camp, and my lumber smells of the camp all the way through.
T: That is a lashing notch. That is a dressed edge and that is a lashing notch, and the two of them
  together make a scaffold batten. They are standing on my lumber to reach something higher than they
  can reach. Three years I have been short of board, and the trolls on that crag have been building.

@ stn_one_road_four_names
Title: One Road, Four Names
D: Attache Zul'jana has listened to a camp full of orcs call it the Vine Road for three years. It is
  the fourth name the road has had and the shortest. She will trade you the third for the whole length
  of it, top to bottom, which nobody in Grom'gol has ever walked.
A: You want to know what the Stonehand are building. Start with what you are standing on. Walk it from
  the Marosh ford at the top to the seam at the bottom, all of it, and mind the pythons where the
  canopy comes down to the road. Then come back and I will give you one of the older names. One of
  them. I am not handing you the set.
T: Zul'marosh Dakka. The stair road. It never ran south to anywhere, it ran up, and your camp put a
  gate on the wrong end of it and called the rest of it vines. Somebody here will ask you where you
  were the day the sky changed. I am not going to. I have had that answer out of eleven of you now and
  not one of you knew what to do with it either.

@ stn_the_other_half
Title: The Other Half
D: Herbalist Mossa sells the half of the jungle that will not kill you, and she buys the raw cut from a
  Darkspear supplier down in Booty Bay who has started quoting her a tariff instead of a price. The
  same bark grows two hours from the south gate.
A: Mangrove bark, strips off the water-side root and not off the trunk. The camp calls it mistvale bark,
  which is wrong but shorter. Dead'wa. The water that stands. Say it whichever way suits you, I am
  going to write the real one in my book either way. Take the strips and keep the stiltstalkers off you
  while you cut, because they do not rush and they do not stop.
T: Trunk bark. You cut the trunk. It will do for a wash and it will not do for a draught, so I will
  sell it as a wash and nobody will die of it. Two hours from the gate. Two hours, and I have paid a
  goblin's tariff for three years to have the same bark come the long way round by sea.

@ stn_standing_water
Title: Standing Water
D: Field-Medic Sulaa has a ward full of rotting feet and a year of her own reports blaming the lagoon
  for it. She is asking for this quietly, at the back of her tent, and she would rather you did not
  mention having been asked.
A: I need to know what that water does. Walk the lagoon shore and stand in it, and get the crocolisks
  thin enough that standing in it is a thing you can do for a while. I am not asking you to be careless
  with yourself. I am asking for one person who is not one of mine, and not under me, to come back and
  tell me whether that water rots a foot in a week.
T: It does not. Then it is the boots. The whole camp's boots came off the airship in one lot three
  years ago and it was a bad lot, and the quartermaster who signed for them is still signing for
  things, and I have written four reports about a lagoon. I am going to ask you to leave that out of
  anything you say in the hall. Then I am going to go and say it to him myself.

@ stn_including_me
Title: Including Me
D: Berth-Master Zekka will tell anyone who stands still that everything in Grom'gol came off that
  airship, including him. He wants a crate back out of the lagoon. It went over the side of the berth
  stair in the camp's first season and he describes it, twice, as stores.
A: Camp stores. Went off the stair into the water back when the mangroves still came up to the wall,
  and the crocolisks moved in after, which is the only reason it is still down there. Four bundles,
  whatever is left dry inside them. Watch the toads more than the lizards. A toad out there does not
  look like anything at all until it is on you.
T: That is not stores. That is a mask, a cord, and a drum skin that has been wet for three years, and
  my name is on the side of the crate in the column for cargo, because that is how I came. Listed
  between the salt and the rope. I packed my own tribe into a camp requisition so that nobody would
  read it out at the foot of the stair. Keep the cord. I do not want it looked at.

@ stn_less_wrong_than_it_was
Title: Less Wrong Than It Was
D: Mapmaker Ashi has drawn the Zul'Marosh crag three times and each sheet has more stair on it than
  the last. She has been assuming she miscounted, because the other explanation is a ruin that is
  getting bigger.
A: The lower court first, then the mouth above it. Stand in both and count courses. Not an impression,
  not about that many. Count them, and bring the number back, because I have three sheets with three
  different numbers on them and all three of them are mine.
T: More than the last sheet. Then it is not a ruin. I have been drawing it as a ruin for two years with
  the little cross hatching we use for a thing that has stopped, and there is no mark on any chart I
  own for a thing somebody is still working on. I am going to have to invent one and then argue about
  it with Orgrimmar for a year.

@ stn_the_stair_ends_in_a_socket
Title: The Stair Ends in a Socket
D: Scout Hanzo has been to the top of the Steps. Nobody in Grom'gol has asked him about it, because
  nobody in Grom'gol believed the stair went anywhere. It goes to a flat court with a socket cut in the
  middle of it and nothing standing in the socket.
A: The stairwardens hold the middle flights and they hold them properly, in reliefs, which is not a
  thing I have seen a jungle troll bother to do. Get through enough of them to work past the second
  landing, and bring me stone. Loose course stone off the stair itself, the old grey stuff, from where
  it has fallen and not from where it is still laid.
T: Five faces. The old stone is dressed on five faces and the new work is dressed on four, and you only
  leave the fifth rough if you know what is going on top of it. Whoever is laying that new course has
  never seen the stair finished. They are copying the outside of a thing. Three years of patient good
  work by somebody who does not know what it was for.

@ stn_nobody_left_who_built_it
Title: Nobody Left Who Built It
D: Zul'Marosh is the oldest troll work in this jungle. Zul'jana's own tribe has no story about building
  it and no story about leaving it, and she says a tribe with no story about a place is a tribe that
  was never there.
A: The Rotvine call themselves keepers of that stair. They are not. They are sitting in a house calling
  themselves the family. Their vinespeakers carry knot-cord, which is a record, and I want five of them
  off them. I want you to hear me say that I am asking you to take a people's writing off their bodies,
  because I am not going to pretend to you that it is less than that.
T: Recent. Every cord in your hand was tied inside three years. They kept nothing before that and then
  something started them, all at once, the way a camp starts a ledger when it has decided it owns
  something. Do not ask me what. I have a guess, and the guess belongs further south than here, with
  trolls who never stopped keeping theirs.

@ stn_the_long_way_round
Title: The Long Way Round
D: Runner Teza came back round the fen instead of through it and says she would do it again. What she
  has not said to anybody is that she came back without her pouch, and the fen has had it for three
  weeks.
A: This is a favour and I am asking it as one. There is a courier pouch out on the causeway short of the
  fen landing, in the rot where the creepers sit. Get out to the landing, get the creepers off the
  ground, and look where the gangplank meets the bank. I have not reported it. I would rather go on not
  having reported it.
T: Lamp oil. Six jars of lamp oil and a requisition number, and the number is for the stores tent I
  walk past every morning. I went round a fen in the dark and then worried about it for three weeks
  over lamp oil, and the part I cannot get out of my head is that nobody has come asking for the lamp
  oil either.

@ stn_three_flags
Title: Three Flags
D: Signalman Grut runs two flags for an airship coming in and three for one that is not stopping. Three
  have gone up four times this season, each time with the fen smoking, and the camp has gone four weeks
  short on account of it.
A: Four resupplies turned away off my tower and I ran the flags for every one of them. Smoke over the
  fen means fire, fire in a jungle means a mooring nobody will risk, and so up go three flags. Go east
  and find me what is burning out there. The oozes are thick on that ground. If they are what is
  burning, bring me a piece of one and I will take the loss myself.
T: Gas. It is gas. Those bladders come up out of the rot and the oozes go through them like a boot
  through ice, and this camp has gone hungry four times for a smell. Nobody ordered those flags. I read
  the smoke and I ran them, on my own judgement, four times. I am going to write that down before
  somebody upstairs writes a kinder version of it for me.

@ stn_the_hill_runs_warm
Title: The Hill Runs Warm
D: The outflow under Rotvine Hollow is what rots the fen, and Hanzo has put his hand in it twice to be
  sure of the one strange thing about it. It runs warm. Caves do not run warm, and the water coming out
  of that hill sits lower than the water standing inside it.
A: Go in as far as the first standing water and look at the line on the wall above it. The stranglers
  hold the entry galleries and they come down out of the roof, so keep your head up and your back to
  the stone. I want a yes or a no on one thing and you do not need to go deep for it: is there more
  water in that hill than is coming out of it.
T: Then something is holding it back. A hill that fills and does not empty has a plug in it, and a plug
  is either stone, which does not move on its own, or it is alive, which does. I have been reporting
  that outflow as weather for two years. Weather is what you call a thing you have not walked into yet.

@ stn_what_was_holding_it_back
Title: What Was Holding It Back
D: Zul'jana has heard Hanzo's answer and did not need the second half of it. The Rotvine did not name
  their hill after the rot, she says. They named the rot after the hill, and they were right to.
A: Rootmaw. A root mass with a mouth grown across the throat of the lower gallery, and it has been
  drinking that cistern since before anybody alive started counting. Take it out of the water. And
  bring me the tokens off the drowned down there, and look at the marks on them before you take
  anything, because the drowned in that hill are not Rotvine and never were.
T: Stairkeepers. That token is a count of courses, which is how a keeper signed for the stair he kept,
  and the number cut into these is bigger than the stair is. The stair was taller. Somebody took the
  top off it and left no story about doing it, and the Rotvine up there are building back toward a
  number they found written on a dead man.

@ stn_hapka_still_counting
Title: Hap'ka, Still Counting
D: Overseer Mokra has four turned-away resupplies, a fen that stinks when it rains, and a cistern in
  the hill above it that has just started draining into her water. She is not interested in the stair.
  She is interested in the smell.
A: Whatever is at the bottom of that hill is in my water now, and a berth-master will not bring an
  airship down into a stink. The Rotvine sent one of their own up there to keep the stair for them and
  he drowned doing it, and by every account he has not let that stop him. Go down to the sump and
  finish it, and bring me whatever he is carrying, because he is carrying something.
T: Cord. Knotted cord, wet through, and three years of it by the length. Take it to the attache. She
  read it in front of me, she rolled it up, and she told me it was not for me. Three years I have held
  this camp and I have just been told politely, in my own hall, that the thing I paid for is not mine
  to read. Ask her again when you are further south than this. She will not say it to me twice.

@ stn_keep_to_it
Title: Keep To It
D: Sentry Vrak holds the south gate and tells everyone the same thing about the road. What she does not
  tell them is why the panthers have learned the gate, and she has kept it out of her reports, because
  the answer is in the ground outside the west wall.
A: Greenpelts on the apron. They come right up to the wall now, in daylight, and they are not hunting
  the wall, they are waiting at it. Thin them off the gate and off the first bend of the road. Keep to
  the road while you do it. There is nothing worth seeing off it on the west side and I would take it
  as a courtesy if you did not go looking.
T: Clear. For about a week. Three years we have put our dead outside the wall, because the ground
  inside the palisade is rock a spade bounces off, and they have worked out which day we do it. Are you
  staying? I am not making conversation. I am counting what I have got and you are either in the count
  or you are passing through.

@ stn_twelve_bunks_and_a_fire
Title: Twelve Bunks and a Fire
D: Innkeeper Rukha has twelve bunks, a hearth and the only dry wood in Grom'gol, which is not dry.
  She wants resin, and she is the only person in this camp who will say out loud that she does not care
  where anybody came from.
A: Resin off the buttress trees. It burns wet, which is the whole miracle of the stuff, and the boars
  root it out of the bark and tread it into the mulch, so you will be taking it off a boar one way or
  another. And before you go out there: I do not care where you are from. Not from here is the whole
  of it, and that is true of every single body behind this fence, mine included. Nobody else in this
  camp is going to say that to you, so I say it early and I say it to everyone.
T: That will keep the fire honest for a month. Your name is on a bunk now. In chalk, on the post, same
  as everyone's. The ones who have been here since the camp landed still wander past and check theirs
  is still up there, every few weeks, and not one of them has ever asked me why I would rub it off. I
  have never rubbed one off in my life.

@ stn_the_baron_charges_me_to_land
Title: The Baron Charges Me To Land
D: Windrider-Keeper Pazzik pays Baron Revilgaz for the privilege of setting down in Booty Bay and has
  paid it every week since the camp landed. He wants a second roost that is not Booty Bay. He has
  already picked the coast north of the camp, without having been to look at it.
A: North end of the coast trail, past the ford. I want to know if there is sky over it. A wyvern wants
  open water on one side and nothing overhead on the other, and the raptors work that trail hard, so
  you will be earning the look. Count the raptors while you are at it. I price a route by what eats the
  courier, not by how far it is.
T: Canopy to the waterline. No roost, then, and the Baron keeps his landing fee for another year. You
  lot gave me a word for the thing I run off that tower, you know. Timetable. I called it the schedule
  for years and a schedule is what you hope for. A timetable is the thing you are late against. Best
  word anybody ever handed me and I did not have to pay a copper for it.
