# Silverpine dungeon quests (Widow's Lair, Ambermill Tower, Shadowfang Keep). Location hints kept.

@ spn_wl_brood
D: Corvane has seen the inside of the Widow's Lair from its mouth and counted what lives there: hatchlings by the thousand, and silk-bound weavers tending them like nurses. He wants the first rooms thinned before anyone tries the deeper ones, because a brood that size will spill into the woods the moment it feels the walls give.
A: Inside the Lair, around the Web Heart. Eight hatchlings, five weavers. The hatchlings first. The weavers sit above them and spit. Go in with a party, and keep your torch low. They don't like the light, but they like the heat.
T: Fewer legs. That's the best thing I can say about the Lair right now. Take this. I'd say rest, but there's nowhere safe to do it between here and the next room.

@ spn_wl_silka
D: The hive buds off a single queen: Broodmother Silka, in the west lair of the Web Heart. Everything Longleg ever was came from her eggs, and everything he might have been will come from her next. Corvane wants that line ended, and he wants it done before the next clutch hatches.
A: West of the Web Heart. Follow the egg sacs. She is what they're all running from. Don't fight her where she's laid. Draw her out, and take the room back.
T: No more eggs. For the first time since the plague, the woods will have fewer legs than last year. Thank you. I'll burn the sacs myself. I'd like to watch.

@ spn_wl_skitter
D: A second queen-brood sits in the east lair under a weaver named Skitter, who hangs her victims from the ceiling for later and does not seem to hurry. Corvane has watched it from the mouth and wants to stop watching, because he has started to recognise the faces.
A: East of the Web Heart. Look up before you step in. If the ceiling is moving, you've waited too long. Take a healer, and keep to the walls.
T: Hung up like laundry. The whole east lair. Well. Not any more. Thank you. I'll cut them down myself, one by one. Someone should.

@ spn_wl_venom
D: Pell's venom theory has a new requirement: stalker venom, which is stronger than the Silverpine spiders' glands by a good half. Five sacs from the Lair's Venom Stalkers would let her finish a batch she has been unable to start for a month.
A: Venom Stalkers, inside the Lair. Cut the sac at the base. Seal it. Run. I cannot stress enough how strongly I'd advise running. I have a list of the people who didn't.
T: Oh, that is beautiful. Thick as syrup, green as a corpse. The Dark Lady's Apothecaries will want to hear about this. Not yet, though. Let me enjoy it a while first.

@ spn_wl_larder
D: Corvane wants to know how many people the hive has eaten. The answer lies in two places, deep in the Lair: the Larder, where they hang the fresh ones, and the Husk Pit, where they drop what's left. He has put off asking for three weeks, and has finally run out of excuses.
A: Far west and far east of the Web Heart. Stand in each long enough to count. Don't touch anything. Don't take anything. I only need the number.
T: Forty-one, and I stopped counting. Thank you. I'll send letters to a few families who'll hate me for it. It's the right thing to do, and it's the thing I least want to do.

@ spn_wl_journal
D: A silk-wrapped journal page, pulled from a Brood Warden's carapace. The hand is a hunter's, and the last entry begins: "I have counted the exits, and there are only two." Apothecary Pell was keeping a list of the missing, and one of them wrote this.
A: Take it to Pell, at the Rear Guard, with the cauldron. He keeps the list. Don't read it on the way. I know you'll be tempted. Some things are better delivered than understood.
T: Marek Dunn. I had him as "lost to wolves". A spider is worse, and better. I'll correct the list. It's the only thing I can do for him now, and I'm going to do it properly.

@ spn_wl_velaxis
D: Above the Web Heart is the Matriarch's Spire, where Velaxis nests: older than Silka, larger than Skitter, and not fond of company. Corvane wants the hive's head cut off, and will not pretend that it is anything but a death sentence for whoever tries it.
A: Up the north neck from the Web Heart. The Spire is lit by her eyes. Go with your best party, and bring everything you have. I'll hold the road behind you. It's the least I can do.
T: Eight eyes, all closed. I'll rest easy tonight, and so will the woods. You've earned every copper, and a good deal more than I can pay. Come back anyway. I'd like to hear the story from the beginning.

@ sps_at_vestibule
D: With Quillfire silent, Orsolo dares to wonder what the waygate was keeping. The tower's lowest rooms are a vestibule and a reading hall, and he cannot bring himself to look at either. He wants someone to stand in both and say whether the lamps are still lit.
A: Step into the waygate. The vestibule is first. The reading hall is up the stair. Don't touch the lamps. If they're lit, don't read by them. They've been burning for a very long time, and I don't think they were meant for us.
T: Still lit. Violet. So it's listening. Thank you, truly. I'd hoped it had stopped. I think I'd have liked to be wrong.

@ sps_at_mages
D: The reading hall is staffed by the dead: Kirin Tor and Forsaken mages who kept studying after they stopped breathing, and imps bound to the lecterns to keep the pages turning. Orsolo wants the hall cleared so a scribe can walk it, and so the old books can be read by someone alive.
A: The Reading Hall and the galleries above. Six Kirin Tor, six Forsaken, four imps. Mages cast first, imps hold the doors. Take a party, and bring someone who can dispel. These were good scholars. They'll be very good at killing you.
T: A scribe can walk it now. Perhaps even a very brave librarian. I used to have a good sense of what a hall like that was worth. I think I'd forgotten.

@ sps_at_tomes
D: Tome Wraiths are the pages of a book that learned to hover. Orsolo has a theory about who wrote the book, and the theory frightens him more than the wraiths do. He needs six torn pages to prove it, and he needs someone to bring them back without reading a word.
A: Tome Wraiths haunt the galleries. Take the pages off them. Do not read aloud. It's not that the words are dangerous. It's that I don't want to hear them in your voice.
T: I know this hand. I know this hand. Hawren. It was Hawren all along. Hawren, who taught me to read. I should have known. I think I did know. I think I just didn't want to.

@ sps_at_hawren
D: Magister Hawren taught Orsolo to read, and keeps the Archivist's Gallery still, though he no longer remembers a student's name. He is surrounded by his own pages, and he is still teaching, to no one. Orsolo cannot do it himself.
A: The Archivist's Gallery, west of the reading hall. He is surrounded by his own pages. He'll call you by someone else's name. Don't correct him. Let him be kind to you. Then do what must be done.
T: He'd want to be remembered for something better. I'll try to oblige. Thank you. I think I'll start writing it down tonight, the things he taught me. Someone should have them.

@ sps_at_spellblades
D: The mill's saws were enchanted to run themselves. The tower's enchanted blades are the same kind of fool: bright, quick, and indifferent to who they cut. Haskell has lost two apprentices to the lesser ones, and does not trust the greater.
A: All through the tower, seven of them. They're the ones that glint. They don't tire. I'd say wear heavy armour, but they cut through that too. Wear a friend.
T: Quieter. I've got my last two apprentices counting sawblades and sorting nails. That's all I'll ask of them for a month. Thank you. I think you saved the trade.

@ sps_at_reagents
D: The Alchemy Lab was Dalaran's best-stocked apothecary outside the Violet Citadel, and its alchemists never stopped brewing. Haskell wants five sealed reagent vials, before the lab's slow poison seeps out of the tower and into the mill-yard's wells.
A: The Alchemy Lab, north-east of the lower hall. Forsaken Alchemists carry them on their belts. Five vials. If one leaks, drop it and walk away. Don't try to catch it.
T: Sealed, thank the Light, and not yet leaking. I'll have them neutralised by dawn. I didn't think you'd manage. I should be ashamed. I'm not. I'm relieved.

@ sps_at_ledger
D: A flask-stained ledger pulled from an alchemist's coat. Page after page of orders for "reagents of discretion", each one initialled H. Someone at the mill supplied that lab for years, and Haskell would very much like to know whether it was a friend.
A: Take it to Haskell, at the mill. He'll recognise the initials. Be gentle when you tell him. He has more of these than he lets on.
T: H. Helbrim. He sold the mill its lamp-oil, and I'd have said he was a decent man. Not any more. Thank you. I'll sit with this for a while. I think I'll need to.

@ sps_at_helbrim
D: Apothecary Helbrim runs the lab, if running is the word for a man who has been brewing the same batch for decades. He calls it research. Haskell calls it murder. The lab has been releasing something into the stonework for years, and Helbrim has not noticed, or has not cared.
A: The Alchemy Lab. Follow the smell. Wear something you'd be happy to burn. And don't breathe through your mouth. I've known a fellow who tried.
T: It's over, then. Not a clean ending, but a final one. Thank you. I'll have the lab sealed, and I'll sleep in the mill tonight. I've missed the smell of sawdust.

@ sps_at_atiesh
D: The tower's last occupant is Archmage Atiesh, who keeps the Observatory where the waygate was aimed. Orsolo wants to know what he was aiming at, and hopes the answer dies with him. He cannot decide whether he wants to know or fears to.
A: The very top, in the Observatory. The stair is long. Don't stop on the landings. If you hear someone counting, don't answer. He's counting the steps, and every answer is one he can use.
T: He aimed it at the stars. Just the stars. And then the stars aimed back. Thank you. I'll write that down, and then I'll never write about it again.

@ sps_at_orrery
D: The Orrery is the Observatory's twin: a round room full of little worlds, all still turning. Orsolo wants someone to stand in both and put out the lamps by being there, because the lamps seem to answer only to the living. It is the last task of the tower.
A: Observatory first, then the Orrery to the east. Stand a minute each. Count your breaths. They'll go out when they're sure you're real.
T: Dark. Both of them. The waygate sleeps. I'll lock it and keep the key somewhere stupid. Thank you. I'm told some things should be closed forever. I'm a scholar. I'm not a good judge.

@ sps_sfk_bailey
D: Nobody comes out of Shadowfang Keep and talks about the forecourt. Marl has stood at the portcullis for years and has never seen what lies past it, and he wants someone to walk through the gatehouse, into the Outer Bailey, and say what is inside.
A: Through the portcullis, the gatehouse passage, and out into the bailey. Stand by the fountain. It's dry. Tell me what it's like. I haven't been past the gate in all my time here, and I've started to wonder what I'm guarding.
T: A dry fountain and a lot of bones. I'd imagined worse. You've got the nerve to go further. I've known only a handful who could walk in there and come back unchanged. I'd like to know how you do it.

@ sps_sfk_hounds
D: Packbreaker Rynn knows the worgen of the hills. The ones in the keep are worse: Arugal's own hounds, kennelled and fed on things that were once people. She wants the kennels thinned before she calls them a pack, because she has been wrong about them before.
A: The Kennels, west of the bailey. Eight keep worgs, six slavering. They bark at nothing. It isn't nothing. Don't go in alone, and don't turn your back on the dark.
T: A thinner pack. Now it's a fight I can pick. Thank you. I'll let you know when I've picked it. I'd like you there.

@ sps_sfk_rethilgore
D: Rethilgore keeps the keep's hounds, and keeps them hungry. He was a decent man in Pyrewood before Arugal took him, and he fed the village's dogs from his own table. Rynn would like him buried before the keep makes him decent again.
A: The Kennels, the big pen in the west wing. He'll have a chain. He'll have a bowl. Don't let him feed you. He used to say that was the only kind of love he knew.
T: He fed them first, even then. I can respect that, I suppose. Thank you. I'll take his chain. I think I'd like to keep it. A reminder of what the keep does to people.

@ sps_sfk_razorclaw
D: The Butcher's Yard is east of the bailey, and Razorclaw keeps it: a worgen with a cleaver where a hand ought to be, who butchers whatever Arugal sends him. Marl has heard the chopping from the gate for years, and has never learned to sleep through it.
A: East of the bailey. Follow the sound. When it stops, it's already too late. When it starts again, run.
T: The chopping's stopped. Thank the Light. I'll sleep, for the first time in a long while. Thank you. I think I'll take a walk tomorrow, in the quiet.

@ sps_sfk_guards
D: The keep's old guard never left their posts. Guardsmen wail where they stood, and the officers still give orders to no one, in voices that carry through the stone. Marl wants them relieved, one way or another, so the keep can finally hear its own silence.
A: The Great Hall and the passages around it. Eight guardsmen, five officers. Officers first, then the men. They'll salute you. Don't salute back.
T: Relieved. All of them. It's a kindness, and I'm glad it wasn't me who had to. Thank you. I'll write their names in the log. I don't know all of them. I'll learn.

@ sps_sfk_keys
D: The Haunted Servitors still carry the keep's keys on their belts, polished by centuries of obedience. Marl wants four of the rings, so the doors to the cloisters and the armoury stop being a gamble, and so the next party doesn't walk into a trap by choosing the wrong lock.
A: The Great Hall and the cloisters. Servitors carry a ring each. Take the ring. Be polite. They haven't been thanked in a very long time.
T: The right doors, and none of them haunted. Almost enough to make a man hopeful. Thank you. I'll hang them on the gatepost, and let the keep know who holds them.

@ sps_sfk_silverlaine
D: Baron Silverlaine owned Pyrewood and Shadowfang and half the pines between, and he sits on his dais in the Great Hall and receives no one. He was a cold man in life, and he has not warmed. Marl would prefer the hall empty, and the dais cold.
A: The Great Hall, at the far end. He doesn't stand. He doesn't need to. If he speaks, he'll speak as a lord to a servant. Don't answer. Don't kneel.
T: A fitting end, I suppose. He'd have called it a very modest funeral. Thank you. I'll have the hall swept. It's been waiting for a clean floor.

@ sps_sfk_moonwalkers
D: Arugal's cult is half sorcerers and half worgen who haven't yet decided. Rynn calls them moonwalkers and darksouls, and says the keep teems with them. She wants twelve of them down, six of each, before the moon is full.
A: All through the keep, wherever you hear chanting. Casters first. Don't let them finish a line. If they finish, they pull on something I can't name.
T: Fewer chants. Perhaps the moon will go unremarked tonight. Thank you. I'll count that as a victory, and I count very few.

@ sps_sfk_cloister
D: West of the Great Hall a stone cloister runs to Fenrus' crypt, and nobody has walked it in years. Marl wants it walked from start to end, by someone who'll tell him whether the crypt door is open. He has been afraid of that door for as long as he has been at the gate.
A: Out of the west door of the Great Hall, down the cloister, into the crypt. Walk, don't run. If the cloister begins to feel longer than it is, stop. It's counting.
T: Open. I'd hoped. I'd feared. Both, honestly. Thank you for telling me. I'll make sure the next party is ready.

@ sps_sfk_fenrus
D: Fenrus was Arugal's first experiment, and his least successful. He devours things in the crypt, and nothing that goes in comes out. Marl would like to be the exception, and would like you to be the first.
A: In the crypt, at the end of the cloister. Mind the sarcophagi. They breathe. Don't let him circle you. He learns quickly, and he's never forgotten a face.
T: He ate his handler, and then his handler's replacement. Thank you for being the exception. I'll mark the crypt closed on the map. I don't want to see it opened again.

@ sps_sfk_sons
D: The inner keep is stocked with Arugal's best: Sons of Arugal in half-turned shapes, Lupine Horrors too big for the doors, and Deathsworn Captains who command them. Rynn will not call them a pack. She calls them what they are, and what they are is a cult with fangs.
A: The crypt, the armoury and the upper landing. Six Sons, three Horrors, four Captains. Don't fight them in a corridor. Back into the open, and make them come to you.
T: Not a pack. A cult, with fangs. Fewer of those is a victory. Thank you. I'll carry the news myself. They'll want to know it's possible.

@ sps_sfk_arugal
D: Archmage Arugal opened the door that let the worgen into Lordaeron, and he sits at the top of the keep now with his tower of shadow, watching his work ripple outward. Marl has stood at his gate for years and would like to see the other side of it, at last.
A: The upper landing, and then the Sanctum. Don't stop. Don't answer when he speaks. He'll offer to explain. He'll offer to forgive. Nothing he says is true. Nothing he says is untrue either, and that's worse.
T: Arugal, dead. I'll lock the gate and sit for a while. Thank you. You'll never need to buy a drink at my post. I don't sell any, but if I did.
