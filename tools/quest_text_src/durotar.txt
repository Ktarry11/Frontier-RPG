# Durotar: only the two over-long Tiragarde quests, trimmed to fit the offer dialog. Their meaning is unchanged: this is the game's first "you are killing a person" beat, and the player/NPC difference shows here on purpose.

@ durotar_tiragarde_approach
D: Watchwarden Kelgrim has held this post at the edge of Tiragarde Keep longer than he likes to admit. The humans washed up here years back, Kul Tiras sailors, shipwrecked or marooned, and nobody ever got a straight answer out of the survivors before putting them down. They dug in. They built walls out of the ruin's old stones. Now they raid the coast road when they are hungry, which is often. Kelgrim does not call them monsters. He calls them "the stranded," and he says it like it costs him something. Thin them at the outer wall. Leave their captain for later.
A: Eight of them, whichever kind you find first: marines, deckhands, the ones with the long rifles on the wall. They fight like people who know they are already dead men walking, because in every way that matters to them, they are. Do not mistake desperate for weak. And do not go near the one who commands them. Not yet. We will talk about him when you are done here.
T: Eight down. Good. You did not hesitate, which tells me something about you, though I could not say what yet. Sit a moment before you go back in. There is still the matter of their captain, and that conversation deserves more than me shouting it at you between sword-swings.

@ durotar_tiragarde_captain
D: Captain Thalenvorde commands what is left of the Tiragarde garrison, and Kelgrim has been putting off this conversation since you first walked up to his post. Thalenvorde is a person. Not a boar, not a demon: a man with a rank and a crew and a life before this island that Kelgrim suspects looked ordinary once. Killing a beast asks nothing of you. Killing a person does, whether or not you asked to fall into a world where the second is suddenly on the table. He will not talk you out of it. The garrison will not stop raiding while Thalenvorde commands it. But he wants you to walk in understanding what you are about to do, instead of finding out after.
A: I will not pretend this is the same as the boars in the valley, or the demons in that cave. Thalenvorde bleeds like you do. He probably has people somewhere who would grieve him, the way people back on your blue planet would grieve you, if news like that could even reach them. None of that changes what has to happen. It only means you should know what you are carrying afterward. Go. Finish it.
T: It is done, then. I can see it on you, the particular quiet that comes after. I remember mine, if you can believe an orc admitting that. Thalenvorde will not lead raids on the coast road again, and the valley is a little safer for it. That part is true, and it matters. Keep what you took off him. Not as a trophy. As a reminder of where you stand now. You crossed a line today that does not uncross. Now you know what it weighs.
