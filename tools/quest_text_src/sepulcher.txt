# Sepulcher Keep (L13-15). Forsaken military keep above Silverpine. Dry, tired, procedural. Tension: one small beat.

@ sep_keep_tour
D: Commander Vane does not give orders to people who cannot find the latrines. Before anything else, walk the terraces: the lower court, the middle terrace and the graveyard wing. Look at everything, touch nothing that is not yours, and report back. He will ask three questions. You will be tested on the second.
A: Three stops: the court inside the gate, the middle terrace, and the Raven Hill graveyard to the west. If a soldier salutes, salute back. If a soldier does not, do not take it personally. Some of them stopped caring about rank around the time they stopped breathing.
T: You didn't fall off anything. That's a better start than most. Sit down, if you must. I'll find you work. The Keep has more of it than it has people, and the people have more of it than they have patience.

@ sep_woods_cull
D: The Keep sits above the pines, and the pines are full of worg and the leavings of the plague. Vane wants the approach to the gate kept short of anything with teeth. He doesn't mind the occasional visitor. He minds the occasional visitor being eaten on his doorstep.
A: In the woods south of the Keep, along the road you walked in on. Eight worg prowlers, six feral ghouls. Don't stray far from the road. The ghouls follow the sound of a heartbeat, and I'm told you've got one left.
T: Fewer teeth on the approach. I'll note it in the ledger. It will be the third thing I've noted this week. The first two were complaints.

@ sep_dispatch
D: Vane has written to Captain Vesk of the Rear Guard. It is three lines long. He would like it delivered by someone who will not read it, and, failing that, someone who will not remember it. Both are tall orders, and he has decided you will do.
A: North along the road to the Rear Guard. Vesk is at the gate. Put it in his hand and wait for him to read it. He'll swear. That is not your fault, and it is not an answer.
T: Three lines. Of course. It's rude, and exactly right. Thank you. Vane will pretend he doesn't care what I said. He cares very much. Take this for the road back.

@ sep_relic_shards
D: Relic Hunter Darne pays for grave-goods, and the skeletal groundskeepers of Raven Hill carry the best of them: ring-shards, buckle-bone, whatever they were buried with. She wants six, intact. Chipped ones are worth nothing. Whole ones are worth a fortune in the Undercity.
A: From the groundskeepers at Raven Hill, to the south. Don't chip them. Don't smash them in the fight either, which is harder than it sounds. A skeleton falls apart a lot more readily than it holds together.
T: These are good. Old and honest. The collectors in the Undercity will weep. Not that they can, of course. They will make the sound, which is more than most manage.

@ sep_marrow
D: Yseult carves bone into combs, buttons and, occasionally, regret. Risen husks have the best marrow, she says, and they never complain about the harvest. Her workshop smells of clove and cold stone. She is out of marrow, and out of patience for the substitutes.
A: Risen husks, wherever the field is thickest. Six dry marrow-bones. Pale ones, not brown. The brown ones have gone to rot, and I won't carve what's rotted. A comb should outlast its owner.
T: Dry and pale. I could carve a chapel out of these. I'll settle for a comb. Here, a first run. Tell no one I gave it away. I have a reputation to keep up, and it is a miserly one.

@ sep_wasp_stings
D: Windfall wasps in Malden's Orchard are full of a venom Wrenn has been trying to buy for years. The orchard won't sell it. The wasps won't either. Wrenn has run out of polite ways to ask, and has settled on the impolite one: send someone else.
A: Malden's Orchard, north of the Raven Hill road. The stings are on the abdomen. Handle with tongs. Six of them. Don't let them touch your skin, whatever you may feel about the state of your skin.
T: Six stings and no stinging. You have steadier hands than most. I'll brew these tonight. The Apothecary will bless you. The Apothecary will also send a bill, but that's another matter.

@ sep_stone_names
D: Reader Cass reads the stones of the Raven Hill wing. Most are blank, rubbed smooth by rain and by something that rubs back. She wants three clusters visited and the weeds pulled away. "Read them aloud," she says. "It helps. I don't know who it helps. But it helps."
A: The graveyard wing, west of the middle terrace. Three clusters of stones. Clear the moss. Read what's there. If nothing's there, read the nothing. That's the part that matters.
T: Three more names. I'll write them on the wall, so they stay. I used to think it was a pointless habit. I've since decided it's the only one worth keeping.

@ sep_ellowyn_sleepers
D: The tomb-keeper of the chapel yard counts sleepers each dusk. Some have stopped lying down. Groundskeepers and husks both wander from the Raven Hill plots in search of a bed that isn't theirs. Ellowyn has grown used to the knocking. She has not grown used to the apologies.
A: At Raven Hill, south of the Keep. Eight groundskeepers, six husks. Send them back where they belong. If you can, tuck them in. They like to be tucked in. They don't know why.
T: Fewer wanderers. I'll sleep better. Metaphorically. Thank you. The chapel yard is quiet tonight, and I think it will stay that way for a while. I have always liked a quiet graveyard.

@ sep_gate_crows
D: Crypt Guard Molsen is tired of the crows. They pick at the Keep's banners, steal buttons off his coat, and one of them has learned to open the gate-latch. He thinks it is a joke at his expense. He would like the joke to stop being funny.
A: The Dead Field, east of the road. The harrowers feed them. Thin both: eight crows, five harrowers. Look for the one with the notched wing. That's the one with the latch.
T: The latch-crow? Dead. Good. I owe you a drink I can't taste. Take this instead. I'll sleep at my post tonight and not have to count the buttons in the morning.

@ sep_hermit_oil
D: The Hermit has not left his lean-to since the Plague. His lamp is nearly dry. Spider-fat burns clean, he says, and he cannot hunt it himself, because spiders have opinions about hermits. He has been reading the same book for thirty years, and the final page is getting hard to see.
A: Silverpine spiders in the Skittering Dark. Five clean glands, not burst. Tell no one I asked. People ask a great many questions of a man who wants light.
T: I can read again. Not that there is much I wish to read. But the light is good, and the book is shorter than I remembered. Thank you. I'll finish it tonight.

@ sep_osk_road
D: Osk walks between the Keep and Tirisfal every autumn, ostensibly to pray. He has no idea who he is praying to. He asks that the road between be made less murderous, so that he can go on not knowing in peace.
A: The woods north and east of the Keep. Six spiders, three treants, mainly. If you meet anyone else on the road, wave. That's how you tell the living from the Forsaken: the living wave back.
T: A quieter road. I'll walk it once more before the snow. I'll pray to whoever is listening. It might be you. It might be nobody. I have decided I do not need to know.

@ sep_cracked_locket
D: Taken from a skeletal groundskeeper's rib-cage: a cracked silver locket, with a single pressed flower inside. The petals have gone brown, but the stem is still green at the heart. One of the figures wandering the lower court, a nameless one, might know it.
A: You turn the locket over. The clasp is cracked. The lower court of the Sepulcher has a figure who answers to nothing in particular. Perhaps it will answer to this.
T: ...yes. That one. Thank you. I think... I think I had a daughter. I think she picked the flower. I don't remember the colour of her hair, but I remember she was proud of it.

@ sep_wreaths
D: The Mourner weaves wreaths from what the dead leave behind. Risen farmhands carry wilted sprays in their pockets: last year's offerings, never delivered. She wants six, and she wants them before the first frost, because after the frost they stop being flowers and start being a rumour.
A: The farmhands of Olsen's Farthing, south of the Keep. Six wreaths. If one of them looks at you and seems about to ask for it back, give it to them. You can find another.
T: Even wilted, they mean something. I'll lay them where they were meant to go. It's the only thing the dead have ever asked of us: that someone should finish the errand.
