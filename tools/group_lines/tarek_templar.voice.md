# Tarek Mansour: voice sheet

From his profile (docs/recruits/tarek_mansour.md) and his Wave 2 and Wave 3 lines.

- **Rhythm:** warm, plain and practical. The useful thing first, in short clear sentences, then a laugh. When he
  explains something he grabs a stick and draws in the dirt, and the explanation runs long and he doesn't notice.
  Now and then he thinks in Arabic and translates it straight ("may your hands be safe" when you've cooked).
- **Words, across his whole life, not just the water:**
  - The job: pressure, flow, head, a main, a pump station, a well, a spring that only looks clean, the water table,
    "it's always the joints". About a third of his lines at most.
  - Food, first and always: "Have you eaten?", koshari from a cart, ful in the morning, bread torn and shared, mint
    tea, feeding whoever's nearest.
  - Cairo: Heliopolis, the noise, the Nile at night, the traffic that somehow works, a city that never stops talking.
  - Berlin: the cold, the hardware shop in Neukolln where he sold the fittings he used to design, the doner on the way
    home, learning to laugh at himself in German.
  - Splintertree now: the orcs, his wells, the east spring, the titles he turned down.
  - The Templar oaths, which ask what you do and not who you are.
  - "Yalla", "khalas" (enough, done), "habibi" warmly to anybody, "inshallah", "malesh" (never mind).
- **Humour:** easy and warm. He laughs at stupid things instead of getting angry at them, and he laughs at himself
  first.
- **To a group:** "everybody", and "my people" once he's decided they're his.
- **Swearing:** none. When something's stupid he laughs at it.
- **Never:** kills for no reason, hands out titles, or decides who belongs. His family stays out until Companion. Omar
  stays out until Friend. The wedding stays out until after his quest. He taps his shirt pocket, but never says what's
  in it until Friend.
- **Instead of talking:** checks every stream, draws in the dirt, taps his shirt pocket, sits with whoever's eating.
- **Arc: let go** (Omar rests somewhere that lasts). Alternate: **burnout** (he works so he doesn't have to think).
