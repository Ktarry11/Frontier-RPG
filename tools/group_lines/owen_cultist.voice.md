# Owen Pryce: voice sheet

From his profile (docs/recruits/owen_pryce.md) and his Wave 2 and Wave 3 lines.

- **Rhythm:** quiet, gentle and honest, with a soft Barry lilt. Not Rhodri's quick valleys patter: Owen is slower and
  lower, the voice of a man who talked to a radio at three in the morning. He says the worst thing about himself first.
  Sometimes he stops halfway, listening, then picks the sentence back up.
- **Words, across his whole life, not just the lorry:**
  - The job: the cab, the M4, laybys, the services at Membury, the tachograph, a night run, the CB and its handles,
    "over" when he's finished speaking. About a third of his lines at most.
  - Barry: the beach at Whitmore Bay in the rain, the island, chips on the front, the chapel hymns he hums (Calon Lan,
    Cwm Rhondda), the bakery smell of a Saturday.
  - Rugby on the telly in a pub, a pint of Brains, the radio phone-ins at three in the morning.
  - The dark and the voice in it. He says plainly that something talks to him, and never what it says.
  - Being useful: carrying, mending, sitting up with somebody who can't sleep.
  - "Now then", "mind", "bach" to someone he's fond of, "Duw" when he's surprised. "Tidy" only now and then.
- **Humour:** soft, self-deprecating, a bit gallows. He jokes about the voice before anybody else can.
- **To a group:** "everybody", and "my lovely lot" once he trusts them.
- **Swearing:** almost never. "Duw, Duw" at most.
- **Never:** hides the worst thing, mocks anybody lonely, or repeats what the voice says. His mother stays out until
  Companion. What the voice says stays out until Friend.
- **Instead of talking:** hums hymns under his breath, writes in his notebook, holds the snapped CB handset when it's
  quiet, stops to listen in dark places.
- **Arc: let go** (he tells the voice no, and learns to live in the quiet). Alternate: **slide** (he keeps listening).
