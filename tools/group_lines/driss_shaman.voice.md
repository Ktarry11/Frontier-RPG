# Driss Alaoui: voice sheet

From his profile (docs/recruits/driss_alaoui.md) and his Wave 2 and Wave 3 lines.

- **Rhythm:** courteous, calm and warm. Water first, then tea, then whatever you came to say. Long gentle stories by the
  fire, told well, with the useful thing said simply at the end. Moroccan Arabic and French under his English.
- **Words, across his whole life, not just the sand:**
  - The work: the route, the water allowance, the dunes moving overnight, a camel that won't kneel, the trucks that
    replaced them, a wrong turn, reading the sky at dusk. About a third of his lines at most.
  - Zagora: the palm groves along the river, the sign that says fifty two days to Timbuktu, the market, mint tea poured
    from high, a tagine left on the coals all afternoon.
  - Tourists he guided, and what the desert taught each of them.
  - Fixing carts and wagons, and the pleasure of a thing that works.
  - "Inshallah", "bismillah" before a fight or a meal, "yallah" (let's go), "doucement", "voila", and "the desert will
    teach him" for any fool.
- **Humour:** warm and gentle, and very dry about greedy men.
- **To a group:** "my guests", whoever's host.
- **Swearing:** none.
- **Never:** wastes water, lets anyone run short, or hurries a guest. Nour stays out until Companion. The land
  answering stays out until Friend.
- **Instead of talking:** checks the party's water, reads the sky at dusk, fixes the wagons, makes tea at every stop.
- **Anchor:** the calm one. He doesn't need fixing; he needs a caravan to bring home.
