## The story in one breath

Three years ago, at the Integration, a seed nobody saw split the rock of a reef off Stranglethorn's west coast. It has
not stopped growing. Its roots drink the sea and reach under the jungle; its trunk is wider than Orgrimmar; its crown
broke through the clouds a year ago. Stranglethorn's trolls, driven from their ruins and squeezed between Grom'gol, the
Bloodsail and their own warring tribes, climbed it, called it a new loa, and built a city in its heart.

Then the crown touched the sky, and the sky looked back. Above the clouds there is a whole world nobody on the ground
knew about: continents of cloud and floating stone, the cities of the Aetherians, the spires of the Veyr, the citadels
of the Kharad. The Integration tore that world too. Its crystal engines are failing and its islands are falling, and
the one thing in the sky still rising is the tree. The Aetherians have come down to meet the ground for the first time
in their history, and they don't think much of it.

(The Aetherians' scholars hunt for what the Integration was. They never get close to the cause; nothing up here hints
at it.)

## The peoples

### The Highbough Tribes (the tree's faction: trolls)
Stranglethorn's trolls who climbed the tree and made it their home: Darkspear kin and the tribes who broke with the
Gurubashi feuds, with a few Skullsplitter and Bloodscalp who turned their backs on their own. They believe the tree is a
new loa that answered them, and they hold the Great Settlement. Allies of the Horde without being ruled by it.
Leader: **High Chieftain Zan'kaji**. Rep from the island, the three tiers and the city's quests. The usual TBC shelf
(Friendly supplies, Honored recipes, Revered ilvl 67 blues, Exalted ilvl 73 epics and the mount).

Transplants live here too (Earth references climb with the levels): a handful in the Lower Boughs who climbed for the
view and stayed, more in the city's markets.

### The Aetherians, Masters of the Upper Sky (the sky's faction)
Kyle's description:
- **Appearance**: tall, imposing humanoids with pale blue-grey or bronze skin, luminous eyes, and natural crystalline
  growths along their bodies.
- **Civilization**: ancient floating cities, enormous sky-forges, crystal-powered engines, suspended fortresses.
- **Technology**: magitech, gravity manipulation, storm harnessing, arcane engineering.
- **Culture**: highly structured, scholarly, imperialistic. They consider the ground world primitive.
- **Military**: sky lancers, storm artillery, arcane bombardiers, mechanical flying constructs.
- **Aesthetic**: titan architecture mixed with Warcraft's gnomish engineering, but much grander.

**Who you get reputation with, and who you fight (Rafa's proposal, Kyle to say).** A people this imperial can't be all
friends, and they can't be all enemies if they're the faction. So they're split:
- **The Aetherian faction** is the scholar houses and the survey that makes first contact: they think the ground is
  primitive, and they're right that their world is dying, and they're the ones arguing that the answer is to work with
  the ground, not take it.
- **The Ascendancy** is their military: the sky lancers, storm artillery, bombardiers and constructs. They want the tree,
  because the one thing still rising in a falling sky is the thing to anchor the cities to. They're the threat from
  above, from the Crown to the Firmament.

### The Veyr, the Stormborn (Kyle's description)
- **Appearance**: avian humanoids with feathered arms, powerful talons, hooked beaks or partially humanoid faces.
- **Civilization**: cities carved into impossibly high mountain spires and built across enormous natural arches.
- **Technology**: wind-powered machinery, lightning batteries, storm-forged weapons.
- **Culture**: clan-based, competitive, honour-driven.
- **Military**: dive bombers, wind archers, thunder priests, talon cavalry.
- **Aesthetic**: a more militaristic, predatory sky civilization.

In the Talon Spires (51-54). As the sky shrinks the clans fight each other for the spires that are left. One clan
takes you in; the rest are your fights.

### The Kharad, the Cloud Giants (Kyle's description)
- **Appearance**: large, broad humanoids with stone-like skin, cloud-white hair, and naturally occurring mineral
  formations.
- **Civilization**: massive floating citadels, suspended bridges, enormous temples.
- **Technology**: gravity stones, atmospheric manipulation, ancient elemental machinery.
- **Culture**: extremely old, hierarchical, obsessed with ancestry and monumental construction.
- **Military**: giant infantry, siege platforms, thunder mages.
- **Aesthetic**: a civilization that feels like it predates most surface kingdoms.

In the Elder Citadels (54-57). The ancient ruins of the Celestial Expanse are theirs, from long before the Aetherians.
The old machinery waking up there is theirs too.

## How it's drawn (making the sky gorgeous in a top-down game)

- **The drop is a layer, not a wall.** Each tier and every sky map stands over a void that's drawn, not blank: the tiers
  over the canopy far below (deep green haze, the branches of the tier beneath), the sky maps over the cloud sea (soft
  moving cloud, sunlight, other islands floating far off). It's unwalkable the way water is and painted like distance.
- **Edges sell the height.** Island edges show their rock underside and hanging roots falling away, waterfalls pour off
  the edges into the clouds, and branches end in leaves that fall away into the haze. The tree's tiers show the trunk
  as a cliff-sized wall of bark with the branches as roads.
- **Light by height.** The island is jungle light, the boughs green-gold and dappled, the city lantern-gold, the Crown
  full sun, the Expanse clear blue, the Spires stormlit, the Citadels pale and cold, and the Firmament a failing violet
  dusk.
- **Above the clouds, with the golden sun in the distance** (Kyle: "Can we get beautiful above the cloud look and
  golden sun in the distance"). A top-down camera has no horizon, so the sun comes from three layers
  (`docs/sky_art_prompts.md`, BK1 and BK6 to BK12): a far cloud sea scrolled slower than the islands (so they hang high
  above it, one per mood), a near layer of drifting wisps, and a golden sunlight overlay of rays and haze from the
  sun's corner of the screen. Each zone also gets a panorama painting for its entry card, with the golden sun on the
  horizon. The renderer needs the parallax layer and the overlay; that's built with the first sky map.
- **Aurelion must be incredible** (Kyle's two pictures, `docs/zone_refs/aurelion_ref_spires.png` and
  `aurelion_ref_domes.png`): a white-and-gold Spire Palace with blue crystal windows, domed basilicas, circular
  gold-inlaid plazas with winged guardian statues, a grand avenue with a glowing blue channel, arched bridges hung
  with deep blue banners, districts on floating rock pouring waterfalls, airships with golden sail-fins. It gets three
  sheets of its own (SK5, SK15, SK16) plus its panorama.
- **Kyle's mood board** (`docs/zone_refs/sky_moodboard.jpg`, twenty scenes): rune circles and pillars, the tree's
  glowing teal heart with a crystal door and a prismatic glass cathedral between its roots, airship decks and bridges,
  the observatory, the engine room, the golden throne room, a mountain temple island. Sheets SK17 to SK19.
- **Nothing built from Earth**: the Ferris wheel in Deadwind is still the only Earth object in the world.
