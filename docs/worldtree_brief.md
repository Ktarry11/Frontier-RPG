## The story in one breath

Three years ago, at the Integration, a seed nobody saw split the rock of a reef off Stranglethorn's west coast. It has
not stopped growing. Its roots drink the sea and reach under the jungle; its trunk is wider than Orgrimmar; its crown
broke through the clouds a year ago. Stranglethorn's trolls, driven from their ruins and squeezed between Grom'gol, the
Bloodsail and their own warring tribes, climbed it, called it a new loa, and built a city in its heart.

Then the crown touched the sky, and the sky looked back. Above the clouds there is a whole world nobody on the ground
knew about: continents of cloud and floating stone, the cities of the Aetherians, the spires of the Veyr, the citadels
of the Kharad. The Integration tore that world too. Its crystal engines are failing and its islands are falling, and
the one thing in the sky still rising is the tree. The Aetherians have come down to meet the ground for the first time
in their history, and they don't think much of it.

(The Aetherians' scholars hunt for what the Integration was. They never get close to the cause; nothing up here hints
at it.)

## The road: 42 to 60, the tree and three sky continents

The tree is the road up. The sky is three continents, each with its capital, as far apart as Kalimdor and the Eastern
Kingdoms (Kyle, 20 Nov 2026: "The sky gets 3. We can think of the distance between them will be similar to how kalimdor
and EK is"). You don't walk or skiff between them: the long airship routes cross the open sky the way the zeppelins
cross the sea, and each continent has room to grow more zones later.

The levelling road crosses the sky and comes back: the Aetherian continent to 51, the Veyr continent to 54, the Kharad
continent to 57, then home to the Aetherian continent's dying heart for 57 to 60, the way a ground character crosses
between Kalimdor and the Eastern Kingdoms.

**Doors for later (like Moonglade's):** a dungeon in the Middle Boughs' heartwood (what's eating the tree), a dungeon in
an Aetherian sky-forge in the Firmament (55-60), and a raid door in the Firmament. Built later, each its own call.

**Into the tree (Kyle, 19 Nov 2026: "We likely will need a 'Dungeon' and some 'caves' where these are just going deeper
into the world tree a bit").** The tiers get caves that go into the wood itself: hollows in the trunk, tunnels the
borers have eaten, sap chambers. One cave a tier: the Lower Boughs' goes into the trunk off the ledge, and the Middle and
Upper Boughs each get theirs. The dungeon is the Middle Boughs' heartwood, deeper into the tree than any cave.

**Towns.** Kyle, 19 Nov 2026: "The sky continents will get 3 capital cities. Great settlement will be large but not
capital city large." So the sky has three capitals, one for each people: Aurelion (the Aetherians), a Veyr city in the
Talon Spires and a Kharad city in the Elder Citadels, each on its own continent with a long-airship dock, an inn, a flight path, a bank, an auction house, mail and
trainers. The Great Settlement is the tree's town, big and full, with the same services, but sized below a capital. Rootwater Isle, the Lower Boughs and the Crown get small groups of quest
givers, not towns. One flight path a zone at most.

## The peoples

### The Highbough Tribes (the tree's faction: trolls)
Stranglethorn's trolls who climbed the tree and made it their home: Darkspear kin and the tribes who broke with the
Gurubashi feuds, with a few Skullsplitter and Bloodscalp who turned their backs on their own. They believe the tree is a
new loa that answered them, and they hold the Great Settlement. Allies of the Horde without being ruled by it.
Leader: **High Chieftain Zan'kaji**. Rep from the island, the three tiers and the city's quests. The usual TBC shelf
(Friendly supplies, Honored recipes, Revered ilvl 67 blues, Exalted ilvl 73 epics and the mount).

Transplants live here too (Earth references climb with the levels): a handful in the Lower Boughs who climbed for the
view and stayed, more in the city's markets.

### The Aetherians, Masters of the Upper Sky (the sky's faction)
Kyle's description:
- **Appearance**: tall, imposing humanoids with pale blue-grey or bronze skin, luminous eyes, and natural crystalline
  growths along their bodies.
- **Civilization**: ancient floating cities, enormous sky-forges, crystal-powered engines, suspended fortresses.
- **Technology**: magitech, gravity manipulation, storm harnessing, arcane engineering.
- **Culture**: highly structured, scholarly, imperialistic. They consider the ground world primitive.
- **Military**: sky lancers, storm artillery, arcane bombardiers, mechanical flying constructs.
- **Aesthetic**: titan architecture mixed with Warcraft's gnomish engineering, but much grander.

**Who you get reputation with, and who you fight (Rafa's proposal, Kyle to say).** A people this imperial can't be all
friends, and they can't be all enemies if they're the faction. So they're split:
- **The Aetherian faction** is the scholar houses and the survey that makes first contact: they think the ground is
  primitive, and they're right that their world is dying, and they're the ones arguing that the answer is to work with
  the ground, not take it.
- **The Ascendancy** is their military: the sky lancers, storm artillery, bombardiers and constructs. They want the tree,
  because the one thing still rising in a falling sky is the thing to anchor the cities to. They're the threat from
  above, from the Crown to the Firmament.

### The Veyr, the Stormborn (Kyle's description)
- **Appearance**: avian humanoids with feathered arms, powerful talons, hooked beaks or partially humanoid faces.
- **Civilization**: cities carved into impossibly high mountain spires and built across enormous natural arches.
- **Technology**: wind-powered machinery, lightning batteries, storm-forged weapons.
- **Culture**: clan-based, competitive, honour-driven.
- **Military**: dive bombers, wind archers, thunder priests, talon cavalry.
- **Aesthetic**: a more militaristic, predatory sky civilization.

In the Talon Spires (51-54). As the sky shrinks the clans fight each other for the spires that are left. One clan
takes you in; the rest are your fights.

### The Kharad, the Cloud Giants (Kyle's description)
- **Appearance**: large, broad humanoids with stone-like skin, cloud-white hair, and naturally occurring mineral
  formations.
- **Civilization**: massive floating citadels, suspended bridges, enormous temples.
- **Technology**: gravity stones, atmospheric manipulation, ancient elemental machinery.
- **Culture**: extremely old, hierarchical, obsessed with ancestry and monumental construction.
- **Military**: giant infantry, siege platforms, thunder mages.
- **Aesthetic**: a civilization that feels like it predates most surface kingdoms.

In the Elder Citadels (54-57). The ancient ruins of the Celestial Expanse are theirs, from long before the Aetherians.
The old machinery waking up there is theirs too.

## The ascent: from Azeroth to the sky (Kyle, 19 Nov 2026: "Do NOT skip out on design of going from azeroth to the sky")

The climb is the zone's spine: you should feel every step of height between the reef and the sun. Five things change
together as you go up, tier by tier, so no step is a jump cut.

| Where | Levels | Under your feet | What you see below the edge | The light | What lives here | The falling-sky thread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rootwater Isle | 42-44 | sand, lagoon, root bark | the sea | jungle daylight | sap-drunk beasts, Bloodsail, Bloodscalp | a bronze wing in the sand; nobody knows what made it |
| The Lower Boughs | 44-45 | branches as wide as roads | the jungle canopy far below (W1 row 4) | green-gold, dappled | apes, spiders, pythons, Bloodscalp climbers | more pieces, caught in the branches; the trolls call them sky-bones |
| The Great Settlement | city | heartwood, plank and stone | the lower tiers through the lanterns | lantern gold, waterfalls | the Highbough Tribes | the city's scholars buy every piece; a broken construct in the market |
| The Middle Boughs | 45-47 | the heartwood, hollows, old troll stone | mist below the branches; the canopy gone | hazy, cooler | the rot, harpies in the hollows | a crashed scout construct, still twitching |
| The Upper Boughs | 47-48 | pale wind-scoured bark | the cloud tops, close (W1 row 6) | white, windy, the first gold from above | wyverns, harpies, gust elementals, storm wisps | the Fallen Skiff across two branches: a ship, made by someone |
| **The Cloudbreak** | 48 | a rope ladder and the last branches | nothing: inside the cloud | grey-white, then blinding | none: a climb, not a fight | the ladder ends in the light |
| The Sunward Crown | 48-49 | gold-lit branches and cloud | **the cloud sea** (BK1, scrolling) | **the golden sun** (BK6) for the first time | sky beasts, storm elementals; the Aetherian survey ship | first contact: they've been watching the tree grow |

**The Cloudbreak** is its own short map: the top of the Upper Boughs, where the branches climb into the cloud layer.
One way up, a rope ladder and branches in thick white fog that thins as you climb, no monsters, no quests to do in it
(a few voices of trolls who climbed before you, whatever they left on the way). At its top you step out into sunlight,
the zone's panorama card (BK7, the troll lookout over the cloud sea) shows once, and you're on the Crown. It's the one
map in the game built only to be walked through, and that's the point: the moment the ground ends.

**After the Crown, travel is by air.** You can't walk off the tree: the Aetherian continent's islands are reached by the
Aetherian survey skiff, offered once first contact is made, then by sky-skiffs between the islands (each sky zone's
flight path). The other two continents are a long way off, as far as Kalimdor is from the Eastern Kingdoms: the long
airships at the three capitals' docks make those crossings, like the zeppelins. Going home is the Great Settlement's bat roost or the skiffs back to the
Crown: the climb itself only ever goes up once.

**What the renderer needs** (built with the tree's maps, not before): the drop layer painted per tier (the W1 drop
tiles, then BK1's cloud sea scrolling slower than the ground for parallax), a light overlay that strengthens with height
(none on the island, BK6's sun faint at the Upper Boughs, full on the Crown), and the panorama card on first entry.

**Rules it keeps**: no map changes with quests (every run sees the same world); nothing built from Earth; the
Aetherians hunt for what the Integration was and never get close to its cause.

## How it's drawn (making the sky gorgeous in a top-down game)

- **The drop is a layer, not a wall.** Each tier and every sky map stands over a void that's drawn, not blank: the tiers
  over the canopy far below (deep green haze, the branches of the tier beneath), the sky maps over the cloud sea (soft
  moving cloud, sunlight, other islands floating far off). It's unwalkable the way water is and painted like distance.
- **Edges sell the height.** Island edges show their rock underside and hanging roots falling away, waterfalls pour off
  the edges into the clouds, and branches end in leaves that fall away into the haze. The tree's tiers show the trunk
  as a cliff-sized wall of bark with the branches as roads.
- **Light by height.** The island is jungle light, the boughs green-gold and dappled, the city lantern-gold, the Crown
  full sun, the Expanse clear blue, the Spires stormlit, the Citadels pale and cold, and the Firmament a failing violet
  dusk.
- **Above the clouds, with the golden sun in the distance** (Kyle: "Can we get beautiful above the cloud look and
  golden sun in the distance"). A top-down camera has no horizon, so the sun comes from three layers
  (`docs/sky_art_prompts.md`, BK1 and BK6 to BK12): a far cloud sea scrolled slower than the islands (so they hang high
  above it, one per mood), a near layer of drifting wisps, and a golden sunlight overlay of rays and haze from the
  sun's corner of the screen. Each zone also gets a panorama painting for its entry card, with the golden sun on the
  horizon. The renderer needs the parallax layer and the overlay; that's built with the first sky map.
- **Aurelion must be incredible** (Kyle's two pictures, `docs/zone_refs/aurelion_ref_spires.png` and
  `aurelion_ref_domes.png`): a white-and-gold Spire Palace with blue crystal windows, domed basilicas, circular
  gold-inlaid plazas with winged guardian statues, a grand avenue with a glowing blue channel, arched bridges hung
  with deep blue banners, districts on floating rock pouring waterfalls, airships with golden sail-fins. It gets three
  sheets of its own (SK5, SK15, SK16) plus its panorama.
- **Kyle's mood board** (`docs/zone_refs/sky_moodboard.jpg`, twenty scenes): rune circles and pillars, the tree's
  glowing teal heart with a crystal door and a prismatic glass cathedral between its roots, airship decks and bridges,
  the observatory, the engine room, the golden throne room, a mountain temple island. Sheets SK17 to SK19.
- **Nothing built from Earth**: the Ferris wheel in Deadwind is still the only Earth object in the world.

## Every map is a complete zone (Kyle, 19 Nov 2026: "Dont forget we need complete zones, so include secrets, gatherables etc.")

No map is done until it has all of this, checked by the QA tools rather than assumed:
- **Gathering**: herbs, ore veins (with their gems and crystals) and wood at the zone's level band, from the existing
  ladders weighted for the zone, never a high-tier material before 60 (pure-60 rule); none in towns.
- **Fishing**: pools big and easy to see, fish that feed cooking and alchemy; fish tracking works.
- **Chests**: at the zone's level, a low chance to respawn in the open world.
- **Creatures**: subzone tables at 90% their own mobs, one rare per subzone that has one (rares only in subzones), each
  rare with its own drops; mob-dropped crafting materials that say where they drop; vendor trash and coin by type.
- **Secrets**: hidden things worth finding, for exploring off the path. Placed blind and never written down where, and
  never told to Kyle (counts only, `world.hidden_zones.registry()` checked before any place is named).
- **Quests**: about 30 a zone, ~70% from hubs, varied (not just kill and fetch), givers not standing at their targets,
  chains out to other zones, monster-drop starters and the odd mail quest, Earth references climbing with the levels.
- **Towns** (the two cities): inn, flight path, bank, auction house, mail together; one class trainer and paired
  profession trainers where their trades are; level-right shops that fit who's selling; every NPC with a purpose.
- **Recipes**: found as well as trained (drops, rares, the factions' shelves, a hidden vendor or two).
- **Doors and portals**: real entrance art in front of every way in or out, nothing right in front of an arrival.
- **Factions**: rep from the zone's quests and kills, the shelf on its quartermaster, the Exalted mount.
- **Checked**: the walk both ways through every link, the levelling run with the zone in, the rare census, prop and
  NPC clearance, encounter tables, gathering and fishing placement, and a look at every dressed map.
