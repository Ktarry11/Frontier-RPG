What this settles, or nearly settles:
- **Research shape (question 3): progress over several copies.** The first copy is the one you keep. "A couple more"
  are destroyed to research it "properly", so roughly two or three duplicates to learn the item's upgrade.
- **Then materials** pay for each upgrade, on top of the research.
- **The upgrade is a choice, not a fixed +%.** At each upgrade the crafter picks what the new budget goes into:
  - **boost a stat the item already has** (more agility on an Agi/Stam/Crit chest);
  - **or add a stat it doesn't have** (put hit rating on that chest).
- **Size: about 2 to 3 item levels' worth of stats per upgrade.** That replaces the old "+10%" starting number with a
  budget measured the way the rest of the game measures gear.

Still to hammer out, given the above:
1. **How many upgrades an item can take**, and whether each one is the same size (say three steps of +2 to +3 ilvl).
2. **Which stats can be added.** Anything on the item's stat template (an agility leather chest can take hit or
   crit, not intellect)? Or anything at a higher price? Is there a cap on how many different stats one item carries?
3. **Does an added stat cost more than boosting an existing one?** Adding hit is worth more than adding agility, so
   the budget weights from the item curve should decide the amounts.
4. **Can it be undone or re-rolled** (pay again to move the points elsewhere)?
5. **Does research teach the whole family** (every leather chest from one raid tier) or one item?
6. **Whether this replaces the upgrade kits on found gear** (today +10%, then +20%) or sits alongside them. Crafted
   gear keeps the kits either way.

## What it would be

**Any gear you didn't craft** (raid drops, dungeon boss drops, quest rewards, world drops, rare drops) can be
**reworked by the trade that matches it**:
- leatherworking for leather and mail;
- tailoring for cloth;
- blacksmithing for plate and metal weapons;
- woodworking for staves and bows;
- jewelcrafting for rings and necklaces;
- engineering for its own kind of trinket.

You need:
1. **the upgrade plan for that item, learned by RESEARCH** (below);
2. **the trade at the right skill** (the item's level sets the skill it asks for);
3. **a bill that scales with the item:** expensive for raid gear, built around the end-game-only materials (the 16 to
   18 held tiers in each line, which exist for exactly this); cheaper for levelling gear, from the materials of its
   own level band.

The result is the same item, a little better. It's how crafting meets everything else in the game: the world gives
the item, the crafter makes it theirs.

## Research: duplicates are the key

Boss gear is farmable, so duplicates pile up. Instead of vendoring a second copy, a crafter of the matching trade
**researches it: the duplicate is destroyed, and the crafter makes progress toward that item's upgrade plan.** Once
the plan is known, the crafter can upgrade the copy they're wearing, and any later copy.

Why it's good:
- Farming a boss you've already "finished" still pays, because every duplicate is research.
- The upgrade is earned twice over (the world drops the item, the crafter spends copies to learn it), so it can't be
  bought or rushed.
- It gives duplicates a home that isn't the vendor, which also keeps them out of the auction house economy.
- Since it covers levelling gear too, crafters have something to do with spare greens and blues all the way up, not
  just at the end.

## Rules already on the books that it has to respect
- **It can never lift an item past epic** ("A kit can't upgrade past epic! Hard no."). An epic stays epic; this raises
  its numbers, not its rarity.
- **Upgrades never rename an item.** The improved state shows on the item card, the way kit upgrades already do.
- **Post-game materials only in pure 60 places.** The end-game bill's materials only come from pure 60 zones and
  raids.
- **One consistent stat formula.** The upgrade has to go through the same budget maths as everything else, so an
  upgraded item still sits on the curve that leaves room for the next tier.
- **Crafted gear is excluded** (it already has its own kit upgrades).
