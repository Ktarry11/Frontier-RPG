# Faction pages: before and after (21 Nov 2026)

Eight pages rewritten to the Liquidators' bar: plain-spoken, specific, contractions, no knowing last line, no dashes. Same facts, nothing about what happened changes. The other eleven are kept as they were, and the Unnamed's is never touched.

## Thunder Bluff

**Tagline, before:** A people still answering to the name of a city that is no longer standing.

**Tagline, after:** The tauren of a city that fell when the world changed, still carrying its name.

**Before**

> Thunder Bluff stood on four rock spires above Mulgore and had never been taken by anyone. When the world changed, nothing climbed those spires. The ground beneath them went, and the city went with it.
>
> What Mulgore is now has no good name. The grass still grows. The sky is still up there. Everything else about that plain is wrong, and the things walking it kill war parties without breaking stride. Four times the tauren have gone back for the spires. They will talk about three of those.
>
> Cairne Bloodhoof was in the city when it fell. Nobody saw him after. Nothing has been found since - no body, no sign, nothing to bury - so the chieftain's seat stands empty. Elder Longtread speaks for the tauren and has turned down the title every time it has been put to him, because taking it would settle a question that is not settled.
>
> They are guests now. Camp Taurajo, the warbands in the southern Barrens, and more of them in other people's towns every season. Every nation that took them in did it without conditions and without being asked twice, and the tauren have been thanking them for it ever since, which carries its own weight.
>
> And they are still called Thunder Bluff. There is no Thunder Bluff. They have had this pointed out, agree politely, and go on using the name.
>

**After**

> Thunder Bluff stood on four rock spires above Mulgore, and no army ever took it. When the world changed, the ground under the spires gave way, and the city went down with it.
>
> Mulgore's still there, after a fashion. The grass grows and the sky's where it should be, but the plain itself is wrong now, and the things that walk it kill war parties without slowing down. The tauren have gone back for the spires four times. Three of those war parties came home.
>
> Cairne Bloodhoof was in the city when it fell, and nothing of him has been found. There's no body to burn and nothing to bury, so the chieftain's seat stands empty. Elder Longtread speaks for the tauren, and he's turned the title down every time it's been offered, because taking it would mean Cairne is dead, and nobody's proved that.
>
> They live as guests now: at Camp Taurajo, with the warbands in the southern Barrens, and in other people's towns, more of them every season. Every nation that took them in asked nothing for it. The tauren pay it back in work, on every wall and every road they're given.
>
> They still call themselves Thunder Bluff. It's the name of a people now, not of a place, and it'll stay that way until they stand on the spires again.
>

## Orgrimmar

**Before**

> The Horde came ashore in Durotar with what it could carry. Orgrimmar went up out of red stone and the bones of the ships, and the city still looks like what it is.
>
> Thrall holds it. He was born in chains and got his people out of them, and it left him careful in a way that sits oddly on a warchief - he would sooner keep what the Horde has than reach for more. There are voices in the city that disagree, and they are not quiet.
>
> The city's regard is a ledger kept by quartermasters, not by singers. Who held the Barrens road. Who went down into the Chasm when the Burning Blade came up out of it. Who was on the wall when it mattered.
>
> When the world changed, Orgrimmar opened its gates to the people it threw up, faster than anywhere else on the continent and with less argument. Thrall has never explained that in public. Those who were in the camps with him do not need him to.
>

**After**

> The Horde came ashore in Durotar with what it could carry, and Orgrimmar went up out of red stone and the timbers of the ships that brought it. The city still looks like what it is: built fast, by people who couldn't afford to be choosy.
>
> Thrall holds it. He was born in chains and got his people out of them, and it left him careful. He'd rather keep what the Horde has than reach for more, and there are voices in the city who say so, loudly and often.
>
> The city keeps its regard in quartermasters' ledgers: who held the Barrens road, who went down into the Chasm when the Burning Blade came up out of it, who was on the wall when it mattered.
>
> When the world changed, Orgrimmar opened its gates to the people it threw up faster than anywhere else on the continent, and with less argument. Thrall's never said why in public. The ones who were in the internment camps with him have never needed him to.
>

## The Darkspear Tribe

**Before**

> The Darkspear were down to their last island when the Horde found them. It was a bargain, struck between two peoples who had nothing, and the trolls have kept their half of it with a thoroughness that embarrasses larger nations.
>
> Then Zalazane took the isles. He went through the tribe with hex and voodoo until their own kin came out of the surf at them, and the Darkspear pulled back to the mainland and began again.
>
> They do not say the isles are lost. They say the ones still out on the water are not back yet. That is a different thing, and they will correct you on it.
>
> The tribe keeps its own spiritwalkers, its own trainers, its own account of who stood with them when it was bad. That account is older than the Horde's and it is not the same list.
>

**After**

> The Darkspear were down to their last island when the Horde found them. It was a bargain between two peoples with nothing, and the trolls have kept their half of it to the letter ever since.
>
> Then Zalazane took the isles. He went through the tribe with hex and voodoo until their own kin came out of the surf at them, and the Darkspear pulled back to the mainland and started again.
>
> They don't say the isles are lost. They say the ones still out on the water aren't back yet, and they'll correct you if you say it the other way.
>
> The tribe keeps its own spiritwalkers, its own trainers and its own account of who stood with it when things were bad. That account is older than the Horde's, and it isn't the same list.
>

## The Forsaken

**Before**

> The Scourge raised Lordaeron's dead and set them walking. Then the hold on them slipped, and what straightened up in the ruins remembered everything - names, houses, the faces of their families, and exactly what had been done to them.
>
> They took the undercroft beneath the palace and made a country of it. The throne room overhead still has the old king's blood dried into the floor. Nobody down there calls that tragic. They call it theirs.
>
> The Apothecarium sits at the heart of the city and worries everyone, allies included. They are the only people on the continent studying the plague that made them. Ask them why and you will be told: a cure. Read the ledgers and you may arrive somewhere else.
>
> What they will not forgive is the Scarlet Crusade, who kill them on sight for the crime of having been murdered, and the cults still working for the thing that did it. Standing with the Forsaken is mostly a tally of how many of those two you have put back in the ground.
>
> They are tired, blackly funny, and very hard to impress. They have been dead a while. Little is new.
>

**After**

> The Scourge raised Lordaeron's dead and set them walking. Then its hold on them slipped, and what straightened up in the ruins remembered everything: names, houses, the faces of their families, and exactly what had been done to them.
>
> They took the undercroft beneath the palace and made a country of it. The throne room overhead still has the old king's blood dried into the floor, and nobody down there calls that a tragedy. It's theirs now.
>

## The Steamwheedle Cartel

**Before**

> The Cartel is not a nation and has never pretended to be. It is a company with harbours, and those harbours are the only stretches of coast where a Horde ship and an Alliance ship tie up at the same pier and nothing happens.
>
> The neutrality is the product. Bring a war into Ratchet and the goblins will not take a side. They will put you on a boat, bill your faction for the disruption, and have the dock working again by the evening tide.
>
> Gazlowe runs the port, and runs it better than the margins deserve. He has turned down three offers to move the whole operation somewhere richer. No goblin will explain that, and more than a few have tried to buy the explanation out of him.
>
> Their regard is bought rather than earned and they are honest about it. Pirates on the water and bandits on the Barrens road cost the Cartel money. Anyone who brings that number down is good for business, and good for business is as near to friendship as Ratchet deals in.
>

**After**

> The Cartel isn't a nation and has never pretended to be. It's a company with harbours, and those harbours are the only stretches of coast where a Horde ship and an Alliance ship tie up at the same pier and nothing happens.
>
> The neutrality is what it sells. Bring a war into Ratchet and the goblins won't take a side. They'll put you on a boat, bill your faction for the trouble, and have the dock working again by the evening tide.
>
> Gazlowe runs the port, and runs it better than its margins deserve. He's turned down three offers to move the whole operation somewhere richer, and nobody in Ratchet knows why.

> Their regard is bought rather than earned, and they're honest about it. Pirates on the water and bandits on the Barrens road cost the Cartel money. Anyone who brings that number down is good for business, and in Ratchet that's as close to friendship as it gets.

## The Booty Bay Port Authority

**Before**

> There is no road into Stranglethorn. The range closed the land route and never opened it again, which left two ways in: an airship berth the Horde flew in over the top, and this cove.
>
> That made Booty Bay the only deep-water harbour still joining anything to anything, and a harbour in that position stops taking instructions. The Baron did not declare anything. He simply began deciding which hulls left on which tide, and after the third season nobody was asking Ratchet.
>
> The Steamwheedle Cartel has not formally lost the port and will say so at length. Factor Weezel still signs the manifests, still files them, and still gets a polite reply. The replies do not change what sails.
>
> What the Authority sells is the same thing the Cartel always sold - a pier where two enemies can tie up and nothing happens - except that here it is enforced rather than negotiated. The bruisers are the law, the Baron is the court, and the tariff is not up for discussion. People complain about the landing fee until the first time they need the harbour to hold.
>

## The Aetherians

**Tagline, before:** The scholar houses of Aurelion, the Masters of the Upper Sky, and the survey the House of the Patient Lens sent down to the World Tree.

**Tagline, after:** The scholar houses of Aurelion, who rule Caelora, the Aetherians' continent above the clouds, and the survey the House of the Patient Lens sent down to the World Tree.

**Before**

> Above the clouds there's a world nobody on the ground knew about, and the Aetherians have ruled the upper sky for as long as their records go back. They're tall and pale, with crystal growing along their arms and light in their eyes, and their cities float on engines of crystal older than any kingdom below.
>
> Since the world changed, their sky has been dying. The engines are failing, islands are falling, and the council of the scholar houses can't agree on why. Some houses say the World Tree is draining the sky and should be studied before anyone touches it. The Ascendancy, their army, says the tree is the only thing still rising and should simply be taken.
>
> The House of the Patient Lens sent a survey down to the tree to talk to the people living on it. It's the first time in their history the Aetherians have come down to meet the ground, and most of them still think the ground is primitive. The ones who've met it are starting to change their minds.
>

**After**

> Above the clouds there's a continent nobody on the ground knew about. The Aetherians call it Caelora, and they've lived on its floating islands for as long as their records go back. They're tall and pale, with crystal growing along their arms and light in their eyes, and Aurelion, their capital, floats on engines of crystal older than any kingdom below.
>
> Their sky has been dying for centuries, slowly, the way a fire goes out. The engines fail one by one, islands fall and the cloud thins, and lately it's all been going faster. The council of the scholar houses can't agree on why. Some houses say the World Tree should be studied before anyone touches it. The Ascendancy, their army, says the tree is the only thing in the sky still rising, and should simply be taken.
>
> The House of the Patient Lens sent a survey down to the tree to meet the people living on it. It's the first time in their history the Aetherians have come down to the ground, and most of them still think the ground is primitive. The ones who've met it are starting to change their minds.
>

## The Highbough Tribes

**Before**

> Three years ago a seed split the reef off Stranglethorn's west coast, and the tree that came out of it has never stopped growing. Its roots drink the sea, its branches carry whole villages, and its crown has gone up through the clouds.
>
> Stranglethorn's trolls, squeezed between Grom'gol, the Bloodsail and their own feuding tribes, were the first to climb it. Darkspear kin and the tribes who'd broken with the Gurubashi feuds, with a few Skullsplitter and Bloodscalp who turned their backs on their own, built a landing at its roots and a city in its heart.
>
> They call the tree a loa that answered them when nothing else would. The Highbough Tribes hold it now, allied to the Horde without answering to it, and they remember who helps them keep it.
>

**After**
