## Who he is

Vuk is a far better cook than he is a fighter, and he says so himself, with some bitterness.

He's blunt to the point of rudeness about small things and completely reliable about large ones. He'll insult your cooking, your
posture and your boots, and then stand in front of you when something comes over the hill.

He's generous. He feeds everyone. He insults them while he does it, and nobody minds.

He took to this world badly and is only now coming out of it. The coming out looks like him feeding people and insulting them.

He's grieving something he won't name. He keeps a second bowl ready at every meal and can't say who it's for.

What annoys him: bad cooking, waste, people who won't eat, and being called a cook. What he loves: beans cooked properly, a card game,
loud regulars, rakija, and Mila.

## Before

He grew up in Novi Sad, by the Danube, in his uncle's kafana. His father drank and left; his uncle Dragan raised him in the kitchen. When
Dragan died, Vuk was 29 and the kafana was his: a kitchen, a card table, and forty regulars who came for the beans and stayed because there
was nowhere else.

He married Jelena at 25. Mila was born when he was 27. They divorced when Mila was six, without much bitterness. He worked too much and
drank a bit too much, like his father, and Jelena wouldn't have it. He stopped drinking after. They had Mila on alternate weeks.

It wasn't his week when the Integration came.

Every night after closing, he and Mila used to play dominoes at the card table, on his weeks. She always wanted the double six.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in the kafana, closing up, when it happened. He landed in the Aspen country with the double-six domino from the kafana set in his
pocket.

Two years followed that he describes in three words: "I was nothing." Nobody presses him on it.

What brought him back was being handed a cooking pot in a camp and being too irritated by how badly it was being used to leave it alone.
He's cooked for whoever's nearby ever since, and he still says he isn't a cook any more.

He hasn't found Mila or Jelena. It wasn't his week, so she was with her mother, on the other side of the city.

## Now

He's in Rocky Pass, the cleft up to the summit of the Aspen country, at the front with an axe, badly, and at the fire afterwards doing
the thing he's actually good at.

He carries the double-six domino in his glove. He puts it on the table when he sits down to eat. He's never explained it, and nobody
asks.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** he won't say.
- **Hope:** that Mila and Jelena are together and safe.
- **Dream:** a game of dominoes with Mila, and letting her have the double six.
- **Fear:** that it wasn't his week, and he wasn't there.
- **Fear:** becoming his father.
- **Fear:** that the second bowl will always be empty.

## How he's changed

In Novi Sad he was a gruff kafana owner and a good part-time father, trying hard not to be his own father.

Here he lost two years to nothing and came back through cooking. He's gruffer and kinder. Part of what the player gives him is somebody
who eats everything he makes and asks for more.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** he won't say. It's Mila.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. Word goes round the Aspen country that there's a Serbian cook at the pass whose beans are the best on this world. Vuk grumbles about
   people talking.
2. A caravan of settlers coming over the pass is attacked in the cleft, and Vuk holds the line with you, axe and all, better than he's ever
   fought.
3. When it's over, a girl climbs down from the last wagon and follows the smell of the beans to his fire.

**Ending (happy):** it's Mila, twelve now, with Jelena behind her. They'd been in a camp in the lowlands and heard about the beans. Mila
sits down at his fire without asking, the way she used to sit at the card table. Vuk fills the second bowl. Neither of them says anything
for a while. Then Mila asks if he's still got the double six, and he puts it on the table, and lets her have it.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**The Double Six.** A cast-iron domino tile, double six, from the kafana set in Novi Sad. He's carried it in his glove for three years and
put it on every table he's eaten at. He gives it to you after Mila comes. He says she's got the rest of the set now, and the second bowl's
full, and you should have the piece that waited.

## Voice notes

- Gruff, blunt and funny. Contractions, insults that are compliments, and food in every sentence.
- His English is heavy and direct, with Serbian: "ajde" (come on), "bre", "živeli" when he raises a glass.
- He tells everyone their food's terrible and then feeds them.
- He swears constantly in Serbian, and never in front of children.
- **He won't talk about** Mila until Friend, or the two years at all.
- **Habits:** cooks at every stop, tastes everyone's food and criticises it, sets out a second bowl, and puts the domino on the table.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** holds the line clumsily and bravely, and cooks brilliantly at every rest. Insults everyone's cooking. Very gentle
  with children in quests.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with blunt, loud people, and has a running feud and friendship with Sebastián over who cooks better.
  Fatherly to the young, grumpily. Clashes with fussy eaters.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the week that wasn't his. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he
