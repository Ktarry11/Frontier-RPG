## Who he is

Iker is never tired. He's never met anyone he couldn't shout into running one more lap, and he's never been tired in his life.
Here the shouting turned into something real. When Iker tells a fighter they can keep going, they really can.

He's loud, upbeat and competitive about absolutely everything. He keeps score of everything, including things nobody else
thinks are a competition.

He believes in people more than they believe in themselves. That's what makes him a good coach. It also makes him exhausting.

He's warm and generous, and he loves a party. He's the first to dance and the last to leave.

Under all the noise, he's carrying ninety children he couldn't find.

What annoys him: people who give up, people who say "I can't" before they've tried, and losing. What he loves: pelota, his
mother's burnt cheesecake, a fishing boat coming home at dawn, Unai, and the moment a kid does something they didn't know they
could.

## Before

He grew up in Lekeitio, a fishing town on the Bay of Biscay. His father fished and his mother, Miren, ran a bakery famous for
its burnt cheesecake. He played pelota against the town fronton wall, which is older than the church, from the moment he could
throw.

He trained as a PE teacher and came home to teach at the town school: ninety kids, one fronton wall, and an old bicycle he rode
everywhere. His pelota team won the regional schools final twice, and he still talks about the second time.

He was with Unai, a fisherman on his father's old boat, for six years. They lived above the bakery. Unai left at four in the
morning and came back at noon, and Iker had the coffee ready.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on the school field, in the middle of a PE lesson with thirty kids, when it happened. He landed in Arathi in the rain,
alone, with his grandfather's black txapela on his head, the Basque beret he wore for every match.

He started a running club for the farmhands within a week. The first time his voice actually healed someone, he didn't notice.
He just thought they'd been lazy before.

The whole school was in session that day. Ninety kids. He doesn't know where any of them landed. He hasn't found Unai or his
mother either.

## Now

He's in Arathi West, with a whistle round his neck and a line of tired farmhands behind him. They complain about him all day,
and they've never been fitter in their lives.

He wants to build a fronton wall in Arathi and teach somebody here how to play properly.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find even one of his ninety kids.
- **Hope:** to find Unai and his mother.
- **Dream:** a pelota final on the old fronton in Lekeitio, with Unai in the crowd and his mother's cheesecake after.
- **Fear:** that the kids landed somewhere without anyone to look after them.
- **Fear:** stopping. If he stops, he'll have to think.
- **Fear:** that his cheerfulness is only noise.

## How he's changed

In Lekeitio he was a happy, loud coach with a partner, a mother and a town that loved him.

Here he's become something like a battlefield chaplain, shouting people back onto their feet. He's still loud. He's quieter at
night than he used to be. Part of what the player gives him is somebody to keep score against.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find his kids, and to build a fronton.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He asks you to help him go through every list at every hub in reach, for ninety names. He's got them all written down,
   from memory.
2. A farmer in Arathi says she saw Basque children once, on a road, two years ago, heading for Stromgarde. You follow it.
3. The trail goes cold at the ruins. Nobody at Stromgarde remembers them.

**Ending (still unknown):** there's no answer. Not one of the ninety, not Unai, not his mother. Iker stands in the ruins for a
long time, very quiet, which is the strangest thing you've ever seen him do. Then he goes back to Arathi and builds the fronton
wall against a barn, stone by stone, with the farmhands. He teaches their kids pelota. He says he's going to coach every kid he
can reach, because some of his ninety are out there being coached by somebody, and he hopes that somebody's loud. He keeps the
list of names in his pocket.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Iker's Txapela.** His grandfather's black Basque beret, worn for every pelota match he ever played and on the day he landed. He
gives it to you once the fronton's built. He says he's got a team to coach now, and the coach doesn't wear the beret, the champion
does.

## Voice notes

- Loud, fast and relentlessly encouraging. Contractions, sports shouting, and lots of "come on!".
- His English is enthusiastic, with a strong Spanish and Basque accent: "aupa" (come on), "kaixo" (hello), "venga".
- He keeps score of everything and announces it.
- He swears in Spanish and Basque when he loses, which he hates.
- **He won't talk about** the ninety kids until Friend, or Unai until Companion.
- **Habits:** blows his whistle, counts everyone's reps, challenges people to races, and goes quiet at night.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)
