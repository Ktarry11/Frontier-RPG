## Who she is

Sophia is precise. She spent fourteen years holding a sentence in one language while it finished in another, getting
the tone right as well as the words. She listens harder than anyone you'll meet.

She thinks before she speaks, and she'll tell you she's doing it. You get a better answer and she gets less shouting.

She's dry and funny once she relaxes, and she has opinions about everyone's grammar, including yours. She'll correct
you, apologise for correcting you, and then correct you again.

She doubted herself for two years here. She was better prepared for arcane work than almost anybody, because it asks for
exactly the kind of attention she's spent her life on, and it took her two years to believe it.

She hates being the centre of attention. Her whole career was being the invisible reason a room understood itself, and
she liked it that way.

What annoys her: people who talk over each other, careless words, and people who think translation is just swapping
words. What she loves: a good dictionary, strong coffee, a room that finally understands itself, and the moment a hard
sentence comes out right.

## Before

She grew up in Thessaloniki. Her father, Rui, was a Portuguese ship's engineer who came for a job at the port and
stayed for her mother, Eleni, a Greek literature teacher. Sophia grew up speaking Greek at home, Portuguese with her
father, and English and French at school.

She trained as an interpreter in Athens and Geneva and passed the European Parliament's exams at 27. She moved to
Brussels at 30. For fourteen years she sat in a booth with a headset, interpreting debates in Greek, Portuguese, English
and French, and nobody knew her name. She was proud of that.

She married a Belgian lawyer, Pieter, at 32. It ended at 37. They wanted different things, and he wanted her to talk
more at dinner. They're still friendly.

Her father was diagnosed with dementia when she was 39. Her mother looked after him. Sophia flew home every month.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was in the booth, mid-sentence, when it happened. She landed on the coast near Theramore with her Parliament
pass still on its lanyard round her neck.

Greece and Belgium came down on different sides of a mountain range. She's worked out what that means more carefully
than she's admitted to anyone. Her parents, if they came through, are a very long way away, on the other side of
country nobody crosses lightly.

She spent two quiet years being competent and assuming she wasn't. What broke it was a goblin who asked her to
interpret a contract dispute, paid her properly, and came back.

## Now

She lives in Theramore, where four languages and two factions arrive on the same dock and nobody's paid to make that
work. She makes it work, for money. She's been in the room for three negotiations that would've gone badly without her.

She's writing a grammar of this world's arcane. She's convinced magic has a syntax and every mage here is speaking it by
ear. She's forty pages in. She's shown it to two people. One laughed and the other asked for a copy.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to finish the grammar, and have one mage read it and say "of course".
- **Hope:** to see her parents again.
- **Dream:** to sit with her father and have him know her.
- **Dream:** to be in a room where everybody understands each other because of her, and nobody notices.
- **Fear:** that her parents are on the other side of the mountains and she'll never get there.
- **Fear:** that her father's forgotten her completely.
- **Fear:** being wrong in public.

## How she's changed

In Brussels she was the invisible voice in the headset, good at her work and quietly lonely.

Here she found out she's powerful, and that terrified her for two years. Now she's starting to enjoy it. She speaks up
more. She takes credit, a little. Part of what the player gives her is somebody who asks what she thinks, and waits.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to cross the mountains and find her parents.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A Greek trader brings word that there's a settlement of people from Thessaloniki on the far side of the mountains, and
   that her mother's name is on its list.
2. The crossing is long and dangerous. You go with her.
3. You reach the settlement, and her mother is there.

**Ending (sad):** her mother is alive, thin and tired, and so glad to see her she can't speak. Her father is alive too.
He's sitting in the sun outside the house, and he doesn't know who Sophia is. He's polite to her, the way he'd be polite
to any visitor. She stays a week. On the last day he calls her by her mother's name. She takes that as the best he can
give her. She comes back with you, because her work is on this side of the mountains, and she writes to her mother every
week.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Sophia's Parliament Pass.** Her European Parliament pass on its blue lanyard, the photo faded, the one that got her
into the booth every morning for fourteen years. She was wearing it when she landed. She gives it to you after the
crossing. She says she doesn't need to prove who she is any more.

## Voice notes

- Careful, exact and quiet. Contractions, but every word chosen. She pauses before answering.
- Her English is perfect, slightly formal, with a European lilt.
- She corrects people's grammar and apologises for it.
- She swears in Greek when she's frightened and Portuguese when she's annoyed.
- **She won't talk about** her parents until Companion, or her father's illness until Friend.
- **Habits:** writes everything down, mouths words in other languages while she listens, touches her lanyard when she's
  nervous, and always sits at the edge of a room.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** reads every inscription and translates it. Listens to what enemies say and tells the party.
  Thinks before acting, which is good in a puzzle and bad in an ambush. Quietly brilliant in a crisis once she stops
  doubting.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with clever, quiet people and anyone who loves words. Tires quickly of loud people.
  A natural friend for Kesten, who also likes things written down properly.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is doubting herself, and losing her father. A skill idea to look at
  then: once a fight, she reads the enemy's next spell and turns it back on them.
