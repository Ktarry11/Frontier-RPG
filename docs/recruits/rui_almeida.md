## Who he is

Rui is gentle, almost unbearably so. He apologises for everything, speaks softly, and would rather go without than inconvenience anyone.

He carries something that frightens him badly. He took it in his first year when the alternative was dying, and he's been paying for it ever since.
He's never once tried to make it sound like a good decision.

He won't say what it is. He'll tell you what it does, and that has to be enough, because the rest he can't say out loud.

He lets people assume the worst about him, because arguing would mean explaining, and explaining is the part he can't do.

He's stubborn about one thing only: getting out. He keeps asking, carefully, everyone who might know.

What annoys him: almost nothing, except people who make a show of power. What he loves: the sea at dawn, grilled sardines in summer, fado on the
radio, his mother's caldeirada, and the moment the nets come up full.

## Before

He grew up in Peniche, the son of a fisherman who drowned when Rui was fourteen. At sixteen he went to sea himself, to send money home. Twenty years of
nights out and mornings in, a hold full of fish, and a fishing co-operative that went from forty boats to nine in his working life.

He sent money home to his mother and his sisters, Inês and Sofia, every week. He thinks about the money more than he thinks about them, and he knows
that's a strange thing to be ashamed of, and he's ashamed of it anyway.

He never married. He was always at sea. He mended nets in the evenings with a wooden net needle his father carved.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at sea, hauling nets in the dark, when it happened. He landed on the Tanaris coast, alone, with his leg broken on the rocks, and the net needle
through his belt.

It was catastrophic. He was alone, he was hurt, and something offered. He said yes. Something in the dark took up residence in him and mended his leg,
and it's been there ever since. It's very effective. He heals the party with it. It frightens him every day.

He hasn't found his mother or sisters.

## Now

He's at the Lashings on the coast of Northern Tanaris, as close to the sea as he can be. In a party he's behind everyone, extremely effective and
visibly uncomfortable about it.

He sleeps nearest the fire. He's never said why, and nobody's worked out that it isn't the cold. In the dark, the thing in him is louder.

He wants out, quietly, without anybody making a fuss. He's asked three priests and two shamans, carefully and in general terms, and nobody's given him
an answer he believes. He keeps asking.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to get the thing out of him, even if it costs him.
- **Hope:** that his mother and sisters are safe, and not needing his money.
- **Dream:** a night at sea, in the dark, without being afraid.
- **Fear:** the dark.
- **Fear:** that the thing will want something one day that he can't refuse.
- **Fear:** that he cares more about the money he sent than the people he sent it to.

## How he's changed

In Peniche he was a gentle, dutiful fisherman who sent every coin home and worked himself thin.

Here he made a terrible bargain to survive, and he's become frightened, careful and quietly brave. Part of what the player gives him is someone who
doesn't assume the worst.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to get out.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A Darkspear sea-witch on the Tanaris coast tells him there's an old rite: a person can give the thing that lives in them to the sea, if they go
   out alone, at night, in a storm, in a boat they built themselves.
2. You help him build a small wooden boat, the way the old Peniche boats were built, and you stand on the shore while he rows out into the storm.
3. He's out there a long time. The boat comes back without him.

**Ending (happy):** at dawn, he walks out of the surf further down the beach, half-drowned, shaking, and laughing. He gave it to the sea, and the sea
took it, and gave him back. He's weaker now. He can't heal the way he could. He doesn't care. That night he sleeps away from the fire, in the dark, for
the first time in three years, and in the morning he tells you what it was. He says it's gone, so he can say it now.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Rui's Net Needle.** A salt-bleached wooden net needle his father carved, used to mend nets on the Peniche boats and through his belt the night he
landed. He gives it to you after the storm. He says he doesn't mend nets any more, and he'd like you to have something his father made with his hands.

## Voice notes

- Soft, gentle and apologetic. Contractions, "sorry" constantly, and long silences.
- His English is careful and quiet, with European Portuguese: "desculpa" (sorry), "obrigado", "pois", "saudade" when he's homesick.
- He won't say what it is until after the quest.
- He doesn't swear. When he's frightened he goes very quiet and moves nearer the fire.
- **He won't talk about** what it is until the quest, or the money until Friend.
- **Habits:** apologises, sleeps nearest the fire, watches the sea, and turns the net needle in his fingers.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** heals very effectively and is miserable about it. Uncomfortable in dark places and cults. Apologises when the party
  takes damage. Happiest near the sea.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with gentle, quiet people, and with Owen, who also heard something in the dark. People who assume the worst
  hurt him, and he lets them. Protective of anyone frightened.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the thing he gave to the sea. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, a
  plain, clean healing tide that washes over the whole party, his own and nothing else's.
