## Who she is

Saoirse gets in front of things. Camogie is running at people holding a stick and expecting to be run at in return, and she
has an instinct for standing in front that predates any of this.

She's loud, funny and completely without self-pity. Of all of them, she's the one most people would want beside them. She'd
find that embarrassing and change the subject.

She's honest about having had an easy time of it here, and a little ashamed of how easy. She arrived in a world that wanted
somebody to stand in front, and that's the only thing she's ever been unambiguously good at.

She's a coach before she's anything else. She sees what people could be and shouts at them until they get there.

She throws her sliotar, the hard leather ball from camogie, at people who are getting above themselves, underarm and
accurately. The party has learned to catch.

What annoys her: self-pity, people who won't try, and being thanked. What she loves: a hard match, a pint of stout, a
minibus full of shouting teenagers, and the moment a player does the thing she's been drilling for weeks.

## Before

She grew up in Kilkenny, the youngest of two, the daughter of a farmer and a nurse. She had a camogie stick in her hand at
five and played for the county from 17. At 26 her knee went in a county final, and that was the end of playing. She took up
coaching the same year, and loved it more than she'd ever expected.

For eight years she coached under-16 girls: a pitch, a knee that clicked, and a minibus full of fifteen-year-olds she drove to
matches at seven in the morning.

Her parents died within a year of each other when she was 29. Her brother Ciarán was in Australia, and they spoke twice a
year out of habit rather than warmth.

The honest version, given without drama: there was nobody whose absence reorganised her. She says the girls on the team are
the ones she thinks about.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was driving the minibus to an away match at seven in the morning, with eleven girls in the back, when it happened. She
landed alone in Silverpine with her sliotar in her jacket pocket.

She doesn't know where the girls landed. That's the part she can't put down. She's checked every list she can reach for
eleven names.

## Now

She's at the Sepulcher in Silverpine, and she goes into Shadowfang Keep wherever the line's thinnest, taking the hits with a
cheerfulness other people find unsettling.

She wants to coach again. Not fight. Coach. She's started with four of the youngest at the Sepulcher, transplant teenagers,
on the grounds that somebody has to teach them to fall properly. She runs it at seven in the morning because that's
apparently who she is.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find even one of the eleven girls.
- **Hope:** a team again.
- **Dream:** a county final, from the sideline, with her girls winning.
- **Fear:** that the girls landed somewhere bad, without anyone in front of them.
- **Fear:** that she's only good for taking hits.
- **Fear:** her knee going again at the wrong moment.

## How she's changed

In Kilkenny she was a cheerful coach whose life was other people's daughters, and who didn't let herself need anyone.

Here she's a shield, and she's good at it, and she misses coaching more than she'd ever say. Part of what the player gives her
is someone who stands in front of her, for once.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to find out what happened to the girls from the minibus, and to coach again.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A trader in Tarren Mill mentions a group of Irish teenage girls who arrived together and are living with a farming family
   in Hillsbrad. Saoirse is on the road within the hour.
2. The farm's been raided, and the girls have gone up into the hills to hide. You track them with her.
3. You find them, cornered, with four of them holding the others behind them, sticks in their hands, the way she taught them.

**Ending (happy):** it's seven of her eleven girls, older now, and still standing in front of each other. Saoirse gets in front
of them and you get them home. The other four were never with them, and nobody knows where they are. She takes the seven back
to the Sepulcher, and now she has a team. At seven the next morning she has them on the training ground, complaining, with the
four Sepulcher teenagers. She says it's the best day she's had in three years.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Saoirse's Sliotar.** A scuffed, grass-stained camogie ball from her last county season, carried in a net on her belt and
thrown at the party for three years. She gives it to you once the girls are training again. She says the team has new balls
now, and you've caught this one enough times to keep it.

- She swears happily and creatively.
- **She won't talk about** the girls until Companion, or her parents until Friend.
- **Habits:** throws her sliotar, rubs her bad knee, gets up at dawn, and stands in front.
