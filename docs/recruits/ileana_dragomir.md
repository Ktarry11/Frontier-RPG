## Who she is

Ileana is precise. Eleven years of being corrected in public by a man with a stick, and then doing it exactly right forever
after. She's scathing about anything done carelessly and has no patience at all for effort without precision.

She's the first on her feet when somebody goes down. Every time, without comment. The party works that out long before she
admits it.

She's honest about her own faults in a way almost nobody is. She was unbearable for two years here, by her own account, and
she knew she was doing it while she did it.

She's funny in a dark, cutting way that takes a while to read as affection.

She wants an ensemble. Not an audience. Three or four people who can hold a line while she holds hers. She said this once, in a
way that made it clear she meant the party, and then immediately criticised somebody's footwork so nobody could answer it.

What annoys her: sloppiness, sentimentality, people who don't practise, and being told to relax. What she loves: a perfect
phrase, black coffee, cold mornings, Bartók, and Andrei, though she'd say it once and not again.

## Before

She grew up in Sibiu, the daughter of a schoolteacher and a church organist. She had a violin under her chin at four and practised
every day of her life after that. She went to the conservatory in Cluj, and at 26 won a seat in the second violins of the
philharmonic: a section, a stand partner, and the particular hell of being very good at something that has four hundred people
better.

She married Andrei, a composition teacher at the conservatory, at 27. They decided together not to have children, and both
revisited it privately.

The year before the Integration was a bad one. Andrei had an affair, briefly, with a student. They nearly separated. They were
coming out the other side of it. She describes the bad year in detail and the coming-out in one sentence, and the one sentence is
the part she can't say twice.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was in rehearsal when it happened, in the middle of a Bartók quartet. She landed in Tirisfal without her violin. The only thing
that came through with her was the shoulder rest, still strapped to her back for the walk home.

Two years of being unbearable followed. She drove people off. She's since apologised to four of them individually and been
surprised each time that it was accepted.

She hasn't found Andrei. They'd left the last conversation unfinished.

## Now

She lives in the Undercity, where the cold suits her, and she goes down into the Plague Depths beneath it, where the dead
there are a problem that needs precision. She stands at the back, where the cold comes off her in a way the front rank has learned
to stand clear of.

The shoulder rest is strapped across her back. There's no violin. Nobody's asked her about it twice.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** an ensemble.
- **Hope:** to finish the conversation with Andrei.
- **Dream:** to play again. Anything.
- **Fear:** that she drove Andrei off for good, and then the world took the chance to finish it.
- **Fear:** that she's only good at being cold.
- **Fear:** her hands forgetting.

## How she's changed

In Cluj she was a brilliant, prickly violinist in a marriage that had nearly broken.

Here she lost the instrument and found the cold, and she was cruel for two years before she caught herself. She's still sharp.
She's started apologising. Part of what the player gives her is an ensemble.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to play again, and to find Andrei.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A Forsaken luthier in the Undercity, who made violins when he was alive, offers to make her one, if she brings him the wood and
   gut and horsehair, from places nobody sensible goes.
2. You gather them with her. She criticises everything you do, and saves your life twice.
3. The violin is finished. It's not a good violin. It's the only one.

**Ending (happy):** she plays it for the party, at an inn in Brill, badly out of practice and furious about it. A man in the
corner stands up halfway through. It's Andrei. He's been teaching music to Forsaken children in Brill, because they can't sleep
and music helps. He heard the Bartók and knew there was only one person on this world who'd play it that sharply. She finishes the
piece before she goes to him. Then she finishes the conversation, out loud, in one sentence, and this time she can say it twice.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Ileana's Shoulder Rest.** The black curved shoulder rest from her violin in Cluj, strapped across her back for three years with
no violin to put it on. She gives it to you once she's playing again. She says the new violin needs a new one, and this one
waited long enough.

## Voice notes

- Sharp, dry and exact. Contractions, short sentences, and criticism that sounds like an insult and is usually advice.
- Her English is precise and formal, with a Romanian accent and Romanian swearing.
- She talks about fights like music: tempo, entries, holding a line.
- She swears coldly in Romanian.
- **She won't talk about** Andrei until Friend, or the bad year at all, except once.
- **Habits:** corrects people's posture, taps rhythms on her staff, touches the shoulder rest, and is first to the fallen.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** criticises everyone's technique and then saves them. Precise and deadly at the back. Very cold to
  careless people. First to anyone who falls.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with precise, disciplined people, especially Haruto and Kesten. Clashes with loud, careless
  ones. Has a hidden soft spot for anyone who apologises properly.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is the unfinished conversation, and the years of being cold. A skill idea to
  look at then: once a fight, she holds the line for the whole ensemble, freezing every enemy's next action.
