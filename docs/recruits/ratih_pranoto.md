## Who she is

Ratih looks for the one wrong thing. Fifteen years of patrolling a Sumatran forest taught her to read the land for it: a snare, a path, a
silence where there should be birds.

She's quiet, direct and unsmiling. She doesn't waste words or movements. She'd rather be alone, and for most of her life she was.

She's dedicated to a degree nobody's ever paid her for. She was the only person in the province who cared, and the government funded the
uniforms and little else.

She's lonely in a way she's never minded. What she finds absurd, and hard, is that here people thank her.

She wants to belong to something properly. Not as a woman alone in the wild, but part of a line, rostered and briefed.

What annoys her: noise, poachers, people who walk without looking, and being thanked. What she loves: silence, a dawn patrol, birdsong, her
nephew's drawings, and the moment she finds the wrong thing.

## Before

She grew up in a village in Riau, at the edge of the forest, the daughter of a rubber tapper. She joined the forest service at 24 and spent
fifteen years on patrol: long walks, poachers, illegal loggers, and a rifle she rarely carried. Most of the time she was alone, and she was
the only one who cared.

On one patrol, after a flood, she found the brass gauge plate from a river marker torn off and washed into the mud. She sewed it onto her
sleeve. It's been there ever since.

Her parents died when she was in her thirties. Her sister Dewi married and moved to Medan. Ratih had promised to visit Dewi and her nephew
Arif that summer, and kept putting it off for one more patrol.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was on a dawn patrol when it happened. She landed on the Shimmering Flats, where there's nothing to hide behind and nothing grows, the
opposite of everything she knew.

She went to work. Within a month she had a patrol route of her own invention and nobody's permission. The second year was harder, for a
reason she finds absurd: out here, people thank her.

She hasn't found Dewi or Arif. She thinks about the visit she put off.

## Now

She walks a route she drew herself across the Shimmering Flats, looking for the one wrong thing: a trap, a hidden cache, a track that
shouldn't be there. It's easier to see the wrong thing on the salt, where nothing hides.

She reports every find to the tauren of Freewind Post and signs none of the reports. She wants their guard to take her on properly. She's
asked twice. The answers were polite. She's going to ask a third time, and she's decided what she'll say if the answer is the same.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to be part of a line. Rostered, briefed, belonging.
- **Hope:** that Dewi and Arif are safe.
- **Dream:** the visit she put off.
- **Fear:** that she'll always be a woman alone in the wild, and that it's her fault.
- **Fear:** missing the one wrong thing.
- **Fear:** that she chose patrols over her family.

## How she's changed

In Sumatra she was a lone, dedicated ranger who cared more than anyone and was thanked by no one.

Here she's still alone, and people thank her, and it's made her want to belong somewhere for the first time. Part of what the player gives
her is a line to stand in.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to be taken on properly by the Freewind guard.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. She finds the one wrong thing that matters: tracks on the salt leading to a smuggling ring that's been trapping creatures and selling
   them, and hiding weapons for raiders, under the Flats.
2. You follow the tracks with her, quietly, to the ring's hidden camp, and break it up.
3. She signs the report for the first time, with her full name, and takes it to Freewind herself.

**Ending (happy):** this time she doesn't need to ask. The tauren captain reads the report, and the signature, and offers her a place on the
guard: rostered, briefed, part of the line. She says yes before he finishes. She's given a patrol of three young tauren to teach. She tells
you she's going to visit Dewi and Arif the first chance she gets, wherever they are, and that she isn't putting it off for one more patrol.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Ratih's Gauge Plate.** A small brass river-gauge plate, stamped with depth marks, torn off a flood marker in Sumatra and sewn onto her sleeve
for years. She unpicks it and gives it to you once she's on the guard. She says she has a proper badge now, and this one marked how high the
water came.

## Voice notes

- Short, quiet and direct. Contractions, very few words, and no small talk.
- Her English is careful and clipped, with Indonesian: "ayo" (come on), "sudah" (enough), "hati-hati" (be careful).
- She tells people to be quiet a lot.
- She doesn't swear. When something's wrong she goes completely still.
- **She won't talk about** Dewi and Arif until Companion, or her loneliness until Friend.
- **Habits:** scans the ground constantly, walks silently, touches the plate on her sleeve, and turns away when thanked.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** spots traps, ambushes and hidden things. Wants the party quiet. Silent and deadly with the bow. Embarrassed by
  thanks after a fight.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with quiet, competent people, and with Rosa and Rhys, fellow people of the wild. Uneasy with talkers.
  Gently pleased by anyone who includes her.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is always being alone. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, she marks
  the one wrong thing in a fight, and every ally's attacks on it can't miss.
