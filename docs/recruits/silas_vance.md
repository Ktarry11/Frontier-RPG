## Who he is

Silas reads people. He reads a dock the way other people read a face: which crate's light, which captain's lying, who's
about to be robbed by the man standing next to him. He sells that at a fair price, and it still faintly embarrasses him.

He's charming and evasive. Ask what he did before and you'll get a different answer each time, all of them port work,
none of them checkable. It's a game, and also a wall.

He's never committed to anything. He flips a coin for decisions he doesn't want to make, which is most of them. He's
started to suspect that never committing isn't the same as being free. He's only got as far as suspecting it.

He has a code, and it surprised him. He came here expecting no rules and found a frontier with sharper rules than
Antwerp ever had, because out here the people who break them don't get a hearing. It took him four months to understand
that, and it reorganised him completely.

He's kinder than he lets anyone see. He notices who's about to get hurt, and more and more often he steps in.

What annoys him: bullies who pick on people who can't see it coming, liars who are bad at it, and being asked directly
how he feels. What he loves: a busy dock, a good con done to someone who deserves it, a cold beer, and his brother.

## Before

He grew up in Antwerp, near the docks. His father was an English merchant sailor who was home three months a year and
then not at all. His mother raised Silas and his brother Jens, four years younger, on a cleaner's wage.

Silas drifted through his twenties: bar work, deliveries, a bit of this and that. At 28 he got a job as a security
guard at the port, watching camera feeds and checking manifests. It was the first steady job he'd ever had.

The docks had an arrangement. Everybody took a little, and nobody took enough to become a problem. Silas never joined
in. He never reported it either.

Jens did join in. He was 25 and wanted easy money. He got in deeper than a little, and when the police finally came,
Jens went to prison for two years. Silas had seen it coming on the cameras for months and said nothing, to anyone. Jens
was released six months before the Integration. They hadn't spoken since the trial.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on a night shift, watching the camera feeds, when it happened. He landed on the docks at Ratchet with a Belgian
two-euro coin in his pocket. He's flipped it for every decision since.

He landed delighted: no record, no reputation, nothing to live down. He's embarrassed about that now in a way he
wouldn't have predicted.

There's nobody he's looking for, he says. He mentions that more often than a man who meant it would.

## Now

He lives in Ratchet, a port, the only shape of place he's ever been able to read properly. A goblin on the middle pier
pays him a retainer not to tell anyone else what he notices. Silas considers it the most legitimate income of his life.

He wants a berth. Not to own anything, he's not built for it. A cut, a pier, a reason to be in the same place next
year.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a berth and a reason to stay.
- **Hope:** that Jens is all right.
- **Dream:** to say sorry to Jens for the months he watched and said nothing.
- **Fear:** that he's the same as the men on the docks who took a little, just quieter about it.
- **Fear:** committing to something and losing it.
- **Fear:** being seen properly.

## How he's changed

In Antwerp he was a drifter with a steady job, watching other people make choices on a screen and never making any.

Here the rules are sharper, and he's found he has a code after all. He steps in now. He's still slippery, still won't
answer a straight question. Part of what the player gives him is somebody who won't let him flip a coin on the things
that matter.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to stop being the man who watches and says nothing.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A sailor in Ratchet mentions a young Belgian working the docks at Booty Bay, quick with numbers and in some trouble
   with the wrong people. Silas flips his coin about whether to go. You don't let him.
2. At Booty Bay you find Jens. He's caught up in a smuggling crew that's about to sell him out to save themselves.
3. This time Silas doesn't watch. He steps in, and you help him get Jens out.

**Ending (happy):** afterwards, on the pier, Silas tells Jens about the cameras and the months he said nothing. Jens
says he always knew. He says he'd have done it anyway, and he was angry for a long time, and he isn't now. They have a
beer. Jens comes back to Ratchet with him. Silas takes a cut of a pier from the goblin, his first commitment, and he
doesn't flip a coin to decide it.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Silas's Two-Euro Coin.** A Belgian two-euro coin, worn smooth from three years of being flipped for every decision
he didn't want to make. It was in his pocket the night he landed. He gives it to you after Booty Bay. He says he's
making his own decisions now, and he'd rather you had it than be tempted.

## Voice notes

- Quick, light and evasive. Contractions, jokes, and answers that don't answer.
- His English is fluent, with a Flemish accent and an English sailor's swearing from his father.
- He talks about people like cargo: who's heavy, who's light, who's lying about what's inside.
- He swears casually, in English and Dutch.
- **He won't talk about** his past straight until Companion, or Jens until Friend.
- **Habits:** flips his coin, watches every exit, reads everyone in a room before he sits down, and always sits with his
  back to a wall.
