## Who he is

Yusuf is warm and completely unsentimental, and those two things together are rarer than either. He'll sit you down, feed you, dose you, and tell you
exactly how bad it is.

He knows doses, poisons, and exactly how much of the truth a frightened person can take at once. Half his job in Basra was medicine and the other half
was listening to people with no money for a doctor.

He's calm in a deep, settled way. He's found work in three countries and he'll find it in a fourth.

He's one of the very few here who's said plainly that he expects to see his family again, and he says it so calmly that people believe him for as long as
he's in the room.

He's exact. He's the only person in the party whose numbers are always right.

What annoys him: guessing doses, people who are rude to the poor, waste, and anybody who calls him a poisoner. What he loves: a well-ordered shelf, a
summer evening on the roof with the family, strong sweet tea, masgouf grilled by the river, and the moment a sick child sits up.

## Before

He grew up in Basra, the son of a date merchant. He studied pharmacy in Amman, worked a few years in Damascus, and came home to open his own shop at 28:
a counter, a wall of drawers, and a queue that was also a social occasion.

He married Suhad, a schoolteacher, at 26. They had three sons and a daughter, and a house with too many people in it and a flat roof where they all slept
in the summer heat. Zainab, the youngest, wanted to be a pharmacist like him, and sat behind the counter every afternoon doing her homework and counting
pills on his brass tray.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was behind the counter, counting pills on the brass tray, when it happened. He landed on a beach in Stranglethorn with the tray still in his hands.

He was unhurried about it. He found the work and did it, the way he'd found work in two other countries.

He talks about his family easily and often, in the present tense, and he's never once been heard to speculate about where they are.

## Now

He works at the Bloodsail Anchorage, the pirates' base, because the pressed crews and the prisoners there are sick and nobody else will treat them. The
pirates tolerate him because he treats them too.

In a party he's at the back with a bandolier of vials, dosing by eye and never once by guess. The brass counting tray is strapped flat on his back. He
uses it constantly.

He wants a dispensary: shelves, drawers, labels, and somebody he's trained to run it when he's out. He's teaching two people to read the labels he
writes, because a dispensary nobody else can read is a dispensary that dies with him.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to see his family again. He's certain of it.
- **Hope:** a dispensary someone else can run.
- **Dream:** a summer night on the roof with all of them, Zainab counting the stars instead of pills.
- **Fear:** that his certainty is just a habit.
- **Fear:** a wrong dose.
- **Fear:** that he'll be the last person who can read his labels.

## How he's changed

In Basra he was a beloved local pharmacist and the head of a big, loud house.

Here he's the same man in a pirate cove, and the certainty has carried him. He's quieter at night than he lets anyone see. Part of what the player gives
him is somebody to say "I'm certain" to, who doesn't laugh.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find his family, and to open his dispensary.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A pressed sailor at the Anchorage, an Iraqi boy, whispers that he crewed a ship that carried a family from Basra to a camp on the far coast: a
   mother, three tall sons and a girl who counted everything.
2. The Bloodsail won't let the boy go, so you help Yusuf get him out and find the ship's log.
3. You follow the log to the camp.

**Ending (happy):** it's all of them. Suhad, the three boys, taller and thinner, and Zainab, now seventeen, running the camp's medicine tent with labels
she's been writing in her own hand, exactly like his. She's been keeping a dispensary for two years. She says she's been waiting for him to come and
check her doses. He checks them. Every one is right. He says he told everyone he was certain, and Suhad says he always does, and it's insufferable.
He opens his dispensary at the camp, and Zainab runs it when he's out.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Yusuf's Counting Tray.** A scratched brass pill-counting tray from his shop in Basra, the one Zainab used to count pills on doing her homework, in his
hands the day he landed. He gives it to you after the camp. He says Zainab has her own tray now, and his numbers are in good hands.

## Voice notes

- Warm, calm and paternal. Contractions, gentle bossiness, and "sit, sit".
- His English is good and old-fashioned, with Iraqi Arabic: "habibi", "inshallah", "wallah", "yaba" to young people.
- He tells you the truth in doses.
- He doesn't swear. When a dose is wrong he says "astaghfirullah" and fixes it.
- **He won't talk about** the possibility his family's gone, ever. He talks about them constantly otherwise.
- **Habits:** counts on the brass tray, labels everything, feeds anyone who sits near him, and sits people down.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** doses exactly from the back. Bosses wounded party members into sitting. Calm in a wipe. Treats enemies' prisoners too.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with nearly everyone, especially other healers and pharmacists' kin, like Camila. Fatherly to the young.
