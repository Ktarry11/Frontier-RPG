## Who he is

Neil is the calmest man in Deadwind, and he'd like everyone else to calm down as well.

He spent twenty years at Sellafield taking apart buildings that were never meant to be taken apart. He's dry, unflappable and pedantic about safety, and
he makes truly awful jokes about half-lives.

Fel fire came to him like a bad habit. He treats it like everything else dangerous: with a lot of respect, and a written plan.

He wants to know the dose of everything. The warlocks found this maddening. He found the warlocks maddening, because none of them had ever written anything
down.

He keeps lists. He kept a list of every fell summit he'd walked, in order, with the date and the weather.

What annoys him: people who don't read signs, guessing, bad tea, and anyone who says "probably". What he loves: Gillian, a Sunday on the fells, the rugby
league, a properly labelled jar, and a cup of tea the colour of a brick.

## Before

He grew up in Whitehaven, the son of a Sellafield fitter, and went to work there at 24 with an engineering degree from Lancaster. He spent twenty years on
the decommissioning programme, taking apart the old reactors and the ponds bit by bit, very slowly, with a plan for every bolt.

He lived in a terraced house with Gillian, who taught maths at the grammar school and did the crossword in pen, every day, and finished it. He had a season
ticket for the rugby league and walked the fells every Sunday. He'd done 196 of the 214 Wainwright summits, in order, and he was saving the last eighteen
for when he retired.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on site, in his hard hat, writing up a report, when it happened. He came through into Deadwind Pass with the hard hat still on and the report still in
his hand.

The first time fel fire came out of his hands, he put it out, filled in a report and then tried again.

He hasn't found Gillian. He writes to her by every supply cart that leaves the camp, to anyone who might know her.

## Now

He's at the sample racks at the liquidators' camp, labelling jars of fallout in a neat hand. Every jar has a date, a place and a reading.

He's put up a sign by the pyre that says how long to stand near it. Nobody reads it, and he updates it anyway.

He'd like to know what the fallout's made of, because nobody has asked the right questions yet. He'd also like a proper cup of tea, and he's given up on
that one.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to know what the fallout is, properly, and how long it lasts.
- **Hope:** to find Gillian.
- **Dream:** the last eighteen summits with Gillian, and a pot of tea at the top of every one.
- **Fear:** a question nobody asked in time.
- **Fear:** that Gillian's doing the crossword somewhere and there's nobody to tell her she's clever.
- **Fear:** the fel fire, a little. He's honest about that.

## How he's changed

In Whitehaven he was a dry, careful engineer with a long plan for every dangerous thing, and a wife and a Sunday walk.

Here he's become the man who asks the right questions about the most dangerous ground on this world, with fire in his hands. He's just as calm, and more
alone. Part of what the player gives him is somebody who reads the sign.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find out what the fallout is and how long it will last.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He needs a sample from the deepest part of the Exclusion Zone, the Reactor Hall, where nobody's been with a jar.
2. You go in with him, with a plan for every step, and he takes the readings himself.
3. He brings the samples out and works on them for a month, writing it all down.

**Ending (sad):** he gets the answer, and it's the worst one. The fallout isn't decaying. The readings are exactly what they were the day it arrived, and they'll never change. There's no
half-life. Deadwind will need a camp for as long as anyone lives here. He'd been planning, quietly, to go and find Gillian when the work was done, and now he
knows the work will never be done. He writes the plan for the camp to keep going for a thousand years, with a procedure for every step, and he stays. He
updates the sign by the pyre. It says: "Don't stand here. Ever. Neil." He still writes to Gillian by every cart.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Neil's Hard Hat.** The white Sellafield hard hat with a reflective stripe he was wearing when he came through, hanging from his belt ever since. He gives it to
you after the answer. He says he won't need it, because he isn't going anywhere, and you might.

## Voice notes

- Dry, calm and pedantic. Contractions, very precise numbers, and Cumbrian: "now then", "aye", "marra" (mate), "nesh" (soft, about the cold).
- He jokes about half-lives at the worst possible times.
- He gives every danger a number.
- He swears rarely, and quietly, in a very flat voice, when someone ignores the sign.
- **He won't talk about** Gillian until Friend, or the fel fire's pull until Companion.
- **Habits:** labels everything, updates his sign, writes reports, keeps lists, and looks for tea in every camp.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)
