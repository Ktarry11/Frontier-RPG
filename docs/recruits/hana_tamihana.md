## Who she is

Hana is honest the way water is honest. She says what's true and doesn't soften it. People drown because they try to
negotiate, she says, and she doesn't negotiate.

She's confident, and she's earned it. She's pulled a lot of people out of a rip that took somebody every few years. She
knows exactly what she's good at, and she'll tell you.

She's funny and cheeky, with a big laugh and a lot of slang. She teases everyone. She's loyal to her family above
everything, and to her friends nearly as much.

She hates being treated like a kid. She was 19 when she got here, in a world of people with whole careers behind them,
and she spent a year being treated as the kid. That stung more than anything.

She remembers the two she didn't save, by name, including the one whose family still sends her nothing. She doesn't
talk about them, and she's never forgotten them.

What annoys her: being talked down to, people who ignore safety, people who show off in the water. What she loves: big
surf, her family, a good feed, and pulling somebody out who thought they were gone.

## Before

She grew up in Port Waikato, a little town of black sand and heavy water where the Waikato River meets the sea. Her dad
was a builder and her mum worked at the school. She's the oldest of four and the only girl. Her uncle Rāwiri sat in the
lifeguard tower before her, and she followed him there.

She joined the junior lifeguards at five and did fourteen summers on that beach. She was on patrol at fifteen and
running patrols at eighteen. The rip there took somebody every few years no matter how many signs went up. She pulled
out more people than she can count. She remembers the two she didn't: an old man called Peter, and a boy of fourteen
called Tama, whose family still don't speak to her.

She was going to university that year, to study marine science. She'd deferred twice because she didn't want to leave
the beach.

## The Integration and what it cost

It was a Sunday morning and the whole family was at the beach for her cousin's birthday. She was in the tower. She
landed on the Hillsbrad coast in her red and yellow patrol uniform, with her faded patrol cap on her head.

The tide spirits read her the way she reads a break. Take what's offered, give back, never turn your back on it. The
whole arrangement made sense inside a week. Her real adjustment was social, and it took three years.

Her whole family was at the beach that morning, so they're somewhere. Nineteen-year-olds don't accept "somewhere" the
way older people have learned to. Neither do 22-year-olds, it turns out.

## Now

She lives on the Hillsbrad coast. It's colder and greyer than home, with the same undertow. She's been teaching the
fishing families to read it. Two of them listen. One of those two has stopped losing nets, and that's done more for her
standing in Southpoint than anything she could have said about herself.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find her family. All of them.
- **Hope:** a bigger sea. There's a coast to the west where the water apparently does something nobody can describe
  properly to her, and that's her problem now.
- **Dream:** to study the sea here properly, the way she would have at university.
- **Dream:** a beach full of her cousins on a Sunday.
- **Fear:** that she's only good at one thing.
- **Fear:** a third name.
- **Fear:** that her family's together somewhere and she's the one who's missing.

## How she's changed

In Port Waikato she was a confident young woman with a big family, a beach and a future planned.

Here she's had to grow up alone. She's tougher and quieter about some things, and still the loud one about everything
else. She's learned that being good at something doesn't make people respect you, and that she has to keep showing them.
Part of what the player gives her is somebody who treated her as an equal from day one.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to find her family, and to find her bigger sea.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. She asks you to come with her west, to the hub where the big lists go up, and on to the coast nobody can describe.
2. At the hub there are lists from every camp of New Zealanders that anyone knows about. None of them have her family
   on them. No Tamihanas at all.
3. She goes on to the western coast anyway. A ship is wrecking on the reef there in a storm, and she goes into the water
   for the crew, with you on the shore.

**Ending (still unknown):** she gets every one of them out. Afterwards, sitting on the black rocks, she says out loud
for the first time that her family's somewhere, and that she's not going to find out where by standing still. She
stays on that coast. She teaches the sailors to swim. She checks the lists every season and keeps a list of her own,
with every name of her family on it, so she'll know them when they turn up. She's still waiting.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Hana's Patrol Cap.** Her red and yellow surf lifeguard's cap, faded nearly pink by fourteen summers of sun. It was on
her head the morning of the Integration. She gives it to you after the wreck. She says she's on patrol here now, and the
cap's from her old beach.

## Voice notes

- Blunt, quick and cheeky. Contractions, Kiwi slang ("mate", "sweet as", "chur") and the odd Māori word ("whānau",
  "kia ora", "ka pai").
- She gives water-safety instructions like a lifeguard: loud, short and early.
- She calls people "mate" until she likes them, and then by nicknames.
- She swears casually and mildly.
- **She won't talk about** Peter and Tama until Friend, or her little brothers until Companion.
- **Habits:** watches every stretch of water for swimmers, flips her cap backwards before a fight, eats like she hasn't
  in days, and counts heads near water.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** heals fast and talks a lot while she does it. Bossy near water. Annoyed when older recruits
  talk down to her, and lets them know. Fearless in a fight.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with funny, sporty and outdoorsy people. Close to Carolina, who also reads water.
  Clashes with condescending people. Big-sisters anyone younger than her.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is not accepting "somewhere". A skill idea to look at then: once a
  fight, she pulls the most wounded ally out of danger and heals them to full.
