## Who he is

Marcus is cheerful, and he means it. He's also the kind of man who's been proved right a lot of times and seen it
change nothing, and that's left a mark under the cheerfulness. Both are true. He's given up trying to make them fit.

He talks. Weather, pressure, cloud, what the wind's going to do in an hour. He explains things because he loves them,
and he's good at it, and he notices when you've stopped listening and laughs at himself.

He's patient. He spent a career writing warnings for people whose job was deciding the warning was inconvenient. He
learned to say the same true thing calmly, again and again, and not to take it personally. He still takes it
personally. He just doesn't show it.

He's careful about being the outsider with a clipboard. He knows things about the Durotar coast the orcs don't, and
he hasn't worked out how to say so without sounding like every other stranger who turned up and told them how to
live.

He's a romantic about storms. He chased them as a student in a beaten-up car and loved every minute. Here the storm
answers when he calls it. He's fine about that, he tells everyone. He mostly is.

What annoys him: people who ignore warnings, people who think the weather is small talk, and anybody who says "it'll
probably be fine". What he loves: a big sky, good data, a cup of something hot on a cold morning, and his nephews.

## Before

He grew up in Scarborough, in Toronto's east end. His father drove a city bus and his mother worked in a bank, and they
both wanted him to be a doctor. He watched an ice storm take the power out across half the city when he was 12 and
decided he wanted to know why weather did what it did.

He studied atmospheric physics. As a student he chased tornadoes across the prairies in a Corolla, badly and happily.
Then he spent fifteen years forecasting from a building with a window, watching storms on screens and writing the
warnings.

The warnings were the part that wore him down. He'd see something coming days early and write it up properly, and
somebody in an office would decide it was inconvenient, or too expensive, or not worth worrying people about. Twice it
went badly. Once, a flood took out a trailer park his warning had named. He doesn't talk about that.

He never married. He had a few long relationships, and they all ended because he worked too much and talked about
weather on holiday. He was the fun uncle to his sister Grace's two boys, Daniel and Joseph. He took them storm
watching from the car.

His father died when he was 38, and his mother went back to Lagos. She thought his job was a waste of a good degree
and told him so every Christmas until the last one.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at work when it happened, watching a front come in on a screen. He landed on the Durotar coast in the middle of
a dry lightning storm, with no rain in it and thunder with no warning, and the first thing he felt was that he could
feel the pressure dropping on his skin.

He treated the whole thing as fieldwork, and it carried him through mostly in one piece. That's better than most
people managed, and he knows it.

The part that wrecked him privately for a year was this. On Earth, a storm was something he could predict and never
touch. Here he can touch it. He spent fifteen years unable to do anything about what he saw coming, and now he can,
and he doesn't know what to do with how angry that makes him about the old life.

His mother, Grace and the boys aren't on any list he's found. He checks the Toronto lists whenever he passes a hub.

## Now

He lives in Durotar in a stone hut on a ridge where he can see the weather coming off the sea. He keeps records of
every storm and where every strike lands.

His notes have already told him something about this coast that the orcs don't know. The big storms come in a pattern,
and the next big one is going to hit the coast road and Razor Hill harder than anything in living memory. He's been
trying to find a way to tell them without sounding like he's lecturing them about their own land.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** one person on this world who cares what's in his data.
- **Hope:** that Grace and the boys are somewhere safe.
- **Dream:** to stand in the middle of the biggest storm there is. He's a meteorologist. He knows how that ends.
- **Dream:** to write a warning and have people listen to it.
- **Fear:** being right early again and having it change nothing.
- **Fear:** that his cheerfulness is just a habit he picked up so people would listen to him.
- **Fear:** that he wasted fifteen years watching.

## How he's changed

In Toronto he was a good forecaster who'd learned to be ignored politely. He was cheerful at work, tired at home and
the best uncle in the family.

Here he can do something about the weather, and it's changed him. He's braver. He's angrier underneath than he was. He
still smiles and explains. Part of what the player gives him is somebody who listens the first time.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to be listened to in time.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He shows you his notes. The great storm is coming in days, not weeks, and it'll hit the coast road and Razor Hill.
2. You help him get proof the orcs will accept: readings from a high peak and a word from the coast's oldest shaman,
   who lives somewhere dangerous and doesn't like visitors.
3. The shaman believes him and carries the warning to Razor Hill. The town starts getting ready, but the camps along the
   coast road don't listen.

**Ending (sad):** the storm hits. Razor Hill holds, because it was ready. The camps on the coast road are wiped out,
and people die there that his warning named. Marcus spends the night pulling people out of the water, and he gets a
young orc boy out alive. In the morning the old shaman asks if she can keep his notes, so the next generation knows the
pattern. He gives them to her. He doesn't talk for a day. He says afterwards that he was right early again, and this
time it changed some things, and he's going to have to learn to live with "some".

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Marcus's Weather Meter.** The handheld weather meter he had clipped to his belt the day the worlds joined. It reads
wind and pressure, and the battery died in the first month. He carried it for three years anyway. He gives it to you
and says he doesn't need it to know what the sky's doing any more.

## Voice notes

- Warm, chatty and a bit nerdy. Contractions, and long explanations he cuts short when he sees your face.
- Canadian: polite, says "sorry" a lot, and gets very calm when he's angry.
- He narrates the weather like a forecaster: "pressure's dropping", "front's coming in", "four minutes".
- He doesn't swear much. When he does, it's at the sky.
- **He won't talk about** the trailer park until Friend, or his mother's last Christmas until Sworn.
- **Habits:** looks up before he looks at you, licks a finger and holds it up for the wind, writes everything down, and
  hums when a storm is coming.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** warns you before a fight what's coming and is usually right. Calm and clear in a crisis. Gets
  quietly upset when the party ignores a warning and then wipes. Loves open country and big skies, and hates caves.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with curious people and anybody who listens. Grates with people who brush him off,
  and especially with people who are always sure. A natural friend for anybody else who studied something on Earth.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is being ignored, and his anger at the old life. A skill idea to look at
  then: once a fight, when the party is about to take a big hit, he calls it in time and they take far less.
