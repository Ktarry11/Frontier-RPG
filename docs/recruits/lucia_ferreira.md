## Who she is

Lúcia is warm. She talks to everyone, touches your arm when she laughs, and makes friends in the time it takes to trim a
fringe.

She's a shameless gossip who never repeats a word said in her chair. She knows everything about everyone at the
Crossroads and she'll tell you none of it. She'll tell you plenty about herself, though.

She's devout. Her grandmother raised her on rosaries and saints' days, and Lúcia still prays the way she was taught, out
loud and with her whole heart. Here the sun answers when she asks, which surprised her a lot less than it surprised the
priests.

She's generous. She charges a copper and a blessing, and most people pay the copper and pretend not to want the
blessing.

She misses singing in a church more than almost anything.

What annoys her: rude customers, people who mock prayer, and bad haircuts done by someone else. What she loves: gossip,
music, a full salon on a Saturday, her mother's voice at noon, and singing with other people.

## Before

She grew up in Goiânia, in central Brazil, raised mostly by her grandmother, Dona Zuleide, while her mother worked long
shifts at a bakery. Her grandmother lit a candle every Sunday for everybody on their street, by name, and Lúcia kept the
list for her.

She trained as a hairdresser at 18 and opened her own salon at 26, on a busy street: pink tiles, two chairs, a radio that
only got one station, and a queue on Saturdays. Her cat, Dona Flor, slept in the window. Her mother phoned every day at
noon, and Lúcia put the phone on speaker so the customers could join in.

Her grandmother died when Lúcia was 25. Lúcia took over the candle list and kept lighting them every Sunday.

She'd had a boyfriend for four years, Marcelo, who wanted to move to São Paulo. She didn't. They split up the year before
the Integration, kindly.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was cutting a customer's hair when it happened. She landed in the Barrens with her scissors in her apron pocket and
the candle list folded in her purse.

The first thing she did on this world was cut a grunt's hair, and he tipped her a copper. She started praying at the
Crossroads because she didn't know what else to do, and on the second day the light came down.

She hasn't found her mother. Every day at noon she stops what she's doing for a moment.

## Now

She sits at the Crossroads by the inn, with a stool, a bowl of water and a queue of caravan guards. She cuts hair, heals
cuts and blesses anyone who'll sit still for it.

She wants a proper salon with a door that shuts and a mirror that isn't a bucket of water. She also wants to find a
church, any church, where someone will sing with her.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a church where someone sings with her.
- **Hope:** a salon with a door and a real mirror.
- **Dream:** her mother's voice on the phone at noon.
- **Fear:** that the light will stop coming if she stops believing it will.
- **Fear:** forgetting the names on her grandmother's list.
- **Fear:** praying alone for the rest of her life.

## How she's changed

In Goiânia she was a cheerful hairdresser with a full salon, a mother on the phone and a church on Sunday.

Here she's had to keep her faith alone, and it's grown stronger and quieter. She's still loud and funny. She also knows
now that she can be on her own. Part of what the player gives her is a congregation of a few people who'll at least sit
still while she prays.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to find a church where someone will sing with her.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A tauren caravan guard tells her that the tauren of Camp Taurajo sing to the sun every dawn. She wants to hear it.
2. The road to Camp Taurajo is dangerous, and the camp is wary of strangers since Mulgore. You help her earn their trust,
   by helping their wounded.
3. On the last morning, she's invited to the dawn song.

**Ending (happy):** it's not her church and it's not her language, and it doesn't matter. The tauren sing to the rising
sun, and Lúcia sings with them in Portuguese, and the sun answers both. An old tauren woman tells her to come back every
dawn she can. Back at the Crossroads she builds a little shelter with a real door and a real mirror, and a wall where she
lights a candle every Sunday for everybody on her grandmother's list, and for the tauren of Mulgore, by name, as many as
they'll tell her.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Dona Zuleide's Candle List.** Her grandmother's list of everybody on their street in Goiânia, in two people's
handwriting, folded soft. Lúcia had it in her purse the day she landed. She gives it to you once the names are on her
candle wall. She says they're written somewhere safe now.

## Voice notes

- Warm, fast and chatty. Contractions, lots of questions, and stories about people.
- Her English is enthusiastic, with a Brazilian accent and Portuguese words: "querido", "nossa", "meu Deus", "graças a
  Deus".
- She prays out loud, often, and doesn't care who's listening.
- She doesn't swear. She says "meu Deus" instead, with feeling.
- **She won't talk about** her mother until Companion, or her grandmother's death until Friend.
- **Habits:** fixes people's hair without asking, touches her saint's medal, stops for a moment at noon, and hums hymns.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** chats through every fight and prays before every boss. Heals with real warmth. Gets everyone's
  gossip at every camp. Gets upset by cruelty and loudly says so.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with warm, sociable people and makes friends with the shy ones by force. Bonds with the
  other faithful. Clashes with cynics, then keeps trying.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is praying alone. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, she prays
  out loud and every ally's healing is stronger for a few turns.
