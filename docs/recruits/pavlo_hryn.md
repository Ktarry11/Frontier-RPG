## Who he is

Pavlo is steady. He spent sixteen years standing in front of a room of fourteen-year-olds who'd rather be anywhere else
and keeping it together by presence alone. He does the same thing in a shield wall and finds it much easier, because a
gnoll at least wants something comprehensible.

He's gentle. He's very big and very calm, and he never raises his voice. He doesn't need to.

He's a teacher to the bone. He explains things, he puts events in order, and he sees patterns. He taught the same four
wars every spring and he knows how ordinary people end up doing terrible things.

He grieves quietly for the work he lost. Nobody here needs a history teacher, and it's taken him most of three years to
stop being wounded by that.

He's dry and wry, with a teacher's humour. He'll make a joke about something that happened four hundred years ago and
then look disappointed when nobody gets it.

What annoys him: people who don't learn from what's happened, bullies, and certainty without knowledge. What he loves:
strong tea, a good question from a child, old maps, and his son.

## Before

He grew up in Lviv, the son of a tram driver and a librarian. He studied history at the university there and became a
teacher at 37, late, after years working in a museum. He loved it. He disagreed with the curriculum and taught it anyway,
and every year he reached most of his thirty students.

He married Oksana, an archivist, at 25. Their son Andriy was born when Pavlo was 27. Oksana died of cancer when Andriy
was 15. Pavlo raised him alone after that. They were close. They argued about history at dinner every night.

Andriy went to Kraków to study engineering. He was 23 when the Integration came.

Pavlo always carried his steel tea flask. Oksana gave it to him on his first day as a teacher.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in his classroom, in the middle of a lesson, when it happened. He landed in Silverpine with his tea flask and his
chalk-dusted jacket.

He did badly, and he's open about it. For a year he looked for a school. There isn't one. There's a lot of work for a
man who'll stand still while something comes at him, and he took it. The taking was a small grief he's never dressed up
as a calling.

He's been looking for Andriy on every list, and he hasn't found him.

## Now

He holds the road at the Sepulcher in Silverpine North, against whatever comes up it from the south. It's steady work and
he's good at it, and he'd swap it tomorrow.

He's still going to teach. He has six children at the Sepulcher, twice a week, and he teaches them whatever he can
remember of a world that isn't coming back. Two of them have stopped coming. He's decided not to ask why.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find Andriy.
- **Hope:** a real school. Even one room.
- **Dream:** to argue about history with his son at dinner again.
- **Fear:** that nothing he taught mattered.
- **Fear:** that he's only good for standing still.
- **Fear:** that the people who end up doing terrible things are people just like his students.

## How he's changed

In Lviv he was a widowed teacher who loved his work and his son.

Here he lost both, and he became a wall. He's stronger and sadder, and he's held onto the teaching like a rope. Part of
what the player gives him is somebody who asks him questions.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find his son.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A Pale Flame scout tells him that there's a young Ukrainian in the Scarlet Crusade, an engineer, rising fast.
2. You go with Pavlo to the Scarlet Monastery grounds, under truce, to meet him.
3. It's Andriy.

**Ending (sad):** Andriy has joined the Crusade. He believes all of it, and he's good at it. He tells his father the
Crusade gave him a reason to live when everything else was gone. Pavlo spends two hours trying to talk him out of it.
He uses everything he knows about how ordinary people end up doing terrible things, and none of it works. Andriy hugs
him, says he loves him, and goes back inside. Pavlo walks back to the Sepulcher in silence. The next day he goes to the
two children who stopped coming and asks them why, and then he teaches the six of them harder than he ever has.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Pavlo's Tea Flask.** A dented steel flask with no lid, the one Oksana gave him on his first day as a teacher. He's
carried it through sixteen years of classrooms and three years of holding the line. He gives it to you after the
Monastery. He says he'd like it to go somewhere that isn't a wall.

## Voice notes

- Slow, gentle and dry. Contractions, a teacher's careful explanations, and long pauses.
- His English is good and formal, with a Ukrainian accent.
- He puts things in order: first this, then this, then this.
- He doesn't swear. When he's angry he gets very precise.
- **He won't talk about** Oksana until Companion, or Andriy until Friend.
- **Habits:** drinks tea from the dented flask, draws maps in the dirt, asks "and why do you think that is?", and stands
  very still in a fight.
