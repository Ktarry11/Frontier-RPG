## Who he is

Taras is dry and morbid, and very gentle with animals. Nine years of walking tourists through Pripyat taught him to make jokes about the end of the world in
a calm voice.

He's careful. He knows exactly how dangerous invisible things are, and he counts heads going out and counts them again coming back.

He loves dogs more than most people. He knew every dog at every checkpoint in the Zone by name, and they all knew his pockets had sausage in them. Kashtan,
a Zone stray he fed for years, came through with him.

He's the one person on this world who knows the yellow Ferris wheel by heart. When it turned up in Deadwind, he was the first to volunteer.

He told himself the wheel was a coincidence for about a day, and then he stopped telling himself that.

What annoys him: tourists who touch things, people who aren't afraid of what they can't see, and cruelty to animals. What he loves: Kashtan, his mother's
tomatoes, instant coffee in a minibus, a quiet village, and every dog he's ever met.

## Before

He grew up in Kyiv, the son of a liquidator who worked the cleanup after the disaster in 1986 and died of it slowly when Taras was a teenager. Taras became a
guide in the Exclusion Zone at 29, partly to understand what had killed his father.

For nine years he drove a minibus that smelled of instant coffee, walked tourists through Pripyat past the school and the swimming pool, and ended every tour
under the yellow Ferris wheel that never once turned. He lived in a flat with a balcony full of his mother's tomatoes.

He fed the dogs at every checkpoint. Kashtan, a brown shepherd-mix, was his favourite, and met his minibus every morning at the gate.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at the checkpoint, feeding Kashtan, when it happened. They came through together. He had a dosimeter badge clipped to his collar, the yellow and black
one he wore for every tour.

Kashtan settled in faster than he did. The dog found the first hunting trail in a week, and Taras followed him onto a witch hunter's road.

He hasn't found his mother.

## Now

He's at the liquidators' camp at Ariden's Watch in Deadwind Pass, checking everyone through the wash troughs at the gate. He counts heads when people go out
and counts them again when they come back. Kashtan won't set a paw where the ground's gone bad, and Taras has learned not to either.

He wants to stand under the wheel one more time and work out why it came here when nothing else did.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to understand why the wheel is here.
- **Hope:** a quiet village, a lot of dogs, and nothing radioactive at all.
- **Dream:** his mother's balcony, a tomato off the vine, and Kashtan asleep on his feet.
- **Fear:** the invisible things.
- **Fear:** losing Kashtan.
- **Fear:** that he'll end like his father.

## How he's changed

In Kyiv he was a dry, careful guide to the worst place on Earth, still trying to understand his father's death.

Here the worst place on Earth followed him, and he's become its keeper again. He's darker and gentler. Part of what the player gives him is someone who
stays on the path.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to stand under the wheel and understand why it's here.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He asks you to come with him through the Exclusion Zone, all the way to the foot of the wheel.
2. The way is the most dangerous ground in Deadwind. Kashtan leads, and won't put a paw wrong.
3. You reach the wheel. He stands under it, exactly where he stood at the end of every tour for nine years.

**Ending (still unknown):** nothing happens. There's no answer, no sign, no reason. It's just the wheel, rusting, as it always was. He stands there a long time.
Then he says that in nine years of tours, the one question every tourist asked him was why, and he never had an answer then either. He takes a photograph in
his head, the way he did for them, and walks back out, counting heads. He stays on at the gate. He says somebody who knows the wheel by heart should be
the one keeping people away from it.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Taras's Dosimeter.** The yellow and black dosimeter badge he wore on every tour of the Exclusion Zone, clipped to his collar when he came through. He gives it
to you after the wheel. He says the liquidators have proper ones now, and you should have something that tells you when to turn back.

## Voice notes

- Dry, morbid and calm. Contractions, guide's patter, and Ukrainian: "dobre" (good), "bud laska" (please), "Bozhe" (God).
- He talks to people like tourists: stay on the path, don't touch that.
- He talks to Kashtan more warmly than to anyone.
- He swears in Ukrainian when someone touches something that glows.
- **He won't talk about** his father until Friend, or his mother until Companion.
- **Habits:** counts heads, checks his dosimeter, feeds every dog he meets, and follows Kashtan's nose.
