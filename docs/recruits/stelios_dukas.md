## Who he is

Stelios is unshockable, and it has nothing to do with callousness. Nineteen years of composure, paperwork, and being the steadiest person in the worst
room of somebody's life.

He's dry and ironic, with a funeral director's gallows humour. He's very funny about death, which unsettles people until they realise how much
he respects it.

He's courteous in an old-fashioned way. He's told four thousand people the worst thing they'll hear that year, and he did it gently every time.

He's tired of being praised for being calm. Composure was his job for nineteen years. Praising him for it is like praising a baker for being awake.

He's devoted to the dead, in a practical way. Every transplant who dies here with nobody who knows their language deserves a name and a rite, he
says, and he's given one hundred and forty of them.

What annoys him: disrespect for the dead, sloppy records, people who say "it's just a body", and being called morbid. What he loves: a properly
kept register, a well-conducted rite, Greek coffee, Cavafy's poems, and his wife's library.

## Before

He grew up in Thessaloniki, above his father's funeral home, and he inherited it at 34. He's said that inheriting it wasn't a choice, and that he
would have chosen it, and that both of those are true, and people find the combination difficult.

For nineteen years he did arrangements, families, and the particular courtesy of a man who knows exactly what to say.

He married Despina, a librarian, at 28. She ran the city's old library and kept a brass date stamp on her desk. She gave him one as a joke when he
took over the business, so he could stamp the register. He did, every day. Their son Nikos is an architect in Athens.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in the middle of a funeral, at the graveside, when it happened. He landed in the Tanaris desert with Despina's brass date stamp in his coat
pocket.

He was calm throughout. The dead here occasionally get up, and he found that more interesting than frightening.

He hasn't found Despina or Nikos.

## Now

He lives in Glittervein, past the Abyssal Sands, where there are many dead and nobody else to keep records of them. He does the paperwork. He's
extremely good at it, and he's used it four times to find out who a body was before it stopped being anyone.

He conducts rites for the transplants who die here with nobody who knows their language. He's done one hundred and forty. He keeps a register,
stamped with the date each time, and he's written to every hub he can reach asking that the names be posted. Three have replied.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** every transplant who dies here named and remembered.
- **Hope:** that Despina is keeping a library somewhere.
- **Dream:** an evening in Despina's library, reading, with her stamping returns.
- **Fear:** a name lost forever.
- **Fear:** finding Despina's name in somebody else's register.
- **Fear:** becoming merely morbid.

## How he's changed

In Thessaloniki he was a dignified, ironic undertaker, a happy husband, and the steadiest man in every sad room.

Here he's found that the dead need him more than ever, and he's quietly made himself their record keeper. He's wearier and more devoted. Part of
what the player gives him is a living person to talk to.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to get the register posted at every hub, and to know about Despina.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A camp in the Sands has lost dozens to a sickness, and the dead are rising and wandering. Nobody knows who they were. Stelios asks you to help him
   lay them to rest properly.
2. You calm the risen and help him identify each one by what they carry. He names every one.
3. He takes the register to the biggest hub he can reach and asks them, in person, to post it.

**Ending (still unknown):** they post it. Within a month, families start coming to Glittervein to find their dead, and Stelios meets each of them,
gently, the way he did for nineteen years. One woman thanks him for giving her brother a name, and he has to step outside for a while. But Despina
never comes, and she's on no list he's seen. He doesn't know where she is. He keeps a page at the back of the register blank, with her name at the
top. He says he'll stamp it with a date if he ever learns one, and he hopes he never has to.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Despina's Date Stamp.** A brass date stamp with a wooden handle, from the old library in Thessaloniki. His wife gave it to him as a joke when he
took over the business, and he's stamped every name in his register with it. He gives it to you after the register's posted. He says the hubs have
their own stamps now, and he'd like this one to mark something happier.

## Voice notes

- Dry, measured and courteous. Contractions, gallows humour, and gentle formality.
- His English is excellent and formal, with Greek: "ela" (come), "Panagia mou", "kalo taxidi" (safe journey) for the dead.
- He speaks of the dead with great respect and great dry wit.
- He doesn't swear. When something's disrespectful he says "that will not do".
- **He won't talk about** Despina until Companion, or his own fear of the register until Friend.
- **Habits:** stamps his register, reads the names aloud, adjusts his half-moon glasses, and lays the dead out properly wherever he finds them.
