## Who he is

Mateo loves the sky. Up on his mountain in Chile the sky was so clear it stopped being scenery and became a place. Here he arrived
under the wrong stars and spent his first winter learning them from scratch, alone, on his back in the grass, happier than he'd been
in a decade.

He's talkative. He'll correct you on technician versus astronomer immediately, and then talk for an hour anyway.

He's gentle, patient and a little dreamy. He's the kind of man who'll stop in the middle of a fight to look at a comet.

He's guilty about his family. He went up the mountain every month for seventeen years, and his girls thought it was boring, and he
agreed with them, and he went anyway.

What annoys him: light pollution, people who never look up, and being called an astronomer. What he loves: a moonless night, a
cold dome at four in the morning, his daughters, and the moment someone looks up properly for the first time.

## Before

He grew up in La Serena, on the Chilean coast, the son of a fisherman. He studied electronics and got a job at the observatory at
31: cold domes, cable runs, and the four hours before dawn when the astronomers slept and the mountain belonged to him.

He married Paula, a nurse, at 27. They had two girls, Antonia and Josefa, who thought the mountain was boring. He agreed with them.
He went anyway, every month, two weeks up and two weeks down, for seventeen years. Paula held everything together while he was
away, and he knows it.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in the dome at four in the morning, fixing a cable, when it happened. He landed in Thousand Needles at night, under a sky full
of stars he didn't know, with the red-light headlamp he used in the dome still on his head.

He found the sky first and everything else second, and for a long while that worked. The reckoning came in his second winter, when
he'd finished naming the constellations and had to come back down and be a person.

He walks to the hub at Freewind Post twice a year with two names folded in his pocket, his daughters', reads the whole list, and
walks back. He talks about the stars on the way out and says very little on the way home.

## Now

He lives on top of a mesa in Southern Thousand Needles, where the air is dry and the nights go on forever. The goblins of the flats
think he's harmless and let him climb their towers. From the top of one he can see most of the sky at once, and he's begun to
suspect it's the finest observing site on this world.

He's making a map of this sky, drawn properly, by somebody who understands that a sky is also a calendar and a clock and a promise.
He's eleven months in.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to finish his map of the sky.
- **Hope:** to find Paula and the girls.
- **Dream:** his daughters on the mountain, looking up, not bored.
- **Fear:** that he chose the sky over his family, and that he's still doing it.
- **Fear:** that the girls have grown up and won't know him.
- **Fear:** clouds.

## How he's changed

In Chile he was an absent husband and father who loved the sky more easily than his family.

Here he lost his family and found the sky, and he's had to face which one he really wanted. He's come down off the mountain more.
Part of what the player gives him is somebody to share the sky with.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to finish the map, and to find his family.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He finishes the star map. He realises it can guide caravans across the desert at night by the stars, the way the old navigators
   did, and he gives copies to every caravan leader he can find.
2. One caravan gets lost in a storm and doesn't arrive. He goes out after it with you, navigating by his own stars.
3. You find the caravan, pinned down by raiders in the dunes, and get it out.

**Ending (happy):** in the back of the caravan is a Chilean woman with two teenage girls, heading for Freewind Post because they'd
heard of a mad Chilean who maps the sky. It's Paula, Antonia and Josefa. They'd been following his star map without knowing it was
his. The girls tell him the stars here are actually quite good. He takes them up the goblin tower that night and makes them look
up. Neither of them is bored.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Mateo's Red Headlamp.** A battered headlamp with a red filter, the kind astronomers use so they don't ruin their night vision,
worn every night in the observatory dome for seventeen years. It was on his head when he landed. He gives it to you after the
caravan. He says he's got his family to look up with now, and you should look up more.

## Voice notes

- Warm, rambling and enthusiastic. Contractions, long tangents about the sky, and corrections about his job title.
- His English is fluent and Chilean: "po", "cachai", "ya".
- He names stars and constellations as he goes.
- He doesn't swear much. When clouds roll in, he swears at them.
- **He won't talk about** his family until Companion, or the guilt until Friend.
- **Habits:** looks up constantly, points out stars to anyone nearby, stays up all night, and touches the two names in his pocket.
