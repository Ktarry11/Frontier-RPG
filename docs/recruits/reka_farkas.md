## Who she is

Réka is patient in a very particular way. She made films one frame at a time for twelve years, moving puppets a millimetre and taking a picture, and
it turned out to be the best training for necromancy anyone's ever had.

She's sly and funny, with a dry, slightly macabre sense of humour. She makes jokes about her dead crow and expects you to laugh.

She's precise. She treats the dead the way she treated her puppets, with patience and a lot of small adjustments. The first time she raised something,
she said she knew exactly where she'd gone wrong with the elbow.

She's tidy about it. The people on the Black Strand don't like what she does, but they like that she cleans up after herself.

She's an artist first. She doesn't want power. She wants to make something move that's never moved before.

What annoys her: sloppy necromancy, people who call it evil, and bad animation. What she loves: a perfect frame, her cat, strong coffee, old Hungarian
cartoons, and the moment a still thing looks alive.

## Before

She grew up in Pécs, the daughter of a schoolteacher and a puppeteer who ran a little puppet theatre. She spent her childhood backstage, watching her
father bring wooden figures to life. He died of a heart attack when she was nineteen. She moved to Budapest and started making stop-motion films,
because it was the closest thing to what he did.

For twelve years she animated in a studio above a bakery in the eighth district. Her short film, about a puppet who wanted to become a real bird, won a
prize at Annecy. She lived alone with a cat, Kocka, who hated every puppet she ever made.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was in the studio, moving a puppet's arm a millimetre, when it happened. She landed in the Dry Deep with the little wooden artist's mannequin she'd
been using as a reference still in her hand, with a wire in its arm.

The first time she raised something, she said she knew exactly where she'd gone wrong with the elbow. She's been refining ever since.

She hasn't found her mother. She misses the cat, and she's embarrassed by how much.

## Now

She's on the Black Strand, in the black sand under the Dry Deep, raising crows out of whatever the beetles leave.

She wants to make something move that's never moved before, and she's careful not to say what. She's also looking for a good camera. She knows there
isn't one, and she keeps looking anyway.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to make a film, here, somehow.
- **Hope:** that her mother is teaching somewhere.
- **Dream:** her father's puppet theatre, and him watching her film.
- **Fear:** that she's become what people say necromancers are.
- **Fear:** that she'll never make anything that's art again, only things that walk.
- **Fear:** forgetting her father's hands.

## How she's changed

In Budapest she was a quiet, prize-winning animator living alone with a grumpy cat, chasing her father's craft.

Here her craft has become something people fear. She's sharper and darker in humour, and more determined to make it beautiful. Part of what the player
gives her is an audience.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to make something move that's never moved before: a film.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. She tells you her secret: she wants to make a moving picture, here, for the children of the Black Strand, who've never seen one.
2. There's no camera. So she designs a zoetrope, a spinning drum of drawings, and she needs glass lenses, a goblin gear and black lacquer from dangerous
   places. You get them with her.
3. She draws the frames: a little wooden puppet who wants to become a real bird.

**Ending (happy):** she shows it on the Black Strand, to every child she can find, in the dark, by lantern. The puppet flaps its wings and flies. The
children scream with delight. It's the first thing in her life on this world that's moved without anything dying first, and she cries. The children
make her show it forty times. She starts drawing a longer one, and she gives the wooden mannequin its wire back.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Réka's Wooden Mannequin.** A tiny wooden artist's mannequin with a wire in its arm, the last thing she was animating in her studio in Budapest. She
gives it to you after the zoetrope. She says she doesn't need a reference any more, she remembers how things move, and it's the only puppet she never
raised.

## Voice notes

- Sly, quick and dry. Contractions, macabre jokes, and very precise descriptions of movement.
- Her English is fluent and wry, with Hungarian: "jó" (good), "na" (well), "szia".
- She talks about the dead like puppets: weight, timing, follow-through.
- She swears in Hungarian, creatively.
- **She won't talk about** her father until Friend, or the film until the quest.
- **Habits:** adjusts her crow's bones, sketches movements, flips her fringe out of her eyes, and fidgets with the mannequin.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with artists and wry people, especially Ileana, another artist necromancer, and Stelios, who respects the dead.
