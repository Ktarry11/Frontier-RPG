## Who he is

Osvaldo is enormously talented at something nobody here has any use for, and that's most of what's wrong with him.

He's blunt to the point of rude. He uses as little English as he can get away with, though it's better than he lets on. He's never once been unkind
about anything that mattered.

He stopped playing. There was nothing to play and nobody to play with, and he put it down, and it stayed down. The fire came after that, and he'll
tell you the order matters. He was already empty when he went looking.

He's kinder than he admits. He's twice stayed up all night with people who were in a bad way, saying almost nothing, just sitting there. Both of
them told somebody about it. He denied it both times.

He has a flat expression that unsettles people until they get used to it.

What annoys him: people who talk through music, chatter, pity, and anyone who asks him to play. What he loves: a perfect high note, Cuban coffee,
his mother's black beans, his brother's piano, and silence after a song.

## Before

He grew up in Havana, in Centro, the younger son of a dockworker and Caridad, who sang in church and taught both her boys music. His brother
Rubén was the piano prodigy. Osvaldo took up trumpet so he'd have something Rubén didn't.

For sixteen years he played in a hotel band, six nights a week, for tourists who talked through all of it. Rubén was on piano, better than him,
and knew it. They fought and played together every night.

His mother was ninety-one and lived three streets from the hotel. He went to see her every afternoon before the set. He's said one sentence about
her in three years.

He never married. He says he was married to the band, and the band was always cheating on him.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on the bandstand, mid-solo, when it happened. He landed on a beach in Stranglethorn with his trumpet mouthpiece in his fist, the trumpet
itself gone.

He stopped playing. The fire came after. The Bloodsail found him on the beach, saw what he could do with it, and paid him to burn ships for them.
He didn't much care who he burned for. He was empty.

He hasn't found his mother or Rubén. The one sentence he's said about his mother is: "She was ninety-one, so I know."

## Now

He's aboard the Bloodsail Raider, the pirates' hired fire, when you meet him. He's tired of it. He joins you to get off the ship, and doesn't say
much about why.

In a party he's at the back, doing the loud part, with a flat expression. The mouthpiece hangs at his throat. He's never been seen to use it.
Somebody heard him humming once, badly, and had the sense to say nothing.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** none that he'll admit to.
- **Hope:** that Rubén is somewhere with a piano.
- **Dream:** one more night on the bandstand with Rubén, and nobody talking.
- **Fear:** picking up a trumpet and finding nothing there.
- **Fear:** that the fire is all that's left.
- **Fear:** being asked to play.

## How he's changed

In Havana he was a brilliant, prickly musician who loved his brother and his mother and pretended otherwise.

Here he went empty and filled up with fire, and he burned ships for pirates because nothing mattered. He's starting to care again, which frightens
him. Part of what the player gives him is somebody to sit up with.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** he won't say. It's Rubén, and the music.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. In a tavern in Booty Bay, somebody's playing piano, and playing it the way only one person Osvaldo's ever known plays it. He goes very still.
2. The pianist is being held there, forced to play for the Bloodsail captain who owns the tavern. The captain is the same man Osvaldo used to burn
   ships for. You help him break the man's hold.
3. It's Rubén.

**Ending (happy):** Rubén looks up from the piano and says, "You're late, and you're flat." Osvaldo laughs, which nobody's ever heard him do. Rubén
tells him their mother died the first winter, peacefully, in a camp, with Rubén holding her hand, and that she asked after Osvaldo at the end. It
hurts, and it isn't the worst news, and they both knew. That night Rubén finds him a battered goblin trumpet, and Osvaldo fits his old mouthpiece
to it, and they play together in the tavern, and nobody talks through it.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Osvaldo's Band Badge.** A small brass name badge from the hotel band in Havana, "Osvaldo, Trompeta", that he kept in his pocket with
the mouthpiece. He keeps the mouthpiece, because he's playing again. He gives it to you after the tavern. He says he's not playing for a hotel any more. He's
playing for his brother.

## Voice notes

- Blunt, short and flat. Contractions, very few words, and rude remarks that turn out to be kind.
- His English is better than he lets on, with Cuban Spanish: "asere" (mate), "qué bolá", "coño", "chévere".
- He refuses to talk much, and refuses to play, until the quest.
- He swears in Cuban Spanish, constantly and flatly.
- **He won't talk about** his mother at all, beyond the one sentence, or Rubén until Friend.
- **Habits:** touches the mouthpiece at his throat, hums when he thinks nobody's listening, sits up with anyone who can't sleep, and stands at the
  back.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** devastating fire at the back, saying almost nothing. Sits up with anyone wounded or grieving. Hates chatter during
  fights.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with quiet people and with musicians, especially Ileana, who'd argue music with him. Pretends to be annoyed by
  Gianluca, who never stops talking, and is secretly fond of him.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the emptiness. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he plays one long
  note, and the party's spells hit harder until it fades.
