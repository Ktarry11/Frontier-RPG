## Who he is

Emiliano is a showman. He breathed fire at traffic lights for eleven years, two minutes a red light, and he learned to hold a crowd in the time it takes a
light to change.

He's cocky, and he's earned some of it. The fire here took to him at once, and it comes out of him the colour of a dragon's breath.

He's a talker. He's very good at it. He talked his way into every job he ever had and out of most of the trouble he got into.

He's devoted to his family under all the swagger. He sent money home every week from the age of eighteen, and he's still sending it, on every ship that
leaves Cinderwharf, in case one of them finds them.

He never once burned himself, and he'll show you his hands to prove it.

What annoys him: people who don't clap, people who think street performers are beggars, and being told to be careful. What he loves: a crowd, tlayudas
from the market, mezcal, his sisters, and the moment a whole crowd gasps.

## Before

He grew up in Oaxaca, the eldest of five, after his father left. His mother, Doña Elvira, sold tamales in the market, and Emiliano started working at
fourteen to help. He learned to breathe fire from an old man who'd done it at the same corner for thirty years.

He worked the traffic lights by the zócalo: two minutes a red light, coins from the windows, eleven years. He had a dream of a circus that would take him
on, a real one, with a tent. Every year he auditioned for the travelling circus that came through, and every year they said next year.

He helped his mother raise his four sisters, and paid for two of them to finish school.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was mid-trick at a red light, a plastic bottle of lamp oil on his belt, when it happened. He landed in the Ashen Reach with the bottle still there.

He says the Integration finally gave him the stage he wanted, and he only half means it. The Ashclaim hired him the day he lit their forge from across the
yard.

He hasn't found his mother or his sisters. He sends money home anyway.

## Now

He's in Cinderwharf, by the forges, showing off to anyone who'll watch.

He wants a real show, in a real tent, with an audience that paid to get in. The bottle of lamp oil on his belt is the one he carried at home, and the label's
nearly gone.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a real show in a real tent.
- **Hope:** that his money's reaching his family somehow.
- **Dream:** his mother and sisters in the front row, finally seeing him on a real stage.
- **Fear:** that he's only a street act.
- **Fear:** his sisters going without, because he's not there.
- **Fear:** a silent crowd.

## How he's changed

In Oaxaca he was a cocky street performer and the man of the house at fourteen, carrying a family on coins.

Here he's got real power and a real job, and he still sends the money, to nobody. Part of what the player gives him is an audience that claps.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** a real show, in a real tent.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A goblin circus comes through Cinderwharf, a real one with a tent, and the ringmaster says she'll give Emiliano the main slot if he can bring her
   something nobody's ever seen: a fire act with real dragonfire in it.
2. You help him go somewhere dangerous to study a dragonkin's flame, closely enough to learn it.
3. He learns it. On the night, the tent's full.

**Ending (happy):** he walks out into the ring and breathes a dragon's fire, gold and scarlet, in the shape of a dragon, and the tent screams. In the front
row are a woman and four young women, cheering louder than anyone. One of his sisters saw a circus poster with his name on it in a camp down the coast and
dragged the whole family there. His mother says he should've been careful. He says he was. He joins the circus, with his family in the wagon behind.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Emiliano's Lamp Oil Bottle.** The plastic bottle of lamp oil he carried on his belt at the traffic lights in Oaxaca, its label nearly worn off. He gives it
to you after the circus. He says he breathes his own fire now, and this is what he used when he was just a kid at a red light.

## Voice notes

- Fast, cocky and charming. Contractions, showman's patter, and Mexican Spanish: "órale", "¡qué onda!", "carnal", "chido".
- He talks like he's always performing.
- He shows off his unburned hands.
- He swears cheerfully in Spanish when a trick goes wrong, which is rarely.
- **He won't talk about** his father until Companion, or the auditions until Friend.
- **Habits:** juggles flame, performs for any audience, sends money on every ship, and shows people his hands.
