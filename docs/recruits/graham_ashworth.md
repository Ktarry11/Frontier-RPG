## Who he is

Graham is gentle. Nineteen years of sitting with people at the end of their lives taught him that a slow ending can be a kind one if somebody pays
attention.

He's tired, in the deep way of someone who's done a lot of night shifts, and he's very good company despite it. He has a dry, warm Lancashire humour.

Affliction came to him easily, and that bothered him more than anything else here, until he worked out it's the same patience pointed the other way.
He sat with it a long while before he used it.

He's steady around death, and kind about it. He's the person you want in the room when it's bad.

He misses his husband constantly and talks about him easily. Tom sang in the car whether Graham liked it or not.

What annoys him: cruelty to the dying, people who rush the end, and being called an angel. What he loves: a garden, a proper cup of tea, Tom's terrible
singing, a quiet night, and the moment someone who's frightened stops being frightened.

## Before

He grew up in Blackpool, the son of a bus driver and a seaside café owner. He trained as a nurse at 22 and went into hospice care, at a hospice outside
Preston, and stayed nineteen years: night shifts, families in corridors, and the long quiet hours when someone's going.

He met Tom at 27, at a friend's karaoke night where Tom sang very badly and very loudly. They married at 33. Tom taught music at a primary school and sang
in the car on every journey. They had a terraced house with a garden Graham never had time for.

He wore a nurse's fob watch pinned upside down on his uniform, for taking pulses. Tom gave it to him the year they married.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on a night shift, sitting with a dying man, when it happened. He landed in the Plaguelands with the fob watch still pinned to his chest.

He spent the first year nursing whoever needed it, and the second year learning why the Forsaken didn't.

He's looking for Tom. He goes through the lists at every town he passes, and he hasn't missed one yet.

## Now

He's at the Pale Watch, where the plague comes up close and slow, which he understands better than most. He sits with the dying here too, living and
otherwise.

The watch pinned upside down on his chest still runs. He winds it every morning before anything else. He'd like a garden, and the Pale Watch has a corner
of ground he's been eyeing.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find Tom.
- **Hope:** a garden, finally.
- **Dream:** a drive to Blackpool with Tom singing in the car, badly.
- **Fear:** that his gift for slow endings has become something cruel.
- **Fear:** that Tom is somewhere frightened and alone.
- **Fear:** a quiet car.

## How he's changed

In Lancashire he was a gentle hospice nurse, a happy husband, and a man too tired for his garden.

Here he's learned a dark art that uses the same patience, and he's carried it with as much kindness as he can. He's more tired, and braver. Part of what
the player gives him is someone to sit with him for once.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find Tom.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A traveller at the Pale Watch mentions a choir of transplants at the Bulwark, led by an Englishman who sings terribly loudly and makes everyone join in.
2. The road to the Bulwark runs through plague country, and a patrol you meet on it is dying slowly of something nobody can treat. Graham stays with
   them until the end, and you guard him while he does.
3. You reach the Bulwark at dusk, and you hear singing.

**Ending (happy):** it's Tom, leading a choir of transplant soldiers in a chapel, badly and loudly, exactly as Graham remembers. Tom sees him at the back
and stops in the middle of a verse. Then he finishes the verse, because the choir needs him to, and walks down the aisle. Graham can't speak. Tom says,
"You took your time, love." They plant the garden at the Pale Watch together, and Tom sings to the seedlings.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Graham's Fob Watch.** The nurse's fob watch Tom gave him the year they married, pinned upside down on his chest through nineteen years of night shifts
and three years in the Plaguelands. He gives it to you after the Bulwark. He says Tom's given him a new one, and this one kept time while he waited.

## Voice notes

- Gentle, warm and tired. Contractions, Lancashire speech ("love", "pet", "nowt", "summat"), and dry humour.
- He talks people through frightening things in a calm, slow voice.
- He talks about Tom easily and fondly, in the present tense.
- He swears mildly, northern-style, when someone's cruel.
- **He won't talk about** the patients he lost until Friend, or the fear about his magic until Companion.
- **Habits:** winds the watch every morning, sits people down, makes tea, and takes everyone's pulse without thinking.
