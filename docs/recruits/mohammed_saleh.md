## Who he is

Mohammed is a host. He remembers every guest's name and how they take their tea, and he never lets anyone leave without a second glass.

He's warm and talkative, and very funny, in a gentle, rambling way. His stories go on, and they're worth it.

He's hopeless with numbers and books. His sister did the accounts, and still does.

He's curious more than frightened. The dreams frightened him until Farseer Thrag told him they were true. After that they mostly made him curious.

He keeps people fed and rested, and that's what held him together through all of it.

What annoys him: guests who leave without saying goodbye, rudeness to staff, and being made to do the laundry. What he loves: sweet tea with sage, a
breakfast on the roof looking at Sinai, the Red Sea's coral, his sister, and the moment a tired guest relaxes.

## Before

He grew up in Aqaba, by the Red Sea, the son of a fisherman and a cook. He and his younger sister Layla opened a guesthouse at 29: twelve rooms over a dive
shop, a rooftop where the guests ate breakfast looking across the water at Sinai, and Layla doing the books because he was hopeless at them.

He made the tea himself, every morning. He remembered every diver who'd ever stayed, and they came back for years. He never married. He says the guesthouse
was his family, and Layla was enough sister for anyone.

He locked the front door every night with a heavy brass key, and checked it twice.

## The Integration and what it cost

He and Layla were on the roof, laying out breakfast, when it happened. They landed together in Azshara, with the guesthouse's brass key on a cord round his
neck.

He took the first job going, keeping the inn at Talrendis Landing, and Layla took over the books. Then the dreams started: the same white street running into
the sea, every night, with the queen's court walking it. He sees a little way along time now, backward and forward.

They came through together, which makes him luckier than almost anyone, and he knows it.

## Now

He's by the inn door at Talrendis Landing, with a glass of tea going cold beside him. Layla keeps the inn now, and he keeps the dreams. Lately the dreams are
the busier job.

The brass key round his neck is the guesthouse's front door key. He still locks up at night, somewhere in his head.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to walk the Drowned Promenade awake, once, and see if it looks the way it does at night.
- **Hope:** to keep Layla safe.
- **Dream:** breakfast on the roof in Aqaba, with a full house of divers.
- **Fear:** that the dreams will show him something he can't stop.
- **Fear:** losing Layla, the only family he has.
- **Fear:** the dreams taking over his days.

## How he's changed

In Aqaba he was a cheerful innkeeper with his sister and a full house of guests.

Here he's still an innkeeper, with his sister, which has kept him whole. The dreams have made him strange and curious and braver. Part of what the player
gives him is a guest who listens to the dreams.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to walk the Drowned Promenade awake.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The dream changes. The white street is the same, but now a group of travellers is walking it, and they don't come back. He's sure it's going to happen.
2. He goes to the Drowned Promenade, awake, for the first time, with you, to find them.
3. It looks exactly as it does in the dream. You find the travellers trapped, and get them out, because he knew where to look.

**Ending (happy):** they're a family of Jordanian divers who stayed at his guesthouse four years ago, and they remember his tea. He brings them back to
Talrendis Landing, and Layla puts them up, and he makes them breakfast. That night he dreams of the white street again, and it's empty, and quiet, and he
sleeps through till morning for the first time in two years. He says the dreams are just dreams now, mostly, and he'll help Layla with the laundry.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**The Guesthouse Key.** The heavy brass front-door key from his guesthouse in Aqaba, worn on a cord round his neck for three years. He still locked up with
it in his head every night. He gives it to you after the Promenade. He says he's got an inn here with its own door, and you're welcome at it any time.

## Voice notes

- Warm, chatty and rambling. Contractions, long stories, and Jordanian Arabic: "ahlan wa sahlan" (welcome), "tfaddal" (please, go ahead), "habibi".
- He offers tea before anything else.
- He tells his dreams like stories over breakfast.
- He doesn't swear. When something's rude he says "ayb" (shameful), and means it.
- **He won't talk about** the dreams' worst nights until Friend, or his parents until Companion.
- **Habits:** makes tea for everyone, remembers how everyone takes it, touches the key at his neck, and naps in the afternoons.
