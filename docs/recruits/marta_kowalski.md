## Who she is

Marta can tell what's wrong with a machine from the sound. For nineteen years she was the only person in the building
who could, and management called her whenever a line stopped. They never once called her anything but her first name.

She's blunt. She tells you you've been hitting it, because people always hit it. She doesn't soften anything, and she
doesn't expect you to soften anything for her.

She's proud of her work and a little embarrassed by how much she loves goblin engineering. It's the same noise as the
sewing floor, with more smoke, and she loves it in a way she'd be embarrassed to have written down.

She's tough. She worked her way up from the sewing line and raised two boys on shift work. She doesn't scare easily.

She puts things off when they matter. She's been quoted a price for a pitch of her own, she can afford it, and she's
found four reasons to wait. She knows all four are the same reason.

What annoys her: people who hit machines, management, being called "love", and anyone who calls a fix "good enough".
What she loves: a machine that runs sweet, a strong coffee, her boys, and her husband's terrible singing.

## Before

She grew up in Łódź, a textile city, in a flat near the old mills. Her mother sewed shirts for thirty years and her
father drove a bus. Marta started on the sewing line at 19, and at 32 she moved to maintenance because she kept fixing
the machines herself and someone finally noticed.

She married Tomasz, an electrician, at 24. For fifteen years he worked the other shift, so one of them was always home
with Kuba and Piotr. They saw each other in the doorway twice a day: a kiss, a handover, a list of what the boys needed.
It worked. Tomasz sang badly in the kitchen every morning and she pretended to hate it.

The boys grew up and left: Kuba to Gdańsk, Piotr to Berlin. She and Tomasz had finally started working the same shift.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was at the plant, under a broken machine, when it happened. Tomasz was on his way to work. She landed in the Barrens
with a little brass oil can in her overall pocket.

It took her three weeks, and she'd argue they were mostly about the language. A machine's a machine. She was fixing
goblin gear for money before she could ask where the toilet was.

She doesn't know where Tomasz landed, or the boys. She's checked the Polish lists every season. She hasn't found any of
them.

## Now

She works in the Boulder Lode Mine in the Northern Barrens, keeping the pumps, the carts and the drills going. Everything
there is broken and the goblins charge for the privilege of fixing it. She undercuts them by a third and fixes it
properly. A goblin foreman has threatened her twice and sent her work three times.

She wants her own bench, her own sign, and nobody above her on a floor plan.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** her own bench, her own sign.
- **Hope:** that Tomasz and the boys are together.
- **Dream:** a morning in the kitchen with Tomasz singing badly.
- **Fear:** setting up a life here and Tomasz turning up to find she didn't wait.
- **Fear:** a machine she can't hear.
- **Fear:** being nobody but a first name again.

## How she's changed

In Łódź she was a respected mechanic who was never respected properly, and a wife who saw her husband in a doorway.

Here she's freer, louder and better paid. She's also been waiting three years without admitting it. Part of what the
player gives her is somebody who'll tell her it's all right to stop waiting.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to stop waiting, without giving up.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The mine's main pump is failing, deep down, and the goblins have given up on it. She says she can fix it if somebody
   clears the lower levels first. You go down with her.
2. She fixes it. The goblin foreman offers her the workshop by the pumps, her own bench, with her name over it.
3. She asks you to come with her to every hub she can reach, one more time, for Tomasz and the boys.

**Ending (still unknown):** there's nothing on any list: not Tomasz, not Kuba, not Piotr. She stands at the last hub for a
long time. Then she says Tomasz always sang in the kitchen and he'd sing in any kitchen, wherever he is, and she's going
to stop waiting at the door. She takes the workshop. The sign says "M. Kowalska, Mechanic", her full name. She leaves a
note at every hub saying where she is. She still listens for singing.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Marta's Oil Can.** A small brass oil can, dented and shiny from her hand, that she carried in her overall pocket on the
sewing floor for nineteen years. It was in her pocket when she landed. She gives it to you once her sign is up. She says
she's got a whole workshop of oil now.

## Voice notes

- Blunt, quick and impatient. Contractions, short orders, and no small talk.
- Her English is functional and direct, with a Polish accent and Polish swearing.
- She talks to machines more kindly than to people.
- She swears in Polish freely, especially "kurwa", and never apologises.
- **She won't talk about** Tomasz until Companion, or the boys until Friend.
- **Habits:** listens to every machine she passes, oils hinges without asking, wipes her hands constantly, and waits by
  doors.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)
