## Who he is

Joaquín listens. He was good with the difficult kids because he listened to them, and in the Crusade they made him an interrogator for the same reason.
He was good at it, in a way he'll be sorry for until he dies.

He's gentle, thoughtful and quietly funny, with a teacher's patience. He's the kind of man people tell things to.

He carries what he did without asking anyone to carry it for him. He doesn't want absolution. He wants to say sorry to the right people.

He heals with the same hands he used in the cells, and he fights with them too. He thinks that's fitting.

He's honest about himself to the point of pain. He says he adjusted too well, and that's the whole problem.

What annoys him: cruelty, certainty without doubt, and people who want to forgive him on behalf of others. What he loves: mate in the morning, a good
history book, the novel he's been writing for twelve years, football, and a classroom of difficult teenagers.

## Before

He grew up in Rosario, the son of a railway worker and a librarian. He studied history and taught fifteen-year-olds at a state secondary school for
fourteen years: a classroom, a mate gourd on his desk, and a novel he'd been writing for nine years and never finished.

He was good with the difficult kids, because he listened to them. He married Laura, a teacher, at 27; they divorced at 34, without children, because
she wanted to move to Buenos Aires and he wouldn't leave his students.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in the classroom, mid-lesson, when it happened. He landed in the Plaguelands with the mate gourd from his desk in his satchel.

The Scarlet Crusade took him in during the first winter. They made him an interrogator, because he was patient and he listened. He told himself he was
getting information that saved lives. He was breaking people.

Rory Callahan found him in a cell in Hearthglen at four in the morning, sitting with a prisoner he'd been told to break, not breaking him. They walked out
together, that same night.

## Now

He's at the Bulwark with the Order, healing the hurt that come in from the Plaguelands.

He's writing down the names of everyone he questioned. He wants to find the ones who lived and say he's sorry to their faces. He's found four so far. Two
of them hit him, and he says they were right to.

The mate gourd at his hip is the one from his classroom desk, and he still drinks from it every morning.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to apologise to every person on his list who's still alive.
- **Hope:** to finish his novel, someday, about something else.
- **Dream:** a classroom of difficult teenagers, and nothing to apologise for.
- **Fear:** the names of people who didn't live.
- **Fear:** that he'd do it again, if the reasons sounded good enough.
- **Fear:** being forgiven too easily.

## How he's changed

In Rosario he was a kind, patient history teacher who'd taught the same lessons about how ordinary people do terrible things.

Here he became one of those ordinary people, and walked away, and he's carrying it every day. He's quieter, harder on himself, and kinder to others. Part
of what the player gives him is someone who knows what he did and stays.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find the people he questioned, and to say sorry.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The last name on his list is a boy, Nahuel, seventeen, a transplant the Crusade thought was a spy. Joaquín questioned him for two days. He doesn't
   know what happened to him after.
2. You go with him into Scarlet country to find the records, and get out with them.
3. The records say Nahuel died in the cells, a week after Joaquín was done with him. His mother is alive, in a camp near the Bulwark.

**Ending (sad):** Joaquín goes to the mother and tells her everything: who he was, what he did, and that her son died because of the Crusade and because
of him. She doesn't hit him. She doesn't forgive him either. She asks him to tell her what Nahuel said, in those two days, every word, because nobody
else can. He does, for hours. Then she asks him never to come back, and he doesn't. He crosses Nahuel off his list, and he doesn't feel any lighter, and
he says that's right.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Joaquín's Mate Gourd.** The worn leather mate gourd with a silver bombilla from his classroom desk in Rosario. He's drunk from it every morning, here,
through all of it. He gives it to you after Nahuel's mother. He says he'd like to put something down, and this is the one thing from before he isn't
ashamed of.

## Voice notes

- Gentle, thoughtful and quiet. Contractions, a teacher's careful phrasing, and Argentine Spanish: "che", "dale", "boludo" to friends.
- He listens much more than he talks, and asks good questions.
- He never asks anyone to forgive him.
- He doesn't swear much. When he's angry at himself, he goes very quiet.
- **He won't talk about** the cells until Friend, or Nahuel until the quest.
- **Habits:** drinks mate every morning, writes in his notebook of names, listens to everyone, and offers to carry other people's heavy things.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** heals steadily and fights with his fists. Very uncomfortable around Scarlet interrogations and prisoners in quests. Listens to
  quest givers closely and notices what they're not saying.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with honest, thoughtful people. Close to Bruno, who walked out beside him. He and Pavlo, the two history teachers,

- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is carrying it alone. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he takes a share of
