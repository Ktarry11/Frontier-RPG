## Who he is

Khalid is dry and extremely funny once you know him. In company he's reserved. With friends he's the one making everyone laugh without smiling
himself.

He's patient. Falconry is the waiting, the reading of ground, and the particular relationship with an animal that's choosing to come back to you.
That part of his old life transferred whole.

He's careful and competent. Walk where he walks and you'll have a very boring day, and boring is the aim.

He's more at home here than he ever admits in company. He came through into open country with weather and birds in it and no airport, and if
he's honest, the first year was the best he'd had in a decade. He says that quietly, and only to people he's decided won't misunderstand it.

He's carrying a bird. The hood at his belt is sized for Hurra, the falcon he flew for eleven years, who's been dead or alive for three years
without him knowing which.

What annoys him: noise, people who rush, people who treat animals as tools, and being called a sheikh as a joke. What he loves: a bird coming
back to the glove, strong Arabic coffee with cardamom, dates, the desert at dawn, and his family's loud gatherings.

## Before

He grew up in Al Ain, the inland oasis city, in a big family that had kept falcons for generations. His grandfather taught him to fly a bird at
eight. He worked in logistics at the airport, coordinating cargo, to pay for the falcons.

His bird was Hurra, a saker falcon he raised from a fledgling and flew for eleven years. He had a mews behind his parents' house, a glove, and
weekends in the desert. His family thought the falcons were an expensive eccentricity, and they all came out to watch anyway.

He never married. His mother had a list of suitable women and he kept politely failing to meet them. He says he was waiting for someone who'd
get up at four to fly a bird.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at the airport, at his desk, when it happened. Hurra was at home in the mews. He landed on the edge of the Shimmering Flats with
Hurra's hood in his jacket pocket, because he'd been oiling the leather on his break.

He took three months to adjust, and he has the grace to find that funny.

He hasn't found his family, and he doesn't know what happened to Hurra. He won't get another hood that size.

## Now

He lives in Saltwheel, the goblin racing town on the edge of the Flats, and he works ahead of whatever party he's with, usually invisible,
always exactly where he said he'd be.

He wants a bird. Properly trained, properly flown, not a summoned thing. He's been working on the nest of something enormous in the hills above
the Flats for two seasons, patiently and badly. It's taken food from his hand twice, and twice tried to remove the hand.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a bird of his own, that chooses to come back to him.
- **Hope:** that his family's together, loud, and fine.
- **Dream:** to fly Hurra one more time.
- **Fear:** that Hurra starved in the mews, waiting.
- **Fear:** that he's happier here than he was at home, and what that says.
- **Fear:** a bird that never comes back.

## How he's changed

In Al Ain he was a dutiful son with an airport job and a falcon, living for the weekends.

Here he's free, in open country, and it's the best he's felt in years. He's guilty about that. Part of what the player gives him is someone
who won't misunderstand it.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** a bird.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The enormous bird in the hills above the Flats has a single chick, and raiders are after the nest to sell the chick in Saltwheel. Khalid asks
   you to help guard it.
2. You hold off the raiders over several days, and the mother bird is killed defending the nest. The chick is alone.
3. Khalid takes it in. He raises it the way he raised Hurra, patiently, for a season.

**Ending (happy):** on the first real flight, the young bird goes up over the Shimmering Flats, higher than any falcon he ever flew, and it's gone
for a long time. Khalid stands with his glove up. Then it comes back, and lands on his arm, and chooses him. He names her Hurra, for the other
one, because he says it means "free", and they both are. That night he tells you, quietly, that it's been the best three years of his life, and
he doesn't apologise for saying it.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Hurra's Hood.** A small tooled leather falconer's hood, sized for a saker falcon, oiled on his break at the airport the day the worlds joined.
It's sized for a bird that's been dead or alive for three years without him knowing which. The new Hurra is far too big for it. He gives it to you
after her first flight. He says the first Hurra would want it to go somewhere quiet.

## Voice notes

- Dry, reserved and very funny. Contractions, deadpan jokes, and "boring is the aim".
- His English is fluent and careful, with Emirati Arabic: "yalla", "inshallah", "mashallah", "habibi", and "wallah" when he's serious.
- He talks about people the way he talks about birds: whether they'll come back.
- He doesn't swear. When something's foolish he says "astaghfirullah" under his breath.
- **He won't talk about** Hurra until Companion, or how happy he is here until Friend.
- **Habits:** scouts ahead invisibly, makes coffee at dawn, touches the hood at his belt, and watches the sky for birds.
