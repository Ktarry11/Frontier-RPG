## Who she is

Fiona notices the one entry that doesn't fit, and then she's unbearable about it until somebody checks. She was very unpopular and quite
often right.

She's blunt, dry and relentless. She doesn't do charm. She does evidence.

She catalogues everything. Her first year here was a filing exercise: she catalogued every corrupted thing she met, and the notebook is now
more use than she is.

She's honest about her own blind spots. In her second year she worked out she'd been treating a war as a casework backlog, and that one
landed hard.

She's lonely and proud and doesn't trust easily. She carries all her files on her back because she won't leave a copy anywhere she can't see.

What annoys her: people who don't check, people who act without evidence, and being told she's "a lot". What she loves: a clean case, strong
tea, a dry joke, the sea, and the moment somebody finally checks and she was right.

## Before

She grew up in Aberdeen, the oil city, the daughter of a rig worker and a school secretary. Her brother Graeme went offshore like their father.
Fiona went to university, studied accountancy, and became a fraud investigator for one of the big energy companies.

For nine years she investigated fraud: padded invoices, ghost contractors, and safety records that had been cooked. She flew out to rigs in the
North Sea by helicopter, in an orange survival suit with a yellow whistle on the collar, and she interviewed men who didn't want to talk to her.
The last five years she was based in Dublin.

She found a cooked safety record once, on a rig where Graeme worked. She reported it, the rig was shut for a month, and half the crew never
forgave her. Graeme did, eventually.

She never married. She says nobody ever passed the background check.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was on a helicopter out to a rig, in her survival suit, when it happened. She landed on a Stranglethorn beach with the suit's yellow whistle
still clipped to her collar.

She hasn't found her mother or Graeme.

## Now

She works Southern Stranglethorn, out of Booty Bay. People are being taken from the coast villages, and the thing that hunts them is a cult in
the jungle that leaves a trail of the same shape as a cooked ledger: too neat, and neat in the places nobody looks.

She's mapping it. Three hundred pages, cross-referenced, and she carries the whole thing on her back.

She wants to finish the catalogue and hand it to somebody who'll act on it. Both halves matter, and the second is the one she keeps failing at.
She's had the same conversation with the Booty Bay authorities four times. She takes minutes of those too.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to finish the catalogue, and have somebody act on it.
- **Hope:** that her mother and Graeme are safe.
- **Dream:** a walk on Aberdeen beach in the wind, with nothing to investigate.
- **Fear:** being right and ignored.
- **Fear:** her evidence being used wrongly.
- **Fear:** that she treats people as cases.

## How she's changed

In Scotland and Dublin she was a feared, lonely investigator who was usually right and rarely thanked.

Here she's a hunter of something far worse than fraud, and she's had to learn that a war isn't a backlog. She's still blunt and relentless.
Part of what the player gives her is someone who checks when she asks them to.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to have her catalogue acted on.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. She finishes the catalogue and it points to the cult's hiding place. You help her prove it, gathering the last evidence from the jungle.
2. She takes it to the Booty Bay authorities, and for the fifth time, with you beside her, they finally listen.
3. They act fast and hard.

**Ending (sad):** too fast and too hard. They use her catalogue as a list and raid a village her notes had flagged as "pattern consistent,
unconfirmed", and innocent fishermen die alongside the cult. Her own evidence, used without the care she put into it. She reads the report and
burns her copy of the catalogue on the beach. Then she starts again, from page one, slower, with every "unconfirmed" in red, and she goes to
every raid herself after that so nobody acts on her work without her there. She says being right was never the hard part.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Fiona's Survival Whistle.** The yellow whistle from her orange helicopter survival suit, still clipped to her collar on a fraying lanyard. She
was flying out to a North Sea rig when the worlds joined. She gives it to you after the beach. She says she's not going to drown any time soon,
and you might need someone to hear you.

## Voice notes

- Blunt, dry and Scottish. Contractions, short sentences, and "aye", "wee", "och" and "dinnae".
- She speaks like an investigator: facts first, conclusions after, and "I'll need that in writing".
- She doesn't do small talk.
- She swears precisely and Scottishly.
- **She won't talk about** Graeme until Companion, or the rig she shut down until Friend.
- **Habits:** takes notes in every conversation, carries her files, checks everyone's story twice, and touches the whistle when she's tense.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** stands in front, blunt and immovable. Questions every quest giver's story. Notices inconsistencies in dungeons.
  Takes notes after every fight.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with careful, honest people, especially Henrik and Kourosh. Clashes with people who act without thinking.
  Secretly enjoys being argued with properly.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is evidence used without care. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, she
  exposes an enemy, stripping its protections, and the party's damage against it is much higher.
