## Who he is

Diego is happy. Properly happy, most of the time, and he doesn't apologise for it. Of everyone who came through he's
one of the few who'll say plainly that this world has been good to him.

He's fast. His hands are faster than his mouth and his mouth is pretty fast. He can take a thing apart before he can
tell you why, and he can hear what's wrong with a machine the way some people can hear a wrong note.

He's friendly to everyone, including the Forsaken, who don't know what to do with him. He calls everybody "my
friend", he talks with his hands full of screws, and he hums when he works, badly.

He's stubborn in a cheerful way. Nobody has ever talked him out of anything. He won't argue with you. He'll just smile,
say "sure, sure," and do it his way anyway.

He's younger than he acts in some ways and older in others. He looked after his mother on his own for four years while
his sisters were abroad, and that made him grown up about some things very early. It also meant he never got to be
twenty-two in the normal way.

What annoys him: people who throw things away that could be fixed, people who say "it can't be done" before they've
looked, and being called a kid. What he loves: a broken machine, a full plate, loud music, and the moment something
that didn't work starts working.

## Before

He grew up in Maracaibo, the youngest of five and the only boy. His father left when he was six. His mother cleaned
offices at night and his sisters raised him as much as she did.

When he was seven his mother bought him a wind-up tin beetle from a market stall. He took it apart the same afternoon
to see how it walked, and he couldn't get it back together. He cried about it for a whole day. His mother told him
he'd fix it when he was older. He kept the pieces in a tin for years.

At 15 he left school and started at a phone-repair kiosk in a mall where the power went off twice a day. Everybody
learned to keep working in the dark. He was the best repairman in the mall within a year, and people queued for him
by name.

The country was falling apart. His sisters left one by one: Carla to Bogotá, Mariela to Madrid, Andrea to Santiago and
Luisa to Miami. Each of them told him to come too. He was the last one home, so he stayed and looked after their
mother when she got sick. She died when he was 22. His sisters couldn't get back for the funeral.

When the Integration came eight months later, he had nobody left in the country to lose. He's said that out loud
exactly once, and then he changed the subject to a gearbox.

## The Integration and what it cost

He landed in Deathknell among the Forsaken, with his phone-repair spudger in his shirt pocket. It's a little blue
plastic prying tool, worn white at the tip.

He was frightened for about a week. Then a goblin trader handed him a broken pressure valve to see what he'd do with
it, which is how goblins ask a question. He had it working before the goblin finished talking. He's been paid in parts
ever since, and he spent the next two weeks delighted.

It didn't cost him much, and he knows how that sounds. What it did take was any chance of seeing his sisters again.
He hasn't looked for them on the lists. They were in four different countries, and he doesn't know which ones came
through. He tells people he's fine with not knowing. He isn't quite.

## Now

He goes wherever there's broken machinery: goblin camps, Forsaken workshops, mining sites, anywhere a thing has stopped
working and somebody's willing to let him near it.

He has a half-built clockwork beetle that's been nearly finished for eleven months. Everybody who's travelled with him
has been shown it. Nobody has seen it move. He doesn't tell anybody that it's the tin beetle from the market, rebuilt
bigger and better, and that he can't bring himself to finish it.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a workshop with his name over the door. He'll tell you the exact sign he wants: two colours, with the
  letters slightly crooked, because he likes it that way.
- **Hope:** that at least one of his sisters came through and is all right.
- **Dream:** to finish the beetle and watch it walk.
- **Fear:** that he's happy here because he never let himself be sad about his mother.
- **Fear:** being alone in a room that's gone quiet. He fills every silence because of it.
- **Fear:** a machine he can't fix.

## How he's changed

In Maracaibo he was the boy who stayed. He fixed phones all day and looked after his mother all night, and he didn't
have a life of his own.

Here he's free for the first time. He's cheerful, curious and busy, and he makes friends everywhere he goes. The grief
he never had time for is still waiting. He keeps it in the beetle. Part of what the player gives him is somebody he
doesn't have to be cheerful for.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to finish the beetle, and to open his own workshop.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He finally tells you what the beetle is. It needs a heart: a mainspring that won't wear out, and there's nothing in
   the goblin markets good enough.
2. You help him get the metal from deep in a dangerous mine, and the core from a broken old machine guarded by the
   things that broke it.
3. He builds the heart and winds it.

**Ending (happy):** the beetle walks across the table, turns round and walks back. He laughs and then he cries, for a
long time, about his mother, for the first time. A few weeks later he opens a workshop in Brill, under a sign in two
colours with the letters slightly crooked. A letter turns up there one day from Luisa, who saw his name on a trader's
list of workshops. She came through and she's in Ratchet.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Diego's Blue Spudger.** The little blue plastic tool he used to open phones at the kiosk in Maracaibo, worn white at
the tip. It was in his shirt pocket the day the worlds joined. He gives it to you and says he's got proper tools now,
but this was the first one.

## Voice notes

- Fast, warm and chatty. Contractions everywhere, and he jumps from one thought to the next.
- He calls everyone "my friend" and he means it.
- His English is good and fast, with Spanish word order and the odd Spanish word when he's excited. He says "sure,
  sure" when he's going to ignore you.
- He talks about machines like they're people. "She's tired", "he's being difficult".
- He doesn't swear in front of people he's just met. With friends, "coño" when something breaks.
- **He won't talk about** his mother until Companion, the tin beetle until Friend, or the sisters unless you ask
  twice.
- **Habits:** hums while he works, taps machines to listen to them, takes apart anything left unattended, and always
  has a screw or two in his mouth.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** stops to look at every machine and trap in a dungeon. Cheerful even after a wipe. Wants to
  know how every boss's armour is put together. Hates killing things for no reason but loves taking constructs apart.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with nearly everyone. Bonds fast with other makers and with loud, funny people. A bit
  much for quiet people at first, who usually warm to him.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the grief he kept in the beetle. A skill idea to look at then: once a
  fight, he puts a fallen ally's broken gear right and they get back up with a shield.
