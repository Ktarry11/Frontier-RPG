## Who he is

Karim is self-contained. Fifteen years of standing in empty country deciding where the lines went, mostly alone and entirely content with it.

He's cold on first meeting and very dry once he decides you're staying. He tests people by being blunt and sees who stays.

He took the fel deliberately, after consideration, as the fastest route to being useful in a fight. He'll defend that choice in detail to anybody
who raises it, and he doesn't apologise for the green light any more.

He's honest in a way people find uncomfortable. He says having nobody to grieve made all this easy, and that he knows how that sounds, and that
it's still true.

He's tired of people being sad on his behalf.

What annoys him: pity, imprecision, people who won't ask the question they're thinking, and maps drawn badly. What he loves: empty country, a
true line, black coffee with cardamom, silence, and the moment a survey closes perfectly.

## Before

He grew up in Amman, the second son of an engineer and a schoolteacher. He studied surveying and spent fifteen years in the field: a tripod, a
plumb bob, and weeks at a time in country with nobody in it. He loved it.

He never married. He says it was by preference rather than circumstance, and he means it. His father died when he was 20 and his mother three
years before the Integration.

His older brother Sami moved to Canada in 2011, to Toronto, for work. They called every week at first, then every month, then on birthdays, then
not at all. Karim never decided to stop. It just happened, the way it does.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in the desert east of Amman, surveying, when it happened. He landed on the Shimmering Flats, which looked to him like the most beautiful
empty country he'd ever seen, with his plumb bob in his hand.

He treated it as a survey: a new country, an unmapped one. He was functioning inside a month. He says having nobody to grieve made it easy.

## Now

He holds ground at the front of any party, where the green light makes the people behind him uncomfortable. He lives in the Undercrust, the
cave country beneath the Shimmering Flats.

He's making a map, the real one, properly surveyed, of where all the transit hubs came down and which places on Earth they took people from,
because the hubs make no sense and nobody competent has looked. He's four years into a job that needs twenty, and he says so without minding.

The plumb bob still hangs at his hip. He uses it. The party's learned that when he stops and lets it swing, something ahead is wrong.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to finish the map of the hubs.
- **Hope:** he says he has none about people.
- **Dream:** a true line across a whole continent.
- **Fear:** being pitied.
- **Fear:** the fel taking the parts of him he likes.
- **Fear:** that "nobody to grieve" was a story he told himself.

## How he's changed

In Jordan he was a contented loner who'd let his brother drift away without noticing.

Here he's become formidable, and he's told himself the fel was purely practical. He's warmer with the few people he lets stay. Part of what the
player gives him is somebody who asks about the eyes.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to finish surveying the hubs.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He's worked out roughly where the hub that took people from Amman came down, and he needs to survey it. It's in dangerous, empty country, and
   he asks you to come.
2. You reach it. It's in a place where almost nobody could have survived the landing. There's a camp of survivors nearby, small, with a list.
3. He reads the list, for completeness. Near the bottom, among the dead, is a name he didn't expect: Sami Haddad.

**Ending (sad):** Sami had moved back to Amman the year before the Integration. Karim never knew, because they'd stopped calling. His brother
was in the same city, and he didn't know. Karim stands with the list for a long time, and his plumb bob doesn't swing. Then he says, very flatly,
that it turns out he did have somebody to grieve. He finishes the survey of that hub with great care. He marks it on the map with his brother's
name. He doesn't say anything else about it, and he doesn't pretend it was easy any more.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Karim's Plumb Bob.** A brass surveyor's plumb bob on a worn cord, the one he used in the desert east of Amman and in his hand when he landed. It
swings when something ahead is wrong. He gives it to you after the list. He says he knows where he stands now, and you should have something that
always points straight down.

## Voice notes

- Flat, dry and precise. Contractions, short sentences, and no softening.
- His English is excellent and formal, with Jordanian Arabic: "yalla", "khalas", "inshallah".
- He asks you to ask the question.
- He doesn't swear. When something's wrong he goes quiet and lets the plumb bob swing.
- **He won't talk about** Sami until Friend, or the fel's cost until Companion.
- **Habits:** lets the plumb bob swing at doorways, maps every place he goes, measures distances by eye, and stands in front.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** holds the front. Warns the party when the plumb bob swings. Maps every dungeon. Blunt about everyone's choices,
  including his own.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with precise, honest people, especially Henrik, Kourosh and Fiona. Has a lot in common with Ziad, the other
  Felsworn, and they argue about the fel constantly. Dislikes pity.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is "nobody to grieve". A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he plants
  himself, and every enemy is pulled to him while his armour holds.
