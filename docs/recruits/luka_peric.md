## Who he is

Luka knows the sea well enough to be properly afraid of it. Fifteen years of car ferries across the Adriatic taught him to respect weather, read it early
and never show the passengers he was worried.

He's wry and weathered, with a dry Dalmatian humour. He's the steadiest person in any room, and he knows exactly how much of that is practice.

He's careful with the storm. The first storm he called up nearly drowned him, and he says that's how he knew it was his. He calls it carefully now, the way
he used to read the weather off the sea.

He's a family man who misses his family like a missing limb, and makes jokes about it.

He keeps a ritual: every evening, at the time the last ferry used to leave Split, he blows the ship's whistle once.

What annoys him: people who don't respect the sea, panic on deck, and bad grilled fish. What he loves: the Adriatic at dawn, Ivana's konoba, his son's
terrible water polo, a glass of wine with the crew, and the moment a rough crossing ends in a calm harbour.

## Before

He grew up in Split, the son of a fisherman. He went to sea at 18, worked his way up on the ferries, and captained the Marjan, a car ferry, for fifteen
years: a crew of eight and the run between Split and the islands, in every weather.

He married Ivana, whose family ran a konoba on Brač, at 25. He met her on a crossing in a storm, when she was the only passenger who wasn't seasick. Their
son Marko played water polo, badly, and Luka never missed a match.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on the bridge of the Marjan, mid-crossing, when it happened. He woke on a beach with the ferry's brass whistle in his hand and no ferry.

The first storm he called up nearly drowned him. That's how he knew it was his.

He hasn't found Ivana or Marko. They were on Brač; he was on the water between.

## Now

He's on the Tempest Shallows, near the landing, watching the sky. He's teaching the Ashclaim's crews to read the weather before it reads them.

He wants a boat: a small one, sound and his own. He'd take it home if he knew which way home was.

The brass whistle on his lanyard is from the Marjan's bridge, and he blows it once every evening at the time the last ferry used to leave.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a boat of his own.
- **Hope:** to find Ivana and Marko.
- **Dream:** an evening at Ivana's konoba on Brač, watching the last ferry leave, with nowhere to be.
- **Fear:** the storm getting away from him.
- **Fear:** that his passengers didn't make it, the morning the Marjan disappeared.
- **Fear:** that his family thinks the sea took him.

## How he's changed

In Split he was a steady ferry captain, a happy husband, and the loudest dad at every water polo match.

Here he's become the storm he spent his life steering around. He's still steady and wry. He's lonelier. Part of what the player gives him is a crew.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** a boat, and to find his family.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The Ashclaim offer him a small boat if he'll bring a supply run through a storm that's pinned their ships in harbour for weeks. He takes it.
2. You crew for him. He calls the storm down around the boat and steers through the middle of it, the way nobody else could.
3. On the far side, a merchant ship has been battered and is sinking. He gets alongside and takes the crew aboard.

**Ending (happy):** the youngest sailor on the sinking ship, a tall, clumsy young man who swims like a terrible water polo player, is Marko. He's been
crewing merchant ships for two years, looking for his father on every coast, because his mother told him that if Luka was alive, he'd be near the sea.
Ivana runs a little konoba in a Croatian camp on the coast, and she's been keeping a table by the window for him. Luka gets his boat. He sails it there.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**The Marjan's Whistle.** The brass ship's whistle from the bridge of his ferry, the Marjan, in his hand when he woke on the beach. He blew it every evening
at the time the last ferry left Split. He gives it to you after Ivana's konoba. He says he doesn't need to call the last ferry home any more. He's home.

## Voice notes

- Dry, weathered and calm. Contractions, sea terms, and Dalmatian Croatian: "ajme", "polako" (slowly), "bura" for a hard wind.
- He reads the weather out loud.
- He jokes about the sea and never quite means it.
- He swears in Croatian at the weather, constantly.
- **He won't talk about** the Marjan's passengers until Friend, or Ivana until Companion.
- **Habits:** watches the sky, blows the whistle every evening, checks every boat's lines, and stands at the front when the wind changes.
