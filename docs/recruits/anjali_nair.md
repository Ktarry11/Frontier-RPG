## Who she is

Anjali is patient. Three decades of patience with slow things, and with students who wanted results by Friday. A plant will
tell you what it needs if you're willing to wait several seasons for the sentence to finish.

She's gentle and firm. She never raises her voice, and she's never had to.

She's precise and a little old-fashioned. She labels everything in her own hand. She corrects people's Latin.

She's cheerful in a deep, steady way. She's been through grief and come out the other side, and she has no patience for
self-pity, including her own.

She's quietly ambitious. She'd like to be read in two hundred years, and she says that cheerfully and means it completely.

What annoys her: rushing, carelessness with living things, and students who won't wait. What she loves: a good herbarium,
monsoon rain, filter coffee, her granddaughter, and the moment a plant does what she said it would.

## Before

She grew up in Pune, in a Malayali family who'd moved from Kerala. Her father was an engineer and her mother a schoolteacher
who kept a garden full of plants from home. Anjali spent her childhood in that garden.

She studied botany and taught at a university in Pune for 31 years: a department, a herbarium, and four decades of pressed
specimens labelled in her own hand. Her students were frightened of her and loved her.

She married Suresh, an accountant, at 25. He retired in 2018 and spent two years teaching himself to cook. She had one year
of that. He died of a heart attack in 2019. She describes that year as the best of their marriage, in a voice that invites no
follow-up.

Their son Arjun lives in Bangalore with his wife and daughter, Meera, who was four at the Integration.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was in the herbarium, pressing a specimen, when it happened. She landed in Ashenvale with her hand lens on a cord round
her neck.

She took it slowly, which suited her. She treated the whole first year as a field season and kept a notebook in the way she'd
kept forty of them.

She hasn't found Arjun or Meera.

## Now

She lives in Ashenvale West, where the forest is sick in a patterned way and the night elves know it in their bones without
ever having written it down. She's writing it down. The Sentinels were suspicious for two years and now bring her specimens.

Her notebook is the only systematic record of Ashenvale's understory that exists in any language, and she regards it as a
draft.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to be read in two hundred years. That means a copy somewhere safe, in a language that'll survive, and a student
  who cares.
- **Hope:** that Arjun and Meera are safe.
- **Dream:** to show Meera the forest, and teach her the names.
- **Fear:** her work being lost.
- **Fear:** dying before the notebook's finished.
- **Fear:** that her granddaughter is growing up without knowing her.

## How she's changed

In Pune she was a respected, strict professor and, for one good year, a happy wife, and then a widow.

Here she's found the first place where her patience is a power. She's freer, braver and more adventurous than she ever was.
Part of what the player gives her is someone to teach.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** a safe copy of her notebook, and a student.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The sickness in the forest is spreading faster, and she needs specimens from the worst of it to finish the record. You go
   with her into the corrupted groves.
2. She finishes the notebook. Then she needs a copy kept somewhere it'll survive. She's heard of a library at Kael's Bastion
   where the elves keep everything.
3. She has a student in mind, a young Sentinel called Ilyara who's been bringing specimens for a year, and who has to agree.

**Ending (happy):** the elves at Kael's Bastion take a copy for their gallery and promise to keep it. Ilyara agrees to be her
student, and on the first day she corrects Anjali's spelling of a night elf plant name, and Anjali is delighted. Anjali says
she'll be read in two hundred years now, and Ilyara will still be alive to check.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Anjali's Hand Lens.** A brass botanist's hand lens on a cord, scratched from forty years of field seasons. It was round her
neck in the herbarium when she landed. She gives it to you after Ilyara becomes her student. She says Ilyara has better eyes
than she has now, and you should look closely at things.

## Voice notes

- Gentle, precise and unhurried. Contractions, careful words, and the occasional Latin plant name.
- Her English is formal Indian English, warm and exact. She says "gently" and "slowly" a lot.
- She asks questions like a teacher: "And what do you notice?"
- She never swears. When something's careless she says "that was not well done", and you feel it.
- **She won't talk about** Suresh until Companion, or Meera until Friend.
- **Habits:** crouches to look at every plant, uses her hand lens, presses leaves in her notebook, and drinks coffee very
  sweet.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** heals patiently and steadily. Names every plant in every zone. Upset by wanton destruction of the
  forest. Calm in a wipe.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with patient, curious people, especially Amber, Kesten and Sophia. A gentle grandmother
  figure to the young. Bored by bluster.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is the fear of her work being lost. A skill idea to look at then: a passive
