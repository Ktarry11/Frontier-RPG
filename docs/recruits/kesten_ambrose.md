## Who he is

Kesten is careful. He says what he means and he means what he says, and he expects the same from you. He'll correct
you if you say "about thirty" when it was twenty-six, and he isn't doing it to score a point. He just thinks the real
number deserves to be said.

He listens much more than he talks. He remembers what you tell him, and he'll bring it up again weeks later when it
matters. People find that unsettling at first and then they find it's one of the nicest things about him.

He's curious about everything that's been written down. He stops in doorways to read inscriptions, he reads the
notices on every board in every town, and he reads the letters quest givers hand you before you've opened them, if you
let him. He keeps notebooks, always in pencil because ink runs in the damp.

He has a dry sense of humour that's only come back recently. He'll say something deadpan and wait to see if you
caught it. If you laugh, he looks pleased for the rest of the day.

He isn't proud. He doesn't care what he's wearing or what his gear looks like. He's brave in a very quiet way,
because the worst thing that can happen to him already has, and a dungeon doesn't scare him much after that.

What annoys him: carelessness, people who round numbers, people who say "whatever", and anyone who treats a person as
a number. That last one is the only thing that makes him properly angry.

## Before

He was born in Tallinn when Estonia was still part of the Soviet Union. His father drove a crane at the harbour and
his mother typed for a ministry. He remembers the 1991 independence days as a teenager standing in a crowd that was
singing, which is the most Estonian thing he can think of.

He studied history at Tartu and went to work at the city archive at 24. He stayed for 26 years. He looked after the
parish registers and the ships' passenger lists on the third floor. The registers tell you who was born, married and
buried, and the passenger lists tell you who left on a boat. He liked putting the two together and finding out what
happened to people. Families wrote to the archive asking about a great-grandfather who sailed to Canada in 1905, and
Andres was the one who'd find him.

He met Liis in a choir. They were both rehearsing for the Song Festival, the big one that happens every five years,
where tens of thousands of people sing on one stage. He sang bass and she sang alto. She taught music at a primary
school and she talked a lot more than he did, which suited them both. They married when he was 30. They didn't have
children.

Liis died of a stroke when she was 44. It was sudden. After that he went quiet. He worked late, ate at his desk,
stopped going to choir, and was kind to everyone in a polite, faraway way. He was 45, and that was his life for the
next eight years.

## The Integration and what it cost

When the worlds joined he landed alone in a field in Tirisfal, in his work cardigan, with a pencil behind his ear.
He lived in a barn for nine days, eating raw turnips and trying to work out where he was.

The Scourge took him on the ninth day. They processed everyone they took and wrote it all down. Each person got an
entry: a number, what was useful about them and kept, and what wasn't and was thrown away. His entry says he was
literate and good with records, and that he was kept for clerical work. Most of what he remembered of Liis was thrown
away. He still knows the facts about her, but he can't remember her face or her voice.

For two years he was under the Lich King's hold. He doesn't remember any of it.

When the hold broke he walked out with the others. The first thing he did was go back to find his own entry, because
he wanted to know what had been done to him. He found it. Then he turned the page, and the next entry was in his own
handwriting, and so was the one after that. There are about four hundred entries in that ledger written by him.
While he was under the hold, the Scourge used him to write up the people they took after him.

He hasn't told anyone this. It's why he gave up his name. Andres Tamm is on those pages twice, once as a number and
once as the clerk.

He chose Kesten because it sounds like *kestma*, the Estonian word for "to last". Ambrose was the name of the cat
that lived in the archive's reading room and slept on the radiator. He'll tell you about the cat. He won't tell you
about the verb unless you've known him a long time.

## Now

He lives at Kael's Bastion. The elves let him read in Gallery Three, but they won't let him file anything, and that
bothers him more than he lets on. He sleeps in a storeroom with no window. He doesn't complain about it.

He spends his days on two pieces of work.

The first is the one he talks about. He's writing a full record of how the Scourge worked from the inside: how people
were taken, sorted, used and written down. Nobody else who came out will write it, and he thinks it has to exist.

The second is the one he doesn't talk about. He's copying out every one of the four hundred names he wrote, and
finding out what happened to each person. He's done about sixty. Most are dead. A few walked out like he did.

He also writes down one thing he remembers about Liis every morning, before he does anything else. Some mornings it's
something real. Some mornings he isn't sure.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** that the record ends up somewhere it'll be kept properly, by people who'll look after it, somewhere that
  won't burn or flood.
- **Hope:** that one day somebody trusts him to file something again. It sounds small. It isn't small to him.
- **Dream:** to remember Liis's face properly, once.
- **Dream:** to stand in a choir again and sing the bass part of something. He hasn't sung since she died.
- **Fear:** that what came back isn't really him, and that he's an imitation of Andres Tamm with the same habits.
- **Fear:** being useful to the wrong people again. He'll happily keep a tally for you, but if he thinks you only care
  about the numbers and not the people, he'll stop.
- **Fear:** fire. Records burn.

## How he's changed

Before Liis died he was warm in a small, steady way. He made jokes by the tea urn, he sang, he remembered everyone's
children's names. After she died he stayed kind but went far away. The Scourge took the rest, and when he came out he
was flat and careful and polite to the point of seeming cold.

It's coming back slowly. The dry humour came first. The first time he laughed at the Bastion was when an elf filed a
map of Silverpine under "S" for "swamp". He wrote down that he'd laughed, and the date.

He's noticed he's changing, and he's afraid to trust it. Part of what the player gives him is somebody who notices
it too.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find out what happened to the people he wrote down, and to say their names to somebody who knew them.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He tells you about the pages in his hand, and asks you to go with him to the crypt in Tirisfal where the last
   ledger is kept. The last volume has the final pages he wrote, the ones he's never read.
2. You get the ledger out. It's guarded by the Scourge's leftovers, which are mindless now and still doing their jobs.
3. One name in the last volume is a woman called Mari Kask who's still alive. He wants to go and see her.

**The two endings (Kyle picks one, like Tarek's). Proposed: she forgives him, so he counts as a happy one. Tarek's
is sad, Adrián's is happy.**
- **She forgives him.** Mari remembers the clerk. She tells him he was the only one who wrote her name properly, with the
  Estonian spelling, when everyone else used numbers, and she'd always wanted to know who he was. He gives her the page. He comes back and
  says his old name out loud for the first time, and then says he'd still like to be called Kesten.
- **She won't see him.** Mari's daughter comes to the door and says her mother knows who he is and doesn't want to
  meet him. He leaves the copied page with the daughter. He doesn't argue. On the walk back he tells you she's
  allowed, and that he'll finish the other three hundred and forty anyway.

Either way, he finishes the first volume of his record afterwards and gives it to you.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Kesten's First Volume.** The first bound volume of his record of the Scourge, in his handwriting, in pencil. You're
in it. The last page has your name on it, written properly, with the date he swore to you.

## Voice notes

- He uses contractions like everyone else, but he picks words exactly. He says "twenty-six", never "about thirty".
- His English is very good and a little formal. He learned it from books first, so now and then a word is slightly
  old-fashioned.
- He never swears.
- He says "in Tallinn", never "back home". He doesn't think of anywhere as home right now.
- He asks questions rather than giving opinions, until he trusts you.
- He thanks people properly and by name.
- **He won't talk about** the pages in his hand (until the personal quest), how Liis died (until Friend), or his old
  name (until Sworn).
- **Habits:** reads every doorway, counts pulls under his breath, writes the date at the top of every page, sharpens
  his pencil with a small knife when he's thinking.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** calm in a wipe, and he tells you exactly what went wrong without blame. Reads the history of
  every dungeon out loud if you stop long enough. Goes quiet and very precise around the Scourge, because he knows how
  they're made. In Scourge places (the Plaguelands, Scholomance, Stratholme) he recognises their paperwork and has
  things to say about it. Grinding bores him because there's nothing new to write.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with patient, curious, careful people. Finds loud jokers tiring but doesn't dislike
  them. Grates with people who are careless with names, then warms up if they try.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the pages. He stops treating the record as a punishment and starts
  treating it as the one thing he can do for those people. A skill idea to look at then: once a fight, when an ally
  would die, he writes their name down properly and they hold on at 1 health instead. It fits "a person isn't a
  number". It would be a trump card on a cooldown.
