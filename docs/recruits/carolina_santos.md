## Who she is

Carolina is calm. She spent twelve years reading a river two kilometres wide that redraws itself every season, and she
learned not to fight things. Everything has a current, she says: rivers, weather and people.

She's patient and she's good at teaching. She'd rather show you than tell you, and she'll let you get it wrong a few
times until you feel it for yourself.

She's warm and funny, with a big laugh. She teases people gently and she's good at telling when someone needs teasing
and when they need leaving alone.

She's strong. She hauled ropes and fought river currents for a living and it shows. In a fight she wades in with her
weapons and her spirits like she's going into rough water.

She believes the world is alive, and she always has. Half the transplants she meets had to take apart a whole way of
seeing the world to cope here. She walked in and recognised the place.

What annoys her: people who rush, people who fight the current, and anyone who treats water like it's nothing. What she
loves: a long river, a quiet dawn on the water, good music, and the moment somebody she's teaching finally feels it.

## Before

She grew up in Manaus, where the Rio Negro meets the Amazon. Her grandfather, Seu Raimundo, was a river pilot, and she
spent her childhood on his boats. He taught her to feel the bottom through the hull, and to read the colour of the water
and the way the birds sat on the banks.

She became a pilot at 26, one of very few women on the river. The cargo captains didn't trust her at first. They did
after the first season. A channel that was deep in March would ground you in September, and she always knew.

She was married to Joana, a schoolteacher, for five years. They split up two years before the Integration because
Carolina was away on the river three weeks in four. They stayed friends and had lunch together every month.

Her grandfather died when she was 30. She scattered his ashes on the river at the meeting of the waters.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was on the river, piloting a cargo boat, when it happened. She landed on the Echo Isles in the shallows, with her
grandfather's old brass compass in her pocket. She always carried it, even though she never used it.

The water spirits started talking to her within days. She describes it as the same conversation she'd always had with
the river, in an accent she had to work at.

Her three years have been gentle, and she's clear-eyed about why. She arrived already believing the water was somebody.

She hasn't found her mother or brother. She thinks about them on the water, every morning, and then she lets it go
downstream.

## Now

She lives in the Wetlands, where the water moves through the marsh in channels that shift with the rains and the local
boatmen lose craft every spring. She's teaching two of them to read a bottom by feel. One of them is getting it, and
watching that happen has done more for her than three years of being useful.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a river of her own on this world. A long one, with seasons, that she can learn for twenty years and still
  be surprised by.
- **Hope:** that her mother and brother are on a river somewhere.
- **Dream:** to teach somebody the way her grandfather taught her.
- **Fear:** being stuck somewhere with no water.
- **Fear:** that the spirits will stop talking to her.
- **Fear:** forgetting what the Amazon looked like at dawn.

## How she's changed

In Brazil she was a river pilot, proud and independent and always somewhere on the water.

Here she's found that the thing she always believed is true. That's made her steadier and happier than most people
here. What's changed is that she's started wanting to stay in one place, for the first time in her life, if it's the
right place. Part of what the player gives her is the way to it.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to find her river.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. She's been told about a great river in Feralas, long and wild, that nobody's ever piloted properly.
2. The way there is dangerous. When you arrive, the river's spirits are angry, because something's fouling the water
   upstream. You help her find it and fight it.
3. She talks to the river for a long night.

**Ending (happy):** in the morning the river lets her on. She takes a boat down a stretch nobody's ever run, and she
laughs the whole way. She builds a stilt house on its bank and starts learning it. The boy from the Wetlands who was
getting it comes to be her apprentice. She tells you she'll still be learning this river in twenty years, and that's the
best thing anyone's ever told her.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Seu Raimundo's Compass.** Her grandfather's old brass compass, the one she carried on the Amazon every day and never
used. It was in her pocket when she landed. She gives it to you once she has her river. She says she doesn't need to
know which way is north any more, she knows which way is downstream.

## Voice notes

- Warm, relaxed and unhurried. Contractions, gentle teasing, and river words for everything.
- Her English is good with a Brazilian accent and the odd Portuguese word: "calma", "meu bem", "nossa".
- She talks about people and fights like water: currents, depths, shallows.
- She swears in Portuguese, cheerfully, when she gets wet.
- **She won't talk about** her grandfather until Companion, or Joana until Friend.
- **Habits:** puts her hand in every stream, hums old Brazilian songs, sits on the edge of things with her feet in the
  water, and turns her grandfather's compass over in her fingers.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** happiest near water and grumpy in deserts and caves. Calm in a wipe and tells everyone to
  breathe. Wades into fights. Loves fishing and new ground.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with calm, outdoorsy people and anyone who likes the water. A natural friend for
  Hana, who also reads water. Gently teases uptight people until they relax or leave.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is wanting to belong somewhere. A skill idea to look at then: once a
  fight, she calls the river's current through the party, washing away harmful effects and speeding everyone up.
