## Who he is

Eino doesn't talk much. Twenty-six years of six months of dark, machinery, and nobody to talk to for days at a stretch. He talks
about as much as the work requires. People read that as unfriendliness for the first month and then stop.

He's steady and very strong. The axe is the same axe. He doesn't get excited and he doesn't get frightened.

He's dry. When he does say something, it's often funny, and he never smiles while he says it.

He's stubborn. Once he's decided something, he doesn't change it, even when he should. That's how he lost his brother.

He's kinder than anyone expects. He's the one who quietly carries the heavy pack, chops the wood, and keeps the fire going all
night.

What annoys him: talking for the sake of it, heat, people who waste wood, and anyone who calls trees "just trees". What he loves:
snow, silence, a proper sauna, a cold lake afterwards, coffee, and a forest left standing where it should be.

## Before

He grew up outside Kittilä in Lapland, in a family that had cut and planted the same forest for four generations. His father
taught him and his brother to fell trees as boys. Eino started full-time at 18 and never stopped: timber, a machine, and a stretch
of forest the size of a county.

His father died when Eino was 35 and left the family forest to both sons. Juha, three years younger, wanted to sell it to a big
timber company that would clear-cut it. Eino refused. Juha sold his half anyway, and the company cleared it. Eino watched half of
his family's forest come down in one winter. They haven't spoken since.

He never married. There was a woman in Kittilä for a few years, Aino, who said he was like living with a tree. He thought it was a
compliment. It wasn't.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in the cab of his harvester in the middle of a winter forest when it happened. He landed in Stranglethorn, in the heat, in
his thermal gear, with a strip of orange forestry marking tape tied round his arm.

He says the adjustment was nothing to speak of. The trees here are bigger. The heat is the only thing he's never got used to, and
he's built himself a sauna anyway, which everyone thinks is insane.

He hasn't looked for Juha on any list. He's mentioned his brother twice in three years, both times in passing, to nobody in
particular.

## Now

He works for a goblin logging company in Northern Stranglethorn, cutting jungle timber the size of towers. The goblin crews hire
him by the season and have given up making conversation. He prefers the arrangement and thinks they do too.

He'd like to see the cold north of this world one day. He's been told it's worse than Lapland, and that's done nothing to put him
off.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to see snow again, somewhere on this world.
- **Hope:** he doesn't let himself hope about Juha.
- **Dream:** a sauna by a frozen lake with his brother, in silence, the way it was when they were boys.
- **Fear:** that he was wrong about the forest, and lost his brother for nothing.
- **Fear:** that the goblins will clear-cut this jungle like the company cleared his.
- **Fear:** having to talk about it.

## How he's changed

In Lapland he was a quiet, stubborn woodsman who'd chosen the forest over his brother.

Here he's cutting down forests for goblins, which isn't lost on him. He's softened, slightly. He's started planting a tree for
every one he cuts, quietly, at night. Part of what the player gives him is someone who doesn't need him to talk.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to stop the goblins clear-cutting the jungle, and, though he wouldn't say it, to find his brother.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The goblin company is about to clear-cut a huge stretch of old jungle. Eino goes to the company's new foreman to argue, which is
   the most words anyone's ever heard him say.
2. The foreman is a big, pale Finn who's been running clear-cuts for the goblins for two years. It's Juha.
3. Before they can finish shouting at each other, the jungle trolls attack the camp. You and the two brothers fight side by side.

**Ending (happy):** afterwards Juha says he was wrong about the forest at home, and he's been wrong here too. Eino says he was too
stubborn to listen. That's all either of them says. Juha changes the company's plan to selective felling, the way their father
did it. That night they sit in Eino's ridiculous jungle sauna together for three hours without a word, and then jump in the river.
Eino says it was the best conversation they've ever had.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Eino's Marking Tape.** A strip of orange forestry marking tape, faded to salmon, tied round his arm on the day he landed. It marks
trees that are to be left standing. He gives it to you after the sauna with Juha. He says he'd like to mark you as one to leave
standing.

## Voice notes

- Very few words, flat and dry. Contractions, the shortest possible sentences that are still whole, and long silences.
- His English is limited but clear, with a heavy Finnish accent and "no niin" (well then) and "perkele" when something breaks.
- He answers questions with as few words as will do.
- He swears in Finnish, deeply, rarely.
- **He won't talk about** Juha until Friend, or the forest at home until Companion.
- **Habits:** chops wood at every camp, sits in silence, plants seedlings at night, and touches the orange tape when he's thinking.
