## Who he is

Liam is calm in a burning room. Seventeen years of bushfires and the bad nights on the highway taught him that the most
useful thing in an emergency is a calm voice, and the second most useful is a hand on the person about to run.

He's good company: funny, easy, warm, a great storyteller. People like him straight away.

He's tired. He hasn't slept properly since about 2019, the summer the fires went through the mountains.

He doesn't know what he's for without an emergency. For his first twelve months here, the job was everywhere. Then it went
quiet for a season and he fell apart, and there was no crew around to notice.

He goes in alone. He always has. He knows it's the reason he never got the crew he wanted. Knowing that hasn't changed what he
does when the call comes.

What annoys him: panic, people who freeze in the way, and anyone who calls him a hero. What he loves: a good crew, a cold
beer, a barbecue, and the quiet in the truck on the way back from one that went well.

## Before

He grew up in Katoomba, in the Blue Mountains, the son of a builder and a nurse. He joined the rural fire service at 20 and
trained as a paramedic at 26 because there was no ambulance for an hour in either direction. For seventeen years he did both,
because the roster never cared which badge you held at three in the morning.

The summer of 2019 the fires came through the mountains. He was on the line for weeks. He lost two of his crew and several
houses on his own street, and he went straight back to work afterwards. That's when the sleeping stopped.

He married Jess at 30. She left at 37, because he was never home and never talked when he was. He doesn't blame her.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on a night shift in the ambulance when it happened. He landed in Ashenvale with his paramedic's trauma shears in his
belt pouch.

The first year was the easiest of his life. The second was the hardest, and he can tell you the week it turned. The sun
answers him the way a good crew does, when called and all at once, and he's given up pretending that isn't most of what holds
him together.

He hasn't looked for anyone on the lists. He says he will.

## Now

He works the ground around Grom's Rest in Ashenvale East, where the corruption burns out of people slowly and nobody has a
protocol for it. He's written one. It's four lines long, wrong in at least one place, and the only one that exists.

There are two kids in Grom's Rest, a brother and sister, whose mother didn't come out of the corruption. He's been in that
village fourteen months, and everybody can see what's happened except him.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a crew. Rostered, trained, able to do it without him.
- **Hope:** to sleep through a night.
- **Dream:** a quiet day where nothing's on fire.
- **Fear:** a quiet season with nothing to do.
- **Fear:** going in alone one time too many.
- **Fear:** that he's only any good in a crisis.

## How he's changed

In New South Wales he was a hero to everyone and a stranger to his wife, and the 2019 fires broke something in him.

Here he found the sun and a purpose, and then the quiet nearly finished him. He's still going in alone. He's starting to care
about people for longer than an emergency lasts. Part of what the player gives him is a crew of one.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** a crew that can do it without him.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A wave of corruption rolls through Grom's Rest, worse than anything before. He can't handle it alone, and for once he
   asks for help.
2. You help him train three of the villagers on his four-line protocol, then fight back the corruption while they treat
   people.
3. In the middle of it, the two kids go missing into the corrupted forest. He goes in after them, and this time he lets you
   come.

**Ending (happy):** you bring them back. The crew handled the village without him. That night the girl falls asleep against
his arm, and he sits there until morning without moving, and it's the first night he's slept in three years. The village
asks him to take the children in. He says yes before they finish asking. He rewrites the protocol, five lines now, with a
line about not going in alone.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Liam's Trauma Shears.** A pair of paramedic's trauma shears, the blunt-nosed kind for cutting clothes off the injured, with
"HARTIGAN" scratched into one handle. They were in his belt pouch when he landed. He gives them to you after the kids come
home. He says he's got a crew with their own shears now, and two kids who'll want to borrow them.

## Voice notes

- Warm, easy and very Australian. Contractions, "mate", "no worries", "too right", and calm, clear instructions in a crisis.
- He tells stories at the fire, mostly funny ones.
- His crisis voice is lower and slower than his normal one.
- He swears casually, and never in front of the kids.
- **He won't talk about** 2019 until Friend, or Jess until Companion.
- **Habits:** counts breaths, checks everyone's pupils after a fight, gets up at every noise in the night, and puts himself
  between danger and anyone small.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** heals with a calm voice and goes in alone too often. Brilliant in a crisis, restless when nothing's
  happening. Very good with civilians in quests.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with steady people, and with Cristóbal and Ama, fellow front-line rescuers. Protective of
  the young. Clashes with panicky people, then helps them.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is going in alone. A skill idea to look at then: once a fight, he calls the
  crew in, and every ally gets a burst of healing at once.
