## Who he is

Héctor is a showman. Fifteen years of being El Relámpago de Plata, and nobody outside his family ever saw his face. He has a huge laugh
and a huge presence, and he loves an audience more than anything.

He's much smarter than he lets anyone see. The show is a mask too. Behind it he's watching everyone and missing nothing.

He's superstitious about everything: which foot first into the ring, which hand holds the mask, which way he faces when he sleeps. He says
the trolls' spirits are no stranger than the saints his mother prayed to.

He's warm and generous with fans, and with children above all. He'll stop anything to sign an autograph, even for goblins who can't read.

He's protective of his mask in a way that goes beyond superstition. A luchador who loses his mask loses his name.

What annoys him: people who mock lucha as fake, cowards, and anyone who touches his mask. What he loves: a roaring crowd, tacos al pastor,
Rocío's laugh, his boys in their homemade masks, and the moment a crowd goes silent before a big move.

## Before

He grew up in Tepito, a rough neighbourhood in Mexico City, the son of a market trader. He started training at a lucha gym at 14 and
debuted at 20 as El Relámpago de Plata, in a black mask with silver flames. For fifteen years he wrestled at Arena México on Friday nights.
He was never the biggest star, but the crowd loved him.

He married Rocío at 23. She sewed every mask he ever wore, by hand, and she was the only person outside the family who knew his face. They
had two boys, Toño and Beto, who wore homemade masks to school and wanted to be luchadores like their father.

He retired a year before the Integration with a bad knee and a lot of good stories. He'd started training kids at the gym.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at the gym, teaching kids to fall, when it happened. He landed in Stranglethorn with his mask in his bag, and the first troll who
saw it bowed to him.

A witch doctor at Grom'gol took him in. The trolls told him a mask is a spirit's face, and he said he'd known that his whole life. He
learned shadow and spirit the way he'd learned to fall, by doing it a thousand times. Now the spirits wear his face, and the crowds at the
arena love him.

He hasn't found Rocío or the boys. He'd like to give his mask to his sons one day, and he's afraid he never will.

## Now

He's at the Gurubashi Arena in Stranglethorn, signing autographs for goblins who can't read and posing for trolls.

He wants to put on a real show in the arena: rules, a ring and a crowd that cheers, not one that waits for blood.

He never takes the mask off his belt.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a real show, lucha in the Gurubashi Arena.
- **Hope:** to find Rocío and the boys.
- **Dream:** to put his mask on Toño's face, in a ring, in front of a crowd.
- **Fear:** losing his mask.
- **Fear:** that his boys are growing up without a father, and without a mask.
- **Fear:** a silent crowd.

## How he's changed

In Mexico City he was a beloved masked wrestler, a husband and a father, with a bad knee and a happy retirement ahead.

Here he's become a witch doctor and a showman again, and the spirits have given him a second career. He's still loud and joyful. He's
lonelier underneath. Part of what the player gives him is an audience that knows the man behind the mask.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** a real lucha show in the arena, and to find his family.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He talks the arena's goblin promoters into a lucha night: rules, a ring and a show. They agree if he can fill the stands.
2. You help him find opponents worth fighting and spread the word across Stranglethorn and beyond. The posters go up everywhere, with El
   Relámpago de Plata on them.
3. On the night, the arena's full, and he wrestles the show of his life.

**Ending (happy):** in the front row, wearing homemade masks, are two boys and a woman crying and laughing. Rocío saw a poster in Booty Bay
and knew the mask, because she sewed it. Toño and Beto are bigger now. After the match he takes them into the ring, takes off his mask in
front of the whole arena for the first time in his life, and puts it on Toño's face. The crowd goes mad. He says he doesn't need a mask any
more. He's got his name back.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**El Relámpago de Plata's First Mask.** Not the one he gave Toño, but his very first mask, the one Rocío sewed for his debut at 20, faded and
patched, carried in his bag through the Integration. He gives it to you after the show. He says only family should have it, and you are.

## Voice notes

- Loud, warm and theatrical. Contractions, big announcements, and Mexican Spanish: "¡órale!", "carnal", "ándale", "¡a la verga!" when
  something's amazing.
- He talks about himself in the third person when he's showing off.
- He's very superstitious and does little rituals constantly.
- He swears enthusiastically in Spanish, never in front of children.
- **He won't talk about** his face until Sworn, or his boys until Companion.
- **Habits:** poses for everyone, crosses himself before every fight, touches the mask on his belt, and teaches children to fall.
