## Who he is

Ziad does arithmetic with lives. Six years in a Beirut ambulance taught him triage as a reflex: a van with four spaces and five people
dying. He made those choices for six years. Every one was correct and every one cost him something.

He's fast and restless. Everything bad that happened to him happened while he was standing still, he says, so he doesn't.

He's honest about what he did. Fel magic put a terrible, obvious power in reach, and he took it, for the same reason he chose the fourth
patient over the fifth. He'll argue the arithmetic out loud with anyone who wants to.

He refuses to call it weakness. He was offered the ability to save more people and he took it.

He's handsome and knows it, charming and quick with a joke, and the charm is a little bit armour.

What annoys him: people who stand still, people who judge his choice without doing the arithmetic, and anyone who calls him a saint. What
he loves: speed, strong coffee, Fairuz songs in the morning, his sister's teasing, and the moment someone he's pulled out opens their eyes.

## Before

He grew up in Beirut, in Achrafieh, the son of a pharmacist and a teacher. His sister Lina is four years younger and the only person who
can make him stop talking. He studied nursing, then joined the Red Cross ambulance service at 25.

Six years in a van with a radio, in a city where the road you took mattered more than the medicine you carried. He was there for the big
explosion at the port, and for a hundred small disasters. He got very good at choosing, and choosing got very heavy.

He wore foam earplugs on long shifts, to sleep in the van between calls. He's still got one, on a cord.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in the van, between calls, half asleep with an earplug in, when it happened. He landed in Stranglethorn with the earplug still in
one ear.

He spent twelve months saying yes to things he should have slept on. The fel came to him in a jungle full of wounded, with an offer: enough
power to save all of them, at a price. He owns all of it.

He hasn't found his family. He doesn't let himself look very hard.

## Now

He works the country around Booty Bay in Southern Stranglethorn, where the Bloodsail, the trolls and the jungle trade blows with the town,
and the casualties are constant. He's first on scene. Always. The jungle trolls have a word for him, and he's asked three times what it means
and been told three different things.

He wants out. He's looked into what getting out costs, and the figure is the reason he's still in. The people he pulls out are alive either
way, and that's his whole defence, and he knows how thin it is.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to get out of the fel, someday, without it costing the lives he saves.
- **Hope:** that his family's safe.
- **Dream:** a quiet morning in Beirut, coffee on the balcony, Fairuz on the radio, and nobody dying.
- **Fear:** the fel deciding for him.
- **Fear:** that the arithmetic was an excuse.
- **Fear:** standing still.

## How he's changed

In Beirut he was a brilliant, exhausted paramedic who chose who lived every day and carried every choice.

Here he chose power, for the same reason, and it's changing him: his eyes have a faint green cast now. He still does the arithmetic out loud.
Part of what the player gives him is somebody who'll argue it with him honestly.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to get out of the fel.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A troll witch doctor tells him there's a way to cut the fel out of him, at a jungle shrine. It'll take the power, and it'll hurt. He asks
   you to come.
2. You fight through to the shrine and he goes through with it. The fel is torn out of him. He's just a man again, and he's never felt so
   light.
3. On the way back, a Bloodsail raid hits a village, and there are a dozen wounded.

**Ending (sad):** without the fel he can save four. He chooses, the way he used to, fast and correctly, and the fifth dies in front of him,
someone he could have saved a week ago. He kneels there for a long time. Then he goes back to the shrine and asks for the fel back. The troll
gives it to him and warns him it'll be harder to put down a second time. He says he knows. He doesn't argue the arithmetic out loud any more.
He says he's done arguing. He just works.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Ziad's Earplug.** A single foam earplug on a cord, the kind he used to sleep in the ambulance between calls in Beirut. It was in his ear when
he landed. He gives it to you after the shrine. He says he doesn't sleep much now anyway, and you should have something for blocking out the
noise.

## Voice notes

- Quick, charming and restless. Contractions, fast talk, and dark jokes.
- His English is fluent and Lebanese, mixing Arabic and French: "yalla", "habibi", "khalas" (enough), "ça va".
- He argues the arithmetic out loud, until the quest.
- He swears in Arabic and French, often.
- **He won't talk about** the port explosion until Friend, or the fel's price until Companion.
- **Habits:** keeps moving, counts the wounded before anything else, touches the earplug on its cord, and never sits down while someone's
  hurt.
