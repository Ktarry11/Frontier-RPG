## Who he is

Elie is calm, warm and careful, and very hard to rattle. He drew blood for a living for ten years, and he was the one the nervous patients asked for.

He heals with blood magic, which most bloodmages think is a waste and which he thinks is the only sensible use. He says the work is the same as it ever
was, he just doesn't need the needle.

He's gentle with frightened people. He talks them through it, makes a small joke, and it's done before they've noticed.

He's a dreamer who kept saving. He was saving for medical school in Montpellier for six years, and never quite had enough.

He misses the sea. He grew up beside it.

What annoys him: rough healers, bloodmages who use it to hurt, and people who make the frightened wait. What he loves: his mother's Sunday cooking, the
Corniche at sunset, an old motorbike that runs, and the moment a frightened patient laughs.

## Before

He grew up in Achrafieh, in Beirut, the youngest of three, after his father died when he was nine. His mother Mireille raised the three of them on a
seamstress's wage and cooked for the whole family every Sunday, a table of twenty.

He trained as a phlebotomist and worked at a clinic in Achrafieh for ten years, drawing blood. He wanted to be a doctor. He was saving for medical school
in Montpellier, in France, and he'd been saving for six years. Every time he got close, something happened: the crash, the explosion at the port, his
sister's wedding.

He kept a motorbike running on spare parts and faith, and rode it along the Corniche by the sea every evening.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at the clinic, mid-draw, when it happened. He landed in the dark under Khaz Varun with a tourniquet in his pocket and the blue cap from the butterfly
needle he'd just used clipped to his sleeve.

The first time he felt someone's blood move under his hand, he finished the job before he let himself be frightened.

He hasn't found his mother or his sisters. He doesn't know if the sea came with Beirut.

## Now

He's in the upper halls of Khaz Varun, the Black Hollow, patching up whoever comes out of the dark worse than they went in. The Ashclaim's healers keep
asking him to train them, and he keeps saying yes.

The blue needle cap clipped to his sleeve is from the last blood draw he did at home. He's never taken it off.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to train other people to heal with blood, properly.
- **Hope:** to see the sea again.
- **Dream:** a Sunday at his mother's table, all twenty of them.
- **Fear:** becoming the kind of bloodmage who hurts.
- **Fear:** that he'll always be saving for something he never gets.
- **Fear:** never seeing the sea again.

## How he's changed

In Beirut he was a gentle phlebotomist who dreamed of being a doctor and never had enough money.

Here he's a healer in everything but name, and the dream came true in a way he never expected. He's braver and more sure of himself. Part of what the
player gives him is someone to take him to the sea.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to train healers, and to see the sea.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The Ashclaim ask him to teach a class of six healers his way. He agrees, but he says he needs to see the sea first, because he hasn't for three years
   and he's starting to forget what it sounds like.
2. You take him all the way to the coast, to the Tempest Shallows, through dangerous country, and he heals everyone you meet on the road.
3. He stands in the surf at sunset.

**Ending (still unknown):** it isn't the Mediterranean, and it's enough. He stands there until dark. He says it sounds like the Corniche, almost. On the
way back he asks at every hub for his mother and sisters, and finds nothing. He doesn't know if Beirut came through, or the sea with it, or where his
mother cooks on Sundays now. He goes back to Khaz Varun and teaches his class of six, and on Sundays he cooks for them, the way she did, a table of
twenty if he can find twenty.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Elie's Needle Cap.** The small blue cap from the butterfly needle he used on the last blood draw he ever did in Beirut, clipped to his sleeve for three
years. He gives it to you after the sea. He says he doesn't need a needle any more, and you should keep the last one.

## Voice notes

- Calm, warm and gentle. Contractions, soothing talk, and small jokes for nervous people.
- His English is fluent and Lebanese, mixing Arabic and French: "habibi", "yalla", "ça va", "khalas".
- He narrates healing gently, the way he talked patients through a blood draw.
- He doesn't swear much. When a healer's rough, he says so quietly, which is worse.
- **He won't talk about** his father until Companion, or the six years of saving until Friend.
- **Habits:** asks for your arm, checks everyone's colour, touches the blue cap on his sleeve, and cooks on Sundays.
