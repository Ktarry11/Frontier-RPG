## Who he is

Tarek is kind in a practical way. He fixes things, feeds people and checks the water. He'll ask you if you've eaten,
and if you say no he'll feed you before he talks about anything else.

He's plain-spoken. He likes being told where he's needed and he tells you plainly what he thinks. He has very little
patience for people who are sure they're doing right and won't explain why, because people like that wrecked his life
once.

He laughs easily. He didn't always. He learned it from Omar, who laughed at everything, and he's kept it.

He's an engineer all the way down. Give him a stick and some dirt and he'll draw you why a well failed, and it'll take
an hour, and he won't notice you've stopped listening. He likes things that last and he hates waste.

He's restless. He can't stay away from the wells for long, and a long trip makes him itchy. He works too much,
partly because the work matters and partly because when he's working he doesn't think.

He's careful about trust. He's friendly with everyone and he tells almost nobody anything real. Once he does trust
you, he's the most loyal person you'll ever travel with.

What annoys him: killing for no reason, people who hand out titles and decide who belongs, waste, and anyone who
says "it's always been done this way". What he loves: a clean stream, a good meal shared, an honest argument, and a
fight against something that's actually doing harm.

## Before

He grew up in Heliopolis in Cairo. His father was a pharmacist and his mother taught primary school. His younger
sister Salma was the loud one. He was the son who did well at school, studied engineering at Cairo University, and
made his parents proud.

He spent 14 years on Cairo's water: pumping stations, mains, pressure, all the work nobody thinks about until the tap
runs dry. He was very good at it, and he was careful about everything else.

He met Omar at 32. Omar taught chemistry at a secondary school, he laughed at everything, and he used to mark Tarek's
engineering drawings in red pen, as if they were homework, because he said somebody had to. They were together for
six years and they were careful for all six. They didn't live together, and they had a story ready for everyone.

At a cousin's wedding, when Tarek was 38, they weren't careful for one evening. Somebody saw, and a colleague of Tarek's
made a phone call. The wrong office took an interest. Omar told him to go. Omar had his parents, his sister Nadia and
no money, and he couldn't leave. He told Tarek to go and he meant it. That was the last time they talked.

Tarek left within a week, with two bags, for Berlin.

Berlin wouldn't recognise his degree. He spent three years working in a hardware shop in Neukölln, selling the pipe
fittings he used to design. His mother phoned every Friday. His father didn't phone at all. Salma sent him messages
about nothing, every day, so he'd know she was still there.

## The Integration and what it cost

When the worlds joined he landed in Ashenvale with a pocketful of German coins and Omar's red pen in his shirt
pocket. Omar gave it to him the year they met as a joke, and he'd carried it ever since.

He's the rare one for whom this world has been better, and he says so plainly. The thing poisoning the water here can
be seen and fought. And in three years nobody has asked him a question he had to lie about. He found that out slowly,
the first time he mentioned Omar to an orc and the orc just asked what Omar did for a living.

It cost him his family, as far as he knows. He reads the Cairo lists every few months in Astranaar, for Omar and for
his parents and Salma. None of them have ever been on them. He knows exactly how little that means, and that's the
problem with understanding probability.

## Now

He lives at Splintertree Post in a hut by the east spring. He works the streams coming down out of the corrupted
country, where the water carries the rot further than the rot can walk. He's mapped which springs are clean and which
only look it, and the garrison sends for him before it sends for a priest.

He's sunk three wells. One has already failed, and he can tell you why. He's planning a fourth.

The orcs have offered him a title twice and he's turned it down both times. He's seen what happens to a man who gets
to decide who belongs.

He found a faith here, the Templar oaths. It asks what you do, not who you are. He'll sit with you for an hour and
explain the difference between that and the faith that was used against him, if you're willing to be serious about
it.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** that Omar's alive somewhere. He knows it's a hope and not a plan.
- **Hope:** to teach the people at Splintertree to look after the water themselves, so it doesn't depend on him.
- **Dream:** a well that's still standing in ten years. He calls it building now, not rebuilding, and that took him
  two years to get to.
- **Dream:** to go to a wedding again and dance with whoever he likes, in front of everybody.
- **Fear:** trusting somebody and having them make a phone call. It's why he tells almost nobody anything.
- **Fear:** getting the numbers wrong on a well and making a whole camp sick.
- **Fear:** that he left Omar to take what was coming to both of them.

## How he's changed

In Cairo he was two men: the good engineer and son that everyone saw, and the man with Omar that nobody could. He was
careful every minute of every day for six years.

In Berlin he was nobody. He wasn't allowed to be an engineer and he couldn't go home.

Here he's one man for the first time in his life. He's more open and he laughs more, and he's proud of his work again.
What hasn't changed is the guilt about leaving, and the lists. Part of what the player gives him is somebody he can
tell about Omar who doesn't flinch.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find out what happened to Omar.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. On his trip to the lists he finds a new name, Nadia, Omar's sister. She came through and she's living in a camp of
   Cairo transplants far to the south. He writes to her.
2. Waiting for her answer is hard, so he sinks the fourth well. You help: clear the site of corrupted things, bring
   the stone and timber he needs, and stand guard while he works.
3. Nadia writes back.

**Ending (sad, already in the pilot):** Omar got his class out into the school yard when it happened, and he didn't
make it out himself. Tarek cuts Omar's name into the lining of the fourth well, under the waterline, where nobody will
ever read it. He stops going down to the lists.

The softer ending (Omar alive, teaching in a town far south, and not wanting to be found) stays written but unused.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Omar's Red Pen.** The pen Omar gave him as a joke the year they met, the one he marked Tarek's drawings with. It's
nearly dry. Tarek carried it in his shirt pocket through the Integration and every day since. He gives it to you when
he swears to you. He says Omar's name is in the well now, and he'd like the pen to be with somebody who knows what it
is.

## Voice notes

- Warm, plain and practical. Contractions, short clear sentences, and the useful thing first.
- His English is very good, learned at university and polished in Berlin. Now and then he thinks in Arabic and
  translates.
- He asks "Have you eaten?" and means it.
- He explains with drawings: a stick in the dirt, a finger on a table.
- He doesn't swear. When something's stupid he laughs at it.
- **He won't talk about** his family until Companion, Omar until Friend, or the wedding until after the quest.
- **Habits:** stops at every stream to check it, eats with whoever's nearest, taps his shirt pocket where the pen is,
  draws in the dirt when he's thinking.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** glad to fight undead and demons, unhappy killing animals that did nothing. Checks every
  stream and pool you pass. Feeds the party at every rest. Restless on long trips and always half thinking about the
  wells. After a wipe he takes the blame for his own part and goes again.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with practical, kind people and with anyone who argues honestly. Makes friends fast
  with people who laugh easily. Grates with people who think they're always right, and goes very quiet around anybody
  who likes deciding who belongs. Quietly protective of anyone he thinks has had to hide.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the waiting, and the guilt of leaving. He stops searching and lets
  Omar rest somewhere that lasts. A skill idea to look at then: something built to last, like a shield on the whole
  party that holds until it's broken instead of running out with time.
