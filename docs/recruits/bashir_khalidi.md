## Who he is

Bashir is severe, literal and entirely without small talk. People read that as coldness for about a week.

He works slowly and correctly. Stonemasonry is taking something apart correctly before you rebuild it. He hunts corrupted things the same way:
along the grain, with no interest at all in how long it takes.

He's honest to the bone. He'll tell you everything he finds, including the parts you'd rather he kept to himself.

He's carrying one unfinished sentence. His daughter told him she was going to study engineering instead of anything he'd suggested, and he was
pleased, and he hadn't said so yet.

Under the severity he's a tender father who never learned to say tender things out loud.

What annoys him: careless work, people who cut corners, flattery, and wasted words. What he loves: good stone, sweet mint tea, knafeh from
Nablus, a wall that'll stand for centuries, and his daughter.

## Before

He grew up in Nablus, in a family of stonemasons. His grandfather and father worked the same yard, and Bashir learned to cut stone at twelve.
For 22 years he worked old walls and new ones: a yard, a chisel, and a chalk line snapped across every stone before he cut.

He married Samira at 22. She died in childbirth with their second child, who didn't survive either, when Rana was three. He raised Rana alone,
strictly and devotedly. He wanted her to be a doctor or a teacher.

The week before the Integration, Rana, nineteen and just started at Birzeit University, told him she was going to study engineering instead. He'd
been pleased, and proud, and he hadn't said so yet. He was going to tell her at the weekend.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in the yard, snapping a chalk line across a block, when it happened. He landed in Stranglethorn with the chalk line reel in his hand.

He went looking for Rana immediately and didn't stop for eleven months. He walked to four hubs in that time, on foot, through country that
kills people. He found nothing. He came back, said nothing about it to anybody, and started doing the work he does now.

He keeps a list of the places he hasn't searched. It's getting shorter.

## Now

He works at the edge of the corruption with a lantern, around the Drowned Shrine off Southern Stranglethorn, where the work is slow and almost
nobody volunteers.

He snaps the chalk line before he goes in anywhere. He's done it at every doorway for three years, and he does it without seeming to notice.

He's told one person that he intends to be useful in the meantime. That's the plan.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find Rana and finish the sentence.
- **Hope:** that she's building something somewhere.
- **Dream:** to see a bridge she designed.
- **Fear:** reaching the end of the list.
- **Fear:** that she never knew he was pleased.
- **Fear:** that he's become nothing but the work.

## How he's changed

In Nablus he was a severe, devoted father and a master of stone, who showed love by being strict.

Here he's walked further than anyone, alone, and found nothing, and he's turned his grief into slow, careful work. He's quieter than ever. Part of
what the player gives him is someone he can say the unfinished sentence to.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to search the last places on his list.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He asks you to come with him to the last three places on the list, the hardest, through corrupted country.
2. At each one, there's no sign of her. At the last, a camp of engineers building a bridge, the foreman has never heard of her.
3. He reaches the end of the list.

**Ending (still unknown):** there's no answer. He stands at the unfinished bridge for a long time. Then he says it out loud, to you, the sentence
he never said: that he was pleased she chose engineering, and proud, and that she'd be good at it. He asks the foreman if they need a mason. They
do. He stays for a season and helps them build the bridge, stone by stone, correctly. Then he starts a new list: every bridge being built
anywhere, because if she's alive, she's building one. He says the list is long, and he's glad.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Bashir's Chalk Line.** A stonemason's chalk line reel, blue-dusted, the one he was snapping across a block in Nablus when the worlds joined.
He's snapped it at every doorway since. He gives it to you after the bridge. He says he's found a straight line to follow now, and you should
have something for marking where to cut.

## Voice notes

- Severe, sparse and literal. Contractions, but very few words, and no softening at all.
- His English is careful and formal, with Palestinian Arabic: "yalla", "khalas", "ya rab", "habibti" when he speaks of Rana.
- He says exactly what he found, nothing more.
- He doesn't swear. When something's careless he says "this is not how it's done".
- **He won't talk about** Rana until Companion, Samira until Friend, or the unfinished sentence until the quest.
- **Habits:** snaps the chalk line at every doorway, runs his hand over stonework, works slowly, and drinks mint tea in silence.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** reports everything bluntly. Slow, careful and relentless against corruption. Uninterested in hurrying. Quietly gentle
  with students and young people in quests, which he doesn't notice.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with careful, honest craftspeople, especially Henrik and Haruto. Silent with chatterers. Has a lot in common
  with Fiona, and they'd argue about method.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the unfinished sentence. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he
  marks a line, and corrupted enemies that cross it take heavy holy damage.
