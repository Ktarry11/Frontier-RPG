## Who she is

Rosa is direct. She tells you what she thinks, immediately, and she doesn't care much whether you like it.

She's an expert and she knows it. She spent eight years in the bush learning that a predator isn't cruel and hunger
isn't personal. She got very tired of explaining both to people who needed the lion to mean something.

She loves wild places, because they don't care about her. That was most of what she loved about the savanna.

She's competitive with herself. Here the animals think, and a quilboar runs a plan and changes it when it sees you. She
had to stop tracking and start anticipating, and she was bad at it for a year. She hadn't been bad at anything in a
decade, and it nearly knocked her flat. She came out of it better and she isn't grateful.

She's funny in a blunt, self-mocking way. She'll tell anyone, straight away, that she spent a year being outclassed by
pigs.

What annoys her: people who make animals into symbols, tourists, noise, and anyone who wants the wild to be tame. What
she loves: long sightlines, sitting absolutely still in the dark, a good rifle, and the moment an animal does exactly
what she thought it would.

## Before

She grew up in Madrid, in a flat in Lavapiés, about as far from the sea and the wild as you can get in Spain. Her father
was a bus driver and her mother a hairdresser. She spent every summer at her grandparents' farm in Extremadura, watching
eagles, and decided at ten that she was going to Africa.

She studied biology, then went to Tanzania at 26 for a six-month research job and never came back. She became a guide
out of Arusha: researchers, photographers, and the occasional idiot with money. Eight years of dust, heat and a great
deal of sitting absolutely still.

Her father died when she was 31. Her mother sold the flat in Madrid and moved to Arusha to be near her, and opened a
little hair salon for the expats. Rosa pretended to be annoyed and was secretly very glad.

She had a few relationships, none of them for long. The last was with Wanjiru, a Kenyan bush pilot, which ended over a
disagreement about who was going to move. Before that there was Bram, a Dutch wildlife photographer, which ended because
he talked too much in the hide.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was out in the bush with a research group, at dawn, when it happened. She landed in the Barrens with her binoculars
round her neck and nothing else.

She spent a year being outclassed by pigs. That same year she found out the Arusha hub came down south of the range,
which is another way of saying her mother is on the far side of a mountain that wasn't there.

## Now

She works the Crossroads road in the Central Barrens, where the hyenas have worked out what a caravan is. It's close
enough to savanna to hurt: dry grass, long sightlines, big animals moving slow in the heat. She can tell you what a pack
is about to do four seconds before it does it. She's trying to teach that and finding it won't go into words.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** the coast. No reason. She grew up a long way from the sea, spent her career further from it, and she's
  decided she's owed.
- **Hope:** to get across the mountain to her mother one day.
- **Dream:** somewhere wild with no road through it.
- **Fear:** being bad at something again.
- **Fear:** her mother being alone on the far side.
- **Fear:** that the wild here will turn out to be tame too.

## How she's changed

In Tanzania she was the expert, the best guide in Arusha, sure of everything.

Here she was humbled for a year and came out the other side sharper and a little kinder. She listens more. She doesn't
assume. Part of what the player gives her is somebody to teach, which she's discovering she likes.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to read this world's wild as well as she read the savanna, and then to see the sea.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A hyena matriarch has been leading her pack against the Crossroads caravans with real cunning, and the caravan
   masters want it dead. Rosa takes the job.
2. The matriarch outthinks her twice. The third time Rosa stops tracking and anticipates, and gets ahead of it.
3. She has the matriarch in her sights.

**Ending (happy):** she doesn't shoot. She drives the pack off the road for good, by fire and noise in the right places,
because the matriarch was only hungry, and hunger isn't personal. The caravans are safe and the caravan masters are
furious about the bounty. Then she walks to Ratchet, takes her boots off, and walks into the sea for the first time in
her life, laughing. She says she's owed one more thing now, and that's the far side of the mountain, someday.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Rosa's Binoculars.** Battered field binoculars, the lenses scratched, the strap mended three times. She wore them every
day in the Tanzanian bush and they were round her neck when she landed. She gives them to you after Ratchet. She says
she's learned to see what's coming without them.

## Voice notes

- Blunt, fast and dry. Contractions, short sentences, and no patience for fuss.
- Her English is fluent, with a Madrid accent and Swahili words picked up in the bush: "pole pole" (slowly), "sawa"
  (okay).
- She talks about animals exactly, never sentimentally.
- She swears freely in Spanish.
- **She won't talk about** her mother until Companion, or her relationships until Friend.
- **Habits:** scans the horizon every few minutes, crouches to read tracks, sits very still when she's listening, and
  lifts her binoculars without thinking.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** reads what beasts will do and tells the party. Dislikes killing animals for no reason, but
  isn't sentimental about it. Loves open country and new ground. Impatient with slow parties.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with blunt, competent people, and with Tarek, who also hates needless killing.
  Clashes with sentimental people. Respects anyone who admits they're bad at something.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is the year she was outclassed and the pride it cost. A skill idea to
  look at then: on a cooldown of 10 to 15 turns, she reads the enemy's next three moves, and the party's attacks can't miss for those turns.
