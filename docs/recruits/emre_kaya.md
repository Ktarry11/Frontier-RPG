## Who he is

Emre thinks every lock is a short conversation with somebody who isn't in the room. He's never been a thief. He was the man
you called when you'd locked yourself out of your own livelihood, and he knew the inside of more businesses in his quarter
than their owners did.

He's content. Three contented years here, which makes him odd company among transplants, and he knows it.

He's honest with himself in a way he keeps private. He's content partly because the shop is gone, and so is everybody who'd
have wanted it kept open a certain way. He didn't lose a family. He lost an obligation. He's been lighter since, and he keeps
that to himself.

He's dry and quietly funny. He likes puzzles, patience and silence.

He has a fear nobody else would understand. He's afraid he'll never meet the lock that beats him.

What annoys him: people who interrupt, people who break doors that could be opened, and anyone who calls him a thief. What he
loves: a well-made lock, Turkish coffee, a quiet morning, and his mother's proverbs.

## Before

He grew up above his uncle's lock shop in the old bazaar quarter of Istanbul. At nine his uncle sent him to fetch a stuck
deadbolt, and he found out he could open it. Thirty doors a week for most of his life after that.

His uncle left him a roll of picks and the shop. Emre ran it for twenty-five years and watched four crafts in his quarter die
in his own lifetime: the coppersmiths, the bookbinders, the felt makers, the clockmakers. He knew the men who closed them.

His mother lived with him above the shop. *Bir kapı kapanır, bir kapı açılır.* One door closes, another opens. She said it
constantly. He thought it was a proverb about God until he was about thirty and decided it was a proverb about him. She died
the year before the Integration, and he hadn't decided what to do with the shop.

He never married. He says he never found a lock he couldn't open, and the women he met could tell.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was opening a jeweller's safe when it happened. He landed in Hillsbrad with his uncle's roll of picks in his jacket.

There was no adjustment worth the name. A bigger bazaar with worse customers.

Once, early on, he was working a door in an old ruin with his picks still in the mechanism, and the thing behind the door asked
him a question. He got the door open. He's never said whether he answered.

## Now

He lives in the country around Durnholde Keep in Eastern Hillsbrad. It's a keep full of doors built by people who were serious
about them. He isn't looting it. He's reading it: which lock came first, which were added after somebody got in, and so where
somebody got in.

He wants an apprentice. The trade dies with him otherwise. He's watched that happen four times to men he knew.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to meet the lock that beats him.
- **Hope:** an apprentice.
- **Dream:** his mother's coffee, and her saying the proverb one more time.
- **Fear:** that he'll never meet a lock he can't open.
- **Fear:** that his trade will die with him, like the others.
- **Fear:** that being lighter without his mother makes him a bad son.

## How he's changed

In Istanbul he was a respected craftsman carrying a dying trade, a dying quarter and his mother's expectations.

Here he's free, curious and content, and quietly guilty about it. Part of what the player gives him is somebody to tell the
truth to: about the lightness, and about the question.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to meet the lock that beats him, and to pass on the trade.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He's found a door at the very bottom of Durnholde that's older than the keep. Nobody's ever opened it. He asks you to come
   down with him and keep things off his back while he works.
2. He works it for a whole night. Then he tells you, while he works, what the thing behind the first door asked him three years
   ago: "Who are you opening it for?" He didn't answer. He didn't know.
3. Near dawn, he stops. This one beats him.

**Ending (still unknown):** he can't open it, and he decides not to try anything else. He sits back from it, laughing, the
happiest you've ever seen him. He'll never know what's behind it, and he doesn't want to. He says he's finally met the lock that
beats him, and the answer to the question is that he's opening doors for whoever comes after him. He takes an apprentice, a
sharp young Forsaken girl called Mae who's been picking his pockets for a month, and starts teaching her on that door.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**His Uncle's First Pick.** The oldest pick in his uncle's roll, the one his uncle gave him at nine to open the stuck deadbolt,
worn thin and bright. He gives it to you once Mae has her own roll. He says it opened his first door, and he'd like it to open
something for you.

## Voice notes

- Dry, calm and amused. Contractions, unhurried sentences, and the occasional Turkish proverb.
- His English is good and careful, with Turkish rhythm: "my friend", "inshallah", "tamam" (okay).
- He talks about locks like people having a conversation.
- He hates being interrupted and says so.
- He swears in Turkish, mildly, when a lock's badly made.
- **He won't talk about** his mother until Companion, the lightness until Friend, or the question until the quest.
- **Habits:** studies every lock he passes, turns a pick between his fingers, drinks coffee very strong, and goes completely
  still when he's listening to a mechanism.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** opens every lock and chest and wants silence while he does it. Reads dungeons by their doors. Cool and
  precise in a fight. Fascinated by sealed places.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with patient, clever people, and with Marco, the other locksmith, whom he'd argue with about
  everything. Finds Berat, his countryman, exhausting and loves him for it. Bored by brutes.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the question, and the guilt of feeling light. A skill idea to look at then:
  on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he finds the weak point in an enemy's defence, and every ally ignores its armour for a turn.
