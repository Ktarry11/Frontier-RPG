## Who he is

Henrik is careful. Engineering is being certain that a thing will hold before anybody stands on it. Arcane magic rewards that
temperament more than boldness, a fact he regards as the most underappreciated feature of this world.

He's precise in conversation to a degree people find either restful or maddening, and he's never been able to predict which.

He's calm and dry, with a very quiet, very Danish sense of humour.

He has a method, and it's the whole reason he's alive. He's calculated his own limits to four significant figures and he doesn't
approach them.

He stops calculating at one point, every time, and has done for three years.

What annoys him: recklessness, bad data, people who skip the safety margin, and being asked to guess. What he loves: a well-made
bridge, good coffee, cycling, his notes, and Mette.

## Before

He grew up in Copenhagen, the son of a ship's engineer and a librarian. He studied engineering and spent 21 years designing the
structures inside buildings and bridges: load calculations, tolerances, and a career of saying no to architects.

He married Mette, a physicist, at 26. They argued about everything happily for 25 years. They had two sons, Søren and Mikkel, who
both left home and both rang on Sundays.

On the morning of the Integration, Mette had a nine o'clock lecture.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on a building site, checking a steel frame, when it happened, at ten past nine. He landed in the cold hills above Hillsbrad
with a triangular scale rule in his jacket pocket.

He spent twenty months treating himself as a structure under an unfamiliar load and testing accordingly. His notes are the most
useful document on arcane tolerance anyone has produced here. He regards them as preliminary, and he'll argue the point.

He's worked out, with some care, that Mette would have been in a lecture theatre at ten past nine. He stops the calculation
precisely there, every time.

## Now

He works in the Frostward Descent, off Eastern Hillsbrad, as deep into the frozen workings as he can safely go, where the
arcane behaves in ways that would be impossible to reproduce anywhere else. He measures. He's filled eleven books and will tell
you the eleventh is the only one worth reading.

He wants a standard: a published figure other casters can work to, so the next person doesn't have to find the limit with their
own body.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to publish a standard that keeps people alive.
- **Hope:** that Mette is safe.
- **Dream:** an argument with Mette at breakfast about something that doesn't matter.
- **Fear:** finishing the calculation.
- **Fear:** a caster dying because nobody wrote down the limit.
- **Fear:** that he's using the measuring to avoid the grieving.

## How he's changed

In Copenhagen he was a careful engineer and a happily married man who argued for fun.

Here he's become the most careful mage on this world, and he's never once let himself finish one sum. Part of what the player
gives him is somebody to stand beside him while he does.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to finish the standard, and the calculation.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He needs one last set of measurements, from the very bottom of the Frostward Descent, where nobody's been. You go down with
   him.
2. At the bottom the arcane is at its strongest, and he has to work right at the limit he never approaches. He does it, and he
   lets you watch his back while he does.
3. On the way up, a Danish trader passes through Hillsbrad with a list from a camp of people from Copenhagen, including staff and
   students from the university.

**Ending (sad):** Mette's name is on it, among those who didn't survive. The lecture theatre came down in the Integration. He
reads it twice. Then he finishes the calculation, out loud, to you, all the way to the end, for the first time in three years. He
publishes his standard the next month, and he dedicates it to her, by name, on the first page. He says she'd have found three
errors in it, and he wishes she could.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Henrik's Scale Rule.** A triangular engineer's scale rule, aluminium, worn smooth on the edges, carried in his jacket on the
building site in Copenhagen. He gives it to you after the standard is published. He says he's measured enough, and you should
have something that reminds you to check before you stand on it.

## Voice notes

- Precise, calm and dry. Contractions, careful words, and numbers given exactly.
- His English is excellent and formal, very Danish: direct, understated, and no softening.
- He asks for the objective before he starts.
- He doesn't swear. When something's reckless he says "that is outside tolerance".
- **He won't talk about** Mette until Companion, or the lecture theatre until the quest.
- **Habits:** measures everything, writes it down, checks bridges before crossing, and stops mid-sentence when he gets close to
  the number.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** wants the plan before the pull. Precise and safe with his spells, never over-commits. Disapproves of
  reckless casters. Calm in a wipe.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with careful, precise people, especially Kesten, Haruto and Sophia. Fond of Freja, his
  countrywoman, who teases him. Clashes with gamblers and show-offs.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the unfinished calculation. A skill idea to look at then: once a fight, he
  works right at his limit, and his next spells hit far harder.
