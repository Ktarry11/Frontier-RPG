## Who he is

Gianluca talks without stopping. He remembers what everybody drinks, and he's defused more fights with a joke than most soldiers have with a
weapon.

He reads a room. Thirty years behind a counter, opening at six for the people going to work and closing late for the people avoiding going home.
He knows who's sad before they do.

He's immovable behind a shield, and nobody expected that from the man who won't shut up. He keeps up a running commentary in fights that the enemy
finds as unsettling as everyone else does.

He's warm and generous and a little vain. He's fussy about coffee to a degree that borders on religion. He's tried a dozen local substitutes and
rated every single one out of ten, unkindly.

He's a dreamer who doesn't act. He describes the bar he wants in enormous detail, constantly, and he's done nothing about it in three years.

What annoys him: bad coffee, silence, people who won't sit down, and Milanese people, on principle. What he loves: a proper espresso, the six
o'clock crowd, mortadella, his wife, and the moment a stranger becomes a regular.

## Before

He grew up behind the counter of his parents' bar in Bologna, near the university, and took it over at 22. For thirty years he ran it: a counter,
an espresso machine he rebuilt himself twice, and the six o'clock crowd who never ordered because he already knew.

He married Ornella at 24. She ran the kitchen and the money and, he says, most of him. They had two sons, Luca and Davide, who both moved to Milan,
which he took personally, and both rang on Sundays.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was behind the counter at six in the morning, tamping a shot, when it happened. He landed in Tanaris with the brass coffee tamper still in his
hand.

He built a bar. Not a real one: a spot by a fire where he stood and people came to him. He turned that into a job inside four months. He says he
didn't know what else to do with himself, and that what he actually missed was being the person everyone passes in the morning.

He walks to the hubs every few months. He reads the Bologna list and the Milan list, in that order, and he reads the Milan list twice.

## Now

He's at Steamwheedle Port, by a fire, where the goblins and the sailors and the caravan guards come to him. In a party he's at the front with a
shield, talking.

The brass tamper hangs at his belt. He has no coffee and no machine.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a real bar. A room, a door, a machine.
- **Hope:** to find Ornella and the boys.
- **Dream:** six o'clock in the morning, the machine hissing, Ornella in the kitchen, and the regulars at the counter.
- **Fear:** silence.
- **Fear:** that if he builds the bar, it means they're not coming.
- **Fear:** never tasting a real coffee again.

## How he's changed

In Bologna he was the heart of a neighbourhood, a husband who leaned on his wife, and a father of two sons he missed.

Here he's rebuilt the heart without the room, and found out he could stand in front of danger as well as he stood behind a counter. Part of what
the player gives him is a regular.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** a real bar, and real coffee.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A goblin trader swears he's found a bean in the far jungle that makes a drink Gianluca will rate ten out of ten. Gianluca doesn't believe him,
   and goes anyway. You go with him to get it.
2. The bean grows in dangerous jungle, and the plants are guarded. You get enough to grow.
3. He builds the bar at Steamwheedle Port, with a door and a goblin-made machine, and roasts the first batch.

**Ending (happy):** it's an eight. He's thrilled. He opens at six on the first morning, and the first customer through the door is a woman who's
heard about an Italian at Steamwheedle who rates everything out of ten. It's Ornella. She's been running a kitchen in a camp in Tanaris for two
years. She tastes his coffee and says it's a six. He says she's wrong. They argue about it for an hour, and then she goes into the kitchen without
being asked. The boys are still on the Milan list he reads twice, and he keeps reading it.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Gianluca's Coffee Tamper.** A brass barista's tamper, handle worn smooth, the one he was tamping a shot with at six in the morning in Bologna
when the worlds joined. He gives it to you once the bar's open. He says he's got a goblin-made one now, which is terrible, and you should have
something from the bar that was real.

## Voice notes

- He rates everything out of ten.
- He talks through fights, which unsettles enemies.
- He swears in Italian, cheerfully and constantly.
- **He won't talk about** the boys until Companion, or Ornella until Friend.
- **Habits:** touches the tamper on his belt, remembers what everyone drinks, rates every meal, and stands in front of whoever's sad.
