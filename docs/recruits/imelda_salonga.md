## Who she is

Imelda is a fighter. Her healing isn't gentle and doesn't look like healing. She hits things and her people get better. She can't
explain it in her own language either.

She's blunt and practical. Same six streets her whole life, five in the morning on the quay, boxing in the evenings. She doesn't do
fuss.

She's disciplined. She wraps her hands before anything else, breakfast included. She wraps them herself, because nobody else ever
did it tight enough.

She's funny in a dry, deadpan way, and she has a big, sudden laugh.

She wants things done properly. She's been asking at the arena whether anybody here fights properly, with rules, a count, somebody
keeping time. The answers have all been enthusiastic and none of them have been about that.

What annoys her: dirty fighting, people who think boxing is just hitting, and her mother calling it a phase. What she loves: a
clean fight, grilled fish, her mother's cooking, and the moment she lands a punch she's been setting up for three rounds.

## Before

She grew up in Navotas, a fishing district of Manila. Her father was a fisherman who drowned when she was six. Her mother, Lourdes,
sold fish on the quay, and Imelda was selling beside her by the age of ten.

At 17 she walked into a gym above a tyre shop and asked to learn to box. The owner laughed and then let her stay. She fought
amateur for nineteen years, about ten fights a year, and won more than she lost. She never made money from it. She didn't want to.

Her mother called the boxing a phase for nineteen years and came to exactly one fight. Imelda lost it.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was on the quay at market hour when it happened, with her hands already wrapped for the gym that evening. She landed in Thousand
Needles. She calls that luck and nobody's talked her out of it.

Two summers ago her mother turned up at Freewind Post on a goblin barge, having asked after her in four languages the whole way down
the coast. She lives in the Post now. She still calls it a phase.

## Now

She's at Freewind Post, where the tauren walk the lifts and the drop does the rest, and the injured come in steadily all week. The
Post asks for her by name without being able to say what she is.

She wants a proper fight: rules, a count, a ring, somebody keeping time.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a proper bout, with rules.
- **Hope:** that her mother will watch her fight once more, and see it.
- **Dream:** to win in front of her mother.
- **Fear:** that her mother will never see that it was never a phase.
- **Fear:** losing her hands.
- **Fear:** that she only knows how to heal by hurting something.

## How she's changed

In Navotas she was a fish seller and a boxer, stubborn and proud, with a mother who didn't understand her.

Here she found out her fists could heal, and her mother crossed half a world to find her. She's softer with her mother than she
was. Part of what the player gives her is someone who takes the fighting seriously.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** a proper fight, and her mother in the crowd.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. She hears the Gurubashi Arena will host a bout under rules if somebody can find a fighter worth it. She wants it.
2. You help her train, and help her get her mother to Stranglethorn safely.
3. The bout is against an orc champion, ten rounds, with a count and a timekeeper. Her mother is in the front row.

**Ending (sad):** Imelda wins in the ninth round. Her mother stands up and shouts, the only time Imelda's ever heard her shout.
Afterwards her mother wraps Imelda's hands for her, for the first time, and says it was never a phase. That winter, back at
Freewind, her mother dies in her sleep. She was old and tired from the journey, and she'd seen what she came to see. Imelda wraps
her hands every morning after that, the way her mother wrapped them, too loose, and won't fix it.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Imelda's Hand Wraps.** Soft cotton hand wraps from the gym above the tyre shop in Navotas, washed to grey and rewound every
morning for nineteen years. She gives you the old pair after her mother dies, because she's started using the pair her mother
wrapped. She says these ones were tight enough, and you should keep them.

## Voice notes

- Blunt, short and dry. Contractions, boxing terms, and very few wasted words.
- Her English is functional and direct, with Filipino expressions: "ay", "naku", "sige" (okay), "'nay" for her mother.
- She explains healing as fighting, because it's the only way she can.
- She swears in Tagalog when she misses.
- **She won't talk about** her father until Companion, or her mother's one fight until Friend.
- **Habits:** wraps her hands, shadowboxes when she's thinking, counts under her breath in rounds, and eats like a fighter.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** heals by hitting, which confuses everyone. Wants fights done cleanly. Hates enemies who fight dirty.
  Calm and focused in a long fight.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with disciplined fighters and blunt people, and with Iker, who'd train with her for hours.
  Clashes with show-offs and cheats. Quietly kind to mothers and old women.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is never being seen by her mother. A skill idea to look at then: once a fight,
  a combination of strikes where every hit heals the most wounded ally.
