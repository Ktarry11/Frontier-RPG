## Who he is

Driss reads sand the way a sailor reads water. Twenty-eight years of guiding caravans across ground where a wrong turn costs a camel
and a second wrong turn costs a man. He's careful, patient and almost never wrong.

He's courteous and hospitable to the bone. Water first, then tea, then whatever you came to say.

He's quietly spiritual. He asked the land for permission in his own language when he arrived, and something answered. He mentions
this rarely.

He's firm about the things that matter. He's refused two contracts over the water allowance, and both parties came back with a
better one.

He's warm and gently funny. He tells long stories by the fire, about the desert and about his children, and he's very good at it.

What annoys him: people who waste water, people who think they know the desert after a day, and greed. What he loves: mint tea, a
clear night in the desert, his children, a good camel, and the moment a caravan arrives safe.

## Before

He grew up in Zagora, the son and grandson of caravan guides. His father and grandfather walked the old routes south across the
desert, and Driss learned them on a camel before he could read. When the tourists came, he guided them too, first by camel and later
in a pair of battered Land Cruisers he kept running with his own hands.

He married Khadija at 22. They had four children. Three are grown: Youssef, an engineer in Casablanca, Amina, a teacher, and Karim,
who drives the trucks. The fourth, Nour, was nine, born long after the others, and the light of the house.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was out in the desert with a group of tourists, under a truck, fixing a broken axle with a torque wrench, when it happened. He
landed on the salt flats of Thousand Needles with the wrench still in his hand.

He asked the land for permission in his own language, and something answered. The rest of the adjustment was practical: new stars,
new seasons, and a year of getting the distances right.

Khadija and Nour were at home in Zagora. He hasn't found them, or any of his children.

## Now

He guides caravans out of Freewind Post, across the canyons of Thousand Needles and the Shimmering Flats, where the goblins pay for
a man who gets cargo across intact. He gets it across.

He wants a summer route across the Flats: a way through in the worst heat, when no caravan can cross now. There's a way through
and he has most of it. Finishing it means four more seasons of walking, and he has the seasons.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** the summer route.
- **Hope:** to find Khadija and Nour.
- **Dream:** tea with all four of his children at once, in Zagora, at sunset.
- **Fear:** that Nour is growing up without him.
- **Fear:** a caravan lost on his route.
- **Fear:** running out of water, for anyone.

## How he's changed

In Morocco he was a respected guide, a devoted father, and a man at home in the desert.

Here he's still a guide in a desert, and that's kept him steady. He's lonelier. He tells his stories about his children more often
than he used to. Part of what the player gives him is someone to tell them to.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to finish the summer route across the Flats.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The last stretch of the route needs water, and there's none on any map. Driss believes there's a spring under the salt, from the
   way the land talks to him. You go out into the summer flats with him to find it.
2. You find it, guarded by things that have been drinking from it alone for a long time.
3. The route is open. The first summer caravan he leads across comes from the south, from Tanaris, full of families who've been
   waiting two years for a way across.

**Ending (happy):** halfway across, on the second night, he hears a girl laughing at the back of the caravan, and he knows the laugh.
It's Nour, twelve now, and Khadija with her. They've been in a Moroccan camp in Tanaris for two years, unable to cross the Flats in
summer, waiting for a way. His route brought them. He walks the rest of the way with Nour on his shoulders, though she says she's too
old. That night he makes mint tea for the whole caravan.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Driss's Torque Wrench.** A short, stubby torque wrench from his Land Cruiser's toolkit, the one he was using to fix an axle in the
Sahara when the worlds joined. He's kept every caravan's cart running with it since. He gives it to you after Nour comes home. He says
his route's done, and you've got further to go than he has.

## Voice notes

- Courteous, calm and warm. Contractions, long gentle stories, and the useful thing said simply.
- His English is good, learned from tourists, with Moroccan Arabic and French: "inshallah", "bismillah", "yallah" (let's go),
  "habibi" for his children.
- He offers water and tea before anything else.
- He doesn't swear. When something's foolish, he says "the desert will teach him", and means it.
- **He won't talk about** Nour until Companion, or the land answering until Friend.
- **Habits:** checks the water supply of every party, reads the sky at dusk, fixes carts and wagons, and makes tea at every stop.
