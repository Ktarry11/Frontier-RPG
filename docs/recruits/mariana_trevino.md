## Who she is

Mariana is precise. She spent ten years lining up radiation beams to the millimetre, so they hit the tumour and nothing else. She heals with the Light now, and
she aims it the same way.

She's calm and kind, with a gallows humour she only lets other medics see.

She keeps records of everything. The priests in Orgrimmar said she prayed like an accountant, and she took it as a compliment.

She's brave in a quiet, informed way. When the liquidators asked for someone who wasn't afraid of the word radiation, she packed that night.

She remembers every patient's birthday. On their last day of treatment, she used to ring a little bell for them.

What annoys her: guessing, people who skip their treatment, and doctors who don't explain. What she loves: running at five in the morning, her brother's tacos,
a neat chart, and the sound of a patient ringing the bell.

## Before

She grew up in Monterrey, the daughter of a steelworker and a schoolteacher, with an older brother, Beto, who ran a taco stand by the stadium and fed half the
city on match days. She trained as a radiation therapist and worked ten years on the same treatment floor of a cancer centre.

She had a running club that met at five in the morning, and she ran every day before work. She remembered every patient's birthday, and on the last day of their
treatment she let them ring a little brass bell by the door, the way some cancer centres do.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was on the treatment floor, lining up a beam, when it happened. She landed in the Barrens with her hospital ID badge on its teal lanyard round her neck.

She found the Light the way she'd found the right dose: carefully, and with a lot of notes.

She hasn't found her parents or Beto.

## Now

She's in the infirmary at the liquidators' camp at Ariden's Watch in Deadwind Pass, with a lamp and a stack of notebooks. The Forsaken apothecaries bring her
their worst cases, and they've stopped arguing with her charts.

She wants to keep a log of everything the fallout does to a body, so whoever comes next has something to work from. She'd also like to run again, somewhere
that doesn't glow.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a log good enough that the next healer doesn't start from nothing.
- **Hope:** to find Beto and her parents.
- **Dream:** a five o'clock run in Monterrey, then Beto's tacos.
- **Fear:** a dose she gets wrong.
- **Fear:** the fallout getting into her before the log is finished.
- **Fear:** never ringing the bell for anyone again.

## How she's changed

In Monterrey she was a precise, kind therapist with a running habit and a brother who fed the city.

Here she's become the person who isn't afraid of the invisible thing, in the most dangerous place on this world. She's braver, and more alone. Part of what the
player gives her is somebody who listens to her explain.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to treat the fallout sickness, and to ring the bell again.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A young liquidator, Danylo, has the fallout sickness badly. Her log says it can be treated, with a course of the Light aimed exactly, every day for three
   weeks, and a medicine made from something that grows only in the worst ground.
2. You get the medicine with her, from the Exclusion Zone's worst ground, and she treats Danylo every day, precisely, with notes.
3. On the last day, he's clear.

**Ending (happy):** she gives him a little brass bell she's had a goblin make, and he rings it at the infirmary door, and the whole camp cheers. It's the
first time anyone's beaten the fallout sickness. She writes it all down, so anyone can do it. A month later a letter comes from a Mexican camp in the Barrens:
Beto has a taco stand there, and the liquidators' supply carts have been telling stories about a Mexican healer in Deadwind who rings bells. He's saving her a
table. She goes running the next morning, along the road out of Deadwind, where nothing glows.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Mariana's Hospital Badge.** Her hospital ID badge on its teal lanyard, from the cancer centre in Monterrey, round her neck the day she landed. She gives it to
you after Danylo rings the bell. She says she has her own infirmary now, and doesn't need anyone's permission to be there.

## Voice notes

- Calm, precise and warm. Contractions, clear explanations before every step, and medical terms translated kindly.
- Her English is fluent, with Mexican Spanish: "a ver" (let's see), "mija", "órale", "ándale".
- She explains everything before she does it.
- She swears quietly in Spanish, only in front of other medics.
- **She won't talk about** the patients she lost until Friend, or her family until Companion.
- **Habits:** writes everything down, checks every dose twice, runs at dawn, and remembers everyone's birthday.
