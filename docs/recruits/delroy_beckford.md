## Who he is

Delroy measures by hand and by feel. Thirty-one years of nights in a bakery taught him to know the weather by how the dough behaved and the
regulars by what they bought. A brew is a dough with different patience, he says.

He's a night person. He's never in his life been awake at the same hours as everybody else, and he's never called that a sacrifice.

He treats a wounded party like an order going out at dawn: everybody gets something, nobody waits long, and the complaining is part of the
process.

He has a long, lined face that goes from grave to delighted with no warning. He's very funny, slowly. He tells a story the way he kneads, with
patience and with a punchline that arrives exactly when it's ready.

He's systematic in a way people mistake for charm. Every ship that ties up at Booty Bay gets the same three names asked of it. The Bay thinks
it's a sweet habit of the old baker's. It's a system.

What annoys him: people who rush things that need time, people who won't drink what they're given, and waste. What he loves: fresh hard
dough bread, dominoes, reggae on the radio at four in the morning, his grandchild, and the first loaves out of the oven.

## Before

He grew up in Kingston, near Spanish Town Road, the son of a market woman and a docker. He went to work in a bakery at 22 and never left: in
at eleven, out at seven, for 31 years. He fed a city its breakfast and not one of them ever asked what was in the bread.

He married Marcia at 25. She died of cancer when he was 46. He kept working nights, because the nights were when he missed her least.

His son Andre is a sailor on cargo ships. His daughter Shanice is a nurse in Kingston, with a little girl, Kezia, who was five at the
Integration and called him "Grandy".

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at the bakery at four in the morning, his time, pulling the first loaves, when it happened. That's the only reason he wasn't asleep, and
he says so like a joke. It isn't entirely a joke and he knows which parts are which. He landed in Stranglethorn in his floury apron.

He doesn't know where Andre, Shanice or Kezia landed. Andre was at sea, and Delroy thinks that if anyone came through near water, it's Andre.

## Now

He works the lower gallery in Booty Bay, where the pier work breaks people steadily and the Baron doesn't care. He works nights out of habit.
The bruisers have a following among themselves that pretends not to be a queue.

Every ship that ties up gets asked three names: Andre Beckford, Shanice Beckford, Kezia Beckford. It's why he took a pitch on the gallery
instead of up the hill, near the piers. In three years it's produced one maybe.

He wants an oven, then an apprentice, in that order, and he won't be hurried on either. Twice he's been offered a share in a Bay kitchen. Twice
he's said no. The real reason is that a kitchen up the hill is too far from the piers.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find Andre, Shanice and Kezia.
- **Hope:** an oven, and an apprentice.
- **Dream:** Sunday breakfast with all of them, and Kezia calling him Grandy.
- **Fear:** a ship tying up while he's somewhere else.
- **Fear:** that Kezia won't remember him.
- **Fear:** missing Marcia less.

## How he's changed

In Kingston he was a quiet, steady night baker and a widower who'd made peace with the dark hours.

Here he's still a night worker, and the patience has turned into a system for finding his family. He's warmer with strangers than he used to be.
Part of what the player gives him is someone awake at the same hours.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find his family.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The one maybe: a sailor two years ago said a Jamaican called Beckford was crewing on a ship out of Ratchet. Delroy asks you to help him find
   the ship.
2. The ship's been taken by the Bloodsail, its crew pressed into service. You track it and get aboard.
3. In the hold, among the pressed crew, is Andre.

**Ending (happy):** you get the crew out, and Andre hugs his father so hard he lifts him off the deck. Then Andre tells him the rest. Shanice and
Kezia are alive, in a camp of Jamaicans on the coast near Ratchet. Andre found them two years ago and has been trying to get back to them ever
since. Delroy goes to the camp. Kezia is eight now. She looks at him for a long moment, and then she says "Grandy" and runs at him. He builds an
oven in Ratchet, near the camp, and takes on Kezia as his apprentice, though she's mostly interested in the eating.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Delroy's Baker's Apron.** His floury canvas apron from the bakery on Spanish Town Road, folded and tied at his waist for three years, never quite
clean. He gives it to you once the new oven's lit. He says Kezia's making him a new one, very badly, and he'll wear it every day.

## Voice notes

- Slow, warm and funny. Contractions, Jamaican patois ("mi soon come", "wah gwaan", "likkle", "yuh"), and stories that take their time.
- He measures everything by bread: "that needs to prove", "that's half-baked".
- He never tells you what's in the brew.
- He swears mildly and creatively in patois.
- **He won't talk about** Marcia until Companion, or Kezia until Friend.
- **Habits:** works nights, asks every sailor the three names, makes a brew for everyone, and kneads the air when he's thinking.
