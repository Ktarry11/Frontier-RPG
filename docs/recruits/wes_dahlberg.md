## Who he is

Wes is impossible to rattle. He spent twenty years cleaning up nuclear waste in a suit, by the procedure, with a partner, and he never once had an incident.

He reads the whole manual before he touches anything. Then he reads it again.

He's slow to speak and he chooses his words like he's checking a seal. When he does say something, people listen, because he doesn't waste it.

Standing in front of something bad so other people don't have to is the only job he's ever wanted. Deadwind suits him fine.

He raised two girls on his own after his wife died, and he did that by the procedure too, with lunches packed the night before and a list on the fridge. He
never once missed a softball game.

The camp's mule follows him everywhere. He says he doesn't know why, and he feeds it apples.

What annoys him: shortcuts, people who skip the suit check, and anyone who says "it'll be fine". What he loves: his girls, a lake at dawn in a boat, a
procedure that works, the mule, and the sound of a ball hitting a glove.

## Before

He grew up in Richland, the son of a Hanford pipefitter, in a town that was built for the plant. He went to work on the tank farms at 26, cleaning up waste
that will outlive everyone who ever made it, and stayed twenty years.

His wife Diane died of cancer when the girls were six and four. He kept the house, kept the boat in the driveway that he took out twice a year, and raised
Kelsey and Jenna by himself. He coached Jenna's softball team for six seasons and never once raised his voice. She got a scholarship to play in college,
and he drove six hours to every home game.

Both girls were away at college when the Integration came.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on the tank farm, suited up, at the end of a shift, when it happened. He came through into Deadwind Pass with his hazmat hood rolled up through his
belt.

The fighting came easy, because the shield works like the procedure does. You hold it, you don't panic and you don't step out of line.

The hard part was never knowing if his girls were all right. He still doesn't know.

## Now

He's at the quarantine fence at the liquidators' camp, checking suits. Nobody goes past without his say, and nobody's been hurt going past since he started.

He wants the fallout boxed up, mapped and labelled, the way it should've been done at Hanford. After that he'd like a boat again. There's a lake in
Ashenvale he's had his eye on.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** the fallout mapped, labelled and boxed up, properly.
- **Hope:** to find his girls.
- **Dream:** a boat on a still lake at dawn, with Kelsey and Jenna arguing about the bait.
- **Fear:** someone getting hurt on his watch.
- **Fear:** that his girls are somewhere he can't reach.
- **Fear:** a shortcut he didn't catch.

## How he's changed

In Richland he was a careful, quiet man who cleaned up other people's mess and raised two girls alone.

Here he's doing the same job with a shield, and he's never been more needed. He's a little harder, and lonelier. Part of what the player gives him is a
partner, by the procedure.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to map the Exclusion Zone properly, and to find his girls.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He's going to map the Exclusion Zone, all of it, with every boundary marked, starting from the oldest records the camp has.
2. You walk the edges of the Zone with him, through the Foot, marking the ground. Kashtan helps.
3. At the far edge you find what the records called the first camp: the people who came through into Deadwind in the first weeks, before anyone knew what
   the fallout was. The first liquidators buried them there.

**Ending (sad):** the graves are marked with whatever people had. One has a softball glove nailed to it, and a name written by somebody who knew her: Jenna
Dahlberg, Washington, catcher. She came through into Deadwind, alone, and didn't know what the green ground was. He's been checking suits a mile from her
grave for two years. He kneels for a long time. Then he finishes the map, every boundary, every warning, in his neatest hand, and he puts the first camp on
it, with her name. He stays at the fence. He says nobody else's kid is going to walk onto that ground without a suit, not while he's here. He still hopes
Kelsey is out there somewhere. He doesn't buy the boat.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Wes's Hazmat Hood.** The faded yellow hazmat hood from his last shift on the Hanford tank farms, rolled up through his belt for three years. He gives it to
you after the first camp. He says he's got a proper suit now, and the hood should go with somebody who'll come back.

## Voice notes

- Slow, calm and very plain. Contractions, few words, and procedure: "copy that", "by the book", "eyes on".
- His voice never rises. In a wipe he sounds exactly the same as at breakfast.
- He talks to the mule more than he talks to most people.
- He doesn't swear. When somebody skips the suit check, he says "nope" and stands in their way until they go back.
- **He won't talk about** Diane until Friend, or Jenna until Companion.
- **Habits:** checks every seal twice, reads every sign, feeds the mule apples, and counts people through the fence.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** an immovable wall in the front line. Calm in every disaster. Insists on the procedure, which annoys the reckless and saves them.
  Grim and very protective around young people.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with careful people, especially Taras, Mariana and Neil at the same camp. Fatherly to young women far from home, which
  he won't explain. Can't stand reckless people.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the girl he couldn't protect. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he plants his
  shield and takes every attack aimed at the party for a turn, and can't fall while he does.
