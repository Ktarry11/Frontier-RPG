## Who he is

Owen is honest about the worst thing about him. Something in the dark answered him when he arrived, and the terrible part is
that he was glad. He knows how that sounds. He says it anyway, early, so nobody can say he hid it.

He's lonely, and he's used to it. Eight years alone in a cab through the small hours, with the radio for company. He talked to
himself for a living.

He's gentle and kind, especially with people who are struggling. He's polite in an old-fashioned Welsh way.

He's frightened of himself. He fell straight in. There was no moment of resistance, and he's examined that honestly and found
it worse than the falling.

He keeps a record. He writes down everything the voice says, so that if he ever goes wrong, somebody will have the route.

What annoys him: people who won't say what they mean, people who treat the lonely like they're odd, and silence that's too
long. What he loves: rugby, a pint, the radio at three in the morning, his mother's singing, and being useful.

## Before

He grew up in Barry, on the Welsh coast, with his mother. His father left when he was three. His mother worked in a bakery
and sang in a chapel choir, and she sang to him every night.

He drove buses, then vans, then got his HGV licence at 39 and drove long-haul out of Cardiff: motorways, laybys, a flat he
barely lived in. Eight years of talking to the radio and to himself.

His mother went into a home in Barry when he was 41, with dementia. He visited every second Sunday for six years. She forgot
who he was by the end. He says that makes this easier, and the people who hear it understand that it doesn't.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in his cab on the M4 at three in the morning when it happened. He landed in Hillsbrad with his CB radio handset still
in his fist, the coiled cord snapped off.

He fell straight in. In his first week he went into the Azurelode Mine for shelter, and in the dark something answered when he
talked to himself. It's been talking to him ever since.

What he'll say, when pressed, is that eight years of talking to nobody is good preparation for talking to something.

## Now

He lives deep in the Azurelode Mine in Western Hillsbrad, where the air is heavy and most people get headaches and leave. He
gets sentences. He stays.

He wants to know what it wants. Properly. He's had hints for three years and he's tired of hints.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to understand what's talking to him.
- **Hope:** that he's still the one in charge.
- **Dream:** his mother singing in the chapel, knowing who he is.
- **Fear:** that he's going wrong and won't notice.
- **Fear:** that if the voice stops, he'll be alone again.
- **Fear:** that he was glad.

## How he's changed

In Wales he was a lonely, decent man who'd lost his mother twice, once to the home and once to her forgetting.

Here he found something that talks back, and it's the most dangerous thing in his life and the least lonely he's ever been. Part
of what the player gives him is a voice that isn't in the dark.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find out what the voice wants.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The voice finally gives him more than hints. It wants him to go to the deepest part of the mine and open something that's
   been sealed.
2. You go down with him. On the way, the voice offers him what it knows he wants: his mother, singing, knowing him again.
3. At the bottom is the seal. He has his hand on it.

**Ending (sad):** he doesn't open it. He tells it no, out loud, and then he asks you to help him make the seal stronger. When
it's done, the voice goes silent. It never speaks to him again. He's alone in his own head for the first time in three years,
and it's the loneliest he's ever been. He comes up out of the mine and doesn't go back. He gives his record of everything it
ever said to the Forsaken apothecaries, so somebody has the route. He says he'll miss it, and he's ashamed of that, and he's
telling you because he promised himself he'd always say the worst thing first.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Owen's CB Handset.** The handset from his lorry's CB radio, its coiled cord snapped off, the one he talked into through eight
years of night driving. It was in his fist when he landed. He gives it to you after the seal. He says he doesn't need to talk
into the dark any more, and he'd rather talk to you.

## Voice notes

- Quiet, gentle and honest. Contractions, a soft Welsh lilt, and "mind" and "tidy" and "now then".
- He says the worst thing about himself first.
- He sometimes stops mid-sentence, listening to something nobody else hears. After the quest, he stops doing it.
- He doesn't swear much. "Duw" when he's surprised.
- **He won't talk about** his mother until Companion, or what the voice says until Friend.
- **Habits:** talks to himself, writes in his notebook, hums chapel hymns, and holds the handset when it's quiet.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** holds the line, steady and quiet. Stops to listen in dark places. Uneasy and honest around cults and
  dark magic in quests. Very kind to lonely quest givers.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with gentle, honest people, and with other night workers like Ama and Minh. Distrusted by
  some, which he accepts. Clashes with people who mock the lonely.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the voice, and saying no to it. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown,
  he takes the darkness that would hit the party into himself and holds it.
