## Who he is

Ramiro listens. He learned young that the mountain talks before it moves, and he's never stopped listening. The earth answers him now, which he says is
only fair after all the years he spent listening to it.

He's steady, proud and quietly funny. He doesn't waste words, and the few jokes he makes are dry and land late.

He's a craftsman of the underground. The Dark Iron of Onyxhollow took to him because he hears the rock the way they do. He's teaching their apprentices to
timber a drift the way his father did, and they've stopped laughing at the method.

He's proud of his people, the miners of Potosí, who've gone into that mountain for five hundred years.

What annoys him: careless miners, people who think the earth is dead, and cooperatives that don't pay. What he loves: good stone, coca tea, his
daughters, a football match on the radio after a shift, and the moment the mountain goes quiet and safe.

## Before

He grew up in Potosí, under the Cerro Rico, the silver mountain that's been mined since the Spanish came. His father and grandfather were miners, and so
was he, from the age of nineteen: twelve-hour shifts, a cooperative that never paid on time, and the old offering to El Tío, the spirit of the mine,
every Friday.

He married Rosario at 22. She ran a juice stall in the market. They had two daughters, Maribel and Sonia. Maribel, the elder, wanted to be a geologist,
and he was saving for her to go to university, so she'd study the mountain instead of dying in it.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was deep in the mine, on a twelve-hour shift, when it happened. He came through underground, which was the one thing that made sense, with his brass
carbide headlamp on his belt.

The first stone that lifted itself into his hand frightened him badly. The second one he asked politely.

He wants to find out whether Potosí came through, and whether anyone is still underneath it. His whole shift was in the mine with him.

## Now

He's in Onyxhollow, in the mining quarter, mostly listening. The carbide lamp on his belt hasn't had fuel in three years. He keeps it polished anyway.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find out if Potosí came through, and if his family and his shift are alive.
- **Hope:** that Maribel became a geologist.
- **Dream:** Friday evening, after a shift, with Rosario's juice and the girls doing homework.
- **Fear:** that his shift is still underneath somewhere.
- **Fear:** not hearing the mountain in time.
- **Fear:** that his daughters think he died in the mine, like so many fathers.

## How he's changed

In Potosí he was a proud, tired miner, saving every coin so his daughter wouldn't follow him underground.

Here the earth speaks to him, and the Dark Iron respect him. He's prouder and lonelier. Part of what the player gives him is someone to listen to the
rock with.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find out what happened to Potosí.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. Deep below Onyxhollow, the Dark Iron break into old tunnels that aren't theirs: timbered the way his father timbered, with a Bolivian miners' shrine
   at a junction. Part of the Cerro Rico came through, underground.
2. You go down with him, into tunnels that are slowly collapsing, listening to the rock the whole way.
3. You find his shift.

**Ending (sad):** they didn't make it out. The tunnels came through sealed, and his shift, the men he worked beside for twenty years, died down there in
the first weeks, waiting for a rescue that couldn't come. Ramiro found his way out only because he was in a different drift. He brings them up, one by
one, with the Dark Iron's help, and buries them in good stone under Onyxhollow, with an offering to El Tío. His family wasn't underground; they were in
the city above, and he still doesn't know where the city landed. He keeps the shift's names carved over the tunnel mouth.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Ramiro's Carbide Lamp.** A dented brass carbide headlamp from the Cerro Rico, out of fuel for three years and polished anyway. He gives it to you
after he brings his shift up. He says the men he worked beside are out of the dark now, and he doesn't need a light to find them.

## Voice notes

- Quiet, steady and dry. Contractions, mining terms, and short sentences.
- His English is careful and Andean, with Spanish and Quechua: "compadre", "ya", "pues", "tupananchiskama" (until we meet again).
- He tells people to listen.
- He swears quietly in Spanish when the rock groans.
- **He won't talk about** his shift until Friend, or his daughters until Companion.
- **Habits:** listens to the walls, chews coca when he can find something like it, polishes the lamp, and checks every ceiling.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** warns of collapses and traps in caves. Steady and powerful in a fight. Happiest underground, uneasy in big open skies.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with workers and craftspeople, especially Marta and Eino. Respects the Dark Iron's ways. Quiet with talkers.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the shift he left underneath. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he raises
  stone walls around the party that absorb damage until they crumble.
