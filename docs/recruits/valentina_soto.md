## Who she is

Valentina is a born teacher. Give her a sky and ten strangers and she'll have them all in love with it within the hour.

She's bright and curious and talks fast, in three languages, and gets completely lost in the sky. She'll stop mid-sentence because a meteor went over.

She always found the Southern Cross first, for every tour. She always cried a little when someone saw the Milky Way for the first time, and she never
pretended she wasn't.

She's braver than she looks. She's fought by starlight for two years, and she fights the way she guided, quick and sure-footed in the dark.

She's stubborn about the dark. She carries a little red torch so she doesn't ruin her night eyes, and she makes visitors use it too.

What annoys her: light pollution, people on their phones under the stars, and anyone who says the sky's just the sky. What she loves: the Atacama at night,
her mother's empanadas, the hostel dogs, a clear sky, and the moment somebody gasps.

## Before

She grew up in Antofagasta, the daughter of a nurse who raised her and her little brother Pablo alone. Her mother took them out into the desert once a year
to see the stars, and Valentina decided at nine she was going to spend her life under them.

She moved to San Pedro de Atacama at 21 and guided night-sky tours: a hostel with a telescope on the roof, four dogs that belonged to nobody, and a minibus
that took tourists out past the salt flats at midnight. She was studying for an astronomy degree by correspondence, and she was two courses short.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was on the hostel roof, at the telescope, showing a couple from Brazil the Southern Cross, when it happened. She came through with the red torch on its
cord round her neck.

The first night she looked up, she didn't recognise a single star. She sat down and cried, and then she started drawing new charts.

By the end of the month the stars were answering her, and she's been fighting by their light since. The druids of Moonglade took her in and started
teaching her what the stars mean here.

She hasn't found her mother or Pablo.

## Now

She's in Moonglade, out by the lake, where she can see the most sky. She has a chart pinned to a board, half of this world's sky drawn in, with her own
names for the constellations in pencil.

She wants to finish the chart, all of it, and name the constellations properly. She also wants to know if Earth's sun is up there somewhere, and she's
afraid of the answer.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to finish the chart, the whole sky.
- **Hope:** to find her mother and Pablo.
- **Dream:** taking her mother out into the desert one more time, and showing her a sky Valentina drew herself.
- **Fear:** that Earth's sun isn't up there.
- **Fear:** that she'll never see the Southern Cross again.
- **Fear:** that her mother is looking at a sky she doesn't recognise either, alone.

## How she's changed

In the Atacama she was a bright, talkative guide to a sky she knew by heart, two courses from a degree.

Here the sky is a stranger, and she's become the person who's learning it from scratch, and fighting by it. She's braver, and sadder at night. Part of what
the player gives her is somebody to show it to.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to finish the chart, and to find Earth's sun.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The last part of the sky can only be seen from the highest ridge above Moonglade, on the clearest nights of the year. She needs to be up there for a
   week of them.
2. You climb with her and keep watch while she draws, night after night, through everything that comes up the ridge in the dark.
3. On the last night she finishes it. Then she spends three days checking it against Astrid's northern notebook, star by star, the whole sky.

**Ending (sad):** Earth's sun isn't there. She checks every star that could be it, every one the right colour and the right brightness, and none of them is.
She's sure now. It's not that it's too far to see. It's not in this sky at all. She cries, like she did the first night. Then she takes her pencil and
names the brightest four stars in the south the Southern Cross, anyway, because somebody should, and she teaches it to every visitor at the lake. She says
her mother will be looking up somewhere, at the same sky, and she'll want to know which way is south.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Valentina's Red Torch.** The little red-lens torch she carried on every night tour in the Atacama, so nobody ruined their night eyes, round her neck when she
came through. She gives it to you after the chart. She says you'll need it if you ever want to see the Southern Cross, and she'll show you where it is.

## Voice notes

- Bright, fast and warm. Contractions, three languages at once, and Chilean Spanish: "po" (on the end of everything), "cachai" (get it?), "bacán" (great),
  "qué lindo".
- She explains the sky to everyone, whether they asked or not.
- She stops mid-sentence for meteors.
- She swears in Chilean when someone shines a light in her eyes.
- **She won't talk about** Earth's sun until Companion, or her mother until Friend.
- **Habits:** looks up constantly, makes people use the red torch, names stars, and draws on anything flat.
