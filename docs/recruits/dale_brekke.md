## Who he is

Dale is very hard to hurry. He's spent his whole life waiting on weather, and he's learned that most things worth having come up in their own time.

He's big, slow-spoken and kind, in a plain Lutheran way that doesn't make a fuss. He'll help you load your cart before you've asked, and then he'll go back to
his row without waiting to be thanked.

He knows how to bring a harvest in before the weather turns. When he found he could swing a scythe at the dead, it didn't feel like a new job. It felt like
the old one.

He's an optimist the way only farmers are. He's had hail, drought and a flood, and he planted again every spring.

He tests the soil of every field he walks through, and he writes the results on the back of seed packets in a carpenter's pencil.

What annoys him: hurrying, waste, people who don't wipe their boots, and anyone who says nothing will grow here. What he loves: the smell of the first rain
on dry dirt, Joan's coffee, a combine running clean, Sunday phone calls with his kids, and a field of wheat moving in the wind.

## Before

He grew up on the farm outside Minot, the fourth Brekke to work it. His great-grandfather broke the sod with horses. Dale took it over at 26 when his dad's
back gave out, and he ran two thousand acres of hard red spring wheat, mostly by himself.

He married Joan, who he'd known since confirmation class, and she ran the county farmers' co-op for twenty years and knew everybody's business and kept all
of it to herself. Their kids, Nils and Kari, grew up and went to Fargo and Denver, and they called every Sunday after church.

He'd paid off the combine the spring before. He said it was the first time in his life he hadn't owed the bank anything, and he took Joan out for steak.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on the combine, on the last pass of the harvest, when it happened. He came through into the Eastern Plaguelands with the seed-company cap still on
his head and a packet of seed wheat in his shirt pocket.

He took his time, the way he takes it with everything. He sat in a dead field for most of a day, rubbing the grey soil between his fingers.

The first time he swung the scythe at something dead, he said it felt like the last pass of a long harvest.

He hasn't found Joan or the kids.

## Now

He's out in the plagued fields of the east, working them one row at a time. The Argent Dawn have stopped asking him to come in at night. He'll come in when
the row's done.

The seed-company cap tucked in his belt is the one he wore on the combine, and he puts it on when the work's done. The packet of seed is still in his shirt
pocket. He hasn't opened it.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** wheat that comes up clean in the Eastern Plaguelands.
- **Hope:** to find Joan.
- **Dream:** the whole family at the kitchen table at harvest, with Joan's coffee and too much pie.
- **Fear:** that the ground here is past saving, and so's everything on it.
- **Fear:** that he'll open the seed packet and waste it.
- **Fear:** that Joan's alone and nobody's looking after her the way she looked after everybody.

## How he's changed

In North Dakota he was a patient farmer, with a paid-off combine and a wife he adored, waiting on the weather like his father did.

Here he harvests the dead instead of the wheat, and it's made him quieter, and more stubborn about the living. Part of what the player gives him is somebody
who'll help him bring something in.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to grow clean wheat in the Eastern Plaguelands.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. His seed packets say one field, in a fold of the eastern hills, is very nearly clean. He says it's the one, and he's ready to open the packet.
2. You clear the field with him, row by row, and help him burn out the last of the blight before he sows.
3. He plants it, and you stand guard over it with him through a whole season, against everything that comes out of the dark at it.

**Ending (happy):** it comes up clean, green and then gold, the first clean wheat in the Eastern Plaguelands since the plague. He brings it in with the scythe
by hand. The Argent Dawn bake bread from it at Light's Hope, and the whole chapel eats it. One of the supply drivers tastes it and says it's like the bread
from the farmers' co-op at Southshore, which is run by a North Dakota woman who knows everybody's business and keeps all of it to herself. Dale puts on the
cap. He takes Joan a loaf. She cries, and then she tells him he's late, and he says the row wasn't done.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Dale's Seed Cap.** The green seed-company cap he wore on the combine, on the last pass of the harvest outside Minot, and tucked in his belt ever since.
He gives it to you at Southshore, after Joan. He says Joan's bought him a new one, and she won't have him wearing that old thing in front of the co-op.

## Voice notes

- Slow, plain and kind. Contractions, long pauses, and Upper Midwest: "you bet", "oh for cute", "uff da", "ish" (for gross).
- He talks about everything in terms of weather, seasons and soil.
- He never hurries a sentence, even in a fight.
- He doesn't swear. "Uff da" covers nearly everything, and "well, that's a deal" covers the rest.
- **He won't talk about** Joan until Friend, or the kids until Companion.
- **Habits:** tests soil, writes on seed packets, pats his shirt pocket, and wipes his boots before every door.
