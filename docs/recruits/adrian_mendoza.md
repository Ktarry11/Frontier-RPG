## Who he is

Adrián is fair. It's the most important thing about him. He'll tell you when you're wrong, and he'll tell you just as
plainly when he was. He holds everyone to the same rules, himself first, and he'd rather be disliked than be unfair.

He's formal, and the formality is something he put on so young that he doesn't notice it any more. He says
"properly" a lot. He stands up straight. He explains things once, clearly, and if you make him say it twice he will,
but you'll be able to tell.

Under that he's warm, much warmer than he lets himself look. He warms up to people fast. He notices who hasn't eaten,
who's limping and who's gone quiet. He carries other people's packs without being asked and gets embarrassed if you
thank him.

He's proud. He doesn't like having things taken off him, and he hates being made to look careless. He's also more
easily shaken than he lets on. He refereed for twenty-one years and never once saw anybody die, and here people die in
front of him. He handles it in the moment and has trouble with it afterwards.

What annoys him: people who break their word, people who change the plan halfway and don't say so, and anybody who
won't own up to a mistake. What he loves: a plan that works, a full party where everybody knows their job, and a good
hard fight that was fair.

## Before

His parents had him late. His father was a postman and his mother worked in a dry cleaner's, and they were both in
their forties when he was born. It was a quiet flat with clear rules, and he was a quiet, serious boy.

He loved football and he wasn't any good at it. He was slow and heavy as a kid and was always picked last. At 16 he
found out you could train as a referee, and he knew the laws of the game better than anyone he'd ever played with.
That was his way in. He didn't have to be good at football to be on the pitch, he just had to be right.

He trained as a PE teacher and refereed regional football at weekends for nine years. It was hard work for very
little money, in small towns where the crowd was close enough to spit on you, and sometimes did. He'll tell you it
was harder than the professional game, and he's right.

At 28 he got promoted to the Segunda División and gave up teaching. His father drove two hours to Burgos to watch his
first match from the stand. His father died eight months later, and his mother died the year after. He doesn't have
any brothers or sisters, so after that it was just him.

He lived in a flat above a bicycle shop. He worked every Saturday and half of most Sundays, so friends stopped
inviting him to things, and he didn't chase them. He was engaged once, in his early thirties, to a woman called Elena.
She left after three years. She said he was never there at weekends, and that he talked to her like he was
officiating. He thinks she was right about both.

The match he still thinks about was in his ninth season. Ninety-first minute, the home side drawing, and he gave the
away team a penalty. It was a correct decision, and it relegated the home side. He needed a police escort out of the
ground, and somebody painted his name on a wall near the stadium. Two days later the video showed he'd been right.
Nobody apologised. He didn't expect them to. What he took from it was that being right and being liked are different
things, and you choose being right.

## The Integration and what it cost

When the worlds joined he landed in Ashenvale, not far from Astranaar. He had his whistle in his left pocket and
nothing else.

He was useful within a week, which embarrassed him. He reorganised a supply tent that nobody had asked him to touch,
then argued with the quartermaster about it for twenty minutes and won. He's been winning small arguments like that
ever since.

When the lists of Earth people started going up, he went to check them for anyone he knew. It didn't take long. There
wasn't really anyone to look for. That was the moment he understood that nobody on either side was looking for him
either, and it's stayed with him.

The Integration didn't take as much from him as it took from most people, and he feels guilty about that. What it
showed him was how little he'd had to lose.

## Now

He lives in Astranaar in a small room above the stores. He stands watch, he trains with the Sentinels, and he holds
the front line in any fight he's in, which turns out to be close enough to the old job that he was good at it straight
away.

Without anybody planning it, he's become the person Astranaar brings its arguments to. A trader and a hunter
arguing over a hide, two transplants fighting over a cot, a Sentinel and a carpenter over a fence. They come to him
because he listens to both sides and he doesn't take one because he likes them more.

People come and get him for meals, for the watch and for Ama's card game, and he always goes and he enjoys it. But in
three years he's never once walked up to a fire and sat down without somebody waving him over first.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to belong somewhere without having to earn it. To be there because people want him there, not because
  he's useful.
- **Hope:** to be somebody's first call.
- **Dream:** one proper game of football here. Twenty-two players, a pitch marked out, and him in the middle with the
  whistle. He's never told anyone because he thinks it's a silly thing to want.
- **Dream:** a family of his own. He's 43 and he hasn't given up on it, but he doesn't let himself think about it
  much.
- **Fear:** that if he stops being useful, people will stop asking for him.
- **Fear:** watching somebody die because he held the line wrong.
- **Fear:** being unfair to someone and never finding out.

## How he's changed

On Earth he was respected and alone. He was so used to it that he'd stopped noticing.

Here he's wanted, and he doesn't know what to do with that. He's warmer than he was in Valladolid. He laughs more.
He's noticed he has friends, and he's careful with them, the way you are with something you don't think you'll get
to keep.

The thing that hasn't changed yet is the waiting. He still waits for the wave. Part of what the player does for him
is ask him to stay before he's offered to go.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to stop waiting to be invited.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A transplant called Jaime dies in Astranaar. His Earth friends and the night elf family who took him in both claim
   his things. It's turning ugly, and both sides ask Adrián to settle it.
2. You help him get the facts. You talk to both sides and find Jaime's own wishes, written in a letter he left with
   the innkeeper.
3. The letter goes against the transplants. Adrián rules by it. They're furious with him, and some of them are
   people he plays cards with.
4. That night he walks up to the transplants' fire and sits down without being asked, ready to be told to leave.

**Ending (happy, already in the pilot):** nobody tells him to leave. After a minute somebody passes him the bread. He
thinks about it all the next day, and the next time you stop at a fire he sits down first.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Adrián's Whistle.** The referee's whistle he's carried in his left pocket since the day he landed. He gives it to
you when he swears to you. He says he doesn't need to be the referee when he's with you.

## Voice notes

- Formal and clear, but he uses contractions like anyone else. He says "properly" and "fair enough", and he uses your
  name when he's being serious.
- His English is good. Now and then the order of a sentence comes out Spanish.
- He gives the plan once, in order, like a team sheet.
- He apologises properly when he's wrong, and only once.
- He swears in Spanish under his breath when something goes badly, usually "hostia". He never swears in English.
- **He won't talk about** his parents until Companion, Elena until Friend, or the fire until he's sat down at it.
- **Habits:** pats his left pocket to check the whistle is still there, counts heads before every pull, stands with
  his hands behind his back when he's listening, and always eats last.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** gives the plan before every pull and tells you plainly what went wrong after a wipe,
  including when it was his fault. Hates abandoned quests because somebody was promised. Gets quiet and shaky after a
  death in the party and needs a minute. Loves a full party, bosses and dungeons. Won't put up with grinding for no
  reason.
- **Wave 3, with others:** respects people who own their mistakes. Grates with careless people at first, and then
  likes them if they try. Takes a while to see that teasing can be affection, but once he does he gives as good as he
  gets. Likes people who look after others, because that's what he does too.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the waiting. He stops having to earn his place by holding
  everything up himself. A skill idea to look at then: once a fight, he stops holding the line and spreads it, so the
  whole party shares the damage he'd have taken. It's about letting other people carry some of it.
