## Who he is

Kiet is invisible. Eleven years of being a courier in Bangkok traffic, going into offices, kitchens and back rooms all day, and nobody ever looked
at him twice. He kept a great deal of what he overheard.

He's careful. He's let very few people work out that being overlooked is a skill. He prefers to be underestimated.

He's quick, economical and never still. His hands are always doing something.

He's funny once he trusts you, in a quiet, wicked way, full of the gossip he's collected.

He can't stand silence. Eleven years of engine noise, and then a world where he can hear people breathing two rooms away. He still flinches at
quiet.

What annoys him: people who talk about others as if they're not there, slow routes, and being noticed. What he loves: a fast route, street food,
his grandmother's stories, noise, and knowing something nobody else knows.

## Before

He grew up in Khlong Toei, a crowded neighbourhood by Bangkok's port, raised by his grandmother, Yai Boonsri, after his parents left to work in
the south. She sold sweets from a cart and told him stories every night.

At 19 he became a motorcycle courier: a bike, a bag, and a city where the fastest route changed hourly. He was the fastest in his company and the
one nobody remembered. He heard everything.

His grandmother was eighty-four and couldn't manage the stairs to their flat any more. He carried her up and down every day. She called him her
legs.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on his bike, weaving through traffic, when it happened. He landed in Tanaris with a brass key blank on a wire loop at his belt. It's
cut for nothing. He meant to have it cut for his grandmother's new door, downstairs, so she wouldn't need the stairs, and never got round to it.

He had a route mapped across the Tanaris desert within six weeks, before he had a bed. He says the hard part was the quiet.

He knows his grandmother couldn't have managed. He doesn't let himself think about how.

## Now

He works the Abyssal Sands, south of Tanaris, carrying things across the desert between the camps faster than anyone else can. Everybody talks
near the person carrying something, and nobody watches him. He's mapped the whole desert by how long each route takes at each hour, which is the
first thing he does anywhere, and he's stopped being surprised that nobody else does it.

He wants a standing arrangement with somebody who pays for information and asks for it politely. He's had three offers, and all three were from
people he'd rather owe nothing to.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a patron he can respect.
- **Hope:** to stop flinching at silence.
- **Dream:** a night in Khlong Toei, the street noise, his grandmother telling a story.
- **Fear:** silence.
- **Fear:** being seen.
- **Fear:** that his grandmother waited for him at the top of the stairs.

## How he's changed

In Bangkok he was a fast, invisible courier and a devoted grandson, carrying his grandmother up the stairs every day.

Here he's still invisible and fast, and the silence has made him lonely in a way the noise never did. Part of what the player gives him is
somebody who notices him on purpose.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** a patron worth working for.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. Kiet overhears something he shouldn't: a plan by one of the three would-be patrons to rob and burn a caravan of refugees crossing the Sands.
2. He could sell the information. Instead he asks you to help him stop it. You race the raiders across the desert by his routes.
3. You reach the caravan first and get it to safety.

**Ending (happy):** the caravan's leader, an old Thai woman with a cart of sweets, thanks him by name, and he's so startled at being seen that he
can't speak. She's from Khlong Toei. She knew his grandmother. She tells him Yai Boonsri died in her sleep the night before the Integration, in
her own bed, and never had to manage the stairs or the worlds joining. It's the best news he could have had. The old woman's caravan becomes his
patron: they pay for routes and information, politely. He starts to like the quiet, a little, at night, by her fire.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Kiet's Key Blank.** A brass key blank on a loop of wire, cut for nothing. He meant to have it cut for his grandmother's new door, so she wouldn't
need the stairs. He gives it to you after the caravan. He says she never needed it, and you should have it for a door you haven't found yet.

## Voice notes

- Quiet, quick and wry. Contractions, gossip, and short sentences said softly.
- His English is fast and street-learned, with Thai: "mai pen rai" (never mind), "ja", "khrap" when he's being polite.
- He tells people to keep their voices down.
- He swears in Thai, quietly.
- **He won't talk about** his grandmother until Companion, or the stairs until Friend.
- **Habits:** maps every route, listens to every conversation, fidgets with the key blank, and hums to fill silence.
