## Who he is

Haruto is disciplined. His whole life has been a single stroke of the brush, studied until the hand no longer needs the
mind. He trained under a man who corrected him for eleven years before praising him once.

He believes form matters. Not for ceremony. A rune cut carelessly fails carelessly, and it fails on whoever's standing
closest.

He's reserved, and he says very little about himself. He answers personal questions in a tone that closes the subject.

He's kind in a strict way. He holds his students to a high standard because he respects them. He'd rather have two good
students than four mediocre ones.

He has a quiet, unexpected sense of humour. Very occasionally he says something so dry that it takes you an hour to
realise it was a joke.

What annoys him: carelessness, impatience, shortcuts, and people who call his luck what was thirty years of work. What he
loves: good paper and good ink, early mornings, silence, a well-cut rune, and tea made properly.

## Before

He grew up in Kanazawa, the son of a gold-leaf craftsman. At 22 he went to study calligraphy under Master Fujita, an old
man who corrected him for eleven years before saying a single word of praise. When Fujita died in 2017, Haruto kept the
studio and took on four students.

He never married. For twenty years his companion was Kenji, a potter with a workshop across the river. They had separate
houses and ate together most nights, and they never gave it a name in front of anyone. Haruto says the work was a full
occupation, in a tone that closes the subject, and that's the reason.

Kenji wasn't in the city on the day of the Integration. He was at a pottery fair in Kyoto.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was in his studio, grinding ink, when it happened. He landed in the mountains, at a place where a tauren rune-carver
had made his camp. He had his brush roll tucked inside his jacket.

He asked to be taught. That was the whole of it, and it worked, and he knows almost no transplant tried it. The tauren,
an old carver called Hrunn, taught him for a year. Hrunn had come north out of Thousand Needles, badly hurt, and the wounds killed him.
Haruto finished the curriculum alone, from notes, and thinks the result is incomplete in ways he can specify.

He hasn't looked for Kenji on the lists, because Kyoto is a long way from Kanazawa, and Kenji could be anywhere.

## Now

He lives in Silverpine South, near the old standing stones, where the dark magic from the keep in the south has seeped
into the runes on the stones. Nobody can read them now. He's copying them before they finish degrading. It's slow and
unrewarded, and his copies will be the only record that exists.

He has two students and isn't inclined to lower the standard to reach four. He's also putting together a reference of
every rune form he's ever recorded, in the hope that the discipline outlasts the shortage of teachers.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** four students, all good enough.
- **Hope:** to finish the reference.
- **Dream:** a bowl of tea in Kenji's workshop, with the kiln still warm.
- **Fear:** his hand failing.
- **Fear:** that the discipline dies with him.
- **Fear:** that he wasted twenty years with Kenji by never saying it out loud.

## How he's changed

In Kanazawa he was a master in a quiet studio, private and exact, with a companion he never named.

Here he's become a teacher of something more dangerous than brushwork, and he's had to speak more than he ever did. He
talks to his students now about what he feels when he cuts. Part of what the player gives him is somebody he can say
Kenji's name to.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to finish the reference before the stones fade.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He tells you his right hand has started to shake. Only a little, and only when he's tired. He hasn't told his
   students.
2. He needs the last and oldest stones copied, the ones deepest in the south, close to the keep. You take him there.
3. He tries to cut the last rune himself. His hand shakes, and the stroke goes wrong.

**Ending (sad):** he can't finish it. His older student, a young Forsaken called Edrin, cuts the last rune while Haruto
watches, and does it well. Haruto praises him, which he's never done before, and it's the first time Haruto's let anyone
see him cry. He knows the shake will get worse. He gives the reference to his students to finish. That night he tells
you about Kenji, all of it, out loud, for the first time in his life, because he's decided not to waste any more time.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Haruto's Brush Roll.** A bamboo roll of calligraphy brushes, his master's and his own, carried inside his jacket on
the day he landed. He gives it to you after the last stone. He says his hand won't hold them much longer, and he'd
rather they were kept by someone who saw why they mattered.

## Voice notes

- Formal, spare and exact. Contractions, but very few words, each one chosen.
- His English is careful and correct, learned late, with Japanese politeness in it.
- He corrects posture and grip without asking.
- He never swears. When something's careless, he says "that was careless", and it lands hard.
- **He won't talk about** Kenji until Sworn, Hrunn until Companion, or his hand until the quest.
- **Habits:** grinds ink every morning, stands very straight, watches people's hands, and drinks tea in silence.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** corrects the party's technique. Precise and deadly in melee. Silent for long stretches, then
  says one exact thing. Uneasy with careless magic users.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with disciplined, quiet people: Kesten, Sophia, Pavlo. Clashes with sloppy, loud ones,
  but warms to people who try to improve. A strict teacher to young recruits who ask.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the twenty years of silence, and losing his hand. A skill idea to look
  at then: on a cooldown of 10 to 15 turns, a single perfect rune strike that can't miss, be dodged or be resisted.
