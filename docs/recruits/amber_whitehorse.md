## Who she is

Amber is patient. Her whole career was doing something slow and correct and having nothing to show anybody for ten
years. She planted sedge and waited. She pulled invasive grass in one basin for nine years. She thinks in seasons, not
days.

She's at peace. She'd made a reasonable peace with living alone before the Integration, and she says that peace is the
most useful thing she brought with her. It makes her steady, and it also makes her poor company among transplants,
because she's had the easiest years of her life and most people haven't.

She's restrained. Druidry let her make a thing grow in an afternoon. The first year she used it constantly. The second
year she mostly stopped, because fast growth hides bad habits, and she's seen what happens when nobody waits to check.

She's wry and plain-spoken, with a dry laugh. She has a lot of opinions about how other people use their power, and she
shares them.

What annoys her: shortcuts, people who want results by Tuesday, and anyone who thinks "natural" means "nice". What she
loves: mud, a full seed bank, early mornings, and something coming back that everyone had given up on.

## Before

She grew up in Astoria, where the Columbia River meets the Pacific, in a fishing family. Her father ran a charter boat
and her mother taught school. She spent her childhood in waders.

She studied ecology and spent 23 years restoring wetland on the Oregon coast: waders, a seed bank, and grant cycles that
funded three years of a thirty-year job. She never once got to finish a basin. The money always ran out.

She married Doug, a forester, at 28. They divorced in 2016, without much drama. He wanted to move inland and she
wouldn't leave the coast. She lived alone with two dogs after that, and liked it more than she expected.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was out on a restoration site at low tide when it happened. She landed in the Barrens, the driest place she'd ever
seen, still in her waders, with her soil knife in her belt.

Her eight months of adjustment were the easiest of her life. Her whole career had been patience without authority. Here
she has both.

She hasn't found Beth. She thinks about her sister often and doesn't let it take over.

## Now

She works the Barrens oases, where a sickness has crept out of the caves and into the water. The oases are rotting, and
the plants around them are turning into something else. She's working the rot slowly, one oasis at a time. The tauren
and the druids have watched her for two years, and she knows watching is how they decide.

She has one student and a lot of opinions. She's trying to teach restraint to people who can make a tree in an afternoon.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** one basin, fully recovered, by her own hand, start to finish. She never got one on Earth.
- **Hope:** to teach someone to wait.
- **Dream:** to see the oasis full of birds.
- **Fear:** that people here will use their power the way people on Earth used their money: fast, and without looking.
- **Fear:** running out of time, the way the grants always did.
- **Fear:** that her peace is really just being used to loneliness.

## How she's changed

On the Oregon coast she was patient and frustrated, always three years into a thirty-year job.

Here she's finally been given time and power together. She's calmer than she's ever been, and quietly fierce about
doing it right. Part of what the player gives her is company she doesn't need, and turns out to want.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to bring one oasis all the way back.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. She chooses the worst oasis, the one everyone's given up on. Something in the water keeps the rot coming back.
2. You follow it to its source, a corrupted spring feeding the oasis from underground, and clear it with her.
3. Then she does the slow part, and she makes you wait with her. A whole season.

**Ending (happy):** the oasis comes back. Reeds first, then water lilies, then birds. Her student does half the work,
slowly and correctly, without being told. The tauren come to look. One of the elders asks her to do the next one. She
says she'll need a few years, and the elder says that's fine.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Amber's Soil Knife.** A worn steel soil knife with a wooden handle, the kind restoration crews use to dig plugs of
sedge. She had it in her belt on the tide flats the day she landed. She gives it to you after the oasis. She says she
does most of her planting with her hands now.

## Voice notes

- Slow, dry and plain. Contractions, and short answers that make you wait for the rest.
- American, Pacific Northwest: easy, understated, a little gruff.
- She talks in seasons: "give it a season", "that's a five-year problem".
- She swears mildly when something's been done stupidly.
- **She won't talk about** Doug until Companion, or Beth until Friend.
- **Habits:** crouches to touch the soil, notices every plant, gets up before dawn, and says "wait" a lot.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** patient in a long fight, impatient with rushing. Comments on the land in every zone. Hates
  waste and needless burning. Calm in a wipe.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with patient, quiet people, and with Carolina and Hana, who also read the natural
  world. Clashes with show-offs who waste power. A dry mentor to young recruits.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is the fear of running out of time. A skill idea to look at then: a
  passive where her heals over time grow stronger the longer they run.
