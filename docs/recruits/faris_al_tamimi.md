## Who he is

Faris is a listener. He'll sit with you by the water and say almost nothing, and somehow you'll tell him everything.

He's gentle and patient, the way you have to be with palms that take years to fruit. His grandfather taught him that you look after the water first, and the
palms look after themselves. He's never found a better rule for anything.

He misses home with his whole body. Some days he can feel the river in his arms, and the heat on the back of his neck.

The water spirits here took to him at once. He thinks it's because he talks to water like a person, which he always has.

He's devout in a quiet, warm way. He prays by the lake, turns his grandfather's beads when he's thinking, and he never asks anyone else what they believe.

What annoys him: wasted water, cruelty to animals, and people who talk over the birds. What he loves: the river at dawn, his nephews, Friday lunch with the
whole family, fresh dates, and the sound of water moving.

## Before

He grew up on the family land along the Shatt al-Arab, in a house with a courtyard and a fig tree, with ninety date palms and a big family that ate together
every Friday. His grandfather Kadhim raised the palms for sixty years and taught Faris everything, mostly by the water.

When his grandfather died, Faris took over the palms. He never married. He says the palms kept him busy, and his brother Haidar had enough children for both
of them.

He took his nephews, Ali and Mustafa, out on the river in a flat boat and taught them the names of every bird: the kingfishers, the herons, and the little
pied ones that sat on the boat.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was out among the palms at dawn, checking the water channels, when it happened. He came through into dry country with his grandfather's amber beads on his
wrist and one date stone in his pocket.

For weeks he couldn't sleep without the sound of water. A shaman found him at a spring talking to it, and told him the spring was talking back.

He came north to Moonglade because someone told him there was a lake there. It's the first water since home that he trusts.

He hasn't found his family.

## Now

He's in Moonglade, by the lake, looking after the water and the people who come to it.

He's been trying to grow a date palm from the stone of the last date he had in his pocket. It hasn't sprouted. He keeps trying.

He keeps his grandfather's prayer beads round his wrist, and he turns them when he's thinking.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to plant something here that'll still be standing in a hundred years.
- **Hope:** to find his family, and the boys above all.
- **Dream:** Friday lunch in the courtyard under the fig tree, everybody talking at once.
- **Fear:** the stone never sprouting.
- **Fear:** that the boys are somewhere with no water, and nobody to teach them.
- **Fear:** forgetting the sound of the river.

## How he's changed

At home he was a quiet palm farmer by a great river, with his grandfather's rule and his nephews in the boat.

Here he's become a healer who keeps the water, far from any river he knows. He's still gentle, and more homesick than he lets anyone see. Part of what the
player gives him is somebody to sit with by the water.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to make the date stone grow.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The stream that feeds Moonglade's lake has started running wrong. He can taste it, he says. Something upstream, in Felwood, is spoiling it.
2. You follow the stream up into Felwood with him, through the corruption, and find what's fouling it at the source.
3. He cleanses the spring himself, talking to it the whole time, until it runs clean.

**Ending (happy):** the clean water reaches the lake, and three days later the date stone sprouts. The druids give him a patch of ground by the water for it,
for as long as he wants it. A month later a letter comes from Astranaar, from Haidar: he and the boys came through together, and they've been looking for him
for two years. They come to Moonglade. Faris builds a flat boat and takes Ali and Mustafa out on the lake, and teaches them the names of all the birds here,
which he's had two years to learn. He says the palm will fruit in about eight years, and the boys will be grown, and they'll eat the first dates together on
a Friday.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Kadhim's Prayer Beads.** His grandfather's amber prayer beads, worn round his wrist on the morning he came through and turned every time he's thought about
anything since. He gives them to you after the boat. He says his grandfather would want them to go to someone who looks after the water first.

## Voice notes

- Gentle, slow and warm. Contractions, few words, and Iraqi Arabic: "shlonak" (how are you), "inshallah", "habibi", "yalla".
- He greets everyone with "peace be with you", and means it.
- He talks to water, and to birds, as if they're people.
- He doesn't swear. When someone wastes water, he looks at them, and that's enough.
- **He won't talk about** his grandfather until Friend, or the boys until Companion.
- **Habits:** turns his beads, tastes the water, names the birds, and checks the date stone every morning.
