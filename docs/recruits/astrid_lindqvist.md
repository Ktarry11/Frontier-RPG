## Who she is

Astrid is patient. She spent most of her twenties lying in snow, waiting for the sky to do something. She's very good at waiting for the right moment,
and that's what makes her deadly with a bow.

She's calm and bright, with a quiet, dry humour. She likes the dark and the cold and long silences.

She's a little lonely, and she's always liked it that way. She lived in a cabin with no road to it in winter.

She lost her camera's working life, and that was harder than anything else. Photographing the sky was how she understood it. Now she maps it by hand.

She keeps a habit she knows is pointless: every Sunday evening, she stops what she's doing for the time her brother used to call.

What annoys her: light, noise, impatience, and people who call the aurora "pretty". What she loves: a clear cold night, the northern lights, black coffee
in a thermos, her brother's teasing, and the moment the sky does something nobody's seen.

## Before

She grew up in Luleå, the daughter of a mining engineer and a nurse, and moved to Kiruna at 22 to photograph the aurora. For twelve years she sold
photographs to magazines and took tourists out into the snow at night: a cabin with no road to it in winter, and a camera worth more than her car.

Her brother Erik worked in Stockholm and called every Sunday to ask if she was still alive. She always said "barely" and he always laughed.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was out in the snow on a clear night, photographing the aurora, when it happened. She landed in Felwood with her camera round her neck. It stopped
working within the week, and she found that harder than anything else.

The Sentinels at Talonbranch taught her the bow, and the stars here answered her within the week.

She hasn't found her parents or Erik.

## Now

She's in North Felwood, near the Talonbranch camp, hunting what comes out of the fel when the light's low.

She's mapping the new sky, star by star, in a notebook the size of a brick. The lens cap round her neck belongs to a camera that hasn't worked in three
years.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to finish her map of the new sky.
- **Hope:** to hear Erik's Sunday call again.
- **Dream:** one more photograph of an aurora.
- **Fear:** that she'll forget what the real aurora looked like.
- **Fear:** that her brother stopped calling because there's nobody to call.
- **Fear:** a sky that never does anything new.

## How she's changed

In Kiruna she was a calm, solitary photographer who preferred the sky to people.

Here she's become a hunter and a starcaller, and she's found she misses people more than she expected, starting with a phone call. Part of what the
player gives her is someone to wait with.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to see something in this sky nobody's seen.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. Her map has shown her something: on one night this winter, the stars over Felwood will line up into a pattern, and she thinks something will happen.
2. The night's dangerous, because the fel creatures come out in force. You guard her on a high ridge while she watches.
3. At midnight, the sky over Felwood lights up in green and violet curtains. An aurora, here, the first anyone's seen.

**Ending (happy):** she cries, which she never does. She draws it in her notebook as fast as she can. The next morning a Swedish trader passing through
Talonbranch hears her describing it and says his cousin Erik in a Swedish camp in the north talks about a sister who chased the northern lights. It's her
brother. She sends a letter that day. Erik writes back: "Are you still alive?" She writes "barely", and they write every Sunday after that.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Astrid's Lens Cap.** The lens cap from her camera in Kiruna, worn round her neck on a cord for three years after the camera died. She gives it to you
after the aurora. She says she saw the one thing she always wanted to photograph, and she didn't need a camera to keep it.
