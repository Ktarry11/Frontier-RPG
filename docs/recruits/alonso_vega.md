## Who he is

Alonso feels a fire the way other people feel a draught. Twenty-two years of glaze, temperature and timing, a trade where a whole
year's work turns on four hours of heat going exactly right.

He's warm and easy-going, with a wide, friendly face and a lot of jokes about eyebrows. He's burned three people's eyebrows off being
polite.

He's patient and careful with heat, and almost nobody else is. He's learned five kilns in his life and he learned this world the same
way, as a kiln with an unfamiliar character.

He's hiding a grief so deep he changes the subject before he gets anywhere near it. His wife was at the market when it happened. He's
said the word "market" perhaps twice in three years.

What annoys him: careless heat, rushed work, waste, and people who call his pots "souvenirs". What he loves: a perfect glaze, mole on a
Sunday, the smell of a kiln cooling, his sons, and Lupe.

## Before

He grew up in Puebla, in the family workshop, where his grandfather fired the kiln for forty years and his father after him. He learned
to throw a pot at six and to read a kiln at fifteen. At 24 he took over the firing. For 22 years he ran it: the blue and white tiles and
plates Puebla is famous for, and the four hours of heat that decide whether the year's work comes out right.

He married Lupe at 23. She ran a stall at the market selling the workshop's pottery, and she was the one who could sell anything to
anybody. They had two sons, Rodrigo and Fernando, who both went off to Mexico City and neither of whom wanted the kiln. He minded more than
he said.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at the kiln, at the height of a firing, when it happened. He landed in Stranglethorn with his smoked-glass kiln goggles round his
neck.

He took ten weeks to adjust. He treated it as a new kiln.

Lupe was at the market. He's never said more than that. The first thing he did with fire on this world was make a cup, badly, and he
keeps it on a shelf. It's the cup he'd have given her.

## Now

He lives in the ruins of Central Stranglethorn, where the trolls once fired their idols in a great stone pit. It's the hottest thing he's
found, so it's where he wants to be. He has a pot going in a crack in the rock that nobody's supposed to know about.

He wants a kiln of his own, built right, with somebody to teach. He has the clay. He has the site. He's been putting it off for two years.
The reason he gives is money. The reason isn't money. Building it means admitting he's staying, and that Lupe isn't coming.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a kiln of his own, with an apprentice.
- **Hope:** to know about Lupe, and to never have to know.
- **Dream:** a Sunday at the market, Lupe selling his plates and teasing the customers.
- **Fear:** building the kiln, and what it means.
- **Fear:** a firing gone wrong, and a whole year's work cracked.
- **Fear:** the word "market".

## How he's changed

In Puebla he was a master potter, a husband and father, and the latest in a line.

Here he's become a pyromancer, which is what he always was, with the pots taken away. He's still warm. He's lonelier. Part of what the
player gives him is somebody to talk to while the fire works.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to build his kiln, and to know.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A woman from Puebla passes through Booty Bay, and she was at the same market that morning. Alonso asks you to come with him to find
   her.
2. She tells him. The market square came through into deep water, and almost nobody got out. She saw Lupe at her stall, right up until
   the end.
3. He doesn't speak for a day. Then he asks you to help him build the kiln.

**Ending (sad):** you build it together, in the troll ruins, stone by stone. He fires it for the first time, four hours, exactly right. The
first thing out of it is a blue and white plate in the pattern Lupe always sold best, and he hangs it on the kiln wall. He takes on a young
troll apprentice who's been watching him from the ruins for a year. He says the word "market" out loud, and tells you about her, and he
doesn't change the subject.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Alonso's Kiln Goggles.** A pair of smoked-glass goggles from the family workshop in Puebla, the ones he wore to look into the fire, round
his neck the day he landed. He gives them to you after the first firing. He says his apprentice has her own pair now, and you should have
something for looking straight at hard things.

## Voice notes

- Warm, relaxed and funny. Contractions, eyebrow jokes, and Mexican Spanish: "órale", "ándale", "mijo", "qué padre".
- He talks about everything in terms of firing: heat, timing, cracks, glaze.
- He changes the subject very fast near certain words.
- He swears cheerfully in Spanish when something's too hot, which is often.
- **He won't talk about** Lupe until Friend, or the market until the quest.
- **Habits:** feels the air for heat, tells people to step back, touches the goggles at his throat, and keeps the bad cup on the shelf.
