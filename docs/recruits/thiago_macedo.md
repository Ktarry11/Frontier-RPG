## Who he is

Thiago is encouraging. His whole job was being relentlessly encouraging to somebody who wanted to give up and was entitled to. He's
very hard to discourage, and very hard to say no to.

He's gentle. He's enormous and he moves carefully, like a man who knows how easily bodies break.

He's warm and funny, and he loves people. He sat with transplants in the bad weeks, before anybody organised anything, and he still
does. He doesn't call it a vocation and gets uncomfortable when other people do.

He's certain. He saw a cousin's name on a list in the south eleven months ago and he's been walking south in stages ever since, in
no hurry, because he's decided it's certain.

He's unchanged by the Light, and he finds that funny. The people who know him best say it didn't change him at all, and he agrees.

What annoys him: people who give up on themselves, people who carry the injured badly, and being called a saint. What he loves: his
family, feijão tropeiro, Atlético football, a patient who walks again, and a big Sunday lunch.

## Before

He grew up in Belo Horizonte, the middle child of five, in a neighbourhood where his parents, grandparents, aunts and cousins all
lived on the same three streets. His father was a mechanic and his mother a cook at a school. Sundays were lunch for thirty.

He trained as a physiotherapist and worked ten years at a rehabilitation clinic: a table, a schedule, and people he saw twice a
week for months. Most of them were recovering badly from something terrible. He got them walking again, or got them to accept
that they wouldn't, and he never once let them see him give up.

He was single at the Integration, after a long relationship ended the year before, kindly. His mother had already started lining up
nice girls from church.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at the clinic, strapping a patient's knee with bright blue tape, when it happened. He landed in Stranglethorn with the half-
used roll in his pocket.

He spent eight months being useful to people in a way nobody had asked for. He was the man who sat with transplants in the bad
weeks. The Light came to him in his second year.

He watched somebody's back get broken twice: once by a boar and once by the people carrying him away from the boar. Since then he's
taught four people how to move an injured body without making it worse.

Eleven months ago he saw a cousin's name on a list at a hub in the south: Júlio Macedo. He's been walking south in stages ever
since.

## Now

He's at Grom'gol, in Stranglethorn, on his way south. In the party he stands in the middle, where he can reach either end of it.

The roll of blue tape is nearly finished. He rations it. He's strapped three people's knees with it and he'll tell you their names.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find Júlio, and through him the rest of the family.
- **Hope:** to teach everyone he meets how to carry the injured.
- **Dream:** Sunday lunch for thirty, with everyone talking at once.
- **Fear:** arriving and finding out it isn't certain after all.
- **Fear:** a patient he can't encourage.
- **Fear:** being alone at a table.

## How he's changed

In Belo Horizonte he was the cheerful middle son of a huge family, never alone for a single day.

Here he's alone for the first time in his life, and he's filled it by sitting with everybody else who's alone. The Light didn't
change him. Being alone did, a little. Part of what the player gives him is somebody to sit with him.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to reach Júlio.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He finally makes the last stage south, to Booty Bay, where the list was, and asks you to come with him.
2. The list's old, and Júlio's moved on. You follow him to a camp of Brazilians on the coast, through bad country.
3. You find Júlio, alive, working as a fisherman.

**Ending (sad):** Júlio is so glad to see him he cries. Then he tells Thiago the rest. The family's three streets came through
together, and they landed in a bad place, in the first winter. Júlio is the only one who made it. Thiago's parents, his sisters,
his grandparents: all gone. Thiago sits on the sand for a long time, and for once somebody sits with him. Then he tells Júlio
that he's not dying today, and that this he promises. He stays at the camp for a month, and teaches every fisherman there how to
carry the injured. He comes back to the party lighter in a way he can't explain, and heavier.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Thiago's Blue Tape.** The last few turns of a roll of bright blue kinesiology tape from his clinic in Belo Horizonte, rationed for
three years. He's strapped three people's knees with it here and he knows their names. He gives you what's left after the coast. He
says it's for the fourth.

## Voice notes

- Warm, big and gentle. Contractions, encouragement, and lots of "hey, hey, look at me".
- His English is good with a Minas Gerais accent and Brazilian Portuguese: "uai", "sô", "nossa", "meu irmão".
- He promises things only when he means them, and says so.
- He doesn't swear much. When a patient's hurting, "nossa senhora".
- **He won't talk about** being alone until Friend, or the family until he has news.
- **Habits:** checks everyone's joints, strapping and posture, talks to frightened people, rations his tape, and walks south.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** heals from the middle of the party and encourages everyone. Furious at bad carrying of the injured. Gentle
  with civilians. Never gives up in a wipe.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with nearly everyone, especially other healers, like Ama, Freja and Liam. A natural friend to
  lonely people. Clashes only with people who give up on others.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the family he couldn't reach. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he
  promises an ally they won't die today, and that ally can't fall for a few turns.
