## Who he is

Bakyt reads land. He reads weather in a sky other people call clear and finds water where there apparently isn't any.
Forty years on the high pasture taught him that the ground lies, and he has forty years of practice catching it.

He's calm. Nomads adjust, he says. That's the whole profession. He had the easiest first year of anyone and the quietest
grief underneath it.

He's dignified and courteous in an old-fashioned way. He offers tea to anyone who comes near his tent. He doesn't hurry,
doesn't raise his voice, and doesn't waste words.

He's warmer than he looks, and getting warmer. He's 58, and he's started thinking about who gets his route after him,
and that's made him friendlier to strangers than he used to be.

He grieves properly. The eagle he hunts with here isn't the eagle he hunted with there. He'll tell you the difference is
less than you'd think, and that he grieved the first one properly.

What annoys him: people who rush, people who waste water, and anyone who calls the wild empty. What he loves: his eagle,
strong tea with milk, a long view, and the moment the herd moves the way he meant it to.

## Before

He grew up in a yurt in the Tian Shan, on a route his family has walked for six generations: up to high pasture in
spring, down in autumn. His father taught him the route before he could ride. He hunted with golden eagles, as his
father and grandfather did, and the first eagle he trained, Ak-Kanat, lived with him for twenty-two years.

He married Aigul at 22. They had three sons. All three went to Bishkek for work when they grew up. He understood why. It
was still the end of six generations, and both of those things sat in him at once for twenty years. Aigul died of a
stroke six years before the Integration. He kept walking the route alone.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on the high pasture, bringing the herd up for the summer, when it happened. He landed in the hills of Hillsbrad
with his eagle-hunting glove on his left hand. Ak-Kanat had died the year before. The glove was empty.

What he had to absorb was that this world has pasture his family will never walk. He spent a year of long evenings getting
comfortable with that.

His sons were in Bishkek. He hasn't found them. He prays for them each evening, facing the mountains.

## Now

He lives on the high pasture of Eastern Hillsbrad, grass and wind and a long way between people. The herders out of
Tarren Mill move their goats and sheep on a cycle he recognised in about a week. They've started asking his opinion on
it, which is the only honour he's ever visibly cared about.

He has a new eagle, a young female he calls Jeldyz, "star". She's good. She's not Ak-Kanat. He's at peace with that.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a route of his own here. Mapped, seasonal, defensible, and walked often enough that somebody could follow it
  after him.
- **Hope:** someone to give it to.
- **Dream:** to see his grandsons ride.
- **Fear:** that the six generations end with him, twice.
- **Fear:** being too old to walk the route.
- **Fear:** that his sons think he blamed them for leaving.

## How he's changed

In the Tian Shan he was the last of six generations, proud and quiet and a little bitter about the end of it.

Here he's lost everything and somehow found the bitterness gone. He's softer, friendlier, more patient with strangers.
Part of what the player gives him is someone to walk with.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to finish his route and hand it on.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. He has two legs of the route. The third crosses dangerous high ground where something has been taking the herders'
   animals. You go with him to find it.
2. It's a pack of beasts driven down by something worse above. You and Jeldyz track them together and clear the pass.
3. He walks the full route for the first time, with a young orphan herder from Tarren Mill, Wil, who's been watching him
   for a year.

**Ending (happy):** the route is whole, three legs, spring and autumn. Wil walks it with him and learns it, and Bakyt
teaches him to hunt with an eagle. He gives Wil his route the way his father gave it to him, by walking it together until
the boy could do it blind. It isn't the seventh generation of Asanovs. Bakyt says it's the seventh generation of the
route, and that's what matters.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Ak-Kanat's Glove.** His thick leather eagle-hunting glove, worn shiny, the one that carried his first eagle for
twenty-two years. It was on his hand, empty, when he landed. He gives it to you once Wil has his own. He says Jeldyz has
a new glove, and the old one should go to someone who knows what it carried.

## Voice notes

- Slow, courteous and spare. Contractions, short sentences, and long silences.
- His English is limited but careful, learned from traders, with Kyrgyz and Russian words: "jakshy" (good), "kel"
  (come), "aga" (yes).
- He offers tea before anything else.
- He doesn't swear. When he's angry he goes still and his eagle mantles.
- **He won't talk about** Aigul until Companion, or his sons until Friend.
- **Habits:** reads the sky every morning, talks to his eagle, crouches to touch the ground, and walks first.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** walks first and reads the ground. Calm and slow, irritated by rushing. Respects animals and
  hates needless killing. Loves open country and high places, dislikes caves.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with patient, outdoor people, and especially with Giorgi, another mountain herder.
  Fatherly to the young ones. Quiet with loud people.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is the end of six generations. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown,
  his eagle marks every enemy's weak point, and the party's attacks on them hit harder.
