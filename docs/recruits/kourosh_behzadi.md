## Who he is

Kourosh calculates. Twelve years of establishing, very carefully, how likely a thing is. He finds arcane magic restful by comparison,
because for once the numbers hold still.

He's cheerful in a dry, slightly formal way. He always looks faintly amused.

He's the only person on this side of the mountains who's calculated his own odds of survival, and he won't tell anybody the figure. The
refusal is cheerful rather than grim, and people find that worse.

He's precise and honest, and he refuses to be called brave. The first thing he did on this world was finish the sum he was in the middle
of. That was momentum, not bravery, and he's irritated that people keep wanting it to be the other thing.

He keeps records of everything, including the dates people change the subject.

What annoys him: sloppy estimates, people who say "it'll probably be fine", and being called brave. What he loves: a clean table of numbers,
Persian poetry, strong tea with cardamom, his mother's cooking, and the moment a pattern becomes clear.

## Before

He grew up in Tehran, the only son of a mathematics teacher and Shirin, who worked at a bank and had ambitions for him. His father died
when Kourosh was 20. He studied mathematics and became an actuary, pricing mortality for an insurance company: tables, reserves, a good
office with a bad view.

He phoned his mother every Friday. She thought the job was beneath him. She was right that he was capable of more. She was wrong about what
more would have meant.

He never married. He says the numbers were never favourable, and he's only half joking.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was at his desk, in the middle of a calculation, when it happened. He landed in the hills of Hillsbrad with his silver mechanical pencil
still in his hand. The first thing he did on this world was finish the sum.

In his second year he priced his mother's odds of having survived, properly, using what's known about where the hubs landed and who lived.
Then he stopped looking, because an actuary who won't act on his own table isn't an actuary. He's never told anybody the figure. He's never
told anybody there was one.

## Now

He lives and works in the Crystalline Underdark, where the crystal lattice does something to arcane magic that nobody's characterised.
He's characterising it. The case of notebooks is nearly full.

There's no journal and no readership, and he's writing it anyway, longhand, in a case he keeps dry. He's told two people what's in it. Both
changed the subject. He recorded the date each time.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to finish the characterisation, for whoever comes next.
- **Hope:** that the figure was wrong.
- **Dream:** a Friday phone call with his mother, telling her he's doing something worthy of her.
- **Fear:** that the figure was right.
- **Fear:** that nobody will ever read what he writes.
- **Fear:** that he's using numbers to avoid feeling anything.

## How he's changed

In Tehran he was a careful, ironic actuary who disappointed his mother politely every Friday.

Here he's doing the kind of work she always wanted for him, and he can't tell her. He's still ironic and precise. He's started to want
someone to read his work. Part of what the player gives him is a reader.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to finish his work and have somebody read it, and, though he'd never say it, to be told the figure was wrong.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. The lattice in the Underdark is changing, and if his work's right, it's about to become dangerous to everyone near it. He needs to
   prove it before anybody will move.
2. You help him take the last measurements at the heart of the lattice, under attack from things the change has woken.
3. He finishes the work. It's right. People leave in time.

**Ending (still unknown):** afterwards, sitting on a crystal ledge, he tells you the figure. Thirty-one percent. That's the chance his
mother survived. He's never checked. He says he never will, because as long as he doesn't, the thirty-one percent is still alive. Then he
gives you the first volume of his notes to read, the first person he's let read them. He keeps the figure, and the hope, and the case of
notebooks, and he never finds out.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Kourosh's Silver Pencil.** A slim silver mechanical pencil, clipped inside his robe collar, the one he was finishing a sum with in Tehran
when the worlds joined. He's written everything since with it. He gives it to you after the lattice. He says it's done enough sums, and you
should write something that isn't a calculation.

## Voice notes

- Dry, formal and amused. Contractions, exact numbers, and gentle self-mockery.
- His English is precise, slightly formal and very good, with Persian: "jan" (dear), "khoda hafez" (goodbye), and a line of Hafez now and
  then.
- He gives odds for everything, cheerfully.
- He doesn't swear. When something's foolish, he states the probability of it ending badly.
- **He won't talk about** the figure until the quest, or his mother until Friend.
- **Habits:** writes everything down, records dates, clicks his pencil, and stands exactly at the back.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** gives the odds before every fight, cheerfully. Precise and careful with spells. Records everything. Unbothered by
  danger in a way that unsettles people.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with careful, clever people, especially Henrik, Kesten and Sophia. Quietly fond of reckless people, and
  tells them their odds. Clashes with people who want him to be brave.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is living inside the figure. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown, he
  calculates the enemy's next move exactly, and the party takes far less from it.
