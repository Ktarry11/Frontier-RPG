## Who she is

Ama is steady. She has a level voice that she learned from a ward sister when she was 22, and it's the voice she uses
when everything's going wrong. From the outside it looks like calm. From the inside it's something else, and she'll
tell you that if you ask.

She's strict. She has rules for everyone, and she breaks her own all the time, especially the one about her hours.
Twice now the orc women of Razor Hill have walked her back to her cot with a hand between her shoulder blades.

She's funny. Dry and quick, and you won't always see it coming. She runs a card game in Razor Hill, a vicious one, and
she takes everyone's money, including Adrián's, and enjoys it.

She does arithmetic in her head constantly, the kind no one says out loud on a children's ward: how many beds, how many
nurses, who needs her first. She still runs it in every fight, on a world that doesn't make her.

She's kind in a way that doesn't look like kindness. She tells you off, she makes you sit down, she doesn't let you
argue. You only notice afterwards that she sat with you the whole night.

What annoys her: people who won't sit down when they're hurt, people who make a fuss about small things in front of
people with big things, and being thanked too much. What she loves: children, cards, a quiet night, and the moment a
child who was frightened of her stops being frightened.

## Before

She grew up in Peckham, above her father's mobile phone shop, with a younger brother, Kofi. Her mother was a nurse from
Lagos and her father ran the shop and the church committee. Ama was the clever, bossy one.

She trained as a nurse at 18 because her mother had been one, and she went into paediatrics because she was good with
frightened children. She worked nights on a children's ward in a big London hospital for fourteen years, four cots to a
bay, never enough staff.

She was very good. It cost her a marriage and most of her twenties. She married Kwame, a primary school teacher, at 25.
He wanted children of their own and a wife who was home at night, and she gave every night to other people's children
instead. They divorced when she was 31. She'll tell you the trade was obvious and he was right to go.

Her mother moved back to Lagos when she retired. She died there of a heart attack the February before the Integration.
Ama flew out for the funeral and was back on nights a week later.

She has a notebook. Every child she lost on the ward is in it, by first name, in handwriting so bad nobody else can
read it. That's deliberate.

## The Integration and what it cost

She was at work when it happened. She landed in Durotar in her uniform, with her stethoscope round her neck: a small
children's one, with a plastic bear clipped to the tubing so the little ones would let her listen.

The first two years aren't a subject. Nobody in Razor Hill asks.

What she'll say is this. In London there was always a form, a referral, a reason you couldn't help someone. Here there
was Light in her hands and no limit at all, and for a year nothing told her to stop. She didn't. She healed until she
passed out, woke up and did it again. An orc woman called Shenra, whose son she'd kept breathing through a fever,
finally picked her up off the floor and told her to stop.

She hasn't found her father or Kofi on the lists. She checks, and she doesn't talk about it. She's described her
mother's death once as the only good timing in this whole business, and then changed the subject so hard the other
person apologised.

## Now

She runs the infirmary at Razor Hill. The road brings in whatever the Barrens did to people that week. The children
stopped being frightened of her a long while after the adults did, and she thinks that's the right order.

She's looking for a replacement. Not a student, a real replacement, somebody who can do this when she's out of the
room. She's been looking for two years and has turned down four people for reasons she can't put into words, and she
knows that's the problem.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** to find somebody who can do her job as well as she does, so she can stop.
- **Hope:** that her father and Kofi are together somewhere.
- **Dream:** a full night's sleep in a bed with nobody needing her.
- **Dream:** she still sometimes thinks about the children she didn't have with Kwame. She doesn't let it go further.
- **Fear:** that she can't stop, and that if she stops she'll find out there's nothing else of her.
- **Fear:** adding another name to the notebook.
- **Fear:** that the people she's turned down were good enough and she's the problem.

## How she's changed

In London she was the nurse everybody wanted on shift and the wife who was never home. She was proud and tired.

Here, for a year, she was nearly gone. She gave everything she had to the Light and the people who needed it. Shenra
picking her up off the floor was the first time anybody had looked after her in a long time.

She's softer now than she was in London. She lets the orc women walk her to bed. She laughs at cards. She still can't
stop working. Part of what the player gives her is somebody she trusts to hold the line while she rests.

## What she wants next (her goal and personal quest)

**Her goal:** to find her replacement, and let herself be replaced.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A fever goes through Razor Hill, the bad kind, and it's taking the children first. Ama needs a herb that only grows
   somewhere dangerous, and she won't send anyone else for it. You go with her.
2. On the way back she collapses. She's been healing on the road the whole time and hasn't told you.
3. While she's down, the infirmary is run for two days by Shenra's daughter, Grasha, a young orc who's been fetching
   water and watching Ama work for a year.

**Ending (happy):** Ama wakes up in her cot in daylight. Grasha has kept every child alive, and done it the way Ama
would have. Ama looks at her for a long time and says, "You'll do." Then she sleeps for a day and a half. Her father
and Kofi are still unknown.

## Her keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Ama's Stethoscope.** Her children's stethoscope from the ward in London, with a scuffed plastic bear clipped to the
tubing so frightened little ones would let her listen. She wore it for fourteen years of nights. She gives it to you
once Grasha has her own. She says it's listened to a lot of small hearts, and she'd like it to listen to yours.

## Voice notes

- Firm, quick and dry, and very London. Contractions, short instructions, and no fuss.
- She tells you what to do, not what she'd like you to do.
- She uses the level voice when something's serious. You'll learn to hear the difference.
- She calls people "love" when she's being kind and by their full name when she's cross.
- She doesn't swear in front of children. Away from them, mild London swearing when something stupid happens.
- **She won't talk about** the first two years, the notebook, or Kwame, until Friend. Her mother never, except that one
  line.
- **Habits:** listens to everyone's chest after a fight, counts everyone after a fight, shuffles cards when she's thinking, and always has a
  biscuit in her pocket for a child.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** heals the worst first without being told, does the arithmetic out loud in a bad fight, and
  overspends herself. Tells you off for stupid pulls. Gets very quiet if a child is hurt in a quest.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with steady, sensible people and anyone who plays cards. She'd have a sharp friendship
  with another healer. Hates show-offs.
- **Wave 4, her Sworn skill:** what she resolves is being unable to stop. A skill idea to look at then: a passive where
  she heals less when the party is safe and more when it isn't, because she's finally letting herself rest.
