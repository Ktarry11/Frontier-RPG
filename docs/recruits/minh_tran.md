## Who he is

Minh is calm. Twenty-three years of holding forty futures in his head at once and choosing the one where nobody dies.
It's not his temperament. It's professional conditioning, and he'll tell you that.

He's responsible for everything. He appoints himself to things nobody asked him to do, because nobody else will. That's
exactly the reasoning that got him where he is, and he knows it.

He's precise. He likes to establish the sequence of events before acting. He never lets his voice carry what his pulse
is doing.

He's kind, and gentle, and very tired. He hasn't had one hour where nothing was his responsibility since 2003, and he's
beginning to suspect the problem isn't the schedule.

He has a quiet, slightly formal sense of humour that comes out when he's relaxed, which is rarely.

What annoys him: people who act before they know what's happening, raised voices, and anyone who says "it'll be fine"
without checking. What he loves: a clear sky, strong iced coffee, his daughter's violin, and the moment a dangerous
situation resolves without anybody noticing it was dangerous.

## Before

He grew up in Ho Chi Minh City, the son of a schoolteacher and a mechanic. He trained as an air traffic controller at 24
and worked the radar room at Tân Sơn Nhất for 23 years: a dark room, a scope, and the discipline of never letting your
voice carry what your pulse is doing. He worked nights for most of it, on three hours' sleep.

He married Hoa at 27. Their daughter Linh was born when he was 29. The marriage ended when Linh was 14, mostly because he
was never home and, when he was, he was still in the radar room in his head. Hoa remarried. They stayed polite.

Linh played the violin. He took her to every lesson he could. When she was 20 she went to Melbourne to study music. He
was very proud and missed her badly.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on shift, in the middle of a handover, when it happened. He'd just said "you have control" to his colleague.
He landed in the Barrens with Linh's tuning fork on a cord round his neck. She gave it to him when she left for
Melbourne, so he'd have her A-note with him.

He says the handover was the hardest part, and people assume he means the Integration. He means the handover. He handed
forty aircraft to a colleague and vanished, and he'll never know what happened next.

Two hubs, two continents, and a survivable set of assumptions about Linh. He's permitted himself the survivable set.

## Now

He keeps watch from a hut on a ridge in the Northern Barrens, over a dry riverbed where time slips. Now and then the
water runs for an hour from years ago, or a caravan arrives before it set out. Almost nobody understands how dangerous
that is. He's appointed himself to it. Nobody asked. He's made a rota of one.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** that Linh is safe in Melbourne, wherever Melbourne came down.
- **Hope:** one hour in which nothing is his responsibility.
- **Dream:** to hear Linh play again.
- **Fear:** a handover that goes wrong because he wasn't there.
- **Fear:** that the survivable assumptions aren't true.
- **Fear:** that if he stops keeping watch, something will happen that he could have stopped.

## How he's changed

In Ho Chi Minh City he was a respected controller and an absent father who loved his daughter from a dark room.

Here he can see time move, and touch it, and that's made him even more responsible and even more tired. Part of what
the player gives him is somebody who can take the watch for an hour.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** to find out what happened to Linh, and to hand over the watch.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. A trader brings a list from a camp of Australians, including people from Melbourne. Minh holds it for a full day
   without opening it.
2. The time slip in the riverbed grows dangerous, and something comes through it. You fight it with him, and he has to
   let you hold one side while he holds the other.
3. When it's over, he opens the list.

**Ending (sad):** Linh is on it, among the people who didn't survive the first winter in the camp. Minh doesn't speak for
a long time. Then he takes the tuning fork off his neck and strikes it, and listens to the A-note until it fades. He hands
the watch on the ridge to two young orc shamans he's been training, and he leaves it in their hands for a whole day, the
first day off he's had in more than twenty years. Nothing goes wrong. He says that's the worst part and the best part.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**Linh's Tuning Fork.** A steel tuning fork on a cord, tuned to A, its tines worn bright. His daughter gave it to him when
she left for Melbourne so he'd have her note with him. He gives it to you after the list. He says he doesn't need to
strike it to hear it any more.

## Voice notes

- Quiet, calm and formal. Contractions, but careful ones, and he always gives the sequence first.
- His English is precise, learned for aviation: clear, standard, and slightly clipped.
- He uses controller phrases without noticing: "say again", "copy", "you have control".
- He doesn't swear. When something goes wrong he gets calmer, and that's how you know.
- **He won't talk about** Linh until Companion, or the handover until Friend.
- **Habits:** counts things in sequence, adjusts his glasses before he speaks, touches the tuning fork, and keeps watch
  while others sleep.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** calls out the order of what's about to happen. Calm in a crisis, uncomfortable in chaos. Takes
  every watch at camp unless stopped. Very quiet after any death.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with calm, careful people, and with Marcus, who also watches the sky. Clashes with

- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is never handing over control. A skill idea to look at then: on a 10 to 15 turn cooldown,
