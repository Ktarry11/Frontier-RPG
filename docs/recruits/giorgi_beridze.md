## Who he is

Giorgi is unhurried. He's physically enormous and completely calm. He counts time in seasons, not days.

He chooses words like money. His English has grown in three years, and he still spends it carefully, every word deliberate.
What comes out is often better than what a fluent man would say, because he has no spare words to hide behind.

He's gentle and patient, with animals and people. He's also stubborn as the mountain, especially when nobody takes him
seriously.

He likes being alone. On the mountain he spoke to nobody for weeks and that was normal. Here, being alone is something people
worry about on his behalf, and he finds that both exhausting and touching.

He loves an argument with someone who knows what they're talking about. He's found a tauren herder who argues with him about
grazing, and he enjoys it enormously.

What annoys him: hurry, people who don't listen to the land, and not being taken seriously. What he loves: a good dog, a
fat flock, the smell of the mountain, and his wife's bread.

## Before

He grew up in Kazbegi, in the high Caucasus, where his family have kept sheep for longer than anyone wrote down. Every
summer he took the flock up to the high pasture: a dog, a herd, a wooden hut, and six months without a road.

He married Nino at 22. She was a schoolteacher in the village below, and she thought the mountain was an eccentricity he'd
grow out of. They had two daughters, Ana and Mariam. Every autumn he brought the flock down and came home to them for the
winter, and every spring he went back up.

His lead ewe wore a small brass bell. He's carried that bell since the day he came through.

## The Integration and what it cost

He was on the high pasture in late summer, with the flock, when it happened. He landed in the hills above Hillsbrad with the
lead ewe's bell in his hand. The flock was gone, and so was the dog.

It took him two seasons to adjust. The land here needed reading, and he reads land. What took longer was people.

He hasn't found Nino or the girls. The village was below the pasture, and he doesn't know if it came through.

## Now

He lives in the high ground above Tarren Mill, where the air is right and the ground tells the truth. He's found grazing he
says is as good as anything at home.

He wants a herd. He's serious, and nobody takes him seriously. It irritates him more than he lets on.

Every autumn, on roughly the right day, he walks down to lower ground for no reason anyone can see. He's never explained
it. He's bringing the herd down. There is no herd. He's knotted the brass bell to his belt so it can't ring.

## Hopes, dreams and fears

- **Hope:** a herd of his own.
- **Hope:** that Nino and the girls are together in a village somewhere.
- **Dream:** to bring a flock down in autumn and have Nino waiting at the bottom.
- **Fear:** that he's walking down every autumn to nobody.
- **Fear:** that his daughters are growing up without him.
- **Fear:** that Nino was right, and the mountain was a mistake.

## How he's changed

In Georgia he was a quiet shepherd who lived half his year alone and half his year at home.

Here he's alone all the time, and he's learned to let people worry about him. He's warmer than he was, and sadder. Part of
what the player gives him is somebody who takes him seriously.

## What he wants next (his goal and personal quest)

**His goal:** a herd, and to stop walking down to nothing.

**The personal quest (opens at Friend):**
1. Tarren Mill's sheep keep being taken by something from the hills. Giorgi says if he clears the hills, he'll take the flock
   up to the high pasture himself, and keep it safe.
2. You help him track and clear whatever's taking them.
3. Tarren Mill gives him the flock to keep for the summer. He takes it up.

**Ending (still unknown):** in autumn, on the right day, he brings the flock down, with the bell untied and ringing. Nobody
is waiting at the bottom. He hasn't found Nino or the girls on any list, and nobody knows if Kazbegi came through. He stands
at the bottom for a long time. Then he says that every autumn he'll bring the flock down and ring the bell, so if they're
anywhere near, they'll hear it. He keeps the flock, and Tarren Mill lets him. He's still listening for an answer.

## His keepsake (the new Collection tab)

**The Lead Ewe's Bell.** A small dented brass bell from the lead ewe of his flock in Kazbegi, carried knotted silent on his
belt for three years. He gives it to you after the first autumn with his new flock. He's put a new bell on the new lead ewe.
He's untied the old one, so it can ring now.

## Voice notes

- Slow, sparing and deliberate. Contractions, few words, and long pauses.
- His English is limited but exact, with Georgian and Russian slipping in: "kargi" (good), "modi" (come), "gaumarjos" when
  he raises a cup.
- He talks about people like weather and land: "she is a storm", "he has thin soil".
- He doesn't swear. When he's angry he goes silent and very large.
- **He won't talk about** Nino and the girls until Companion, or the autumn walk until Friend.
- **Habits:** walks slowly, touches the knotted bell, reads the grass, and toasts every meal.

## Seeds for the next waves (not decided, just where this points)

- **Wave 2, in groups:** slow and steady, irritated by rushing. Reads the land in every zone. Gentle with animals, fierce
  with anything that preys on them. Quiet for long stretches.
- **Wave 3, with others:** gets on with patient, outdoor people, especially Bakyt, another mountain herder. A big gentle
  presence for nervous people. Bored by chatterers.
- **Wave 4, his Sworn skill:** what he resolves is walking down to nobody. A skill idea to look at then: once a fight, he
  rings the bell, and every ally who hears it shakes off fear and slows the enemy around them.
