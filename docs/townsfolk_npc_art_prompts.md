# Townsfolk for every town: art prompts (12 sheets, 24 people each)

Kyle, 22 Nov 2026, while the towns were being populated: "Should we build more sprite sheets? Like 24/24 male/female for
different races like we did we blood elves and Caelora... (Especially humans!!!)", then "Our recruits all have unique
art. I'm just thinking npcs, quest givers, and fillers", and on the Earthborn: "Earthborne should adapt to the world.
But they can have some unique flavor. But not so unique we can't reuse".

What we had for NPCs that day: humans 14 men and 14 women, all medieval fantasy, nearly all white, half in armour; orcs
22, trolls 20, tauren 20 and Forsaken 16, each a single pool with men and women mixed. Forty-odd towns are getting
townsfolk, so faces repeat. These twelve sheets are the working people of each race: cooks, porters, elders, children's
minders, the people a town is made of. Same layout as the blood elf sheets (`docs/blood_elf_npc_art_prompts.md`) and the
Aetherian ones (`docs/recruit_art_prompts_sheet6.md`): front-only, so they're NPC looks, never player looks.

```

Each figure has the same sturdy proportion as the attached references, roughly 1 : 1.5 (width : height), and all 24 are the SAME height in the cell. No ground shadow, no ground plane, no text, no letters, no logos, no labels, no grid lines. Match the attached references EXACTLY in style: crisp detailed pixel art with a clean dark outline, the same head size, the same rich colours, consistent lighting from the top-left. THE PEOPLE: EARTHBORN MEN, ordinary people from every part of our modern world who woke up in a fantasy world three years ago and have made a life there. They have ADAPTED: they dress in this world's clothes, linen shirts, wool tunics, leather vests and jerkins, hide boots, cloaks, practical and patched. Their Earth past shows only in SMALL touches that could belong to anyone: a knitted beanie, a wool scarf, a pair of round glasses, a modern haircut, rolled sleeves, a canvas satchel, a plain wristwatch, a bandana. No logos, no printing, no technology, nothing that names a country. These are townsfolk and working people, not warriors: no armour except where it says so, no swords. Tired but capable, a little weathered, some cheerful. Ages from young men to old men. Every one is a different person with a different job, and you could tell any two apart at a glance.

The 24, in order, row by row:
Row 1: 1 a young man in a knitted beanie and a patched wool tunic, a small satchel across his chest; 2 a grey-bearded older man in round glasses and a long brown coat, a book under his arm; 3 a broad farmer in a straw hat, linen shirt and leather suspenders, a hoe-sized trowel at his belt; 4 a lean cook in a stained apron over a dark tunic, a bandana tied over his hair; 5 a carpenter with a pencil behind his ear and a leather tool belt; 6 a former office worker, neat short hair, a tidy grey vest over a linen shirt, a ledger in one hand.
```
